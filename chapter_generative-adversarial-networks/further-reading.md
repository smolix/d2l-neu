# Further Reading
:label:`sec_gan-further-reading`

The chapter taught a few ideas on problems small enough to check: what a critic estimates, what a ranking critic changes, why the game needs a penalty to converge, how to score images and condition them, and where a learned comparison is still needed. In the decade after 2014 the field produced far more objectives, architectures, scores and systems than these ideas require. This page is a guide to the ones a reader of the chapter is best placed to read next. Each entry states the problem it addresses, what it changes relative to the chapter, and why a reader might go there. The topics follow the order of the chapter, and nothing in the book depends on them.

## Objectives and Critic Constraints

**Least squares as a divergence.** :numref:`sec_gan_objectives` derived the divergence that the least-squares *critic* loss evaluates. :citet:`Mao.Li.Xie.ea.2017` started from the other end and asked which target codes make the *generator's* loss a known divergence: with the real and fake codes two apart and the generator's target one above the fake code, minimizing the generator's loss minimizes the Pearson $\chi^2$ divergence between $p + q$ and $2q$. The derivation is a short exercise in the template of that section, and it shows how much the coding constants matter.

**Output activations for $f$-GAN critics.** The variational bound of :numref:`sec_gan_objectives` asks the critic's output $t$ to lie where the conjugate $f^*(t)$ is finite. :citet:`Nowozin.Cseke.Tomioka.2016` meet this with an output activation chosen per divergence. The forward-KL conjugate is finite on all of $\mathbb{R}$ and needs none; the reverse-KL conjugate is finite only for $t < 0$, enforced by $-e^{-v}$ on the network's raw output $v$; the Jensen--Shannon conjugate is finite for $t < \log 2$, enforced by $\log 2 - \mathrm{softplus}(-v)$; and the original GAN's conjugate is finite for $t < 0$, enforced by $-\mathrm{softplus}(-v)$, the log-sigmoid that :numref:`sec_basic_gan` already uses. Their table is the place to look before training a divergence that the chapter did not.

**Three ways to a Lipschitz critic.** The Wasserstein-1 game needs a 1-Lipschitz critic, and no network is 1-Lipschitz by construction. Weight clipping :cite:`Arjovsky.Chintala.Bottou.2017` and spectral normalization :cite:`Miyato.Kataoka.Koyama.ea.2018` bound the product of the layers' Lipschitz constants. The bound holds everywhere but is often loose, so the critic is confined to a much smaller class than the constraint allows. The one-centered gradient penalty :cite:`Gulrajani.Ahmed.Arjovsky.ea.2017` constrains the gradient's norm only at sampled points, which is tight there and says nothing elsewhere. These three guarantees decide how close a trained WGAN critic can come to the Kantorovich potential; :numref:`sec_gan_convergence` explains why zero-centered penalties replaced the one-centered one.

**The relativistic average critic.** :citet:`Jolicoeur-Martineau.2019` proposed, besides the pairing loss of :numref:`sec_gan_relativistic`, a cheaper variant that compares each sample's score with the mean score of the other batch: a real sample against the average generated score, and a generated sample against the average real score. The comparison makes the loss shift-invariant, like the pairing loss, but each sample is still judged against one number, a threshold rather than a rank. The super-resolution network ESRGAN trains with it :cite:`Wang.Yu.Wu.ea.2018`, so it is the variant a reader most often meets in image-restoration code.

## Architectures and Training Devices

:numref:`sec_dcgan` lists the architectural devices of 2015 to 2021 and the problem each addressed. Two lines of work go beyond its table.

**Pretrained features in the critic.** Projected GAN discriminates in the feature pyramid of a frozen pretrained classifier and reports reaching previous best FID values up to forty times faster :cite:`Sauer.Chitta.Muller.ea.2021`. The frozen features change what the critic can estimate: it sees the log ratio only through features trained for another task. StyleGAN-T keeps the construction but uses self-supervised features, partly to reduce the overlap with the network that computes FID :cite:`Sauer.Karras.Laine.ea.2023`; the second entry under Evaluation explains why that overlap matters.

**Training at scale.** Large runs keep R3GAN's recipe but schedule its constants over a burn-in of 20 million images instead of fixing them :cite:`Huang.Gokaslan.Kuleshov.ea.2024`. The learning rate decays from $2 \cdot 10^{-4}$ to $5 \cdot 10^{-5}$ and the penalty weight $\gamma$ falls tenfold with it, from 0.05 to 0.005 on CIFAR-10 and from 150 to 15 on FFHQ at $256 \times 256$. Adam's $\beta_2$ rises from 0.9 to 0.99, and the EMA half-life grows from zero to half a million images on FFHQ and five million on CIFAR-10. The same paper reports that mixed-precision training fails with FP16 and works with BFloat16.

**The cost of the penalty.** An input-gradient penalty needs a second backward pass through the critic. StyleGAN2 computes $R_1$ only once every 16 minibatches, with its weight scaled up to compensate, and calls this lazy regularization :cite:`Karras.Laine.Aittala.ea.2020`. R3GAN lists it among the ten devices it removes when it rebuilds StyleGAN2 from a well-behaved loss :cite:`Huang.Gokaslan.Kuleshov.ea.2024`. A reader who needs the penalty at a large scale will find the trade-off between its cost and its frequency in these two papers. Adversarial post-training of a video diffusion transformer could not take the second backward pass at all, because its fused attention kernels and sharded training did not support it. It penalizes instead the squared change of the critic's output when a real sample is perturbed by small Gaussian noise, which for small noise approximates the $R_1$ integrand scaled by the noise variance; without this term its training collapsed :cite:`Lin.Xia.Ren.ea.2025`.

## Evaluation

**Fidelity and coverage separately.** FID combines two failures in one number: generated samples that look wrong, and data modes that no generated sample covers. The precision and recall of :citet:`Kynkaanniemi.Karras.Laine.ea.2019` separate them through neighborhoods in feature space. Precision is the fraction of generated samples that fall inside the estimated support of the real features, recall the fraction of real samples inside the support of the generated ones. A generator that collapses to one good image keeps a high precision while its recall falls to zero, which FID reports only as a worse score. R3GAN reports recall next to FID for this reason.

**The feature network behind the score.** FID and KID are only as good as the features they compare. :citet:`Kynkaanniemi.Karras.Aittala.ea.2023` show that Inception features respond strongly to ImageNet class information, so a generator can lower FID by matching the distribution of ImageNet classes in its samples without improving them visibly, and critics built on ImageNet-pretrained features can lower FID in the same way. :citet:`Stein.Cresswell.Hosseinzadeh.ea.2023` compare sixteen scores with a large human study, find that Inception-based FID underrates the realism of diffusion models, and recommend DINOv2 features instead. CMMD replaces both the Inception features and the Gaussian fit of FID: it computes the MMD of :numref:`sec_gan_objectives` with a Gaussian kernel on CLIP embeddings, which is unbiased and assumes nothing about the shape of the feature distribution :cite:`Jayasumana.Ramalingam.Veit.ea.2024`. A reader who trains a critic on pretrained features should evaluate with a different feature network.

**Comparisons at the level of models.** The ranking critic of :numref:`sec_gan_relativistic` fits a Bradley--Terry model to comparisons between samples. Chatbot Arena fits the same model to human votes between pairs of language models and ranks the models by the fitted scores, with bootstrap confidence intervals on each score :cite:`Chiang.Zheng.Sheng.ea.2024`. It is the most widely used instance of the construction outside GANs.

## Conditions, Translation and Text

**Modulation as a general layer.** The modulation $a(c) \odot \phi + b(c)$ of :numref:`sec_gan_conditional` has a family of variants that differ in how the feature map is normalized first and where the scale and shift come from. Conditional batch normalization computes them from a question's embedding to steer an image network :cite:`De-Vries.Strub.Mary.ea.2017`, and FiLM drops the normalization and makes the layer a general conditioning device :cite:`Perez.Strub.De-Vries.ea.2018`. Adaptive instance normalization normalizes each image's channels separately and takes the scale and shift from the statistics of a style image :cite:`Huang.Belongie.2017`; StyleGAN's style injection descends from it. SPADE makes the modulation spatial: a semantic layout supplies a separate scale and shift at each location, so the generator can paint a scene from a label map without normalization washing out the layout :cite:`Park.Liu.Wang.ea.2019`.

**Translation systems.** :numref:`sec_gan_conditional` explains why pix2pix adds a critic to its $L_1$ loss and why CycleGAN's cycle loss does not identify the map. The two papers add the systems around these ideas :cite:`Isola.Zhu.Zhou.ea.2017,Zhu.Park.Isola.ea.2017`: an encoder--decoder generator with skip connections between matching resolutions (a U-Net), so that the source's layout reaches the output directly; a patch critic that scores overlapping patches instead of the whole image, which is the local comparison the ambiguity argument asks for; and in CycleGAN a second generator and critic for the reverse direction, trained jointly with the first. They are the place to start for image-to-image applications.

**Text as the condition.** A caption is a sequence of tokens, not one class. GigaGAN feeds it to the generator through cross-attention from image features to the caption's tokens, as text-to-image diffusion models do :cite:`Kang.Zhu.Zhang.ea.2023`. By 2026 diffusion and autoregressive models dominate general text-to-image synthesis. GigaGAN is the reference for what a single-pass adversarial generator achieves at that task, and for the architectural changes it needed to scale.

## Fixed Discrepancies and Learned Ones

The chapter's critics are learned. A natural question is whether a fixed discrepancy, with no second network, would serve as well. Generative moment matching networks minimize the fixed-kernel MMD of :numref:`sec_gan_objectives`, with neither a critic nor an inner optimization :cite:`Li.Swersky.Zemel.2015`. IMLE pairs each data point with its nearest generated sample, which directly penalizes any data mode left without a nearby sample :cite:`Li.Malik.2018`. Sliced Wasserstein generators average one-dimensional transport costs along random projections :cite:`Deshpande.Zhang.Schwing.2018`, and Sinkhorn divergences replace the critic with a differentiable fixed-point solver that interpolates between optimal transport and MMD :cite:`Genevay.Peyre.Cuturi.2018`.

On natural images, several of these became effective only after a learned component was added. Fixed-kernel moment matching was not competitive with contemporary GANs, and MMD-GAN learns the kernel adversarially :cite:`Li.Chang.Cheng.ea.2017`. Sliced-Wasserstein generators added a log-loss discriminator to choose informative projection directions, because random directions carry little signal in high dimensions :cite:`Deshpande.Zhang.Schwing.2018`. Sinkhorn generators show the trade-off within one method: on CIFAR-10, the two regularization settings nearest unregularized optimal transport score about one Inception point below the setting nearest MMD :cite:`Genevay.Peyre.Cuturi.2018`. This agrees with the estimation argument of :numref:`sec_gan_objectives`: a discrepancy can separate distributions in principle and still be hard to estimate from samples in high dimensions. :citet:`Sriperumbudur.Fukumizu.Gretton.ea.2012` study the empirical estimators of MMD, Wasserstein-1 and related metrics and their convergence rates, the theory behind that argument. :citet:`Binkowski.Sutherland.Arbel.ea.2018` analyze the learned kernels of MMD-GAN and, from the same analysis, propose KID, the score of :numref:`sec_dcgan`.

## Adversarial Terms in Recent Systems

:numref:`sec_gan_beyond` explains why an adversarial term survives in one-step generators, autoencoders and vocoders. The systems below show the range of reasons; the numbers are those their papers reported in 2024 and 2025.

**Distillation with real data.** A distilled student that only matches its teacher can be no better than the teacher. DMD2 adds an adversarial loss against real images, which lets its ImageNet $64 \times 64$ student reach FID 1.28, below its teacher :cite:`Yin.Gharbi.Park.ea.2024`. LADD reuses the teacher diffusion model as the critic's backbone and finds that synthetic teacher samples train it well enough to drop the distillation loss, leaving only the adversarial objective :cite:`Sauer.Boesel.Dockhorn.ea.2024`. The FLUX.1-schnell model card states that its twelve-billion-parameter rectified-flow transformer was trained this way to generate in one to four steps :cite:`BlackForestLabs.2024`.

**Few steps without a critic.** An adversarial term is not the only route to few-step generation. MeanFlow trains a one-step generator from scratch, with no teacher, distillation or critic, and reports FID 3.43 on ImageNet $256 \times 256$ :cite:`Geng.Deng.Bai.ea.2025`. Continuous-time consistency models report FID 1.88 on ImageNet $512 \times 512$ with two network evaluations and no adversarial loss :cite:`Lu.Song.2025`. Both replace the critic with a regression target designed to stay informative at low step counts, and they make the case that few-step generation does not need a critic.

**Vocoders and video.** RAF applies the pairing loss of :numref:`sec_gan_relativistic` to waveforms; a BigVGAN-base trained with it, with twelve percent of the parameters, outperforms the full BigVGAN trained with the least-squares loss in perceptual quality :cite:`Lee.Choi.2026`. WaveFM reaches single-step synthesis without a critic, by flow matching followed by consistency distillation :cite:`Luo.Miao.Duan.2025`. For video, adversarial post-training fine-tunes a diffusion generator against real data with a zero-centered gradient penalty from the family of :numref:`sec_gan_convergence`, and produces two seconds of $1280 \times 720$ video at 24 frames per second in one forward evaluation :cite:`Lin.Xia.Ren.ea.2025`.

**Likelihood models as critics at scale.** Direct discriminative optimization, the construction of :numref:`sec_gan_beyond` that uses the likelihood model itself as the critic, fine-tunes pretrained EDM diffusion models and lowers FID from 1.79 to 1.30 on CIFAR-10 and from 1.58 to 0.97 on ImageNet $64 \times 64$ :cite:`Zheng.Chen.Chen.ea.2025`.
