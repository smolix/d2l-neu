# Further Reading
:label:`sec_gan-further-reading`

A reader who has worked through this chapter has seen a few ideas on problems small enough to check: what a critic estimates, what a pairing critic changes, why the game needs a penalty, how to score and condition images, and where a learned comparison is still needed. The literature on these ideas is far larger, and most of it can be skipped. Each entry below states the problem it addresses and what it changes relative to the chapter. The topics follow the order of the chapter, and nothing in the book depends on them.

## First Reading

If you read only five things, read these, in this order.

1. :citet:`Mescheder.Geiger.Nowozin.2018`, for the theorem that :numref:`sec_gan_convergence` quotes without proof: near an equilibrium at which the generator reproduces the data, zero-centered penalties make gradient steps converge for networks, not only on the Dirac-GAN. Read it for the assumptions, which are stronger than they look, and for its tour of methods on the Dirac-GAN, where weight clipping and the one-centered penalty fail.
2. R3GAN :cite:`Huang.Gokaslan.Kuleshov.ea.2024`, the recipe the chapter adopts. The paper argues that a convergent loss makes most architectural devices of earlier GANs unnecessary, on the evidence of one rebuilt network and a handful of datasets. Read its StackedMNIST ablation with the caveat of :numref:`sec_gan_convergence` in mind: StyleGAN2 trains well on faces with $R_1$ alone. Its large runs add one practice worth copying, a penalty weight $\gamma$ that decays together with the learning rate.
3. :citet:`Kynkaanniemi.Karras.Laine.ea.2019`, the source of the precision and recall of :numref:`subsec_gan_precision_recall`. On real image generators it shows how the truncation trick of :numref:`sec_gan_conditional` trades one for the other, a trade that a single FID hides.
4. The entry on the feature network behind the score, below, before you report any FID.
5. DMD2 :cite:`Yin.Gharbi.Park.ea.2024`, for where the critic works today. A student trained only to imitate its teacher can at best equal it. DMD2 adds a critic against real images, and with real images in the loss a student can surpass its teacher: its one-step ImageNet student scores a lower FID than its many-step teacher.

The entries on architectures, conditioning and translation are reference material, for when a project needs them.

## Objectives and Game Dynamics

**The generator's side of least squares.** :numref:`sec_gan_objectives` found that the least-squares critic evaluates a quarter of the bounded divergence $\chi^2(p \,\|\, m)$, with $m = (p+q)/2$. :citet:`Mao.Li.Xie.ea.2017` ask what the generator reaches. They add to its loss $\tfrac12 \mathbb{E}_q[(D - c)^2]$, for a target code $c$, the term $\tfrac12 \mathbb{E}_p[(D - c)^2]$, constant at a fixed critic. At the optimal critic for real code $b$ and fake code $a$ the sum is $\tfrac14 (a + b - 2c)^2 + \tfrac14 (b - a)^2\, \chi^2(p \,\|\, m)$. Any two distinct codes thus lead to the same divergence, up to scale and an additive constant. The paper's codes, real and fake two apart with the target midway, remove the constant; the chapter's zero--one coding gives $\tfrac14$ plus exactly the critic's gap, and the paper reports that the two train alike. Like every entry of :numref:`tab_gan_loss_divergences`, this is a value at the optimal critic; against a fixed critic the generator descends something else.

**Output activations for $f$-GAN critics.** The variational bound of :numref:`sec_gan_objectives` needs the critic's output $t$ where the conjugate $f^*(t)$ is finite. :citet:`Nowozin.Cseke.Tomioka.2016` enforce this with an activation on the raw output $v$: none for forward KL; $-e^{-v}$ for reverse KL ($t < 0$); $\log 2 - \mathrm{softplus}(-v)$ for Jensen--Shannon ($t < \log 2$); and $-\mathrm{softplus}(-v)$, the log-sigmoid of :numref:`sec_basic_gan`, for the original GAN ($t < 0$). Consult their table before training any other divergence, and then distrust the estimate a little. The penalty $f^*(T)$ is charged only through samples of $q$, so a critic that overshoots where $q$ is small is rarely charged in a minibatch, and the estimate can exceed the divergence it is meant to bound (Exercise :exref:`ex_objectives-estimation-and-dimension`).

**Relativistic divergences.** :citet:`Jolicoeur-Martineau.2020` shows that the pairing game of :numref:`sec_gan_relativistic` remains a divergence for any concave payoff $\ell$ with $\ell(0) = 0$, $\ell'(0) \neq 0$ and a positive supremum attained, or approached, at a positive argument. Shifted to $\ell(0) = 0$, the relativistic least-squares and hinge losses qualify, although their games have no known closed-form value.

**Games and their dynamics.** Near its equilibrium the Dirac-GAN of :numref:`sec_gan_convergence` is the bilinear game $\min_\theta \max_\psi \theta\psi$, the standard test problem of the optimization literature on games, with its step scaled by $\ell'(0)$. On $\theta\psi$, a simultaneous gradient step of size $h$ multiplies the distance to the equilibrium by $\sqrt{1 + h^2}$. The *extragradient* method :cite:`Korpelevich.1976` takes a trial step and then moves from the current point with the gradient at the trial point; the same algebra gives $\sqrt{1 - h^2 + h^4}$, below one for every $0 < h < 1$. The logistic loss has $\ell'(0) = \tfrac12$, so the step $0.2$ of :numref:`sec_gan_convergence` is $h = 0.1$ here: near the equilibrium the first method doubles the distance in about 140 steps and the second halves it in about as many. (The run of that section starts farther out, where the game is not bilinear, and doubled its distance in about 300 steps.) Optimistic gradient descent reuses the previous step's gradient instead of taking a trial step :cite:`Daskalakis.Ilyas.Syrgkanis.ea.2018`, and negative momentum makes alternating updates converge :cite:`Gidel.Askari-Hemmat.Pezeshki.ea.2019`. These methods change the optimizer and keep the game. The penalties of :numref:`sec_gan_convergence` change the game and keep the optimizer, which is the route large image GANs took.

## Fixed Discrepancies and Learned Ones

The chapter's critics are learned. Could a fixed discrepancy, with no second network, serve as well? Generative moment matching networks minimize the fixed-kernel MMD of :numref:`sec_gan_objectives` :cite:`Li.Swersky.Zemel.2015`. IMLE pairs each data point with its nearest generated sample, which penalizes any mode left without a nearby sample :cite:`Li.Malik.2018`. Sliced Wasserstein generators average one-dimensional transport costs along random projections, and the paper that proposes them already adds a discriminator that learns the features in which to project :cite:`Deshpande.Zhang.Schwing.2018`.

That last move is typical: on natural images several fixed discrepancies became competitive only once something was learned, and MMD-GAN learns its kernel adversarially :cite:`Li.Chang.Cheng.ea.2017`. Estimation is not the obstacle; :numref:`subsec_gan_objectives_estimation` found that a fixed-kernel MMD converges at the rate $n^{-1/2}$ in any dimension. The trouble is what a fixed kernel on pixels measures. Two different distributions of images look alike to it, so its value is small next to the estimator's noise and tells the generator little about where its samples are wrong; random directions in pixel space fare no better. A learned kernel or projection looks where the two distributions differ. :citet:`Binkowski.Sutherland.Arbel.ea.2018` analyze the learned kernels of MMD-GAN and, from the same analysis, propose KID.

## Networks, Features and Data

**Architectural devices, 2015 to 2021.** Between DCGAN and the plain backbone of :numref:`sec_dcgan`, progress on image GANs came mostly from devices, each aimed at one failure. Progressive growing adds layers as training proceeds, against instability at high resolution :cite:`Karras.Aila.Laine.ea.2017`. Spectral normalization bounds the critic's slopes (:numref:`sec_gan_objectives`), and self-attention gives a convolutional network long-range structure :cite:`Zhang.Goodfellow.Metaxas.ea.2019`. The scale of BigGAN delays collapse without removing it :cite:`Brock.Donahue.Simonyan.2019`. StyleGAN's mapping network untangles the latent space, weight demodulation removes the droplet artifacts of its normalization, and alias-free resampling stops texture from sticking to pixel coordinates :cite:`Karras.Laine.Aila.2019,Karras.Laine.Aittala.ea.2020,Karras.Aittala.Laine.ea.2021`. The R3GAN paper argues that a well-behaved loss makes most of them unnecessary; StyleGAN-family models that keep them remain in wide use.

**The feature network behind the score.** FID and KID are only as good as the features they compare. :citet:`Kynkaanniemi.Karras.Aittala.ea.2023` show that Inception features respond strongly to ImageNet classes, so a generator can lower FID by matching the class distribution of its samples without visibly improving them. The DINOv2 features that :numref:`sec_dcgan` mentions were recommended by a large human study of seventeen scores, in which they agreed with people better than Inception's :cite:`Stein.Cresswell.Hosseinzadeh.ea.2023`, and EDM2 reports the distance in them next to FID :cite:`Karras.Aittala.Kynkaanniemi.ea.2024`. Two more pitfalls lie in how the score is computed. Aliased resizing before the feature network shifts FID by as much as the differences between published methods :cite:`Parmar.Zhang.Zhu.2022`. And the finite-sample bias of FID depends on the generator, so at small $n$ it can reverse a ranking :cite:`Chong.Forsyth.2020`; Exercise :exref:`ex_dcgan-measure-finite-sample-bias` measures it on the sprites.

**Pretrained features in the critic.** The same features can sit inside the critic. Projected GAN discriminates in the features of a frozen pretrained classifier and trains far faster than a critic learned from pixels :cite:`Sauer.Chitta.Muller.ea.2021`, and StyleGAN-T moved to self-supervised features partly to reduce the overlap with FID's network :cite:`Sauer.Karras.Laine.ea.2023`. If your critic uses pretrained features, evaluate with a different feature network.

**The cost of the penalty.** An input-gradient penalty needs a second backward pass through the critic. StyleGAN2 takes it only every 16 minibatches, with the weight scaled up, and calls this lazy regularization :cite:`Karras.Laine.Aittala.ea.2020`. Adversarial post-training of a video diffusion transformer could not take it at all: its fused attention kernels, fully sharded training and activation checkpointing did not support a second backward pass. It penalizes instead the squared change of the critic's output when a real sample receives small Gaussian noise, an approximation of $R_1$; without that term its training collapsed. With it, the post-trained model generates high-resolution clips in one forward evaluation :cite:`Lin.Xia.Ren.ea.2025`.

**Training on limited data.** A critic that sees each training image many times can learn to recognize it, which :numref:`sec_dcgan` checks on the sprites. The remedy of :citet:`Karras.Aittala.Hellsten.ea.2020` and :citet:`Zhao.Liu.Lin.ea.2020` augments both inputs of the critic, inside the generator's gradient path. Augmenting only the real images, as the flips of :numref:`sec_dcgan` do, teaches the generator the augmented distribution, which for flips is harmless only if each image is as likely as its mirror image. Applied to both inputs, an augmentation leaves the matching problem intact as long as it does not map two different distributions to the same one; Karras et al. call such an augmentation *non-leaking* (Exercise :exref:`ex_dcgan-augmentation-leaks`). Their adaptive discriminator augmentation sets the probability of the augmentations during training, from the mean sign of the critic's scores on training images, and trains StyleGAN2 on a few thousand images. With a pairing critic the controller needs a statistic that survives adding a constant to every score (Exercise :exref:`ex_dcgan-scale-free-overfitting-statistic`).

## Conditions and Translation

**Modulation as a general layer.** The modulation $a(c) \odot h + b(c)$ of :numref:`sec_gan_conditional` has variants that differ in what normalizes $h$ first and in where the scale and shift come from. Conditional batch normalization takes them from an embedding of the condition, after batch normalization; FiLM makes the modulation a layer of its own, after any normalization or none :cite:`De-Vries.Strub.Mary.ea.2017,Perez.Strub.De-Vries.ea.2018`. Adaptive instance normalization takes them from the feature statistics of a style image, after instance normalization; it is the ancestor of StyleGAN's style injection :cite:`Huang.Belongie.2017`. SPADE computes them location by location from a semantic layout, after batch normalization :cite:`Park.Liu.Wang.ea.2019`.

**Translation without pairs.** :numref:`sec_gan_beyond` explains why pix2pix adds a critic to its $L_1$ loss. CycleGAN :cite:`Zhu.Park.Isola.ea.2017` gives up the pairs: each direction plays an unconditional game against its target collection, and a cycle-consistency loss asks that translating forward and back return the input. The cycle loss constrains the map without identifying it; when the target carries less information than the source, the generator can hide the rest in imperceptible signals :cite:`Chu.Zhmoginov.Sandler.2017`. Much image-to-image work still starts from the networks of pix2pix :cite:`Isola.Zhu.Zhou.ea.2017`: its U-Net generator, and its critic that scores overlapping patches, which CycleGAN reuses.

## Adversarial Terms in Recent Systems

:numref:`sec_gan_beyond` explains why an adversarial term survives in one-step generators, autoencoders and vocoders. The systems below show where it works today, and where a regression target has replaced it.

**Tokenizers.** ViTok measured both sides of the trade-off between sharpness and squared error that :numref:`sec_gan_beyond` describes. Adversarial fine-tuning of its decoder lowered the pixel-fidelity scores PSNR and SSIM, and it improved the FID of reconstructions far more than that of images generated in its latent space :cite:`Hansen-Estruch.Yan.Chung.ea.2025`. A sharper decoder renders the codes it is given more convincingly; it cannot repair codes that the latent generator got wrong.

**Latent adversarial distillation.** LADD changes three things in the ADD of :numref:`sec_gan_beyond`. Its critic reads the teacher diffusion model's own features in latent space instead of DINOv2's features of pixels, it compares the student with images the teacher generates, and it trains with the adversarial objective alone :cite:`Sauer.Boesel.Dockhorn.ea.2024`. FLUX.1-schnell, a twelve-billion-parameter rectified-flow transformer, was trained this way to sample in one to four steps :cite:`BlackForestLabs.2024`. SDXL-Lightning also reports FID on image patches and finds larger differences between few-step students there :cite:`Lin.Wang.Yang.2024`, as one would expect: the ambiguity a critic resolves sits in fine detail.

**Few steps without a critic.** MeanFlow trains a one-step generator from scratch, with no teacher and no critic :cite:`Geng.Deng.Bai.ea.2025`, and continuous-time consistency models sample in two network evaluations without an adversarial loss :cite:`Lu.Song.2025`. Both replace the critic by a regression target designed to stay informative at low step counts.

**Vocoders.** RAF trains the universal vocoder BigVGAN :cite:`Lee.Ping.Ginsburg.ea.2023` with the pairing loss of :numref:`sec_gan_relativistic` and critics on self-supervised speech features, and reports that a base-size model trained this way surpasses the full-size least-squares BigVGAN in perceptual quality :cite:`Lee.Choi.2026`.

**Likelihood models as their own critics.** A critic can also improve a slow many-step model itself instead of distilling it, and it need not be a second network. Direct discriminative optimization (DDO) :cite:`Zheng.Chen.Chen.ea.2025` takes a pretrained likelihood model $q_\theta$, such as a diffusion model, freezes a reference copy $q_{\textrm{ref}}$, and writes the critic as

$$
D_\theta(x) = \log\big(q_\theta(x)/q_{\textrm{ref}}(x)\big).
$$
:eqlabel:`eq_beyond_ddo`

Training this critic by logistic regression, real data against samples of $q_{\textrm{ref}}$, changes $q_\theta$ itself, with no discriminator network and no second player. If training reaches $q_\theta = p$, then $D_\theta = \log(p/q_{\textrm{ref}})$, the optimal critic between the data and the reference model (Exercise :exref:`ex_adversarial-losses-ddo-plays-log-loss`). In this way DDO improves pretrained diffusion models that were already among the best by FID. It runs in rounds, each replacing the reference $q_{\textrm{ref}}$ by the previous result, and scales the critic of :eqref:`eq_beyond_ddo` to $\beta \log(q_\theta / q_{\textrm{ref}})$ with $\beta$ between 0.01 and 0.1. Let us look at the idealized case, a model family that holds every density and rounds trained to their optimum. The optimal critic is still $\log(p / q_{\textrm{ref}})$, so up to the normalization of $q$, which matters only at second order near $p$, a round ends where $\beta \log(q / q_{\textrm{ref}}) = \log(p / q_{\textrm{ref}})$. Rearranged, $\log(q/p) = (1 - 1/\beta) \log(q_{\textrm{ref}}/p)$: each round multiplies the log ratio by $1 - 1/\beta$. The rounds converge to $p$ only for $\beta > \tfrac12$, and at $\beta = 0.05$ each round multiplies the error by $-19$. In a restricted family, a round that returns its reference sits at a stationary point of the likelihood, since there the round's loss has $\beta/2$ times the gradient of the negative log-likelihood (the same exercise shows this for $\beta = 1$). The reported gains can only come from what the idealization leaves out: a pretrained model away from a stationary point of its likelihood, a ratio estimated from denoising losses, and finite training in each round.
