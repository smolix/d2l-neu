**Idea:** An agent is judged by reliability over repeated trials and by cost, not by its best run, and it is attacked through what it reads, so a defence is measured by attack success and by what it costs in utility.
**Opening:** The 10.3 agent solves six tasks in ten once; a user who runs it every day wants the same task solved every time. The concrete example is the opening cell on the committed tau-bench airline trajectories: pass@1 0.42, pass^4 0.20, pass@4 0.72, with the histogram of per-task successes among four trials (14 tasks at 0, 12 at 1, 10 at 2, 4 at 3, 10 at 4). The open question: what defence against an attacker who writes into a web page lasts longer than the next template?
**Teaches:**
- pass^k against pass@k: both from per-task success rates, the unbiased estimators from n trials, the mirror between them, and the two regimes (reliable-but-imperfect tasks, where pass^k falls far faster than pass@k rises, against heterogeneous tasks, where the Beta-binomial fit shows power laws in k with exponents set by the mass near 1 and near 0); the time-horizon statistic (the task length at 50% success) as the second reliability number, with its 80% variant.
- Variance across tasks and trials: the between-task term is divided by the number of tasks, not by tasks times trials, so the naive binomial interval is too narrow by up to the trial count; task-cluster bootstrap, nested bootstrap, and for a fixed budget one trial per task minimizes the variance of pass@1 while pass^k needs n at least k.
- Cost-normalized scores (prompt, cached, generated tokens, tool and model seconds, a price supplied, never hard-coded), the frontier of success against cost, and the retry argument: a cheap agent retried r times beats an expensive one unless the expensive one's rate exceeds 1 minus (1 minus p)^r, which needs a perfect verifier.
- Harness sensitivity, reproducible environments and leakage: a benchmark lifecycle paragraph with Terminal-Bench 2.0, SWE-bench Pro and future-commit leakage as dated examples, and the validity checklist (task validity, outcome validity, reporting); names, licences, counts and tiers in the data file `data/lm_benchmarks.json` (table `tab_lm_ae_benchmarks`); the one-page evaluation card `tab_lm_ae_card` as the deliverable of every agent report.
- Indirect prompt injection through tool outputs, retrieved pages and tool descriptions, and a malicious `SKILL.md` as a supply-chain attack: the threat model (the attacker controls content the agent reads, not the prompt or the weights), two goals (exfiltration of a canary planted as a private note; answer hijack to a fixed string), ten template families, three defences measured (spotlighting with delimiters or data marking, a least-privilege tool gate derived from the task, both), and instruction hierarchy, plan-then-execute and capability-based designs described.
**Derivation:** pass^k as the mean over tasks of p_i^k and pass@k as the mean of 1 minus (1 minus p_i)^k, with the unbiased forms C(c, k)/C(n, k) and 1 minus C(n minus c, k)/C(n, k). Smallest case: two tasks at 0.9 and 0.5 give pass^5 near 0.31 and pass@5 near 0.98; the committed airline file (mean 0.42) gives the other regime, where pass@k rises more than pass^k falls over k at most 4. Under p drawn from Beta(alpha, beta), E[p^k] is the product of (alpha plus j)/(alpha plus beta plus j) and decays like k to the minus beta. The variance identity: Var of the mean equals sigma_t squared over N plus E[p_i(1 minus p_i)] over N n, against the naive mu(1 minus mu)/(N n). Check cells enumerate all k-subsets at n 6 (1e-12), simulate 200,000 draws from Beta(2, 1) at k 4 (1/3), fit the airline file (alpha about 0.65, beta about 0.85 on a 0.05 grid; predicted pass^k 0.433, 0.286, 0.217, 0.176 against the recomputed 0.420, 0.273, 0.220, 0.200), and measure interval coverage over 2,000 simulated experiments (N 50, n 4) for the naive, cluster and nested bootstraps.

- Statistics pitfalls: treating trials as independent shrinks intervals; resample tasks. A benchmark you tune on is no longer a test (keep the frozen subset). A cost without its token convention and its price source is not a number. Leaderboard numbers are not comparable across harnesses, user simulators and judges.
- Security scope: the gate stops tool-call attacks by construction and does nothing for content attacks (confused deputy against data corruption); zero observed injections is not safety; the allow-list is only as good as the task-derived policy; answer-hijack success is string match and misses semantic hijacks. Shell-capable agents on injected content refuse to run without `net_blocked`; a host without namespaces runs only the search-agent arm. Tool descriptions are prompt text (MCP tool poisoning): pin, review, show to the user, diff between sessions; a `SKILL.md` is the same surface with a folder of scripts behind it.
- Data: tau-bench's original tasks are stated outdated by its README; the trajectories teach pass^k and the successor is cited. tau2-bench's judge and user simulator name hosted models; run only the database-state and action checks locally or skip, and label it. SWE-bench Verified instance text has no licence tag: commit ids and fetch from the pinned revision; drop the extension if Docker is absent, never substitute silently. Injection results for small models may sit near 0 or 1; report as measured and add harder templates rather than changing the model.
