**Idea:** In a multi-turn episode only the agent's tokens are actions, the environment's tokens are transitions, and the loss mask is what makes the policy gradient correct.
**Opening:** The 10.3 agent's episode interleaves its own tokens with tool outputs it never produced; feed the whole transcript to 8.4's trainer and it learns to predict, and then to invent, the observations. The concrete example is the opening cell: 10.1's loop with `search_book` on 100 development questions, Qwen3-1.7B zero-shot, with exact match, mean searches and the fraction without a final answer, next to the single-turn RAG baseline. The open question: how is reward given for a late failure that an early search caused?
**Teaches:**
- The episode as an MDP with tool outputs as transitions: the trajectory probability factorizes into policy terms and environment terms, the environment terms do not depend on theta, so the gradient sums the score only over agent tokens; an unmasked loss adds a term that is nonzero whenever the reward correlates with the observation, and a baseline that depends on the observation is biased.
- Trajectory-level against turn-level rewards (reward-to-go with a history-dependent baseline; earlier rewards drop out of later turns); clipping and group advantages applied at masked positions only; the three aggregations (token mean, sequence mean, constant divisor) and what each rewards.
- Environments as code: a search environment over the book corpus with append-only prompts (token-in, token-out, special-token parsing off so a retrieved passage cannot forge a turn boundary), throughput as the bottleneck (environment idle fraction, search latency against a sandboxed `pytest`), and asynchronous rollouts with a staleness cap in one paragraph (8.5 owns the systems).
- Search and software agents as the shipped forms (Search-R1, SWE-RL), task and environment synthesis at scale (the question-synthesis recipe here; Kimi K2's agentic data synthesis as the pointer), and reward hacking in agentic settings (editing the test, listing several answers for a lenient verifier).
**Derivation:** The multi-turn policy gradient with its mask (`eq_lm_arl_mt_gradient`): the gradient of the expected reward is the expectation of (R minus b) times the sum over agent tokens of the score function, with no observation term; the masked clipped loss divides by the count of masked tokens. Smallest case: a two-turn toy with a softmax over three queries, a stochastic observation in {0, 1} with fixed environment probabilities, a softmax answer conditioned on the observation, and reward 1 when the answer matches f(query, observation), with a block of parameters that gives the model its own distribution over observations; enumerating all (query, observation, answer) triples, the masked estimator's expectation equals the exact gradient to 1e-12 and the unmasked estimator differs by a printed nonzero norm. On a two-layer random Qwen3 configuration in fp32, the masked-loss gradient on the concatenated three-turn trajectory equals the sum of per-turn gradients from separate forward passes within 1e-5 relative, and adding environment positions to the mask changes it by more than 1e-3.

**Writer notes:**
- The two silent breakages: re-rendering the history changes Qwen3's earlier tokens, so trajectories are concatenated from sampled ids (token-in, token-out; the observation format is fixed text with special-token parsing off); and trainer against engine log-probabilities differ, so decide which is "old" and measure the gap (a truncated importance correction from 8.5 only if the gap is large). A truncated or malformed episode gets reward 0 and stays in the batch; dropping it rewards giving up.
- Sequence-mean aggregation rewards shorter responses and under-penalizes long failures; it shows up as the number of searches. A strict outcome reward on a weak model gives mostly zero-advantage groups; check the degenerate fraction before blaming the optimizer. Single-run reward curves are not evidence; the T1 envelope is.
- The Chapter 8 trainer is single-turn (`d2l.grpo_train` has no external mask); the T3 run may use verl or AReaL with the section's `d2l.masked_pg_loss` as the oracle (1e-4 in fp32, to be confirmed), and the T1 run uses the section's loss directly. The editor aligns the two blueprints.
- Floor risk at 0.6B (base exact match near zero): cold-start SFT on a few hundred execution-verified trajectories (teacher Qwen3-8B, rejection-sampled by exact match), a shaped reward for the first 10 steps, or Qwen3-1.7B at T1 with fewer steps. Question synthesis: if the audit error rate exceeds about 10%, tighten the verbatim filter or add a second-model answer check.
- Memory: the 1.7B policy's state is 27.5 GB, sharded by FSDP2 to 3.44 GB per GPU on 8 trainer GPUs beside a TP 1 vLLM replica (3.44 GB plus cache); at T1 the 0.6B policy needs about 6 GB with fp32 moments, and vLLM colocation may not fit in 15 GiB (batched HF generation and a smaller K, or sequential rollout then training with weights offloaded). A100 only: bf16, no FP8, no FlashAttention-3. The 1.7B checkpoint (3.4 GB) goes to the model hub, not `DATA_HUB` (open).
