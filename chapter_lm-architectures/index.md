# Advanced Architectures
:label:`chap_lm_architectures`

The reader has the transformer of Advanced (RoPE, GQA, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, a mixture of experts, sliding windows and sinks, DeltaNet and hybrid stacks) and a configuration file from :numref:`sec_lm_hf_models` with fields that match none of it: a latent rank, an indexer that keeps 512 of a million keys, a layer type called `linear_attention`, an `hc_mult` of 4, an embedding table with 384 million rows. This chapter teaches those fields, one design axis per section, and for each change asks what it saves, what it costs, and which training and serving setup makes the trade worthwhile.

- *Assignment.* The chapter hosts the capstone (`sec_lm_capstone`, "design under a budget"), hosted by the Further Reading notebook; it stands alone on 14.1's `d2l.config_stats` and 5.3's decode model: a design that satisfies a scenario's parameter, memory, latency and training-FLOP constraints with hand-derived claims checked to 0.5% (exact for cache bytes of GQA, MLA and GDN layers), one measured baseline on a released small model (peak memory within 15% and decode tokens per second within 25% of prediction, or a profile that localizes the gap), one rejected design with a pre-registered rule and at least three seeds or a paired interval, and a dossier. - *Noise discipline.* A difference below two standard deviations of the baseline's seed spread is a tie, and ties are the expected outcome of most small-scale architecture comparisons (the 14.4 and 14.7 notebooks say so); paired bootstrap over evaluation sequences or documents; stability claims need a pre-registered divergence criterion (14.7).
