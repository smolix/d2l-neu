**Idea:** Sparsity is a design axis with its own scaling law, and the router's score function, normalization and node limits are co-designed with the network the experts live on.
**Opening:** The `sec_moe` model picked 8 experts of 64 by hand; released models choose 256 small experts with 8 active and one shared, or 896 with 16 active behind a latent projection. The concrete example is the opening cell listing expert count, top-k, shared count, expert width over hidden width, score function and dense-prefix layers for six released models (`tab_lm_moe_models`). The open question: what fixes the number of experts, and where does a model keep knowledge that no arithmetic needs?
**Teaches:**
- Total against active parameters, granularity (k experts of width A over k at fixed active width A) and the fitted law L(S, G) with the optimum at fixed training FLOPs; "a 3B-active MoE is a 3B model" is false for memory and decode traffic (Qwen3-30B-A3B reads 61 GB of weights).
- Router design in released models (`tab_lm_moe_routers`): softmax scores with a scaling factor (V2, Qwen3) against sigmoid scores with normalized top-k and a bias controller (V3, K2, K3, GLM, MiniMax) against the square-root softplus of V4; group- and node-limited routing (V3's 8 groups and 4 chosen) co-designed with expert parallelism; dense early layers (`first_k_dense_replace` 3 in V3 and GLM, 1 in the Kimi models, 0 in Qwen3 MoE and gpt-oss) and why models keep them.
- All-to-all bytes per token with and without a latent: the expected number of nodes a token's k experts touch, n(1 minus (1 minus 1/n)^k) with replacement, capped by node limits, times the dispatched width times dispatch and combine bytes; LatentMoE projects to a smaller width before dispatch (K3's 3,584 of 7,168); experts touched per batch, 1 minus (1 minus k/E)^b, sets decode weight traffic.
- Engram's hashed n-gram lookup memory as a sub-block (`subsec_lm_moe_lookup`): a table indexed by hashed n-grams (up to 4-grams, about 384M rows per table, 8 heads of 256) read beside the MoE layer, costing memory and traffic but no FLOPs; a miniature with gated integration in the notebook; shipped in DeepSeek V4.1-Flash and, as `ngram_*` and `ple_*` fields, in Qwen3.8-Flash-Next.
- Expert specialization measured on a released model: routing traces over code, math and prose against a shuffle baseline; load balance is not specialization.
**Derivation:** Optimal sparsity at fixed FLOPs from a parametric law in total parameters, data and sparsity (the Abnar et al. form with its published coefficients used only structurally, since the units are unknown; the Krajewski et al. granularity form), with D equal to C over 6(1 minus S)N and the interior optimum found by root finding, checked against a 200-by-200 brute-force grid; all-to-all bytes per token with and without a latent. Smallest case: hidden 4,096, top-8, 64 experts over 8 nodes, dispatch and combine at 2 bytes, with and without a latent of half width; a Monte-Carlo over one million tokens at E 256 and n 8 agrees to three decimals, and V3's group limit gives at most 4 distinct nodes.

**Writer notes:**
- Differences from the blueprint: Engram's primary paper is verified (arXiv 2601.07372, "Conditional Memory via Scalable Lookup"); cite it, and keep the sub-block in 14.2 unless lookup tables spread across several releases (the editor's four-condition rule); zero-computation experts are an exercise, not body text; LatentMoE stays (shipped in K3).
- Matched active parameters is not matched FLOPs; the dense-gather layer of `sec_moe` costs grow with E, so the sweep needs the sorted dispatch; normalization, scaling factor, score function and bias interact (V2's softmax with scale 16 and V3's sigmoid with scale 2.5 are not interchangeable); a fitted exponent from 16 points has a wide interval, never extrapolate the optimum beyond the grid; expert statistics from 100K tokens are noisy for rare experts (bootstrap, drop experts below a token threshold).
- Memory and fallbacks: if E 128 exceeds 34 GiB halve the micro-batch and double accumulation; if the sorted loop is too slow at E 128 use `torch._grouped_mm` or cap E at 64; Qwen3-30B-A3B does not fit one A100 40 GB (T2 pair with `device_map='auto'`, batch 2 by 1,024 if above 38 GiB per GPU); `num_hash_layers` semantics remain unverified until the V4 report is read; the code corpus is committed as ids and statistics only.
- Library additions: `d2l.MoEFFN(d, num_experts, top_k, width, *, shared=0, shared_width=None, score='sigmoid'|'softmax'|'sqrtsoftplus', norm_topk=True, scale=1.0, n_group=1, topk_group=None, latent=None, zero_experts=0, bias_rate=1e-3, dispatch='sorted')` (a drop-in `ffn_factory` storing `self.stats`, with `route`, `forward`, `update_bias`); `d2l.moe_dispatch_bytes(num_experts, top_k, hidden, *, latent=None, nodes=8, node_limit=None, b_dispatch=2, b_combine=2, exact=False)`; `d2l.expert_stats`; `d2l.fit_sparsity_law(df)`; `d2l.optimal_sparsity(coefs, C, grid)`; `d2l.train_lm_steps(model, batches, steps, *, lr, warmup, decay_frac, seed, eval_every, eval_fn, hooks=())` (about 30 lines over `d2l.pretrain` with `d2l.wsd_lr` and `d2l.param_groups`; the variant-training loop of 14.4 and 14.7).
