**Idea:** Pre-norm dilutes each layer's contribution as the residual stream grows with depth, and learned mixing across several streams or over earlier layers restores it only when the mixing is constrained to preserve the identity path.
**Opening:** The hidden-state norm of Qwen3-0.6B grows layer by layer while each layer's update stays about the same size, so late layers change the stream by a smaller fraction. The concrete example is the opening cell: residual RMS and update RMS per layer for the loaded Qwen3-0.6B, with the ratio falling with depth. The open question: is depth being wasted, and would width have been the better purchase?
**Teaches:**
- Norm growth under pre-norm: the expected squared norm after L layers grows like L for independent updates and like L-squared for aligned ones, so the RMS grows between the square root of L and L, and a layer's contribution is diluted by the inverse; post-norm and the off-stream variant (`tab_modern-recipe`'s OLMo row) as the alternatives that give up the identity path.
- Hyper-connections: n parallel residual streams mixed by token-wise learned matrices (expansion, mixing, write-back); the product of unconstrained mixing matrices across depth grows or vanishes geometrically; the manifold-constrained version (mHC) projects the mixing matrix onto the doubly stochastic set with a few Sinkhorn iterations, which keeps the product's spectral norm at most one and preserves the stream mean; the traffic cost (about 20d against 3d per token for the plain residual at n 4) and the kernels that hide it.
- Attention Residuals: each layer's input is a convex combination of all earlier outputs, with weights from a pseudo-query against normalized keys, in a blocked form (Kimi K3's 8 blocks of 12 layers plus the embedding source) that stores N d instead of L d; the inputs are convex combinations, so there is no identity path unless the attention puts weight on the previous output.
- DenseFormer's depth-weighted averaging and value residuals as pointers (one difference each; the Further Reading notebook has their sources).
**Derivation:** Doubly stochastic matrices are closed under products, have spectral norm at most one and fix the uniform vector (`eq_lm_res_ds`); unconstrained products are bounded below by the spectral radius to the power L (`eq_lm_res_growth` for the norm). Smallest case: n 4 streams over L 96 layers with seed 0: unconstrained B_l equal to I plus 0.1 times Gaussian noise (log of the product norm over L printed, singular values shown); Sinkhorn with 20 iterations on exponentiated Gaussian logits (column sums within 1e-6, the row residual r_20 against the iteration count), with the product's spectral norm at most 1 plus L r_20 and the mean drift at most L r_20 times the norm; the constrained product's second singular value decays. Sinkhorn itself is an exercise.

**Writer notes:**
- Differences from the blueprint: two arms plus an optional third (the blueprint ran four variants at four rates, 16 runs); three rates; DenseFormer and value residuals are pointers with sources in the Further Reading notebook. Shipped evidence is read from configurations only: `hc_mult` 4 with 20 Sinkhorn iterations in DeepSeek V4-Flash, V4-Pro and V4.1-Flash, `mhc` in GLM-5.3-Flash, `hc_count` 4 in Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, `attn_res_block_size` 12 in Kimi K3; the mHC paper predates the V4 releases.
- More streams are not free capacity (activations scale with n). A finite Sinkhorn is only approximately doubly stochastic and the residual compounds over depth; the constraint preserves the stream mean, not each stream. Residual RMS is not comparable across n-stream variants; compare the mean read-out. Attention Residuals' granularity (layer against sublayer) and the zero-pseudo-query initialization (uniform weights) are modeling choices; read arXiv 2603.15031 §2 before implementing, since no public reference implementation was located (the K3 repository may carry modeling code).
- Fallbacks: if the exact mHC parameterization cannot be matched, test properties and document deviations (paper §3); if HC arms exceed 38 GiB, quarter the micro-batch, then n 2, then fewer layers or `torch.compile`; if stability events are rare, extend the rate grid by 8 for the baseline only or use 24 blocks at T1; if 16 runs exceed the budget, drop the lowest rate for all but the baseline.
- Library additions: `d2l.sinkhorn(logits, iters=20, eps=1e-6)`; `d2l.HyperConnection(n, d, mode='hc'|'mhc', sinkhorn_iters=20)` (X of shape B by T by n by d; token-wise expansion, mixing and write-back as in V4.1's equation 2; asserts properties, not constants); `d2l.AttnRes(num_layers, d, block=None)`; `d2l.ResidualGPT(variant, vocab, d, H, Hkv, L, ...)` with variants `prenorm`, `hc`, `mhc`, `attnres`, `attnres_block`, `dwa`, `value_residual`; `d2l.depth_diagnostics(model, batch)`; `d2l.stable_lr_range(runs, tol)`.
