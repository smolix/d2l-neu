**Idea:** A learned indexer selects which blocks of the context a query reads, the selection is paid for by scoring every key cheaply, and training and inference must agree on the same selection.
**Opening:** 12.7's eviction threw tokens away for good; a model could keep everything and learn which blocks each query needs. The concrete example is the opening cell: DeepSeek-V3.2 and GLM-5 read 2,048 of 131,072 keys (1.56%), MiniMax-M3 reads 16 blocks of 128 plus a local block (0.21% of a million), and V4 reads 512 of its compressed entries plus a 128-token window. The open question: what does the selection itself cost, and when is it cheaper than reading everything?
**Teaches:**
- Block selection: keys pooled per block and scored against the query (MoBA), or a compressed branch plus a selected branch plus a window, shared across a GQA group (NSA); page bounds as a cheaper score (Quest); token-level selection by a lightning indexer whose heads score every key with a ReLU kernel and keep the top 2,048 (DSA), with the main attention in MLA's multi-query form.
- Train-inference consistency: the indexer is trained first to match the dense head-summed attention (a KL warm-up), then frozen into a sparse stage where it receives no language-model gradient and the loss is restricted to the selected set; the selection kernel at training and at inference is the same; inference-time top-k on a dense model is a different regime (12.7).
- Cost: selection scores all n keys, so sparse attention is not O(k); the crossover n above k a over (a minus b) and the speedup cap a over b (V3.2: cap 17.0, 13.4 at 131,072, break-even about 2,176); per-head selection reads the union of heads' sets while group-shared selection pays k at a worse per-head optimum; index reuse across layers (V4.1's `index_source_layer_ids`, GLM-5.3's 21 full and 57 shared indexers) and hierarchical compression (V4's ratios 4 and 128) as the shipped answers.
- Selection quality: the error of attending to a set S is bounded by twice the maximum value norm times the omitted mass, so top-k of the true attention is the oracle; mean-pooled keys can hide a sharp key.
**Derivation:** FLOPs per query for full attention against scoring plus k blocks (`eq_lm_sparse_cost`, `eq_lm_sparse_cap`) and the omitted-mass error bound (`eq_lm_sparse_err`). Smallest case: a 64K context with blocks of 64 and k 16; at V3.2's dimensions a equals 2H(2r plus d_r), 278,528, b equals 2 H_I d_I, 16,384, k 2,048. **Writer notes:**
- The three claims that go wrong: "sparse attention is O(k)" (the indexer scores all n); "the FLOP speedup is the wall-clock speedup" (measure); "top-k on a dense model is the same thing" (a sparse model is trained or retrofitted; the inference-time regime is 12.7's). The compression ratio changes the unit of selection (V4's top-512 at ratio 128 covers 65,536 tokens). The indexer gets no language-model gradient in DSA; say so when the reader asks why it does not drift.
- Precision: DSA scores in fp8 at inference per the V3.2 report; the notebook uses bf16 for training and inference alike and states the convention (2 H_I d_I FLOPs per scored key). The V4.1 hierarchy's "compression divides the scanned entries" is an assumption checked against the report's §2.3.
- Hardware fallbacks: NSA's Triton kernels are not guaranteed on Ada (PyTorch gather only, kernels as design references); FlashMLA's sparse kernels are unavailable on A100 (state it, measure the gather path); if eager capture on Qwen3-0.6B is slow, use 2K documents and one layer; if the one-minute training of the retrieval arm fails, run one seed pair for about 3 minutes or report "did not converge".
- Library additions: `d2l.block_gate_scores(q, k, block, *, causal=True)`, `d2l.page_bound_scores(q, k, page)`, `d2l.block_topk_attention(q, k, v, *, block, topk, window=0, init_blocks=1, group_shared=True, impl='mask'|'gather')`, `d2l.LightningIndexer(d, num_heads_I, dim_I)`, `d2l.token_topk_attention(q, k, v, scores, topk)`, `d2l.indexer_kl_loss(I, p, selected=None)`, `d2l.mass_recall(attn, selected)`, `d2l.recall_task(num_pairs, ctx_len, vocab, seed)`.
