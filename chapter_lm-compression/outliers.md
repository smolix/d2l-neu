**Idea:** A few activation channels carry most of the range, and because the product `a_j w_j` is unchanged by scaling one factor up and the other down, the range can be moved from activations into weights before either is quantized.
**Opening:** The reader has 11.1's finding that eight-bit weights are free and eight-bit activations with a per-tensor scale are not. The opening cell runs Qwen3-1.7B at W8A16, then at W8A8 with one static activation scale per tensor, and prints the perplexity gap, followed by the largest and the median per-channel absolute maximum at one `down_proj` input and their ratio. The question is why a per-tensor scale fails for activations when it works for weights, and the closing question is whether the difficulty can be removed instead of moved.
**Teaches:**
- Where outliers live: a channel survey over the four linear-layer input sites (`qkv`, `o`, `gate` and `up`, `down`) across layers, with streaming statistics (`absmax`, root mean square, the fraction of tokens with a large entry per channel); channel outliers against token-specific massive activations and why the per-token against per-channel distinction decides which scale granularity can absorb them.
- LLM.int8()'s mixed decomposition: outlier columns kept in 16 bits, the rest in INT8, exact in float; an accuracy tool whose two-kernel structure is not a speed recipe.
- The rescaling identity `X Wᵀ = (X diag(s)⁻¹)(W diag(s))ᵀ` and SmoothQuant's balancing choice `s_j = a_j^α / w_j^(1−α)`; where `1/s` and `s` can be fused (into the preceding RMSNorm weight `γ` or into the producing linear layer) and where they cannot (a SiLU does not commute with a scale; a value projection shared by a GQA group must be scaled per group).
- AWQ as the weight-only relative: the same identity, with `s` chosen by a grid search on activation statistics to protect salient weight channels at three and four bits; no gradients, a calibration set, and a clipping search.
- Why the gain depends on granularity: per-token dynamic scales already absorb token-level magnitude, so SmoothQuant helps most for per-tensor static scales; smaller weight groups already isolate outliers, so AWQ helps most at per-channel.
**Derivation:** The rescaling identity and the balancing choice. For every `s` the product of the per-tensor activation range and the per-tensor weight range is at least `max_j a_j w_j`; `α = 1/2` makes every channel's two ranges equal to `√(a_j w_j)` and attains the bound. Smallest case: two channels with `(a, w) = (64, 1)` and `(1, 1)`, where an INT8 per-tensor grid on the unscaled activations wastes six bits on the second channel, and the balanced scaling gives both factors the range 8. The AWQ error term for one group with one salient channel is derived in an exercise.
