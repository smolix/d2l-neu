**Idea:** Training a model through a simulated quantizer, with a straight-through gradient, recovers what calibration cannot, and the formats that models now ship in are block-scaled four-bit formats that the model was trained to fit.
**Opening:** The reader has 11.3's GPTQ model at int4, which lost a point of perplexity that no calibration set recovers, and 7.5's experience that adapters on frozen quantized weights learn. The opening cell prints three perplexities for Qwen3-0.6B at int4: round-to-nearest, GPTQ, and GPTQ followed by a few hundred steps of training with the quantizer in the forward pass. The question is how a gradient passes through a rounding step whose derivative is zero, and the closing question is in which format the trained model should be shipped.
**Teaches:**
- Fake quantization in the forward pass with a latent full-precision weight, the clipped straight-through estimator in the backward pass (gradient passes inside the clipping range, zero outside), and learnable per-group scales as the one-difference refinement; what the estimator is the gradient of.
- A fair comparison: matched tokens and optimizer steps, a BF16 control trained on the same tokens (so that domain adaptation is not credited to quantization awareness), and adapters on frozen quantized weights (7.5) as the third arm; conversion after training must reproduce the fake-quantized forward exactly before any speed is measured.
- Quantization-aware distillation: the same training with a KL loss against the frozen BF16 model as teacher; the route by which Nemotron 3 Nano reached NVFP4 (the fact to confirm in the Nemotron 3 report).
- Block-scaled formats as deployment targets: MXFP4 (E2M1 elements, blocks of 32, a power-of-two shared scale) and NVFP4 (blocks of 16, an E4M3 block scale and one float32 per tensor); the scale rule and the clipping it implies; which tensors a released model keeps in 16 bits; gpt-oss ships its expert matrices in MXFP4 and nothing else; native arithmetic on Blackwell, emulated on Ada and A100.
- Ternary weights (BitNet b1.58) in one paragraph with the absmean quantizer and the statement that such models are trained with the quantizer in the loop from the start; the exercise derives the optimal ternary scale.
**Derivation:** The straight-through estimator as the gradient of a surrogate: the identity inside the clip range, so that with absmax scaling and `clip = 1` the weight gradient is the identity, and with a learned scale the entries outside `[−n, p]·s` get zero weight gradient and a nonzero scale gradient (the LSQ rule). Smallest case: a three-bit staircase over `u ∈ [−6, 6]` with the true derivative, the estimator and the scale derivative drawn. Second, short: the clipping fraction of the MX scale rule as a function of the block maximum (`κ = 6/r`), with the numeric case of a log-uniform maximum.

**Writer notes:** Comparing quantization-aware training to post-training quantization without the BF16 control credits domain adaptation to quantization awareness; the control is not optional. Choose learning rates on validation, never on WikiText-2; use the same data order for every arm. Adapters on frozen quantized weights cannot change the grid assignment of the base; say so when E loses to F. A fake-quantized perplexity is not a deployed result: convert, check the packed model exactly, then time it with 11.1's kernel. "MXFP4" is not one number: block size and scale format decide the error, and MXFP4 and NVFP4 differ in both. Do not call the Ada or A100 run "native FP4"; arithmetic is emulated, at best storage is native (blueprint ch11 "Compute plan" table, reproduced as `tab_lm_q_hw` in 11.1). gpt-oss's MXFP4 experts have no BF16 twin, so re-quantizing a copy measures the simulator, not the training recipe. Ternary post-training quantization of a 16-bit model is not BitNet; those models train with the quantizer from the start. LSQ and the detached absmax scale are different estimators; label which produced a number. Memory: float32 master weights, gradients and AdamW state for 0.6B parameters are about 9.6 GB; if 14 GiB is exceeded use an 8-bit optimizer (7.5) or a smaller batch with accumulation, keeping the matched-budget rule. The gpt-oss end-to-end run (about 13 to 14 GiB plus KV cache, Triton 3.4 or later) is optional and guarded by `torch.cuda.mem_get_info`; the tensor-level inspection is the deliverable. `read_safetensors` may be refused by Xet-backed storage; fall back to downloading shard 0 (about 5 GB) with a printed confirmation. The OCP MX conversion rule (rounding, treatment of block maxima) must be checked against the specification PDF by the author; only `quant_mx('mxfp4')`'s scale rule changes if it differs. The Nemotron 3 report is the source for quantization-aware distillation to NVFP4; the writer confirms the recipe there before quoting it (the secondary coverage in `sota.md` B4 is not quotable). Do not re-teach: the estimator, the formats, 4.3's block-scaling error. Library additions: `d2l.FakeQuantSTE`, `d2l.FakeQuantLinear`, `d2l.quant_mx`, `d2l.decode_mxfp4`, `d2l.read_safetensors`, `d2l.qat_export`, `d2l.ternary_absmean`; artifacts: training logs, the gpt-oss layer-0 statistics JSON, optional QAT codes and scales if under 100 MB.
