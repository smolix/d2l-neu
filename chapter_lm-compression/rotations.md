**Idea:** An outlier is a property of the coordinate system, and a random orthogonal rotation folded into the weights spreads every vector so evenly that four-bit activations become workable.
**Opening:** The reader has 11.2's rescalings, which make eight-bit activations work and still leave four-bit activations far from the model. The opening cell takes one residual-stream vector of Qwen3-1.7B, prints its maximum over its root mean square (near the worst case `√d` when one channel dominates), applies a random sign flip followed by a Hadamard transform, and prints the ratio again (a small constant). The question is why rotating helps when scaling did not, and why the network does not notice the rotation; the closing question is how random the rotation should be.
**Teaches:**
- Hadamard matrices (Sylvester for `2^k`, the order-12 construction for `12·2^k`) and the fast transform in `O(d log d)`; incoherence as a small maximum-over-norm ratio; which Qwen3 widths admit which orders.
- Computational invariance: the residual stream can be rotated by any orthogonal `R` if every writer (embedding, every block's output projections) is multiplied by `R` and every reader by `Rᵀ`, provided the RMSNorm scale `γ` is first folded into the following linear layers; the four rotation sites of QuaRot (residual, inside attention, inside the MLP before the down projection, and the KV cache) and which are free (fused offline) and which run online.
- Why randomness is needed: a fixed Hadamard has adversarial inputs (the all-ones vector); random signs give a sub-Gaussian spread.
- The effect on grids: rotated activations quantize well per token at four bits; weights were already roughly Gaussian and gain less; interaction with group sizes of 16 to 32 (block formats of 11.5).
- Learned rotations as the one-difference relative (SpinQuant: optimize `R` on the Stiefel manifold against the fake-quantized loss) and the variance across random rotations that motivates them; codebooks and lattices named in one sentence with a pointer to the Further Reading notebook.
**Derivation:** The concentration bound. For a unit vector `x` and `R = H D/√d` with random signs `D`, each coordinate of `Rx` is a signed sum with sub-Gaussian proxy `1/d`, so `max_j |(Rx)_j| ≤ √(2 log(2d/δ)/d)` with probability at least `1 − δ`, while the root mean square is `1/√d`; the ratio is `O(√log d)` against the worst case `√d` before rotation. Smallest case: `(8, 0, 0, 0)` under a 4-point Hadamard with and without signs. Second derivation, short: that folding `γ` and rotating readers and writers leaves the function unchanged (the RMSNorm without mean subtraction commutes with an orthogonal map after its scale is removed).
