**Idea:** Unfreezing more of the model buys task accuracy and costs memory and forgetting, and both costs can be predicted from a ledger before the run.
**Opening:** The reader froze a backbone and trained a head in Basics (`subsec_freezing`). Qwen3-1.7B has 28 blocks, and a cell shows that unfreezing all of them for a 77-way intent classifier costs 27.5 GB of static memory at 16 bytes per parameter, while unfreezing the top four costs a fraction and reaches almost the same accuracy. The open question is whether every weight must move at all, which 7.4 answers.
**Teaches:**
- Unfreezing depth and layer-wise learning rates (block $i$ of $L$ at $\eta \cdot \gamma^{L-1-i}$; no decay on norms and biases); why freezing the embeddings also freezes a tied head.
- A task head on the last non-pad token against a generative answer scored by constrained choice (`d2l.score_choices`), with the output-validity rate as the generative arm's extra cost, and when each wins (small against large labelled sets).
- The memory ledger as a function: weights, gradients, master copy, optimizer state, activations and the logits term, per precision scheme (fp32 at 16 bytes per parameter; bf16 with fp32 master at 16, or 20 with an fp32 gradient accumulator; pure bf16 at 8; 8-bit optimizer state at about 10); why 1.7B fits one 40 GB card and 8B does not; the bf16-absorption pitfall (an update below half an ulp of the weight is lost, so pure bf16 at learning rate 1e-5 freezes weights above about 2.6e-3 in magnitude).
- Forgetting measured on the Chapter 1 suite as a retention delta with a paired interval, and replay as the standard remedy (measured in 7.7).
**Derivation:** The static memory $M = P(b_w + b_g + b_m + s\,b_s)$ for weight, gradient, master and optimizer-state bytes, and the absorption threshold: a bf16 weight changes only if $|\Delta| \ge \tfrac12 \textrm{ulp}(w)$ with $\textrm{ulp}(w) = 2^{\lfloor \log_2 |w| \rfloor - 7}$, so with an Adam-sized update $|\Delta| \approx \eta$ the weight must satisfy $|w| < 256\eta$ (sufficient) or $512\eta$ (necessary). Smallest case: $\eta = 10^{-5}$ gives 2.6e-3 and 5.1e-3. The notebook checks the ledger's static terms against `max_memory_allocated` within 3% and the changed fraction of a real projection's weights within one percentage point of the prediction, with stochastic rounding's mean matching $\Delta$.
