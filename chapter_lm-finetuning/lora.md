**Idea:** A rank-$r$ update captures most of fine-tuning at a fraction of the trainable state, and its scale, targets and learning rate decide how much.
**Opening:** The reader takes one weight delta from 7.1 and looks at its singular values: they fall off within a few dozen directions, so the update could be two thin factors (`subsec_mdl-low-rank-updates`). A cell wraps one projection in a LoRA layer with $B = 0$, shows the output unchanged, trains for a hundred steps, and merges the factors back exactly. The open question is what to do when even the frozen weights do not fit, which 7.5 answers.
**Teaches:**
- `d2l.LoRALinear` from scratch: $A$ initialized uniformly, $B$ zero (so the model starts unchanged), fp32 adapters on a bf16 base, the forward `base(x) + scaling * (x A^T) B^T`, `merge`, `unmerge`, `merged_weight` for a quantized base, a context manager that disables every adapter (the reference policy of Chapter 8 at no extra memory), and the state-dict format with its metadata.
- Targets and rank: attention only against attention and MLP at matched parameters; the parameter count $r(m + n)$ per layer (restated), 10.09M for rank 16 on all linear layers of Qwen3-0.6B (1.7%).
- The scale and the learning rate: $\alpha / r$ as the default (PEFT's), $\alpha / \sqrt r$ as the rank-stabilized option, why the best LoRA learning rate is about ten times full fine-tuning's, and the $A$ to $B$ learning-rate ratio.
- Merging into bf16: deltas below half an ulp of the base weight are lost, so merge in fp32 and cast once, or serve unmerged.
- One table of relatives by what each changes: PiSSA (initialization from the top singular directions), DoRA (magnitude and direction parameterization), VeRA (shared random factors with trained scaling vectors), bottleneck adapters, prefix and prompt tuning (where parameters enter), IA³ (per-channel scaling), GaLore (optimizer state projected to low rank, all weights trainable); one DoRA exercise with its gradient identity.
**Derivation:** The scale under one step with $B = 0$. Under SGD the output change is $\|\Delta y\| = \eta \gamma^2 r \sigma^2 |\langle x, x' \rangle| \|g\|$, so rank independence needs $\gamma \propto 1 / \sqrt r$; under Adam the first step sets $\Delta B = -\eta\,\textrm{sign}(\cdot)$ and $\|\Delta y\| \approx \eta \gamma r c \sqrt{d_{\textrm{out}}}$, so $\gamma \propto 1 / r$. Smallest case: $d_{\textrm{in}} = 1024$, $d_{\textrm{out}} = 512$, ranks 1 to 256, where the output change ratio between rank 256 and rank 1 is about 200 at $\gamma = 1$, about 0.8 under $\alpha / \sqrt r$ with SGD, and about 17 under $\alpha / \sqrt r$ with Adam. The LoRA+ ratio: two SGD steps give $\|c_A\| / \|c_B\| \approx \eta_A \eta_B \gamma^2 \|g\|^2 \|x\|^2$, so balancing the two factors wants $\eta_B / \eta_A$ growing with width; the practical recommendation is a fixed ratio up to about 16. The notebook checks both closed forms in NumPy.

**Writer notes:**
- $\alpha$ is a multiplier that interacts with the optimizer; changing $r$ at fixed $\alpha / r$ silently changes the scale; never compare ranks at one learning rate (the minimum moves); trainable fraction is not memory (adapter state is fp32, activations unchanged); dropout above zero makes merged and unmerged paths agree only in `eval()`; target names differ between the Hugging Face model (`q_proj`, `k_proj`, `v_proj`, `o_proj`, `gate_proj`, `up_proj`, `down_proj`) and the book's (`W_q`, `W_k`, `W_v`, `W_o`, `W_g`, `W_1`, `W_2`), and `d2l.LORA_TARGETS` is the one place that maps them.
- The default is `rslora=False` ($\alpha / r$, PEFT's) and the grid reports both (decided 2026-10-29); any recommendation comes from the measurement, and a divergence of $\alpha / \sqrt r$ at rank 64 under the fixed rate is reported as the finding.
- The variants table (`tab_lm_peft_taxonomy`: axis changed, trainable parameters per layer, mergeable or not) replaces the blueprint's §7.5 headline; DoRA is the one exercise; PiSSA is cited through `chapter_mdl-linear-algebra/further-reading.md` where the idea already appears; VeRA, adapters, prefix and prompt tuning, IA³ and GaLore are table rows with their citations in Sources and no prose beyond one sentence each.
