**Idea:** Adapters and checkpoints of one base add, and the sum keeps both tasks if the entries that disagree in sign are handled.
**Opening:** Adding two task adapters' deltas gives a model that does both tasks, sometimes; a cell merges a mathematics adapter and a SQL adapter of Qwen3-0.6B by plain addition and shows one task kept and the other half lost, with the sign-disagreement rate of the two deltas printed next to it. The closing question is whether a new fact belongs in the weights at all or in a retrieved passage.
**Teaches:**
- Task vectors $\tau = \theta_{\textrm{ft}} - \theta_0$ and task arithmetic $\theta_0 + \sum_t \lambda_t \tau_t$, with additivity measured as a linearization residual; why different ancestry or tokenizer cannot be merged (a loss barrier along the interpolation, demonstrated on tiny models).
- TIES (trim to the top fraction by magnitude, elect a sign per entry, average the agreeing entries) and DARE (drop a fraction $p$ at random and rescale by $1 / (1 - p)$), each as one change; SLERP as a norm-preserving interpolation that reduces to averaging for fine-tunes of one base.
- Soups of checkpoints (seeds of one task, the reference model's branch checkpoints) and weight interpolation toward the base as a retention dial.
- Composing adapters: concatenating factors is exact (rank $\sum r_i$), averaging $A$ and $B$ separately is not.
- Forgetting and replay measured on the suite; fine-tuning against retrieval for new facts as the exercise's open question.
**Derivation:** DARE's unbiasedness: with a Bernoulli mask $m$ of keep probability $1 - p$, $\tilde\tau = m\tau / (1 - p)$ has $\mathbb{E}\,\tilde\tau = \tau$ and $\textrm{Var}\,\tilde\tau = \tau^2 p / (1 - p)$, so a layer's output keeps its mean $\sum_i x_i \tau_i$ with variance $(p / (1 - p)) \sum_i x_i^2 \tau_i^2$. Smallest case: $p = 0.9$ multiplies the surviving entries by 10 and the variance by 9. The notebook checks the mean and variance within 2% over $10^4$ masks and the TIES energy fact (the top 20% of Gaussian entries carry about 65% of the energy).

**Writer notes:**
- Merge only within one ancestry and tokenizer (`task_vector` refuses a mismatch and names it); never average LoRA factors separately (merge the products or concatenate); $\lambda = 1 / k$ by habit is wrong for task arithmetic (it adds); tune merge hyperparameters on validation items, never on the report set; SLERP is not better averaging for fine-tunes of one base (the angle between them is tiny); merge in fp32 (bf16 loses small deltas); measure forgetting on the suite, not only on the training-task format; judge knowledge injection by held-out questions, never by training loss.
- Reference implementations: PEFT `add_weighted_adapter` (`cat`, `ties`, `dare_ties`, `svd`) and `merge_and_unload`; the mergekit YAML (`task_arithmetic`, `ties`, `dare_ties`, `slerp`) run on CPU for 0.6B as a second oracle (it trims per tensor and elects by mass, so agreement is up to those two conventions; if it needs its own environment, the from-scratch merges stand alone). Merging into a quantized base is lossy (7.5); merge in bf16 first.
- The fine-tuning-against-retrieval comparison is an exercise, not the notebook's experiment; the four-condition protocol, the paired bootstrap and the rubric are specified above so the exercise is gradable. The judge is Qwen3.5-4B.
- Noise: 200-item evaluations resolve only differences of several points; the committed T2 evaluation cache is the fallback for finer claims.
