**Idea:** Frozen weights can be stored in four bits while the adapters train in sixteen, and the storage format decides the error, the bytes and the speed.
**Opening:** Qwen3-8B in bf16 is 16.4 GB of weights, more than a 15 GiB slot before a single activation, and under LoRA those weights never change. A cell quantizes one projection to 16 levels, prints the error, and shows that a block of 64 values with one scale rounds most of a block to zero when one outlier sets the scale. The open question is how much of the quantization error the adapter must absorb, which the LoftQ cell answers.
**Teaches:**
- The part's quantizers, owned here: absmax and affine integer grids (scale and zero point), per-tensor against per-block scales, the `QTensor` layout (codes packed two per byte along the input dimension, even element in the high nibble, one scale per block of 64, metadata), and how to measure the error (relative Frobenius, signal-to-quantization-noise, per-block guarantees).
- NF4: 16 levels at the quantiles of a normal distribution, normalized by the block's absolute maximum, so each level is used equally often; double quantization of the scales (blocks of 256 scales quantized to 8 bits around their mean) and the bits-per-parameter accounting (4.5 for NF4 with fp32 scales, 4.127 with double quantization, 4.125 for int4 with fp16 scales in groups of 128).
- Dequantization inside the matmul with a custom autograd function that saves codes and scales rather than the dequantized weight (otherwise the memory benefit vanishes); gradients flow to the adapters, never to the codes; what stays in bf16 (embeddings and head, 41% of the quantized weights' bytes at 8B).
- LoftQ: alternate quantization and a rank-$r$ SVD of the residual so the adapter starts by absorbing the quantization error; 8-bit and paged optimizers in one paragraph.
- Three deployments of the result (quantized base plus adapter, merged bf16, merge then requantize) and why they differ.
**Derivation:** NF4 as equal-probability bins against Lloyd-Max. Equal-probability cells maximize the code entropy ($\log_2 k$ bits) while Lloyd-Max minimizes the mean squared error; for a Gaussian with $k = 16$ the Lloyd-Max grid has error $0.0095\sigma^2$ and entropy 3.77 bits, the equal-probability grid with centroid levels $0.0222\sigma^2$ and 4.00 bits. Four levels on a standard normal: equal-probability boundaries at $\pm 0.674$ with centroids $\pm 0.32$ and $\pm 1.27$, against Lloyd-Max boundaries at $\pm 0.98$ with levels $\pm 0.45$ and $\pm 1.51$. The uniform error $\Delta^2 / 12$ and the blockwise absmax error $(1 - 1/B)\,\mathbb{E}[a_B^2] / (12 q_{\max}^2)$ are restated from 4.3. The notebook reproduces the 16 NF4 values (−1, −0.6962, ..., 0.7230, 1) from the inverse normal CDF with offset 0.9677 and compares them with bitsandbytes' table to `1e-7`; blockwise Gaussian error at $B = 64$: int4 0.0116, NF4 0.0084, a fitted grid 0.0076 (in units of $\sigma^2$).

**Writer notes:**
- The headline is Qwen3-8B in NF4 within 15 GiB; the blueprint's Qwen3-14B on a whole 24 GB GPU is dropped, and its arithmetic (14B at 4.127 bits: 9.93 GB static) may appear in an exercise. The 8B static estimate (about 5.7 GiB) leaves room for adapters, activations and logits at sequence 1,024 with checkpointing; the notebook reports the largest sequence length that fits.
- "4-bit" ignores scales (0.125 to 0.5 bits); NF4 is entropy-optimal, not error-optimal; a plain-autograd dequantize in the forward pass saves the dequantized weight and erases the memory benefit; embeddings and head stay bf16 and are a large share of the bytes; adapter-on-quantized and merged-bf16 are different models (report both; never "merge into NF4"); nibble order and block axis differ across libraries (assert on bytes); LoftQ changes learning-rate sensitivity.
- The `QTensor` layout is the contract Chapters 11 and 12 read (12.1's int4 decoder reads `codes` packed along `in_features` with group scales of shape `[out, in/block]`); if those chapters need another nibble order (Marlin, torchao), they re-pack with a documented function; this section does not change its layout without the editor. Every Qwen3 projection width (1,024 to 12,288) is a multiple of every block size in {16, 32, 64, 128, 256}; `n % block_size != 0` raises.
- Reference implementations: bitsandbytes 0.50.2 (`BitsAndBytesConfig(load_in_4bit=True, bnb_4bit_quant_type='nf4', bnb_4bit_use_double_quant=True, bnb_4bit_compute_dtype=torch.bfloat16)`, `prepare_model_for_kbit_training`, `optim='paged_adamw_8bit'`; oracle logits within `1e-2` relative in bf16, to calibrate), torchao 0.18.0 `NF4Tensor` (block 64, scaler block 256), PEFT LoftQ (`init_lora_weights='loftq'`, `LoftQConfig`).
