**Idea:** Instruction tuning is behaviour cloning on serialized conversations with the loss on the assistant's tokens only, and the serialization is part of the model.
**Opening:** Base Qwen3-0.6B continues a question with more questions. A conversation is one string with role markers, and a cell renders a two-turn exchange, prints every token with its label and mask, and shows that the training prefix of turn two is exactly the inference prompt of turn two. The open question is where good conversations come from, which 7.3 answers.
**Teaches:**
- The part's template `d2l_chatml_v1` and `d2l.render_chat` as the only serializer (never `apply_chat_template` internally): roles, the generation prompt, end-of-turn against end-of-sequence, the three special tokens that already exist in the vocabulary.
- Completion-only masks: supervise assistant content plus `<|im_end|>`; what goes wrong at inference if the newline after it is supervised (the model emits it and continues) or if neither is (the model cannot stop); full-sequence loss as a different objective, not a worse one.
- Loss normalization over the effective batch (`num_items` is the supervised-token count of all micro-batches, computed before the first backward), the mean-of-means bias, and the per-sequence alternative.
- Packing with best-fit-decreasing and a block-diagonal causal mask through 4.1's conventions: exactness with RoPE (position reset is needed by varlen kernels and padding-free paths, not for exactness), the fill ratio by strategy, and what an explicit-mask SDPA path does and does not save.
- The prefix invariant as a test: for every assistant turn, the render with `add_generation_prompt=True` is an exact prefix of the render of the next turn.
**Derivation:** The normalization identity. With $K$ micro-batches of $n_k$ supervised tokens and $N = \sum_k n_k$, the intended loss is $L = \tfrac1N \sum_k \sum_i \ell_{ki}$ while the mean of micro-batch means is $L' = \tfrac1K \sum_k \tfrac{1}{n_k} \sum_i \ell_{ki}$; they agree only when all $n_k$ are equal, and the gradient of $L'$ over-weights tokens of short micro-batches by $N / (K n_k)$. Smallest case: two micro-batches of 10 and 90 tokens, where $L'$ weights the short one's tokens 5 times and the long one's 0.56 times. The notebook checks `sft_loss(num_items=N)` against $\nabla L$ to `1e-6` in fp32 and prints the naive version's ratio.

**Writer notes:**
- The template is decided (ch07 §7.2, 2026-10-29) for four reasons the prose gives in one paragraph: Qwen's template renders think blocks only after the last user turn, so its training render is not an extension of its inference prefix; the three special tokens exist in the 151,936-row vocabulary, so no resize; the Jinja compares strings, so it works for the reference model; stop ids belong to `generation_config.json`. Pin the revision and print the template hash; TRL patches templates for known families (`assistant_only_loss=True` needs `{% generation %}` markers), so the training template may differ from the checkpoint's and the notebook diffs token ids on the fixtures.
- Silent breakages: tokenizing prompt and completion separately (BPE merges `\n` across the boundary); supervising the post-`<|im_end|>` newline or the header; padding with `<|endoftext|>` while treating it as EOS; packing without the mask, or with the first label of each segment left unmasked (it must be `-100`); per-micro-batch means.
- Under-trained `<|im_start|>` and `<|im_end|>` embeddings in the base model are a measured risk; the fallback (mean embedding plus noise) is reported as part of the recipe.
- Reference implementations: TRL `SFTTrainer` (`completion_only_loss` or `assistant_only_loss`, `packing=True` with `packing_strategy` in {bfd, bfd_split, wrapped}, `padding_free` with flash-attn only), 20 steps with `shuffle_dataset=False`, batch 1, accumulation 4; then `packing=True`, reporting whether TRL's per-row normalization matches `sft_loss(num_items)` (to be measured).
