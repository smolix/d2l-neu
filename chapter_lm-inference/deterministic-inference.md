**Idea:** The same request returns different logits in different batches because kernels choose their reduction order by shape, and fixing that order makes a model's output a function of the request alone.
**Opening:** The reader runs a prompt alone and then as one row of a batch of eight on 12.1's decoder and gets two different greedy continuations after a few dozen tokens, with temperature zero and the same weights. The opening cell counts, over 64 prompts, how many diverge within 128 tokens and prints the first divergent position's top-2 logit gap. The question is what in a deterministic program depends on the other rows, and the closing question is what it costs to remove the dependence.
**Teaches:**
- Where the batch enters: a GEMM picks its tile shape and split-K strategy from `M` (the number of rows in the step), so a row's dot products are summed in an order that depends on the batch; a split-KV attention kernel picks its split count from the context and the batch; chunked prefill changes which tokens share a reduction; floating-point addition is not associative, so the sums differ in the last bits.
- Why small differences become different tokens: a greedy choice flips when the top-2 gap is below the perturbation, and the flip changes every later token; the rate of flips is a property of the gap distribution.
- Batch-invariant kernels: a fixed tile configuration and no split-K in the GEMM; a fixed split size (not a fixed split count) in attention so a row's reduction tree depends only on its own length; the same rule for RMSNorm and the head; what remains nondeterministic beyond batch (atomics, library autotuning, workspace sizes) and how to turn those off.
- The cost: fixed tiles are not the fastest tiles for every shape; the measured throughput loss across batch sizes; when it is worth paying (reproducible evaluations, debugging, and on-policy reinforcement learning where the sampler's and the trainer's probabilities must agree, restated from the RL chapter).
- Two levels of claim: engine-internal batch invariance (bitwise equality against the engine's own batch-1 run) and agreement with a float32 oracle (equal token ids up to near-ties, which are counted); the second never follows from the first.
**Derivation:** Order dependence and flip probability. In float32, `(1e8 + 1) − 1e8 = 0` while `(1e8 − 1e8) + 1 = 1`; a dot product of `K` terms summed in two orders differs by up to about `K ε max|x_i w_i|`, so the logit perturbation is of order `δ`. A greedy continuation flips at step `t` with probability `P(gap_t < δ)`, and the expected number of positions until the first flip is the inverse of that probability under independence. Smallest case: a three-term sum in BF16 that gives two different answers; then the empirical gap distribution of Qwen3-0.6B on 64 prompts and the predicted first-divergence position against the measured one.

**Writer notes:** This is not sampling noise; it is a deterministic function of batch composition, and the text says so before anything else. The claim hierarchy matters: bitwise equality against the engine's own batch-1 run under `det_mode` (A10 check 4(i)) is the strong claim; equal token ids against a float32 oracle with near-ties excluded and counted (A10 check 4(ii)) is a different and weaker one; agreement between two engines is a third that neither implies. Bitwise invariance may fail with plain PyTorch kernels (cuBLAS can choose different schedules for one shape in different streams or after autotuning); the fallback is to name the first diverging operation and ship token-id equality plus the maximum logit difference, never to loosen the test silently; flag the outcome to the editor because A10 inherits it. The `batch_invariant_ops` library swaps kernels through `torch.Library`; pin its commit, and keep the notebook's own fixed-tile Triton matmul as the path that needs no external package. vLLM's batch-invariance mode is the production comparison (it lists compute capability 8.0 and higher, so Ada and A100 both qualify; its tested-model list may omit Qwen3-0.6B, so the comparison uses Qwen3-1.7B and reads the page at the pinned version). The verify pass of speculative decoding (12.4) has a different `T` from a decode row and so a different reduction unless `det_mode` is on or the pass runs in float32; say so here and again in 12.4. The RL cell runs on 8.5's committed rollouts and never trains. Do not re-teach: `sec_repro`'s determinism settings, the roofline.
