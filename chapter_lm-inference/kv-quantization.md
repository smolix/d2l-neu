**Idea:** Keys have a few channels that are large for every token and values do not, so keys are quantized with one scale per channel over a group of tokens and values with one scale per token, and keys quantize better before the rotary rotation mixes their channels.
**Opening:** The reader has 12.1's int4 decoder, whose weights are 0.55 GB, and a ledger that shows the cache of Qwen3-4B at 32,768 tokens is 4.8 GB on 8 GB of weights. The opening cell prints, from the ledger, the context at which the cache overtakes the weights (about 4,800 tokens for int4 Qwen3-0.6B) and the predicted bandwidth-bound speedup of an ideal five-times-smaller cache at 8,192 tokens. The question is how many bits each stored number needs, and the closing question is whether some entries can be dropped altogether.
**Teaches:**
- The two levers after training, bits per element here and entries in 12.7, with what each saves in stored bytes and in bytes read per step (quantization saves both only with a fused kernel).
- Where the keys' error comes from: per-channel ranges across a 32-token group show a few channels with outsized magnitude; per-token against per-channel scales for keys, and the opposite for values; the measured attention-output error on a grid of {key scheme} by {value scheme} at 2 and 4 bits.
- The KIVI cache: groups along tokens for keys with a channel-wise scale per chunk, groups along channels for values, a full-precision residual window of the latest tokens, a flush rule, packed codes; honest byte accounting that includes scales, zeros, the residual and padding (2-bit with groups of 32 is 3 bits effective).
- Quantizing before the rotary rotation: why post-rotation keys lose their channel structure, the range formula and its knee at `G θ_i ≈ π`, and the price (rotate the whole dequantized key block at read time).
- FP8 keys and values as the production point (native on Ada and Hopper; storage only on A100), with per-tensor or calibrated scales and layers that may be skipped; outlier channels kept dense (KVQuant) as the one-difference relative.
**Derivation:** Quantization error of a key channel pair under the rotary rotation. For a pair `(x, y)` with `|x| ≫ |y|` rotated by `θ_i · pos`, the per-channel range over a group of `G` tokens grows from about `|y|` to about `|x| sin(G θ_i)` for the small channel, so pairs whose frequency satisfies `G θ_i ≥ π` lose the per-channel structure within a group (pair index about 11 of 64 for `G = 32` and base `1e6`). Smallest case: `(10, 0.1)` rotated by 45 degrees becomes `(7.0, 7.1)`, and a 2-bit per-channel grid on the second channel goes from a step of 0.07 to a step of 4.7.
