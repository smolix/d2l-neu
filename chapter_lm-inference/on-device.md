**Idea:** The decode bound holds on any memory system, so on a laptop with a tenth of a GPU's bandwidth the four-bit formats of Chapter 11 pay most, and an integer block dot product is the kernel that makes them run.
**Opening:** The reader has a four-bit checkpoint from Chapter 11 and a GPU decoder from 12.1, and a laptop without a GPU. The opening cell measures the host's memory bandwidth with a large array copy, computes the decode ceiling for Qwen3-1.7B at BF16, Q8_0 and Q4_K_M from that bandwidth and the file sizes, and runs llama.cpp at the three formats to print measured tokens per second next to each ceiling. The question is why a laptop decodes a 4-bit model at a usable speed and a 16-bit model does not, and the closing question is where prefill, which is compute-bound, leaves the CPU.
**Teaches:**
- The memory systems of devices: DDR5 and LPDDR5 bandwidth (tens of GB/s per channel group) against HBM, unified memory on Apple silicon (hundreds of GB/s), the number of memory channels as the parallelism that threads must saturate; the ceiling `β/bytes` as on a GPU, restated from `eq_hw_decode_bound` and `subsec_hw-cpu`.
- The GGUF block formats as a kernel's input (restated from 11.1): Q8_0 and Q4_0 blocks of 32 with one scale, the k-quant super-blocks of 256 with sub-scales, and the type mix of a `Q4_K_M` file; activations quantized on the fly to 8-bit blocks so that the inner loop is an integer dot product.
- The integer block dot product: unpack nibbles, multiply int4 weights by int8 activations with a SIMD dot instruction (AVX2 or AVX-512 VNNI on x86, NEON dot products on Arm), accumulate in int32, scale by the product of the two block scales; a reference in NumPy and the arithmetic intensity it reaches.
- Memory-mapped weights, no copies, threads against channels; why decode saturates at a thread count near the channel count and prefill scales with cores; where the GPU or a neural engine matters (prefill) and where it does not (single-stream decode at a long cache is bound by the cache bytes on every device).
- Picking the model for a byte budget on a device: the fixed-memory finding of 11.1 restated (a larger model in fewer bits); MLX as the Apple-silicon counterpart of the same arithmetic; streaming weights from flash with sparsity (the one-difference relative for devices whose DRAM is smaller than the model).
**Derivation:** Decode and prefill time on a CPU. Decode: `t ≥ max(bytes/β_DDR, 2N/π_int8)` with `π_int8` the aggregate SIMD integer rate; prefill of `n` tokens: `n · 2N/π_int8`, compute-bound. Smallest case: Qwen3-1.7B at Q4_K_M (about 1.1 GB) over a measured 50 GB/s gives a ceiling near 45 tokens per second at batch 1, while a 1,000-token prefill at an aggregate rate of one tera-operation per second takes about three seconds; the notebook substitutes the host's measured numbers.
