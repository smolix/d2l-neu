**Idea:** In a distributed step the time a GPU spends waiting is visible only when every rank's timeline is read together, and the critical path across ranks names the bottleneck.
**Opening:** The reader read a single-GPU profile in `subsec_perf-profiler` and climbed the optimization ladder of `sec_fast_transformer`, and 6.7 left a residual: a layout runs ten MFU points below its prediction. One concrete example: an eight-GPU FSDP step with one extra `.item()` per step on the loss loses a measurable fraction of its throughput, and nothing in rank 0's trace alone says why the other seven wait. The open question: how to find which rank, which stream and which call site lost the time, without reading eight traces by hand.
**Teaches:**
- The multi-rank trace: per rank a compute stream, a communication stream, copy engines and the host thread; `torch.profiler` with CUDA activities, shapes and the collective-parameter events; the Chrome trace format and a viewer; clock alignment across ranks from 6.1's synchronization events.
- The breakdown: for each rank the step is compute, exposed communication, and idle time classified as host wait (the launch queue empty), kernel wait (a kernel waiting on another stream or rank) and other; `d2l.trace_breakdown` reimplements this classification and is tested against Holistic Trace Analysis on a committed trace.
- The critical path: ranks wait at collectives for the last arrival, so the per-rank compute time between collectives names the straggler; jitter alone costs a synchronous step the expected maximum over ranks; a persistent straggler costs every rank its excess; the robust z-score detector over per-rank compute times and its false-positive rate.
- The catalogue of distributed performance bugs, each with its trace signature: a host synchronization (`.item()`, `.tolist()`, `nonzero`, dynamic shapes) as a gap on the compute stream with a host-side synchronize; launch-bound small kernels as a busy host thread and an idle GPU; a data-loading stall as a gap before the step with loader-worker events; an unfused optimizer as thousands of tiny kernels at the step's end; exposed communication as a collective kernel with no concurrent compute (wrong wrapping or prefetch); uneven work (a pipeline stage, a skewed expert rank, a contiguous context shard) as one rank's longer compute; communication kernels slowing concurrent compute (5.7's channels on SMs); allocator thrash as `cudaMalloc` and `cudaFree` during steps; thermal or power throttling as falling clocks in the NVML samples (5.8); profiling overhead itself, and the rule to profile a few ranks for a few steps.
**Derivation:** The jitter tax and the straggler's cost (blueprint ch06 6.8 D5). A synchronous step takes max_r t_r; with independent per-rank noise of standard deviation sigma over n ranks the expected maximum is about mu + sigma sqrt(2 ln n), and one persistent straggler with excess s costs every rank s per step. Smallest numeric case: n = 64 ranks and sigma = 2% of the mean add 2% x sqrt(2 ln 64) = 5.8% to the step from jitter alone, and 16,384 ranks add 8.8%; the detector's per-step false-positive probability for one rank at threshold k is 1 - Phi(k), and for a run of c consecutive flags (1 - Phi(k))^c, which at k = 5, c = 5 is negligible.
