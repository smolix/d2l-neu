**Idea:** When experts live on different devices, routing becomes an all-to-all whose cost is set by the busiest rank, not by the average.
**Opening:** The reader built the mixture-of-experts layer of `sec_moe` with its capacity-buffer dispatch and saw routing collapse and its remedies. One concrete example: Qwen3-30B-A3B's 128 experts over eight GPUs, sixteen per device; each token's top-8 choice names on average several devices, so every layer sends most of its tokens away and brings them back. The open question: how many bytes a layer moves, which rank sets the layer's time when routing is skewed, and what changes across nodes.
**Teaches:**
- Dispatch and combine as two all-to-alls in forward and their adjoints in backward; the permutation that groups token copies by destination (`d2l.dispatch_plan`, a pure function testable on CPU), the per-destination counts and the inverse permutation.
- Capacity factors with static buffers (no host synchronization, dropped overflow) against dropless execution with a counts exchange (one device-to-host synchronization per layer) and grouped matrix products; what the synchronization costs and what the padding costs.
- Gradient groups: replicated parameters (router, attention, norms) reduce over all ranks, expert parameters over the replicas of the same shard only; loss normalization by the global token count so that the two groups scale alike.
- Skew: the rank-load ratio under uniform multinomial routing is near one at realistic token counts, so skew, not sampling noise, is the problem; node-limited routing and per-node deduplication cut inter-node bytes; function-preserving upcycling of a dense FFN into experts with renormalized gates (an exercise with the cell's check).
**Derivation:** All-to-all bytes and the ratio (blueprint ch06 6.6 D1). With n tokens per rank, top-k, hidden size d, element size e_b and e expert-parallel ranks under uniform routing, a fraction (e-1)/e of the n k copies leaves the rank, so wire bytes per rank per layer and step are 4 n k d e_b (e-1)/e (dispatch, combine and their adjoints); expert compute per token is 18 k d f FLOPs for SwiGLU experts of width f, so rho_EP = (2 e_b / 9)((e-1)/e)(eta F / (f beta)), independent of d and k, and fine-grained experts (small f) raise it. Smallest numeric case: d = 1,024, k = 2, e = 8, e_b = 2 gives 4 x 2 x 1,024 x 2 x 7/8 = 14,336 bytes per token per layer, and Qwen3-30B-A3B with f = 768 and d = 2,048 is printed against 5.6's `axis_ratio`. Node-limited routing (D2): with E experts over N nodes and k distinct choices, the expected number of distinct nodes a token visits is N (1 - C(E - E/N, k) / C(E, k)), which is 5.29 of 8 for E = 256, k = 8, against 7 remote copies without deduplication.

**Writer notes:** Pitfalls (blueprint 6.6): a host sync per layer hidden in `.tolist()` or `.item()`; expert gradients reduced over the wrong group or scaled wrong (D3); capacity dropping silently changes the function, compare outputs before trusting a throughput; balanced routing in a benchmark hides the skew of a trained router; counting only the all-to-all and forgetting the counts exchange and the sort; E not divisible by e; upcycling with raw-probability gating; "EP needs NVLink for correctness" (it needs it for speed; the node's bisection sets the all-to-all).
