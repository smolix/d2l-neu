**Idea:** On thousands of GPUs something fails every few hours, so a run is designed to detect, replace and continue, and goodput measures what the cluster-hours actually bought.
**Opening:** The reader has the checkpoint interval of 6.9 with the time between failures as a parameter. One concrete example: a published 54-day run on 16,384 GPUs logged 419 unexpected interruptions, about one every three hours, most of them GPU-related, and every one of them stopped all 16,384 GPUs until a restart. The open question: what the failure rate is on a real cluster, whether every failure must stop everything, and what fraction of a cluster-hour becomes training progress.
**Teaches:**
- Failure statistics: the Poisson rate with its exact interval from an interruption count and an exposure, the job's time between failures as 1 / (N r_node), causes (GPU and HBM, NIC, host, software, silent data corruption), and `d2l.failure_rate(gpus, source)` with the three published rates and what their tables disagree on.
- Detection: collective timeouts (6.1), health checks before launch, straggler monitors (6.8, one sentence), replica checksums for silent corruption and what they can and cannot catch (a corrupted stored state diverges at the next check; a corrupted gradient before the all-reduce is averaged into every replica identically).
- Recovery designs: restart from checkpoint (every GPU idle for the restart and the rework); hot spares and automatic rescheduling; replica-group fault tolerance (a data-parallel replica drops out, the others continue, the replacement heals from a peer's weights); elastic world size with a global data cursor (6.9's `CursorSampler`, `torchrun --nnodes=min:max`); pipeline-template reconfiguration and preemptible-instance training in one sentence each.
- Goodput: the ledger of a cluster-hour (checkpoint stalls, rework after failures, restart time, the jitter tax and stragglers, queueing and allocation), goodput as productive step time over wall time; the cluster level in one paragraph (gang scheduling, why an LLM job cannot be preempted cheaply, queueing delays and utilization in a published trace) with the term's origin in a scheduler that multiplies throughput by statistical efficiency.
**Derivation:** Restart against replica recovery and the first-order goodput (blueprint ch06 6.8 D6 and ch05 5.6 D4). Under restart, a failure costs tau/2 + R on every GPU, so the effective training-time ratio is 1 - delta/tau - (tau/2 + R)/M; with G replica groups healed live, a failure costs the heal time T_heal = S_rg / beta on one group while the others continue at (1 - 1/G) capacity, so the ratio is about 1 - T_heal / (G M). The first-order goodput g is (1 - delta/tau)(1 - lambda (R + tau/2)) with lambda the job's failure rate. Smallest numeric case: M = 3.09 h (the published run's rate), delta = 60 s, R = 10 min: tau* = 1,155 s, waste about 10.4% from checkpoints and rework plus 5.4% from restarts, so about 84% of the time trains; with eight replica groups and a 16 GB shard over a 25 GB/s link the heal costs under a second per failure and the ratio stays within a percent of one, at the price of the lost group's share of throughput while it heals.
