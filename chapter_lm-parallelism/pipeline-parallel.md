**Idea:** A pipeline's cost is its bubble, and every schedule shrinks the bubble at the price of activation memory or of a split backward pass.
**Opening:** The reader split a model in two in `sec_multi_gpu`'s pipeline exercise and counted the bytes crossing the cut. One concrete example: four stages and one batch, three GPUs idle at every moment; eight microbatches fill the gaps, but one device still waits while the others warm up and drain. The open question: how small the bubble can get, what each schedule holds in memory while it waits, and whether the bubble can reach zero.
**Teaches:**
- Stages, microbatches, fill and drain; GPipe (all forwards, then all backwards) and 1F1B (a warm-up of p-s-1 forwards, then alternating), which share the same bubble and differ in activation residency; the embedding and head on the end stages and the imbalance they cause.
- Interleaved stages (v chunks per device) and the bubble divided by v at v times the messages; Llama 3's flexible grouping that removes the divisibility constraint.
- Splitting the backward into input-gradient and weight-gradient halves (zero bubble) and running the weight half in the idle slots; bidirectional schedules (DualPipe) in one paragraph; what each needs in memory and in weight-version bookkeeping.
- A schedule simulator (`d2l.PipelineSim`) with per-stage costs and an executor (`d2l.PipelineRunner`) from `isend` and `irecv`, each microbatch's loss scaled so the accumulated gradient equals the full-batch gradient, including an unequal last microbatch.
**Derivation:** The bubble fraction (blueprint ch06 6.4 D1, D4). With p stages, m microbatches and equal per-stage forward time F and backward time B, the makespan of GPipe and of 1F1B is (m + p - 1)(F + B), the idle time per device (p - 1)(F + B), and the idle fraction (p - 1)/(m + p - 1); 1F1B holds at most p - s microbatches of activations on stage s against m for GPipe. Smallest numeric case: p = 4, m = 8 gives 3/11, and the simulator's makespans with unit costs are 33, 57 and 69 for (4, 8), (4, 16), (8, 16). With the backward split as B = B_i + W and W run in idle slots, the bubble falls to (p - 1)(F + B_i - W) for the unit-cost cases, which is (p - 1) against 3(p - 1) at F = B_i = W = 1; outside those cases the simulator is authoritative and the expression is a lower envelope.
**Notebook:**

4. Calibration on the node: per-stage F, B_i and W of Qwen3-8B's 9-layer stages (committed to `lm-pp-calibration`); `d2l.balance_stages` against the naive 9/9/9/9 split for the head stage.

**Writer notes:** Pitfalls (blueprint 6.4): "1F1B has a smaller bubble than GPipe" is false, it has the same bubble and less memory; equal layer counts per stage make the head stage the bottleneck; m smaller than p leaves devices empty and m far larger adds per-microbatch overhead; FSDP re-gathering parameters per microbatch; measuring the bubble with the optimizer step or data loading inside; ignoring boundary latency when F is small; loss normalization across unequal microbatches.
