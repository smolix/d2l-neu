**Idea:** A student can learn from the teacher's whole distribution over next tokens, not only from the teacher's samples, and the choice between the two is a choice of what the student sees and what it costs to store.
**Opening:** The reader fine-tuned a 0.6B model on a 1.7B model's answers in Chapter 7 and got a weaker copy. A student trained on the teacher's answers sees one token per position; the teacher's distribution at that position holds a probability for every one of 151,936 entries, including the alternatives it nearly chose. The concrete example is one position of one GSM8K solution with the teacher's top five tokens and their probabilities next to the single sampled token. The open question: what do the samples never show, and is it worth 300 KB per token to keep it?
**Teaches:**
- The three decisions that define a distillation method (`tab_lm_kd_design`): who generates the prefixes (teacher, human data or student), what labels each position (a hard token, the full distribution, a top-k slice), and which divergence is minimized. Sequence-level distillation (SFT on teacher samples) and token-level distillation (matching the teacher's distribution at the teacher's own prefixes) are the two worked representatives.
- The divergence table: forward KL (mass-covering; one-sample estimator is SFT on teacher samples; token-level full-vocabulary matching is its Rao-Blackwellized form; logit gradient q minus p at kappa equal to 1), reverse KL (mode-seeking; needs student samples; score-function gradient), generalized Jensen-Shannon with mixing alpha (interpolates). Each row says where it is used (SFT, GKD, MiniLLM, on-policy distillation of 9.2); none is re-derived.
- Top-k teacher logits as storage: tail-bucket coarsening into k plus 1 cells is a lower bound on the full divergence, monotone in k; renormalized top-k is not a coarsening and can over- or under-estimate; m sampled tokens per position give an unbiased alternative with variance 1 over m. Storage arithmetic: 151,936 times 2 bytes is 303,872 bytes per token in bf16 (about 304 GB per million tokens); a top-32 cache is 194 bytes per token (32 int32 plus 32 fp16 plus one fp16 tail), a ratio of about 1,566.
- The compute ledger that every later comparison uses: 2 N_ne per generated or scored token, 6 N_ne per trained token, with the worked instance that scoring 1,000 teacher tokens on the 1.7B model costs 2 times 1.4e9 times 1e3, about 2.8e12 FLOPs, and training the 0.6B student on the same tokens 6 times 0.44e9 times 1e3, about 2.64e12.
- One sentence each: teachers behind an API return text only, so sequence-level distillation is the only option there; mismatched tokenizers forbid logit matching, which the exercise takes up with a vocabulary-agnostic sorted-probability loss.
**Derivation:** The chain rule for sequence divergences: D_KL(P||Q) equals the sum over positions of the expected per-position KL under teacher prefixes, with the reverse version under student prefixes (`eq_lm_kd_chain`), and the bias of top-k truncation. Smallest case: a two-symbol vocabulary with sequences of length 2, teacher P(AA) 0.7 and P(BB) 0.3, and a factorized student with per-position parameter a. The forward optimum is a equal to 0.7, which puts mass 2 times 0.7 times 0.3, that is 0.42, on AB and BA that the teacher never emits; the reverse KL is finite only at a in {0, 1}, with values 0.357 nats at a equal to 1 and 1.204 nats at a equal to 0, the two numbers of `sec_mdl-fwd-vs-rev-kl`. The top-k bias case of the exercises: P (0.5, 0.3, 0.2), Q (0.3, 0.5, 0.2), k equal to 2, where the renormalized loss is 0.25 ln(5/3), 0.128 nats, against a full KL of 0.2 ln(5/3), 0.102 nats.
**Notebook:**
1. Question: at matched student tokens, does training on the teacher's distribution beat training on the teacher's samples, and how much of the distribution must be stored?
2. Model and data: student Qwen3-0.6B-Base with a rank-64 LoRA on all linear layers; teacher Qwen3-1.7B in non-thinking mode (temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8, top-k 20), loaded only in the scoring cell that builds the top-32 cache and in the evaluation cell, never in the training cell. 2,000 GSM8K-train prompts plus 1,000 `chain` prompts; two epochs over the same sample file so tokens are identical across arms; sequence length 512; AdamW with a constant rate after warm-up, chosen once on the SFT-gold arm from {5e-5, 1e-4, 2e-4} by validation loss and reused.
3. Arms: SFT on the human solutions, SFT on teacher samples (sequence-level), forward KL on top-32 logits, reverse KL on top-32, JSD with alpha 0.5 on top-32; full-vocabulary forward KL only on a T2 host (`full_ft=True`).
4. Checks: `d2l.kd_loss` equals `F.kl_div(..., log_target=True)` to 1e-6; the forward-KL gradient at kappa 1 equals q minus p to 1e-6; JSD at alpha 1e-3 divided by alpha is within 1% of forward KL and at alpha 1 minus 1e-3 within 1% of reverse KL; the toy direction cell reproduces 0.42, 0.3567 and 1.2040 within 1e-3 and 1e-4; the top-k bias cell on a Zipf-like vocabulary of 1,000 shows the tail-bucket KL nondecreasing in k and at most the full KL to 1e-9, with the averaged sampled gradient within 3 Monte Carlo standard errors of the full gradient.

**Writer notes:**
- Pitfalls that break the section silently: `F.kl_div`'s argument order gives reverse KL when the writer expects forward; padded embedding rows and unused special tokens leak mass unless logits are masked to the tokenizer length before the softmax; a temperature applied to the teacher but not the student; comparing arms at equal steps instead of equal tokens, or ignoring the teacher's generation cost; treating renormalized top-k as unbiased truncation; greedy teacher outputs are low-diversity, so the sampling temperature decides how much of P the student sees.
- Template mismatch is the first thing to test: Qwen3's non-thinking template inserts an empty `<think></think>`; `d2l_chatml_v1` does not. Store teacher samples as text and re-render both sides with `d2l.render_chat`; a cell asserts that the first 20 tokens of teacher and student renderings agree.
- Reference implementation and tolerance: `d2l.kd_loss` against `F.kl_div` to 1e-6 (fp32, chunked over the vocabulary with `chunk=4096`); TRL's `GKDTrainer` loss function (`generalized_jsd_loss`, name to verify in the pinned release) as the production oracle in one cell; if the API drifts, compare final losses of a one-step run and mark "verify in the pinned release".
- Do not re-teach: the kappa-squared derivation (`subsec_mdl-distillation`), the KL-direction numbers (`sec_mdl-fwd-vs-rev-kl`); the divergences are a table of uses, not derivations.
- Library additions: `d2l.nonembedding_params(config) -> int` (within 3% of 0.44B, 1.4B, 3.6B, 6.95B for the Qwen3 ladder), `d2l.ComputeLedger` (`.add_generate`, `.add_score`, `.add_train`, `.flops()`, `.tokens(kind)`, `.table()`, `.to_jsonl`), `d2l.make_problems`, `d2l.kd_loss(student_logits, teacher_logits, mask, kind='fkl', alpha=0.5, kappa=1.0, chunk=4096)`, `d2l.topk_compress(logits, k=32) -> (idx, logp, tail)`, `d2l.kd_topk_loss(student_logits, idx, logp, tail, mask, mode='tail_bucket')`, `d2l.uld_loss`. `make_problems` is deliberately distinct from 8.4's `d2l.ArithmeticTasks` (flat source); do not merge them. Determinism and train-test disjointness of (template, numbers) pairs are unit-tested.
- Hardware fallbacks: if the T1 cache does not fit, drop to k equal to 16 or 1,000 prompts and print the cache size; if the pinned Transformers cannot load the Qwen3.5-2B tokenizer, use the committed text-only samples and skip the live comparison.
- Settle at authoring: the GSM8K contamination warning threshold (print a warning if GSM8K accuracy exceeds `test_seen_templates` by a margin set after the first measurement); whether `sft_collate` carries the teacher tensors or the arms wrap batches in a generator.
