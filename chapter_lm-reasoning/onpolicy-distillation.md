**Idea:** Scoring the student's own samples with the teacher turns distillation into reinforcement learning with a dense reward and no critic, and trains the student on the states it actually visits.
**Opening:** The 9.1 student goes wrong at token 40 of a solution and never recovers, and the teacher's samples never show the way back, because the teacher never made that mistake. The concrete example is `fig_lm_opd_position_kl`: the per-position reverse KL of the student's own samples to the teacher, flat and then jumping at the first bad token. The teacher is exactly the expert that DAgger asks for. The open question: how much cheaper is this than reinforcement learning with a verifier, and what does it copy that a verifier would not?
**Teaches:**
- The on-policy objective: sample from the student, score each token by the teacher's log-probability minus the student's, a dense per-token reward r_t with no critic; the objective is `eq_kl_objective` with beta equal to 1 and reference equal to the student, whose optimum is the teacher.
- The two estimators: the return-to-go form (unbiased for the sequence reverse-KL gradient) and the discount-zero per-token form (unbiased only with the visited-state distribution held fixed); the difference is the state-distribution term, measured on the toy (cosine about 0.98, relative difference about 0.3).
- `tab_lm_opd_prefix`: who generates the prefixes and who labels the tokens, for SFT, sequence-level KD, forward KL on teacher prefixes, GKD with mixing lambda, on-policy distillation, GRPO and self-distillation with privileged context (OPSD), each a one-line relative.
- The cost ledger against RL: one sample per prompt against K equal to 8, teacher scoring as prefill (compute-bound) against student decode (memory-bound), and why the two orderings can disagree.
**Derivation:** DAgger with a soft expert. With R(x, y) equal to log P minus log pi_ref (`eq_dpo_reward` at beta 1): D_KL(Q||P) equals D_KL(Q||pi_ref) minus E_Q R, so minimizing the sequence reverse KL is the KL-regularized objective; the optimum is pi_ref e^R, which is P; R decomposes per token; the gradient is minus the expectation of the score times the return-to-go. The compounding bound: on-policy distillation minimizes under the student's state distribution (condition (ii) of `subsec_il_error_bounds`), offline distillation minimizes D_KL(P||Q) (condition (i)); for a terminal reward in [0, 1] the regret is bounded by total variation, at most sqrt(D_KL(P||Q)/2) by Pinsker, and by the sum of per-step variations by the hybrid argument. Smallest case: a tabular student and teacher with vocabulary 3 and length 3 (13 prefixes, 27 sequences, seed 0): the identity holds to 1e-10 for a random reference, gradient descent reaches the teacher within total variation 1e-6, and finite differences match the enumerated return-to-go gradient to 1e-8.

**Writer notes:**
- The claim "removes the compounding error" is too strong for a soft teacher and must not be written; the quadratic term belongs to the model in which one deviation irrecoverably loses the remaining reward (blueprint ch09 §9.2, D2 step 6). Say which condition each method satisfies and show the bound.
- The toy comparison confounds sample diversity with training direction; report both directions of KL and claim only the paired count (online lower reverse KL in 16 of 20 seeds in the author's run; re-measure).
- Stale rollouts are partly off-policy and need the ratio rho_t or a refresh; the sampled-token reverse KL is unbiased but high-variance (the full-distribution mode removes sampling noise at the cost of teacher logits); truncated responses give no end-of-sequence reward and must be counted; on-policy distillation copies the teacher's errors, so verifier accuracy is reported, never KL alone; equal steps are not equal work (64 against 128 samples per step).
- Privileged context (OPSD): the student prompt must never contain the hint (`d2l.privileged_prompt` asserts it); evaluation is question-only; the teacher may copy the answer and reward shortcuts, which the text names.
