**Idea:** More samples help until they are correlated; the majority saturates at the fraction of problems the model gets right more often than not, while coverage keeps climbing as a power law that no deployable selector reaches.
**Opening:** The reader samples 16 answers to a problem and takes the majority, and accuracy rises; at 256 samples it stops rising while pass@256 keeps climbing. The concrete example is one `chain` problem with its 16 answers grouped into equivalence classes, the majority wrong by one vote. The open question: why does the vote stop paying, and would one longer trace have been the better purchase?
**Teaches:**
- Self-consistency (majority over answer equivalence classes), best-of-n with a scorer (9.4's tables), weighted voting; the oracle pass@n as a diagnostic that is never deployable; every discarded sample and every verifier token charged.
- Majority accuracy for independent samples: the binomial tail for one wrong class, the limit (1 if p exceeds the largest wrong class, 0 otherwise, a tie split among modal classes), the Chernoff bound with exponent log(2 sqrt(p(1 minus p))) in the binary case, and a case where voting reduces accuracy (p 0.3 against a wrong class at 0.5).
- Correlated samples: per-problem success rates p_i drawn from a Beta(a, b); the majority's limit is Pr[p_i above 1/2]; the variance of the empirical rate carries the factor 1 plus (N minus 1) rho with rho equal to 1/(a plus b plus 1), so the effective sample count tends to a plus b plus 1.
- Coverage and the plateau: pass@N under the Beta model is a power law with exponent a in the uncovered fraction; an imperfect binary verifier (accept correct with alpha, wrong with beta) caps selection at alpha p/(alpha p plus beta(1 minus p)), 0.794 at (0.9, 0.1, 0.3); the KL cost of best-of-n, log N minus (N minus 1)/N, is an upper bound.
- Accuracy against generated tokens as the frontier, with wall-clock reported beside it.
**Derivation:** Majority accuracy for N independent samples at accuracy p (N 3 gives 3p-squared minus 2p-cubed) and its erosion under a shared error mode. Smallest cases (`tts-majority`): binary p 0.6 at N 1, 3, 5, 9, 15 gives 0.6, 0.648, 0.6826, 0.7334, 0.7869; p 0.4 against two wrong classes at 0.3 gives 0.4, 0.424, 0.447, 0.4887, 0.5341; p 0.3 against (0.5, 0.1, 0.1) falls 0.3, 0.282, 0.2738, 0.2362, 0.1871; the bound dominates the exact error for all N up to 33 and its slope between N 257 and 513 at p 0.6 is within 10% of log(2 sqrt(0.24)). Correlation (`tts-correlation`): Beta(2, 1) gives independence 0.6667, 0.7407, 0.8552, 0.9765, 1.0 at N 1, 3, 9, 33, 129 against the Beta-binomial 0.6667, 0.7000, 0.7273, 0.7429, 0.7481 and the limit 0.75. Coverage (`tts-coverage`): Beta(0.3, 2) gives exact coverage 0.1304, 0.4415, 0.7084, 0.8532 at N 1, 10, 100, 1,000 and the asymptotic form agrees within 1e-3 from N 100.

**Writer notes:**
- The single most common error: reporting pass@N as accuracy. The oracle is drawn dashed and labeled on every plot. The second: voting over strings instead of answer-equivalence classes (`d2l.answer_classes` uses 8.4's Math-Verify equivalence; exact string for integers).
- Compare voted accuracy with a sampled single answer at the same temperature, not with greedy; the independence formula is a per-problem statement, not a population prediction; low-temperature or prefix-shared samples are not independent; `n_trials` noise is subset noise, never problem noise.
- Pools: a thinking trace that exceeds its cap is counted wrong and reported separately; the cap is part of the pool name. MATH-500 has no Hub licence tag (record "MIT upstream", PRM800K and MATH, and download at run time with attribution); AIME problems are MAA material, loaded at run time, with the pool storing per-sample correctness only. If pool cost is too high, N_max falls to 32 for the 4B generator and MATH-500 and the plots state the actual N_max; the 0.6B pool to N 256 is the costliest addition and its cost is the first thing to measure.
- Scorers exist only for GSM8K and `chain`; MATH-500 and AIME use majority, oracle and the zero-shot generative verifier.
- Reference oracle: Raschka's voting code on 50 problems (pin a commit); `d2l.majority_vote` breaks ties uniformly.
- Library additions: `d2l.load_tts_pool(dataset, model, mode) -> DataFrame`, `d2l.answer_classes`, `d2l.majority_vote(classes, weights=None, rng=None)`, `d2l.accuracy_vs_cost(pool, method, n_list, n_trials=200, seed=0, cost='tokens'|'gte')`, `d2l.maj_accuracy_indep(p, q, N, ...)`, `d2l.maj_error_bound(p, q, N)`, `d2l.coverage_beta(a, b, N)`, `d2l.fit_beta_mle(correct_counts, n)` (at most 25 lines).
