**Idea:** A reasoning budget is a dial, set by an effort level at inference or by a length penalty at training, and the right setting is a frontier of accuracy against tokens, by difficulty, not a point.
**Opening:** DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at low effort answers a `chain` problem in a few hundred tokens and at high effort in a few thousand, and on the easy problems the answers agree. The concrete example is one easy and one hard problem at the three effort levels with their token counts and correctness. The open question: when is the long trace paying for anything, and can a model be trained to stop earlier without losing the hard problems? The closing points to a tool call in the middle of a trace.
**Teaches:**
- Effort levels as the deployed form of budget forcing: the API's `reasoning` field (a named level or a reasoning-token budget) on DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, whose release encodes effort numerically in the prompt; gpt-oss's three levels set in the system prompt and Qwen3.8's `reasoning_effort` with `preserve_thinking` (earlier reasoning kept across turns) as two other deployed forms; budget forcing itself (truncate the thinking block at B tokens, or extend it by stripping the end-of-thinking marker and appending a wait token up to m times) as the mechanism underneath.
- Adaptive allocation by difficulty: the marginal gain of one more sample, p_i(1 minus p_i)^n, is decreasing, so greedy allocation is optimal for a fixed total; small budgets fund the easiest problems first and nothing goes to p near zero; the practical rule uses a difficulty signal (agreement among four pilot samples, or their mean ORM score) fitted on validation and frozen before the test set, with the pilot tokens charged to every problem; a vote-lead stopping rule as the sequential form.
- Overthinking: the first-correct-position of a trace and its tail fraction; length penalties in RL (R minus mu l) where mu is the exchange rate between accuracy and tokens, the slope of the frontier; a penalty on wrong answers rewards guessing, so the shipped schemes gate the penalty by correctness; the KL-regularized optimum tilts the policy by e^(minus mu l/beta).
- Many samples from a small model against one answer from a larger one at equal FLOPs and at equal wall-clock (the size ladder 0.6B to 8B on the clean splits): the answer depends on difficulty and on the memory-bound decode of `sec_perf_model`.
- One sentence: interleaved thinking (a tool result inside the trace) is the bridge to 10.3.
**Derivation:** Allocation as a knapsack over problems: with a_i(n) equal to 1 minus (1 minus p_i)^n, the separable concave objective is maximized by spending where the marginal accuracy per token is highest; the continuous solution funds the set {p_i above lambda} with n_i equal to ln(lambda/p_i)/ln(1 minus p_i), and a uniform budget is optimal only when the p_i are equal. Smallest case: p equal to (0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 0.8) with a budget of 10 samples, where exhaustive search equals the greedy heap to 1e-12; 300 problems with p_i from Beta(0.5, 1) and a budget of 600 give greedy coverage 0.532 against 0.474 for two samples each (re-measure, seeded). The length-penalty case: with a_tau(l) equal to 1 minus e^(minus l/tau) and mu 0.004, the optimum l-star is tau ln(1/(mu tau)) for mu tau below 1 and zero otherwise, so tau in {10, 30, 100, 300} gives 32, 64, 92 and 0 tokens with accuracies 0.959, 0.882, 0.601 and 0: problems harder than 1/mu are abandoned. The vote-lead rule at p_A 0.8, p_B 0.2 and lead 3 errs with probability 0.0154 and stops after 4.85 samples on average.
