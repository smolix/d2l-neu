**Idea:** A step-level scorer sees what an outcome scorer cannot, the first wrong step, and pays for it in labels that are only as good as the generator that produced them.
**Opening:** Two candidate solutions reach the same wrong answer by different routes; an outcome scorer rates them alike, while a step scorer finds the first wrong step in each. The concrete example is one `chain` problem with two candidates split into `Step k:` lines, with Monte Carlo step labels under them. The open question: who pays for the labels, and does a scorer trained on one generator's mistakes still work on another's?
**Teaches:**
- Outcome against process reward models (`tab_lm_verifier_kinds`): what each reads, what each is trained on, and what each can localize.
- Monte Carlo step labels: K rollouts from each prefix; the soft label is the fraction correct with variance V(1 minus V)/K, the hard label (any rollout correct) is optimistic with mean 1 minus (1 minus V)^K (at K 8: 0.337 at V 0.05, 0.942 at V 0.3); labeling cost is K L (S minus 1)/2 tokens, about 21 times the solution's own cost at S 6 and K 8.
- Credit assignment: the value martingale V(s_j) equals the expected next value, the increments Delta_j are step advantages with zero cross-correlation, Var(R) equals the sum of their second moments, and local credit has the same expected gradient as the outcome weight; the variance ordering is not general and is measured, not asserted.
- Generative verifiers that critique before a verdict (one worked paragraph with a zero-shot instance) and calibration across generators: a scorer trained on pi under-estimates a stronger generator and over-estimates a weaker one (the direction is a prediction, tested in the notebook); AUROC and ECE defined in one short paragraph each.
**Derivation:** The variance of a Monte Carlo step label against rollouts per step, and the martingale identities. Smallest case: a chain of four independent steps each correct with probability 0.8 and R equal to their product, enumerated over 16 outcomes: the martingale and variance identities hold to 1e-12 (Var(R) 0.2418), the outcome weight, the value baseline and the local credit all have the same mean 0.0819 at every step, and the ORM-to-local variance ratios are 1.53, 1.12, 0.84, 0.64 across the four steps (re-measure; the evidence for "not ordered"); one million Monte Carlo labels at V in {0.05, 0.3} and K in {4, 8, 16} agree with the formulas within 3 standard errors.

**Writer notes:**
- A Monte Carlo-trained process scorer learns the value under the labeling generator, not step correctness; this is the section's central caution and the reason the cross-generator drop is predicted rather than discovered. Hard labels are optimistic and soft labels noisy; report both.
- Step boundaries: `d2l.split_steps` uses `Step k:` lines with a blank-line fallback; use the training splitter at evaluation; drop solutions with fewer than 2 or more than 12 steps from PRM training and report the fraction. Qwen3 may ignore the `Step k:` instruction; the blank-line splitter is the fallback.
- Never use the same problems for labeling, training and evaluation; charge verifier inference tokens in every best-of-n comparison; rankings within a generator are more stable than thresholds across generators.
- Reference implementation: TRL's `PRMTrainer` or `RewardTrainer` in the pinned release (to verify) as an optional production cell; `search-and-learn` as the fallback oracle. Released PRMs are not a dependency. `peiyi9979/Math-Shepherd` declares no licence (checked 2026-10-29) and is download-only, analysis-only; `lm-prm-mc-labels-v1` contains no Math-Shepherd rows. PRM800K (MIT) and `Qwen/ProcessBench` (Apache-2.0) are optional.
- Compute: labeling dominates the T2 budget; if it does not fit, lower K to 4 and the problem count to 2,000 and report the ledger. The generator for labeling runs under the rollout engine, never concurrently with scorer training.
- Library additions: `d2l.split_steps(text) -> list[tuple[int, int]]`, `d2l.mc_step_labels(engine, problem, steps, K=8, reward_fn=None, max_new_tokens=256, ledger=None) -> dict`, `d2l.ProcessScorer(model_id, mode='orm'|'prm', lora_rank=32)` with `.forward(input_ids, attention_mask, read_positions) -> Tensor[B, S]` and `.score_solution(problem, solution)`, `d2l.scorer_loss(logits, targets, mask, soft=True)`, `d2l.aggregate_prm(step_scores, how='min'|'prod'|'last'|'mean')`, `d2l.ece(probs, labels, bins=15)`, `d2l.auroc(scores, labels)`, `d2l.select_by_score(scores, correct, n, n_trials=200, seed=0)` (reused by 9.5).
