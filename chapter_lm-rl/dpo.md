**Idea:** The policy itself is the reward model, and the choice of reference and of where the pairs come from decides what the gradient does.
**Opening:** In Advanced the KL-regularized optimum, read backwards, made the reward a log-ratio of policy to reference up to a per-prompt term, so comparisons can train the policy directly with no sampling and no reward model (`eq_dpo_reward`). The reader has that equation; what it does not say is what happens to the probability of the preferred response during training, and a first cell shows it falling. The open question is whether pairs drawn from the policy itself change the answer.
**Teaches:**
- The DPO loss $-\log \sigma(\beta h_\theta(y_w) - \beta h_\theta(y_l))$ and its gradient, a sigmoid weight of the implicit-reward margin times a difference of log-probability gradients, with the same weight on every token of a response (no credit assignment inside the response).
- Reference dependence: the implicit reward is a log-ratio, so the same pairs with a different reference (SFT checkpoint, base model, uniform) train a different policy; the off-policy gap when pairs were written by another model.
- Likelihood displacement: both log-probabilities can fall while the margin grows, because the parameters are shared across responses; the centred hidden-embedding similarity of the pair predicts it.
- One table of relatives by the assumption each changes: IPO (bounded preferences, a squared target $1/(2\tau)$ on the margin), KTO (unpaired feedback with a batch estimate of the reference term), SimPO (no reference, length-normalized log-probability), ORPO (no reference, an odds-ratio term added to SFT), online DPO (pairs sampled from the current policy and labelled by a reward model or judge).
**Derivation:** Likelihood displacement in a linear-softmax policy with features $\phi$ and kernel $K = \Phi \Phi^\top$: one DPO step changes the logits by $\delta z = c K (e_w - e_l)$ with $c = \eta \beta \sigma(-\Delta)$, so the margin moves by $c(K_{ww} - 2K_{wl} + K_{ll}) \ge 0$ while $\delta \log \pi_w = c[(K_{ww} - K_{wl}) - \sum_v \pi_v (K_{vw} - K_{vl})]$ can be negative; in the tabular case $K = I$ it equals $c(1 - \pi_w + \pi_l) > 0$, so displacement needs shared parameters. Smallest case: $\phi_w = (1, 0)$, $\phi_l = (0.99, 0.141)$, a third response $\phi_v = (3, 0)$, $\pi = (0.05, 0.05, 0.90)$ gives $\delta \log \pi_w / c = -0.0170$ while the margin changes by $+0.01998 c$. The notebook checks a real gradient step at $\eta = 10^{-4}$ against the formula to relative `1e-3`.
