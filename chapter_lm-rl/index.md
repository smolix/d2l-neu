# Preference Optimization and Reinforcement Learning
:label:`chap_lm_rl`

The reader derived GRPO and DPO on a toy verifier task in Advanced and saw the KL-regularized optimum in closed form; on a real language model the reward is a second network that can be wrong, sampling takes most of every step, four copies of the model compete for one GPU, and the policy learns whatever the reward left unsaid. This chapter runs the methods the reader already knows on Qwen3-0.6B and measures the symptoms that decide which variant is usable.

- *Notation.* The chapter uses `tab_rl_notation` (owner `sec_mdp`) verbatim: $x, y, r(x, y)$ (prompt, response, verifier score), $K$ (responses per prompt), $\pi_\theta$, $\pi_{\textrm{ref}}$ and $\beta$, $\rho_t$ and $\epsilon_{\textrm{clip}}$, $\hat{G}_t$, $A^\pi$, $\delta_t$, $w$ and $w^-$ for critic parameters, $\pi_e$ for the behavior policy. The chapter intro states, in one short table labelled `tab_lm_rl_symbols`, the rows it adds: $r_\phi(x, y)$ the learned reward, $y_w, y_l$ the preferred and rejected response, $h_\theta(y) = \log \pi_\theta(y \mid x) - \log \pi_{\textrm{ref}}(y \mid x)$ the sequence log-ratio (so the implicit reward is $\beta h_\theta$), $|y|$ the response length with EOS, $L_{\max}$ the constant divisor, the token-level state $s_t = (x, y_{<t})$ (defined in `sec_rl_sequences` prose, not in the table), and the policy lag $k$ in optimizer steps. The inference engine's sampling policy is written $\pi_e$, the table's behavior policy, not a new symbol. The label `tab_rl_notation_contract` does not exist; never cite it. $w$ is never the preferred response (only the subscript of $y_w$); DPO's per-pair weights are $\lambda$; $\epsilon$ is a clip half-width or exploration rate and never a numerical constant (`eps` in code, "a small constant" in prose).
