**Idea:** A reward model is a classifier of comparisons on a language-model backbone, and what it rewards must be measured, not assumed.
**Opening:** The reader fitted a Bradley-Terry reward from comparisons on a gridworld in `sec_regularized`, where the reward was a table. Here a scalar head on the last token of a 0.6B model must generalize from 10,000 comparisons of chatbot answers, and a first cell shows that a predictor that only reads the response length already gets a majority of the public pairs right. The open question is what the trained head rewards beyond length, and how to find out.
**Teaches:**
- The reward head on the last non-pad token, the pairwise logistic loss $-\log \sigma(r_w - r_l)$ with an optional margin and a centering penalty, and preference data as prompt-grouped pairs with a licence and a labeller (human for HH-RLHF, model for UltraFeedback).
- The agreement ceiling: labels with flip probability $\varepsilon$ cap held-out accuracy at $\varepsilon + (1 - 2\varepsilon)\,\mathbb{E}[\sigma(|\Delta^*|)]$, so 70% accuracy can be the noise ceiling, not a weak model.
- Shift invariance (a reward plus any function of the prompt gives the same loss) and calibration (reliability diagram, expected calibration error) as the two properties the loss does and does not fix.
- Length and format correlation measured within prompt, and perturbation probes (filler paragraph, markdown, flattery, truncated last sentence, wrong number, agree-with-the-user) as counterfactual tests of what moves the score.
- The best-of-n curve as the first use of a reward model: n samples from the policy, the highest-scoring one kept, quality read by an independent judge.
**Derivation:** The gradient of the pairwise loss is $-(1 - \sigma(\Delta))(\nabla r_w - \nabla r_l)$ with $\Delta = r_w - r_l$: a push on the margin whose strength vanishes as the pair is already ordered; convexity in $\Delta$ follows from the second derivative $\sigma(\Delta)\sigma(-\Delta)$. Smallest case: best-of-2 with a reward that orders a pair correctly with probability $p$ keeps the better response with probability $p$ against $1/2$ for a random pick, and the noise ceiling at $\varepsilon = 0.2$ and $|\Delta^*| \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ evaluates to 0.50, 0.64 and 0.73. The notebook checks the gradient against autograd in float64 to `1e-10` and the ceiling against a simulated fit with $N = 2 \cdot 10^5$ pairs within three standard errors.
**Notebook:**
1. Question: how accurate is a 0.6B reward model on held-out comparisons, how much of its score is length, and how far does best-of-n move an independent judge?
2. 4. Claim that survives noise: the trained head beats the length-only predictor on its own test set outside the interval, and transfers worse to the human-labelled set; the magnitude and the direction of the length correlation are reported, not interpreted as a ranking of reward models.

**Writer notes:**
- The head reads the hidden state at the last non-pad token; with left padding this is the last column, with right padding it is per-row, and reading position minus one under left padding is the classic silent bug. Masks come from template offsets (`d2l.render_chat`), never from string search; assert that prompt ids plus response ids equal the full templated ids.
- Split by prompt, not by pair: HH-RLHF is multi-turn and prompt-level leakage inflates accuracy. Ties count as wrong (strict comparison). Train one epoch; a second epoch overfits the pair set (report, do not tune away).
- Reference implementation: `transformers` `AutoModelForSequenceClassification(num_labels=1)` for the head (equal outputs within twice the measured bf16 padded-versus-alone difference) and TRL's `RewardTrainer` on the same 4,000 pairs (first 20 losses and final accuracy compared); `d2l.token_logprobs` checked against `-F.cross_entropy` on four sequences including a left-padded one, `atol=1e-3` in bf16 and `1e-5` in fp32; `d2l.lora_disabled` must give bitwise-equal outputs to the base model (B is zero at start).
- `d2l.token_logprobs` chunks the log-softmax over the 151,936-token vocabulary; without `torch.compile` the chunked path is 1.5 to 2 times slower (to be measured) and that is accepted.
- Do not re-teach: `eq_bradley_terry` and the identifiability argument (`sec_regularized`); the bootstrap (`sec_mdl-statistics`); pairing (`subsec_mdl-paired-comparisons`).
