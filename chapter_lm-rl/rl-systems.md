**Idea:** Generation dominates RL compute, so the system separates rollouts from updates, and the price of that separation is a gradient computed from samples of a policy several steps old.
**Opening:** In 8.4 the phase timer put sampling first in every step: a 256-token response costs 256 forward passes at batch width, the update costs one. An inference engine samples several times faster than the trainer's forward pass, but it holds its own copy of the weights, in its own process, with its own kernels and precision. The open question is what a gradient means when its samples came from a sampler that is one, four or sixteen optimizer steps behind.
**Teaches:**
- The RL step as a pipeline of four phases, generation, verification or scoring, update and weight synchronization, timed on a shared timeline with `cuda.synchronize` and read as a Gantt chart; the roofline view (training and prefill at about $6N$ FLOPs per token, decode memory-bound until the batch reaches the ridge).
- Engine and trainer as two services: colocated (one process group, the engine sleeps during the update, weights reloaded in place) against disaggregated (separate GPU pools, weights pushed by NCCL broadcast, CUDA IPC handles or, under LoRA, adapters only); weight bytes $2N$ (1.2 GB for 0.6B, 3.4 GB for 1.7B); FSDP gathers per layer before the push; versioned batches.
- A timing model with its optimum: $T_{\textrm{col}} = (t_g + t_\tau)/G + c + s$, synchronous split $T^*_{\textrm{sync}} = (\sqrt{t_g} + \sqrt{t_\tau})^2/G + s$ at the generator share $G_g^* = G\sqrt{t_g}/(\sqrt{t_g} + \sqrt{t_\tau})$, asynchronous $T_{\textrm{async}} = \max(t_g / G_g,\ t_\tau / G_t + s)$, with the asynchronous-over-synchronous speedup bounded by $1 + 2\sqrt{t_g t_\tau}/(t_g + t_\tau) \le 2$.
- Policy lag and the correction: the sequence-level importance weight $\pi_\theta / \pi_e$ is unbiased, the per-token surrogate is biased and lower-variance, the weights' spread grows with response length, truncation trades a bounded bias for a capped variance, and the trainer-engine log-probability mismatch at lag zero is itself a source of off-policy error.
- The 2026 corrections as one design space in one paragraph: clipped or masked importance weights (robust at small lag, not at large), variance-controlled off-policy updates that scale the learning rate by the effective sample size with a closed-form baseline, off-policy learning without ratios, and single-rollout asynchronous optimization; which one a run needs depends on the lag the layout produces.
**Derivation:** Effective sample size and the lag at which it halves. With $n$ weights $w_i$, $\textrm{ESS} = (\sum_i w_i)^2 / \sum_i w_i^2$; if per-token log-ratios are independent with variance $s^2$ over $T$ tokens, the sequence log-weight is Gaussian with variance $\varsigma^2 = T s^2$ and $\textrm{ESS}/n \to e^{-\varsigma^2}$; with $s^2$ growing linearly in the lag $k$, ESS halves at $k_{1/2} = \ln 2 / (T s_1^2)$. Smallest case: four samples with weights $(1, 1, 1, 1)$ give ESS 4, weights $(2, 2/3, 2/3, 2/3)$ give $16/5.33 = 3.0$, weights $(4, 0, 0, 0)$ give 1. The notebook checks $\textrm{ESS}/n$ against Monte Carlo at $\varsigma \in \{0.3, 0.6, 1.0\}$ (0.914, 0.698, 0.367) within 1% and the enumerated unbiasedness of the sequence weight to `1e-12`.

**Writer notes:**
- Policy lag is the controlled variable of the section; the blueprint's four-layout design (ch08 §8.6) is reduced to the five runs above, with the colocated and synchronous layouts as the lag-zero references. The blueprint's `Qwen3-4B-Base` target is replaced by the running model `Qwen3-1.7B-Base`; if memory allows, 4B is the stretch, with the trainer on at least three GPUs (64.4 GB of state).
- Timing without `cuda.synchronize()` measures queueing, not work. Compare layouts at equal GPU count and batch, in tokens trained per GPU-hour and accuracy per wall-clock hour, never in "steps per hour". Two processes on one GPU is not disaggregation; say so in the T1 cell.
- The engine and the trainer disagree on log-probabilities at lag zero (kernels, precision, sampling path); measure the mismatch on 256 responses (mean, standard deviation, fraction beyond 0.1) before any lag experiment, because it is the floor of every correction.
- Weight synchronization is version-sensitive: `d2l.sync_weights` wraps vLLM's weight-reload and sleep-and-wake hooks (`mode` in `nccl`, `ipc`, `lora_only`); the author records the vLLM, torch and transformers versions in `config.json`. Fallbacks: LoRA-only synchronization if FSDP2 and vLLM contend on NCCL; a PCIe-only node changes the synchronization cost by an order of magnitude, so measure link bandwidth first.
- Do not re-teach: importance sampling and ESS (`sec_mdl-bayesian-computation`), the roofline (`sec_perf_model`), the stale-sample ratio and its clip (`sec_rl_sequences`, `sec_ppo`). Do not quote any system's reported speedup over another; `tab_lm_rls_systems` names what each system does, not how fast.
