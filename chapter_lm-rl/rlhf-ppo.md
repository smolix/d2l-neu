**Idea:** Four models share the GPU, and a per-token KL penalty folded into the reward keeps the policy near the reference while PPO's clipped ratios keep each update small.
**Opening:** The reader's PPO in `sec_ppo` had one network and a simulator that returned a state. Here the environment is the model itself (the next state is the context plus the token), the reward arrives once at the end of the response from 8.1's head, and four copies of a 0.6B model, actor, critic, reference and reward, must fit one GPU together with their activations. The open question is whether the reward model can be skipped altogether, which 8.3 answers.
**Teaches:**
- The four roles, what each needs (gradients, optimizer state, a forward pass only) and the memory ledger that follows: at 16 bytes per parameter the actor and critic cost about 9.5 GB each for 0.6B, the frozen reference and reward about 1.2 GB each; with LoRA the reference is the same weights with adapters off and the critic is a value head on the actor's hidden state.
- The per-token reward: $-\beta(\log \pi_{\theta_{\textrm{old}}} - \log \pi_{\textrm{ref}})$ at every token and the reward-model score at the last response token, a truncated response getting a fixed penalty; GAE over the response with the mask; whitening of advantages (population standard deviation over valid tokens, never over padding).
- k1, k2 and k3 as estimators of the KL value and of its gradient: k1 is unbiased for the value but has zero expected gradient, k3 is unbiased for the value and its gradient is that of the forward divergence, k2's gradient is that of the reverse divergence; where each belongs (in the reward or in the loss).
- The implementation details that change outcomes, as one table: dropout off in every role, left padding with explicit positions, value head initialization, value clipping, one or two PPO epochs over four minibatches, fp32 log-softmax, the EOS penalty.
**Derivation:** Bias and variance of k1 and k3 on a two-token vocabulary where the ratio $\pi_{\textrm{ref}} / \pi_\theta$ is $1/2$ on one token and $2$ on the other, with $\pi_\theta = (2/3, 1/3)$: both estimators have mean equal to the KL, $\tfrac{2}{3}\log 2 - \tfrac{1}{3}\log 2 \approx 0.231$, k1 takes the values $\pm \log 2$ with standard deviation $\approx 0.65$, k3 takes the values $\log 2 - 1/2 \approx 0.19$ and $1 - \log 2 \approx 0.31$ with standard deviation $\approx 0.05$; differentiating with the samples held fixed, the expected gradient of k1 is zero and that of k3 equals the gradient of $D_{\textrm{KL}}(\pi_{\textrm{ref}} \Vert \pi_\theta)$. The notebook checks all four facts on a 5-outcome categorical in float64 to `1e-9` and by Monte Carlo with two Gaussians ($2 \cdot 10^6$ samples).

**Writer notes:**
- The k3 trap: putting k3 in the loss and believing it regularizes the reverse KL. Its value estimates $D_{\textrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta \Vert \pi_{\textrm{ref}})$ but its gradient is the forward divergence's (the book states this at `sec_rl_sequences` with the Tang and Munos citation); the notebook shows the 0.44 max-norm gap on the categorical check.
- Reference log-probabilities must be computed with adapters disabled after the first update, not cached from a policy that has moved; sampling temperature must equal scoring temperature; whiten advantages, not returns, and never on a one-token batch with the unbiased estimator.
- Reference implementation: OpenRLHF's 0.6B configuration on the same recorded batch (advantages within `atol=1e-5` in fp32, loss within `rtol=1e-4`); TRL's PPO trainer only if the installed release has it (its documentation page exists only on `main`).
- Do not re-teach: PPO's surrogate and trust region (`sec_ppo`), GAE's telescoping identity (`sec_actorcritic`), the KL-regularized objective (`sec_regularized`, `eq_kl_objective`), the k1, k2, k3 comparison as losses (`ex_rl-sequences-kl-loss`). New here: their bias and variance as value estimators per token and per sequence, and the four-role memory and time ledger.
- The shared-backbone critic can degrade the policy; the blueprint's fallback is a separate LoRA for the critic. `transformers.generate` is slow at T1; the fallback is 128 new tokens or vLLM.
- Blueprint detail: ch08 §8.2 (`tab_lm_ppo_details` with its source in the N+ paper, `tab_lm_ppo_memory` for 0.6B and 8B, full and LoRA, the hand-computable fixture with $\lambda \in \{0, 0.95, 1\}$ values).
