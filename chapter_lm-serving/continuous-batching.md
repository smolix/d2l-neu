**Idea:** Scheduling per iteration admits and retires requests between decode steps so that no slot waits for the longest output, and chunked prefill under a token budget lets prompts ride on memory-bound decode steps without stalling them.
**Opening:** The reader runs 13.1's static batch of eight on mixed-length requests and the opening cell prints the slot utilization (useful token-steps over `B` times steps), then adds one long prompt mid-run and prints the largest gap between consecutive tokens that the other seven requests saw. The question is how to fill slots as they free and how to take a long prompt without a stall, and the closing question is what the engine does between two GPU steps.
**Teaches:**
- Iteration-level scheduling: schedule per step rather than per request; run the non-attention operators on flattened tokens because only attention needs to know which token belongs to which sequence; the slot-utilization statement `U_static = mean n / E[max n]` (`1/H_B` for exponential lengths).
- The ragged forward: positions, not lengths, drive the rotary embedding; `seq_ids` and positions index the cache writes; attention over a padded gather with the mask `key_idx ≤ q_pos`; two attention calls per layer (decode rows at `q_len = 1`, prefill chunks padded to the longest); exactness of a chunk against full causal attention.
- The scheduler's step in four moves (decodes first, continue running prefills, admit while budget and cache room remain, otherwise defer), with the invariants asserted every step; the three policies (static, prefill-first, chunked) as a handful of lines in one loop.
- The token budget: the free chunk `c_free ≈ P/β − n_dec` of prefill tokens that a memory-bound decode step absorbs at no cost; the stall rate of unchunked prefill; the budget that holds inter-token latency under a limit.
- Between two steps: sampling, detokenization, structured-output masks (the grammar automaton of 10.2 restated in one sentence: a mask per step from the grammar state), scheduling itself; why this CPU work sits on the critical path of a small model and how engines overlap it with the GPU step (asynchronous scheduling, a masks-ahead computation); the engine's deterministic mode as 12.3's `det_mode` applied to the ragged step.
**Derivation:** The free chunk and the stall rate. A decode step with `n_dec` rows is memory-bound with time `(W + n_dec L k_KV)/β`; adding `c` prefill tokens adds compute `2N(n_dec + c)/P`, so the step stays flat while `c ≤ P/β − n_dec` (about 150 tokens on an RTX 4090 with `n_dec = 16`); unchunked, a prompt of `n` tokens stalls every running row for `t_p(n)`, and at arrival rate `λ` the fraction of time in stalls is `λ t_p`, which exceeds 1% at `λ = 0.01/t_p`. Smallest case: rows of 10, 20, 40 and 80 output tokens under static batching leave 53% of slot-steps idle.

**Writer notes:** Padding rows of a graph bucket are slots beyond `n_active` with `pos = 0`; a hand-built step that points padding at slot 0 corrupts a live request (a bug that appears only when the batch is not full). The rotary position of a chunk's first token is `n_computed`, not 0; the chunk-size sweep catches it. Enforce `max_num_seqs < token_budget` or prefill starves. Tie sampling randomness to `(request, position)` with a per-request generator so that preemption and recompute (13.5) change timing but not outputs. "Continuous batching" is not "bigger batches"; its benefit is utilization under variable output lengths, a workload property. Never quote a chunked-prefill speedup without the two tail latencies it came with. The reference `RaggedLM` reads the cache through `view` plus a row copy per layer, so its fitted `β_eff` includes that copy; the fitted efficiency says nothing about vLLM's kernels. `RaggedLM` does not call `forward` for mixed steps (two calls would read the weights twice; one padded call would pad decode rows to the chunk); the two-call variant stays as a comparison arm in the step-time sweep. The ragged step is not graph-captured; graphs are available for pure-decode steps through 12.1 and that is an exercise. The deterministic mode is 12.3's; this section applies it and reports the cost on the ragged step, and A10 check 4 inherits 12.3's fallback (name the first diverging operation, grade token-id equality).
