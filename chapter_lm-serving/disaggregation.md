**Idea:** Prefill and decode want different batch sizes, parallelism and even hardware, so giving each its own pool of GPUs removes their interference and decouples their layouts, at the price of moving the cache from one pool to the other.
**Opening:** The reader has 13.2's chunked prefill, which bounds the stall that a long prompt causes but still spends decode steps on prompt tokens, and 13.7's layouts, which were chosen for decode. The opening cell prints, for the Azure code trace (long prompts, short outputs), the fraction of each colocated step's time spent on prefill chunks and the tail time per token at a tight objective, then the same with prefill on two GPUs and decode on six. The question is what disaggregation buys and costs, and the closing question is which pool a cached prefix belongs to.
**Teaches:**
- The two arguments: interference (every prompt chunk lengthens every decode step; 13.2's stall rate in another form) and resource coupling (prefill wants compute and a high tensor degree, decode wants cache capacity and a large batch; one layout serves neither well); DistServe's pools with independent objectives, Splitwise's machines and the fast backplane, Mooncake's cache-centric scheduler.
- The costs: moving the cache (`α + n k_KV/β_link`), hidden by layer-wise pipelining when the link is fast against the per-token prefill compute; pool imbalance and integer granularity on 8 GPUs; weights stored twice, which shrinks the decode pool's cache; lost prefix locality unless the router restores it (13.6).
- The transfer path: GPU to GPU over NVLink or RDMA with a transfer library (NIXL, Mooncake's Transfer Engine) against host-staged copies; the connector in an engine; what a request's hand-off looks like in the scheduler (`add(req, prefilled=True)`).
- A fluid model of capacity: colocated steady state with `b` decode rows and `b n_in/m` prefill tokens per step against a prefill pool at the utilization its objective allows and a decode pool limited by cache or by the time-per-token objective; the two limits (compute-bound at the objective, where disaggregation cannot beat colocation; a tight time-per-token objective, where it does).
- The ratio of prefill to decode GPUs from a trace (prompt and output lengths set it), and what changes for reasoning workloads with long outputs (the decode pool grows) and agent workloads with repeated prefixes (the prefill pool shrinks when the cache hits).
**Derivation:** Exposed transfer and the fluid model. With `L` layers, pipelining layer `l`'s transfer behind layer `l + 1`'s prefill gives an exposed time of `τ_x + max(0, T_x − T_pre)` with `τ_x = T_x/L`; the transfer is hidden (apart from `τ_x`) when `β_link ≥ k_KV P_eff/(2N)` (13.6's bound restated, now per request). Colocated capacity is `λ_col = b*/(m t_s(b*, b* n_in/m))` at the largest feasible `b*`; disaggregated capacity is `min(n_p ρ_max/τ_p, n_d b_d/(m t_dec(b_d)))` over integer splits `n_p + n_d = 8`, with goodput per GPU `λ/8`. Smallest case: a 4,096-token prompt of Qwen3-30B-A3B is 403 MB of cache and prefills in about 0.14 s at half the A100's peak; NVLink moves it in under a millisecond, a 100 Gb/s network in 32 ms (hidden), a 10 Gb/s network in 0.32 s (exposed by about 0.18 s).
