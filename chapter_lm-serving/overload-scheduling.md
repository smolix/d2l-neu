**Idea:** When demand exceeds capacity the scheduler decides whom to serve, whom to pause and whom to refuse, and the policy, not the hardware, sets goodput and fairness.
**Opening:** The reader offers 13.2's engine 1.3 times its saturation rate and the opening cell prints a queue that grows without bound, a tail time to first token that grows with the length of the run, and a goodput of zero, while throughput is at its maximum. The question is what a server should do with work it cannot finish in time, and the closing question is where a paused request's cache should go.
**Teaches:**
- Admission control: a request that has already waited longer than its objective cannot be good, so shedding it at the head of the queue frees capacity for requests that can be; goodput against offered load with and without shedding; backpressure to the client.
- Preemption: when a running request cannot get a block, pause one; recompute (free its blocks, requeue it with its generated tokens appended) against swap (move its blocks to host memory and back); which request to pause (most recent, lowest priority, largest cache).
- Priorities and deadlines: two classes (interactive with a tight objective, batch with none), earliest deadline first with preemption of batch requests; what starvation looks like and how aging bounds it.
- Fairness across tenants: a weighted fair queue over tokens (a virtual token counter per client, serve the client with the smallest counter, charge prompt and output tokens at different rates); why a heavy tenant otherwise takes the capacity under first come first served; isolation against efficiency.
- Overload across replicas in one paragraph: migrating a running request's cache to a less loaded replica (Llumnix) as the cluster-level form of preemption, with the transfer cost of 13.6.
**Derivation:** Recompute against swap. Pausing a request with `n` cached tokens and resuming it costs `2n k_KV/β_link` by swap (out and back over the host link) and `n · 2N/P_eff` by recompute, so swap wins only if `β_link > k_KV P_eff/N`. Smallest case: Qwen3-0.6B (`k_KV` 112 KiB per token, `N` about 0.44 billion body parameters, `P_eff` 80 TFLOP/s) gives a break-even near 21 GB/s, about what a PCIe host link delivers, so recompute and swap tie for a small model; Qwen3-30B-A3B's break-even is near 6 GB/s, so swap wins. Second, short: under first come first served two tenants offering 10 and 1 requests per second to a capacity of 6 get shares in proportion to demand; the fair queue gives each tenant up to half and the remainder to whoever has demand.
