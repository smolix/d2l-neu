**Idea:** A serving system is judged by how many requests per second it serves within a latency contract, and a queueing model predicts where the latency curve turns up long before the GPU is busy.
**Opening:** The reader sends two requests to 12.1's decoder and the second one waits; with ten clients the wait dominates. The opening cell replays the same 200 requests against the same vLLM server three ways (one at a time, closed loop with 32 clients, open loop at the trace's arrival rate) and prints three different "performance numbers" for one server. The question is which number a service should promise, and the closing question is how to keep the knee away.
**Teaches:**
- What a request experiences: arrival, queue, prefill, first token, decode steps, last token; time to first token, time per output token, inter-token latency, end-to-end latency, throughput; client-observed against server-measured timestamps; service objectives as a pair of limits and goodput as the rate at which at least 90% of requests meet both (restated from `sec_model_serving`'s metrics in one paragraph).
- Workloads: arrival processes (Poisson, Gamma renewal with a coefficient of variation, trace-driven and rescaled traces that keep burstiness), prompt and output length distributions from real traces (conversation against code against agent), the prefill-to-decode token ratio as the number that decides which mechanism matters; open against closed loop and coordinated omission.
- A load generator: open-loop asynchronous replay with intended send times, streaming timestamps per token, the null server that bounds client overhead, and a cross-check against the engines' own benchmark tools.
- Little's law and the first queueing model: the mean wait of a single server, the batch server's formation wait and completion barrier, and a load-dependent birth-death model of iteration-level batching whose capacity grows with the batch until the ridge.
- Energy and cost per token: the GPU's power during the sweep, joules per output token falling with load until the knee, and the cost per million tokens restated from the hardware chapter with a link to `sec_cloud_instances`; one paragraph on exported graphs (a compiled step is what an engine replays; `torch.export`, ONNX and TensorRT-LLM as the export path), which pays comp-perf/further-reading.md:202.
**Derivation:** Little's law `L = λ W` (exact, assumption-free), then the Pollaczek-Khinchine mean wait `W_q = λ E[S²]/(2(1 − ρ))` from residual life, with the batch-server extension (service time the maximum of `B` exponential lengths, about `H_B E[S]`). Smallest case: `ρ = 0.8` with exponential service of mean one second gives a mean wait of four seconds, and deterministic service halves it.
