## Chapter overview

### Draft introduction (about 170 words)

Decoder-only models dominate generation, but most of the text-processing systems around them still run on encoders and embeddings: search boxes, spam and toxicity filters, data-quality classifiers (Chapter 3), deduplication, and the retrieval step that gives an agent (Chapter 10) access to documents it was never trained on. This chapter covers the pretrained encoder family and the embedding models built on it, then spends most of its length on the systems that use them. It starts with static word vectors, the shortest route to the idea that meaning can be read from co-occurrence statistics. It then contrasts two uses of the same pretrained encoder: a task head fine-tuned for one task (Section 2.2), and a contrastively trained model that maps any text to one vector with no further fine-tuning (Section 2.3). Sections 2.4 and 2.5 turn the vectors into a retrieval index and a retrieval-augmented generation system over a corpus we all know, this book. Every stage is measured on its own, with error bars.

### Learning objectives

After this chapter a reader can:

1. Explain skip-gram with negative sampling (SGNS) and GloVe as two factorizations of the same co-occurrence statistics, and verify the shifted-PMI optimum and the SVD-of-PPMI closed form numerically (2.1).
2. Fine-tune a pretrained encoder for sequence classification, sentence-pair classification and token tagging, and state, with a measured cost comparison, when a 150M encoder beats few-shot prompting of a 1.7B decoder (2.2).
3. Explain why mean-pooled pretrained token states retrieve poorly (anisotropy and frequency), train a contrastive text-embedding model with in-batch and mined negatives, and quantify the effect of batch size, temperature, dimension and quantization (2.3).
4. Derive asymmetric distance computation (ADC) for product quantization, and measure the recall, latency and memory trade-offs of flat, IVF-PQ and HNSW indexes against BM25 and hybrid retrieval (2.4).
5. Build a RAG pipeline over `d2l.book_corpus`, evaluate each stage on `d2l.book_qa` with paired-bootstrap intervals, and classify and fix its failures by stage (2.5).
6. Derive RAG-Sequence and RAG-Token, the top-k approximation and the gradient that reaches the retriever (2.5).

### Autograder checks (exact tolerances)

| Check | Rule | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| Schema | every chunk has the fields of 2.4's schema; `docs[doc_id]['text'][char_start:char_end] == text` | exact |
| Coverage | the union of chunk character ranges covers at least 99.0% of non-code characters of every document, and `n_tokens <= 600` for at least 99.5% of chunks (Qwen3 tokenizer) | as stated |
| BM25 oracle | top-10 ids agree with `bm25s` (Lucene method, k1=1.2, b=0.75) on 200 fixed queries | at least 99% of rank-1 ids equal; scores within relative 1e-4 |
| Retrieval floor | Recall@10 on `private`, using span-overlap relevance (`d2l.gold_chunk_ids`) | at least `F`, where `F` = (Recall@10 of the staff reference hybrid solution on `private`) − 0.03; the reference value is measured once per term and not stated here |
| Latency and memory | median retrieval-only latency per query, batch 1, and index RAM | at most 50 ms and at most 2 GB (design targets; staff may relax after pilot) |
| Determinism | two runs of `search` on 100 queries return identical top-10 ids | at least 99% identical |
| Answer accuracy | judge verdict (fixed Qwen3.5-9B bf16 judge, rubric of 2.5 Part 4) on `private` answerable questions | point estimate at least the 2.5 baseline's; full marks require that the 95% paired-bootstrap interval of the difference (10,000 resamples, `d2l.paired_bootstrap`) has lower bound above −0.02 |
| Citation precision | fraction of cited chunk ids that are gold-relevant (span overlap) | same rule as answer accuracy |
| Abstention | on unanswerable items, abstention rate at least the baseline's, and false-abstention rate on answerable items at most the baseline's + 0.05 | as stated |
| Integrity | citations are a subset of the retrieved ids; the program reads no file under `qa_test*`/`private*` | exact |

Grading bands: Level 1 (pass) = all checks except the two "full marks" interval conditions; Level 2 = Level 1 plus at least one of accuracy or citation precision improved with a paired-bootstrap interval that excludes 0 and no other metric worse by more than 0.02 (lower bound).

#### Narrative arc

1. **The question.** Opening cell: cosine similarity between one-hot vectors of any two distinct words is 0, so one-hot input cannot express that "bank" is nearer "loan" than "banana". Second cell: Spearman correlation of *random* d=100 vectors with WordSim-353 human judgments on the covered pairs, over 20 seeds: mean near 0 with a spread that fixes the noise floor for the rest of the section (printed, not asserted).
2. **The distributional hypothesis and counts** (`subsec_lm_dist_hyp`). A word is characterized by the contexts it keeps; build the symmetric window co-occurrence matrix `X` on PTB (window 5, min count 10, `<unk>` excluded); the long-tail histogram of counts motivates smoothing. Figure `fig_lm_we_counts`: rank-frequency of PTB words on log-log axes with the subsampling threshold `t=1e-4` marked.
3. **The corpus, subsampling and the noise distribution** — the heading carrying **`:label:` `sec_word2vec_data`** (legacy; cited by `mdl-mutual-information.md` for the `0.75` exponent and by `scaling-laws.md` for PTB). Content, in this order: PTB (source, 887k tokens; the 5 MB text that `sec_scaling-laws` concatenates onto *The Time Machine*); subsampling with discard probability `P(w) = max(1 − √(t/f(w)), 0)`; dynamic context windows; the noise distribution `P_α(w) ∝ f(w)^α` with `α = 3/4` (`d2l.RandomGenerator`, `d2l.get_negatives`); batching with masks (`d2l.batchify`). One compressed cell shows the batch layout; cut the retired section's PyTorch-only plumbing prose.
4. **Skip-gram with negative sampling** — the heading carrying **`:label:` `subsec_negative-sampling`** (legacy; cited by the SGNS exercise in `mdl-mutual-information.md`). It must use *exactly* the notation of that exercise: center vector `v_w`, context vector `u_o`, score `s(w,o) = u_oᵀ v_w`, `K` noise words drawn from `P`, and the averaged loss
   `ℓ = −Σ_{w,o} p(w,o) log σ(s(w,o)) − K Σ_w p(w) Σ_o P(o) log σ(−s(w,o))` (label `eq_lm_sgns_loss`).
   It also carries **`:eqlabel:` `eq_sigma-f`** on the definition `σ(x) = 1/(1+exp(−x))`, because the same exercise cites `:eqref:` `eq_sigma-f`. Content: why the softmax over `V` words is expensive and why positives alone diverge (cite the retired argument in one sentence); the binary-classification view; CBOW and hierarchical softmax in two sentences each, with hierarchical softmax moved to Exercise 2.1.6.
5. **SGNS from scratch** (`subsec_lm_sgns_scratch`). Two embedding tables (center `V`, context `U`), `d2l.sgns_loss`, an Adam loop (embedding dimension 100, 5 negatives, window 5, batch 512, lr 2e-3, 5 epochs as in the retired notebook; these are the retired notebook's settings), nearest neighbors of "chip", "stock", "bank". Report which table is used for evaluation (`V`, and `V+U` as a variant, Levy et al.).
6. **What SGNS optimizes: shifted PMI and the 0.75 power** (`subsec_lm_sgns_pmi`). State: at the optimum `s*(w,o) = pmi(w,o) − log K + log(p(o)/P(o))`; for `P = p` this is `pmi − log K`; for `P_α` the extra term is `(1−α) log p(o) + const`, which lowers the target score of frequent contexts less than of rare ones. State, do not prove; point to the exercise. Cell: Derivation D3's numerical check (toy matrix).
7. **Truncated SVD of PPMI** (`subsec_lm_svd_ppmi`). `PPMI(w,o) = max(pmi, 0)` (optionally with context-distribution smoothing `α=0.75` and shift `log K`), best rank-`k` approximation via `scipy.sparse.linalg.svds`, embeddings `U_k Σ_k^p` with `p ∈ {0, ½, 1}`. #### Derivations

**D1. Gradients of the SGNS loss (supports the from-scratch loss).**
*Statement.* For one center word with vector `v`, a context word with vector `u_o` (label 1) and `K` noise words `u_k` (label 0), the loss `ℓ = −log σ(u_oᵀv) − Σ_k log σ(−u_kᵀv)` has `∂ℓ/∂u_o = −σ(−u_oᵀv) v`, `∂ℓ/∂u_k = σ(u_kᵀv) v`, `∂ℓ/∂v = −σ(−u_oᵀv) u_o + Σ_k σ(u_kᵀv) u_k`.
*Assumptions.* None beyond `σ'(x) = σ(x)(1−σ(x))`.
*Steps.* (1) `d/dx[−log σ(x)] = −σ(−x)` since `σ'(x)/σ(x) = 1 − σ(x) = σ(−x)`. (2) `d/dx[−log σ(−x)] = σ(x)`. (3) Chain rule with `x = uᵀv` gives the three lines. (4) Interpretation: each noise word is pushed away from `v` with weight equal to the model's current probability that it is a context word, so already-separated noise words contribute almost no gradient.
*Check cell.* Random `v, u_o, u_k` (d=8, K=5, float64, seed 0); compare the three formulas with `torch.autograd.grad` of `d2l.sgns_loss`; tolerance `1e-10`.

**D2. The GloVe objective from the ratio argument.**
*Statement.* If the model `w_iᵀ w̃_k` is required to make co-occurrence *ratios* depend only on differences of word vectors, then (with a homomorphism assumption) `w_iᵀ w̃_k + b_i + b̃_k = log X_ik`, and the weighted least-squares loss `J = Σ_{ik} h(X_ik)(w_iᵀw̃_k + b_i + b̃_k − log X_ik)²` is the natural fit.
*Assumptions.* `P_ik = X_ik/X_i`; a function `F` with `F((w_i − w_j)ᵀ w̃_k) = P_ik/P_jk`; `F` is a homomorphism from `(R,+)` to `(R_{>0},×)`.
*Steps.* (1) Ratios `P_ik/P_jk` carry the signal; for k=ice, j=steam, i=solid the ratio is large, for k=water it is near 1. (2) Require `F((w_i − w_j)ᵀ w̃_k) = P_ik/P_jk`. (3) Homomorphism gives `F(x−y) = F(x)/F(y)`, so `F(w_iᵀw̃_k) = P_ik = X_ik/X_i`. (4) `F = exp` (the continuous solution), so `w_iᵀw̃_k = log X_ik − log X_i`. (5) `log X_i` does not depend on `k`; absorb it in `b_i`; restore the symmetry between `k` and `i` with `b̃_k`. (6) Fit by weighted least squares: the weight `h` is 0 at 0 (empty cells do not enter, since `log 0` is undefined), increasing, capped at `x_max`. (7) Contrast with the SGNS optimum of Section step 6: both fit the same log-count structure, SGNS through a classification loss, GloVe by regression.
*Check cell.* Build a 50×50 matrix `X = a_i b_k` plus noise on a rank-4 log-structure; fit `glove_loss` by full-batch L-BFGS; verify that the fitted `w_iᵀw̃_k + b_i + b̃_k` matches `log X_ik` with RMSE below `1e-3` on the noiseless matrix (float64).

**D3. Numerical verification of the shifted-PMI optimum (statement only; the proof stays in the exercise).**
*Statement.* With a free score `s(w,o)` for every pair, the averaged loss `eq_lm_sgns_loss` is minimized at `s* = log(p(w,o)/(p(w)P(o))) − log K`.
*Check cell.* Draw a random 8×10 joint `p(w,o)` (Dirichlet, seed 0), marginals `p(w)`, `p(o)`, noise `P ∝ p(o)^{0.75}`, `K=5`. Minimize the averaged loss over the 80 free scores with L-BFGS in float64; compare with the closed form; tolerance `1e-6`. Then repeat with `P = p(o)` and confirm `s* = pmi − log K`. Print the extra term `log(p(o)/P(o))` and check it equals `(1−α) log p(o) + log Z_α` with `Z_α = Σ_o p(o)^α`.

**D4. SVD of shifted PPMI as the closed-form counterpart.** Statement and check only: for the matrix `M = PPMI − log K` clipped at 0, the rank-`k` truncated SVD minimizes `‖M − W Cᵀ‖_F` (Eckart–Young, `subsec_mdl-eckart-young`); the check cell compares `‖M − U_kΣ_kV_kᵀ‖_F` with `√(Σ_{i>k}σ_i²)` to `1e-8`, and with the residual of an SGD-trained rank-`k` factorization (which must not be lower).

#### Pitfalls and misconceptions

- Two tables, one choice: evaluating `U` instead of `V` (or their sum) changes the numbers; state the choice.
- Forgetting the mask: padded context positions must not enter the loss (`d2l.batchify`).
- Subsampling by *token type* versus by *occurrence*; keep the retired code's per-occurrence draw.
- "Word2vec does analogies" is a claim about a cosine query with the inputs excluded; without exclusion the result is often a query word (Linzen).
- Analogy and bias demos describe the training corpus, not an algorithm flaw; debiasing by projection hides rather than removes (Gonen and Goldberg).
- SGNS "is" PMI factorization only at the optimum with free scores; finite-dimensional, finite-epoch SGNS differs, and the `0.75` shift changes the target.
- A static vector has one point per *type*; "bank" (river) and "bank" (money) share it.
- Evaluating on pairs outside the vocabulary silently shrinks the test set; always print coverage.

#### Summary (draft bullets)

- Meaning can be read off co-occurrence: a word is represented by the contexts it keeps.
- SGNS replaces the softmax over the vocabulary by binary classification against `K` noise words drawn from `f(w)^{3/4}`, with frequent words subsampled.
- At its optimum SGNS fits shifted PMI; truncated SVD of PPMI is the closed-form counterpart and GloVe a weighted regression on log counts.
- On word similarity, algorithm choice matters about as much as hyperparameters; read intervals, not rankings.
- Analogies, neighbor lists and bias probes reflect the corpus; they are probes, not guarantees.
- A language model's embedding table is a static vector per token, often tied to the output layer; context enters above it, which is the subject of 2.2.

#### Derivations

**A. Cost per example and break-even.**
*Statement.* Let `N_e`, `N_d` be non-embedding parameter counts of encoder and decoder, `L` the tokens of an example, `ρ ≥ 1` the ratio of decoder prompt tokens (instruction + shots + example) to `L`. Forward FLOPs per example are `F_enc ≈ 2 N_e L` and `F_dec ≈ 2 N_d ρ L` (prefill-only scoring; attention terms ignored). The break-even number of queries for fine-tuning (cost `C_ft ≈ 6 N_e L_tr E n_tr` for `E` epochs over `n_tr` labeled examples of length `L_tr`) is `Q* = C_ft / (F_dec − F_enc)`.
*Assumptions.* Dense forward pass of `2` FLOPs per non-embedding parameter per token (`sec_perf_model`); attention FLOPs are a minor addition at `L ≤ 512`; the shared few-shot prefix is not cached (the cached case is `ρ → 1 + (instruction share)`).
*Steps.* (1) Per token, a forward pass costs `2N` FLOPs (the book's rule). (2) Encoder: `2 N_e L`. (3) Decoder: prompt length `ρ L`, so `2 N_d ρ L`; only one output position is scored, so no decode phase. (4) Ratio `F_dec/F_enc = (N_d/N_e) ρ`. (5) Fine-tuning costs three times a forward pass per token (forward and backward, the `6N` rule of `sec_scaling-laws`): `C_ft = 6 N_e L_tr E n_tr`. (6) Equate savings to cost for `Q*`.
*Numbers from the Hub totals (arithmetic, no measurement):* `N_e = 149.66 M − 50,368 × 768 = 149.66 M − 38.68 M = 110.97 M`; for Qwen3-1.7B `N_d = 2,031.74 M − 151,936 × 2,048 = 2,031.74 M − 311.2 M = 1,720.5 M` (tied embeddings counted once), so `N_d/N_e ≈ 15.5`, and with 8 shots of similar length `ρ ≈ 9` the FLOP ratio is about 140.
*Check cell.* Compute `F_dec/F_enc` from the two configs with `d2l.cost_per_example`; then measure wall-clock ms per 1,000 examples (`d2l.Benchmark`, batch 32, bf16, same GPU) and print measured-to-predicted ratio. The two will differ (kernel efficiency, attention, padding); the text discusses the gap and states the break-even `Q*` for the section's MNLI run using measured times. No tolerance: this is a reconciliation, with the discrepancy explained, not a pass/fail test.

**B. Attention cost of alternating local and global layers.**
*Statement.* For `n_g` global and `n_l` local layers at length `L` with window `w` (tokens attended per query), the attention score-and-value FLOPs are proportional to `n_g L² + n_l L w`; relative to all-global, the cost ratio is `ρ_attn = (n_g L + n_l w)/((n_g + n_l) L)`.
*Steps.* (1) One head's scores cost `2 L W d_h` for `W` attended keys per query and the weighted sum another `2 L W d_h`; so a layer costs `∝ L W`. (2) Global: `W = L`. Local: `W = w`. (3) With `global_attn_every_n_layers = 3` and 22 layers, layers `0, 3, …, 21` are global: `n_g = 8`, `n_l = 14`. (4) At `L = 8,192`, `w = 128`: `ρ_attn = (8 × 8192 + 14 × 128)/(22 × 8192) = 67,328/180,224 ≈ 0.374`.
*Check cell.* Build the actual boolean masks for the 22 layers (global: all ones; local: band of half-width `w/2` from `config.local_attention`), count attended pairs, and compare with the formula to within 1% (the band edge effects are O(w/L)). Print the edge-effect correction for `L = 512`.

**C. Padding waste and the effect of unpadding.**
*Statement.* For a batch of `B` sequences with lengths `ℓ_i` padded to `ℓ_max = max ℓ_i`, the fraction of wasted token positions is `1 − Σ ℓ_i /(B ℓ_max)`; unpadding removes this fraction from every per-token operation (matmuls, norms) though not the attention bookkeeping.
*Steps.* (1) Padded cost `∝ B ℓ_max`; unpadded `∝ Σ ℓ_i`. (2) The ratio is `Σℓ_i/(Bℓ_max)`. (3) For lengths drawn from the empirical MNLI pair-length distribution (tokenizer lengths of the training subset), sorting by length into buckets reduces the waste further.
*Check cell.* Draw 2,000 batches of 32 from the empirical lengths with seed 0, compute the waste with and without length bucketing, and compare the predicted speedup `1/(1 − waste)` with the measured speedup of `attn_implementation='flash_attention_2'` (unpadded) versus `'sdpa'` (padded) on the same batches (skip the measured half if FlashAttention-2 is not installed, and say so).

#### Pitfalls and misconceptions

- Using `[CLS]` pooling with a checkpoint whose head expects mean pooling (the ModernBERT config says mean); always read `config.classifier_pooling`.
- Dropping `[SEP]` or swapping premise and hypothesis; ModernBERT has no segment ids, so the separator is the only marker.
- Truncation that cuts the hypothesis; truncate the premise first (`truncation='only_first'`).
- Evaluating on the GLUE test sets (labels hidden); use validation, and never tune on it (tune on a train split).
- Seeds: a single run on 250 examples can differ from another seed by more than the method gap.
- Padding to the longest sequence in the dataset instead of the batch; or comparing speed with and without unpadding without saying which kernel was used.
- Comparing an encoder fine-tuned on `n` examples with a decoder that sees `k` shots as if the information budgets were equal; the encoder also sees `n` labels at training time.
- Treating the decoder's per-example cost as only the example tokens when the shots are re-encoded every call.
- NLI artifacts: a high accuracy does not show reading comprehension unless the hypothesis-only baseline is far below it.

#### Summary (draft bullets)

- BERT-style pretraining masks 15% of tokens (80/10/10) and predicts them from both sides; RoBERTa removed next-sentence prediction and trained longer with dynamic masks; ELECTRA supervises every position.
- ModernBERT keeps the recipe and modernizes the block: RoPE, GeGLU, no biases, alternating local and global attention, unpadding, an 8k context, and code in the data.
- A task head on the token vectors turns the encoder into a classifier, a pair classifier (jointly encoding both texts) or a tagger; sub-word label alignment is the main bookkeeping.
- On small data the seed can matter as much as the method; report seeds and paired intervals.
- Per example, a small fine-tuned encoder needs a small fraction of a prompted decoder's FLOPs (Derivation A); the decoder wins when labels are absent or change.
- The fine-tuned model is one model per task; Section 2.3 trains the same encoder to produce one reusable vector per text.

#### Derivations

**D1. What the `log B` ceiling implies for batch size, and what the gradient does to negatives.** (Builds on `eq_mdl-infonce_bound`; the bound itself is not re-proved.)
*Statement.* (i) A batch of `B` candidates can certify at most `log B` nats; to certify `I` nats one needs `B > e^{I}`. (ii) For `ℓ_i = −log p_ii`, `p_ij = softmax_j(s_ij/τ)`, the gradient is `∂ℓ_i/∂s_ij = (p_ij − δ_ij)/τ`; the total weight on negatives is `Σ_{j≠i} p_ij = 1 − p_ii`; as `τ → 0` the negative weight concentrates on the largest `s_ij`, and as `τ → ∞` it becomes uniform `1/(B−1)·(1 − p_ii)`. (iii) A false negative `j*` (a passage relevant to query `i` that is not its pair) receives weight `p_ij*`, which is large exactly when the model already ranks it high.
*Assumptions.* Scores `s_ij = q_iᵀd_j ∈ [−1, 1]` (normalized vectors); fixed `τ`.
*Steps.* (1) By the proposition in `sec_mdl-infonce`, `Î_NCE = log B − L ≤ log B`, so a loss near 0 cannot certify more than `log B`; set `log B ≥ I`. (2) Write `ℓ_i = −s_ii/τ + log Σ_j e^{s_ij/τ}`. (3) Differentiate: `∂ℓ_i/∂s_ii = −1/τ + p_ii/τ = −(1 − p_ii)/τ`; for `j ≠ i`, `∂ℓ_i/∂s_ij = p_ij/τ`. (4) The positive's pull equals the sum of the negatives' pushes `(1 − p_ii)/τ`, so the loss rebalances itself and vanishes as `p_ii → 1`. (5) The ratio of two negatives' weights is `p_ij/p_ik = e^{(s_ij − s_ik)/τ}`: a gap of `Δ` in score is amplified by `e^{Δ/τ}`; with `τ = 0.05` a `0.1` gap is a factor `e² ≈ 7.4` and a `0.3` gap is `e⁶ ≈ 403`. (6) If a fraction `π` of the passages are relevant to a query but unlabeled, a batch of `B` contains `(B − 1)π` such false negatives in expectation; each is pushed away by weight `p_ij*`, which grows with its score. (7) Consequence: larger `B` adds more negatives *and* more false negatives; lower `τ` concentrates the gradient on the nearest (hardest, likeliest false) negatives; this is why mined negatives are filtered by rank.
*Check cell.* Random normalized `q, d` (`B = 32`, `m = 16`, float64, seed 0): compare `torch.autograd.grad` of `d2l.info_nce_loss` with respect to the score matrix to `(P − I)/(τB)` (tolerance `1e-10`), verify `Σ_j≠i p_ij = 1 − p_ii` to `1e-12`, verify the loss is nonnegative and `log B − L ≤ log B` on 1,000 random batches, and print the weight ratios of step 5.

**D2. InfoNCE in the large-batch limit is alignment plus uniformity; why that removes anisotropy.**
*Statement.* With `s = zᵀz'/τ` for unit vectors, `ℓ_i = −z_iᵀ z_i⁺/τ + log B + log (1/B) Σ_j e^{z_iᵀ z_j/τ}` exactly. As `B → ∞` the last term tends to `log φ(z_i)` with `φ(z) = E_{z'} e^{zᵀz'/τ}`, so the expected loss minus `log B` tends to `A + U`, with alignment `A = −(1/τ) E[zᵀz⁺]` and uniformity `U = E_z log φ(z)`. The uniform distribution `σ` on the sphere is a strict local minimizer of `U` and the global minimizer of the energy `E(μ) = ∬ e^{zᵀz'/τ} dμ dμ`.
*Assumptions.* Negatives are i.i.d. from the marginal over passages; unit-norm outputs; `τ > 0` fixed. (Wang and Isola 2020 prove the asymptotic statement for the two-sided loss and discuss uniformity for the Gaussian kernel `e^{−t‖z−z'‖²}`, which equals `e^{2t zᵀz'}` up to a constant on the sphere; the one-sided version here is the same argument.)
*Steps.* (1) Split `log Σ_j e^{s_ij/τ} = log B + log mean_j e^{s_ij/τ}`; this is an identity for every `B`. (2) The summands are bounded by `e^{1/τ}` and below by `e^{−1/τ}`, so by the law of large numbers `mean_j e^{z_iᵀz_j/τ} → φ(z_i)` almost surely, and `log` is continuous on that interval; the positive appears in the sum with weight `1/B`, an `O(1/B)` correction. (3) Taking expectations gives `A + U`. (4) *Energy.* On `S^{m−1}` the kernel `k(z,z') = e^{zᵀz'/τ}` has the Funk–Hecke expansion `k = Σ_n a_n Σ_Y Y(z)Y(z')` over spherical harmonics, with `a_n > 0` for all `n` (the coefficients are modified Bessel functions of the first kind with positive argument), so `E(μ) = Σ_n a_n ‖μ̂_n‖² ≥ a_0`, with equality iff all harmonic moments `μ̂_n`, `n ≥ 1`, vanish, i.e. `μ = σ`. (5) *Uniformity.* For `μ = σ`, `φ` is constant, `φ ≡ c = a_0`, so `U(σ) = log c`. Perturb `μ_ε = σ(1 + ε h)` with `∫h dσ = 0` and `h` a harmonic of degree `n ≥ 1`: then `φ_ε = c + ε λ_n h` with eigenvalue `λ_n ∈ (0, c)`. Expanding `log`, `U(μ_ε) = log c + ε·0 + ε² ∫h² dσ (λ_n/c)(1 − λ_n/(2c)) + O(ε³)`; the first-order term vanishes because `∫ K h dσ = λ_0 ∫ h dσ = 0`, and the second-order coefficient is positive since `0 < λ_n/c < 1`. So `σ` is a strict local minimizer of `U`; global minimality is Wang and Isola's claim (cited, and checked numerically below, not re-derived). (6) *Anisotropy is the first moment.* For independent `z, z'` with mean `μ̄ = E z`, `E[zᵀz'] = ‖μ̄‖²`: the mean pairwise cosine equals the squared norm of the mean vector, which is 0 for `σ` and near 1 for a narrow cone. The degree-1 harmonic perturbation of step 5 is exactly a shift of `μ̄`, and its second-order cost in `U` is what contrastive training removes. (7) *Consequence.* Contrastive training reduces `‖μ̄‖` and the higher moments together; centering (`R1`) removes only the degree-1 part, so it is a partial fix.
*Check cell.* (a) The identity of step 1 to `1e-12` on random batches. (b) On `S²`, `φ ≡ sinh(t)/t` for `t = 1/τ` (since `u = zᵀz'` is uniform on `[−1, 1]`): compare a `10⁶`-sample Monte Carlo (seed 0) of `E e^{t zᵀz'}` with the closed form at `t = 20`, relative tolerance `0.02` (blueprint-time check: ratio 1.0025). (c) On `S^{15}` compute `U` for three distributions with 20,000 samples each: uniform, a cone (Gaussian around a fixed pole, normalized) and two antipodal clusters; the uniform one must give the smallest `U` (an empirical check of the cited global claim). (d) On `S²` with `h(z) = √3 z_3` and `ε = 0.2`, compute `U(μ_ε) − U(σ)` by quadrature over `u`-slices and compare with the second-order formula `ε²(λ_1/c)(1 − λ_1/(2c))` where `λ_1/c = coth t − 1/t = 0.95` at `t = 20` (blueprint-time check: the ratio of exact to predicted is 1.012 at `ε = 0.2`; tolerance 3%). (e) `mean pairwise cosine = (‖Σ z_i‖² − n)/(n(n−1))` for distinct pairs, tolerance `1e-12`.

#### Pitfalls and misconceptions

- Pooling and prefix mismatch: a checkpoint trained with last-token pooling, left padding and a query instruction gives degraded vectors under mean pooling, right padding or no instruction.
- Normalization: dot product on unnormalized vectors ranks by norm as well as angle; cosine does not.
- Right padding with last-token pooling reads a pad token; always index by the mask.
- Duplicate queries (or duplicate passages) in a batch create false negatives; use a no-duplicates sampler.
- Hard negatives from the top of the ranking are often relevant passages (step 6); skip the top ranks or filter by a stronger scorer.
- Temperature is a hyperparameter of the geometry (scale 20 versus 50) and interacts with the batch size.
- Truncating Matryoshka dimensions of a model not trained for it loses much more than truncating one trained for it.
- Binary quantization needs the float query (or rescoring) for good recall; Hamming-only search compares two bit-strings and discards magnitude.
- A single high MTEB average does not predict your domain; leaderboards can be contaminated.
- Long texts are truncated silently at `max_len`; log how many R3 chunks exceed it.

#### Summary (draft bullets)

- Pooled token states of a masked-language-model encoder are not trained for cosine similarity; they sit in a narrow cone and retrieve poorly.
- Contrastive training with in-batch negatives turns pooled vectors into text embeddings; the loss is a softmax cross-entropy over the batch with temperature `τ`.
- Batch size is the number of negatives and `log B` the most it can certify; `τ` sets how sharply the hardest negatives, and hence the false negatives, dominate the gradient.
- In the large-batch limit the loss is an alignment term plus a uniformity term on the sphere; uniformity removes the mean direction that causes anisotropy.
- LLM-derived embedders (last-token or learned pooling, instruction-prefixed queries) lead public benchmarks, trained on much more and synthetic data.
- Matryoshka training and int8/binary quantization trade bytes per vector against recall; measure on your own data.

#### Exercises

1. *(check)* A model trained with `B = 256` in-batch candidates reports an InfoNCE loss of 0.1 nats. What is the largest mutual information the estimator can certify? Answer sketch: `log 256 − 0.1 ≈ 5.45` nats; never more than `log 256 = 5.55`.
2. *(check)* Why is it harmful to put two pairs with the same query in one batch? Answer sketch: the second pair's passage is a positive for the first query but is treated as a negative, with weight `p_ij`, which is large because it is relevant.
3. *(derive)* Prove step 6 of D2: for independent `z, z'` with common mean `μ`, `E[cos(z, z')] = ‖μ‖²`, and for a finite sample with distinct pairs the mean pairwise cosine is `(‖Σ z_i‖² − n)/(n(n−1))`. Show that centering the data sets the first quantity to 0 but not `Σ_n ‖μ̂_n‖²` for `n ≥ 2`. Answer sketch: `E[zᵀz'] = E z · E z'`; expand `‖Σz_i‖² = Σ‖z_i‖² + Σ_{i≠j} z_iᵀz_j`; centering removes the degree-1 harmonic only.
4. *(derive)* For the symmetric loss (query→document plus document→query), show the gradient with respect to `s_ij` is `(p_ij^{q→d} + p_ij^{d→q} − 2δ_ij)/(2τ)`, and explain how the false-negative weight changes. Answer sketch: differentiate the average of the two softmax cross-entropies; a false negative is penalized in both directions.

|---|---|---|
| `'sections'` (default) | `sec512` | Structure-aware. Parse blocks (paragraph, list, table, display-math block, fenced cell, quote); a block is atomic. #### Derivations

**A. Asymmetric distance computation in product quantization: cost, memory and error.**
*Statement.* Split `x ∈ R^m` into `M` sub-vectors `x^{(1)}, …, x^{(M)}` of dimension `m/M`, each quantized by its own codebook `C_j = {c_{j,1}, …, c_{j,K}}`, `K = 2^b`, to `q_j(x)`; the code of `x` is `(k_1, …, k_M)`, using `M b` bits. For an unquantized query `y`, the asymmetric distance is `d_A(y, x)² = ‖y − x̂‖² = Σ_j ‖y^{(j)} − c_{j,k_j}‖²`, computable with `M` lookups into a table `T[j, k] = ‖y^{(j)} − c_{j,k}‖²` that costs `K m` multiply–adds to build. (i) Memory: `M b / 8` bytes per vector plus a fixed `K m · 4` bytes of codebooks. (ii) Error: with `e = x − x̂`, `|d(y,x) − d_A(y,x)| ≤ ‖e‖` and `|d(y,x)² − d_A(y,x)²| ≤ ‖e‖ (2 d_A(y,x) + ‖e‖)`; `E‖e‖² = Σ_j D_j` where `D_j` is the mean squared error of sub-quantizer `j` on the data. (iii) For inner-product scoring, `yᵀx − yᵀx̂ = yᵀe` has mean 0 over each Voronoi cell when the codebooks are k-means fixed points (each centroid is the cell mean), and `Var(yᵀe) ≤ ‖y‖² E‖e‖²`. (iv) ADC is better than symmetric distance computation (SDC), which also quantizes `y`, because SDC adds the term `‖y − ŷ‖`.
*Assumptions.* Euclidean distance; codebooks trained by Lloyd's algorithm to a fixed point (item iii only); data distribution unchanged between training and querying.
*Steps.* (1) Euclidean distance decomposes over a partition of coordinates: `‖y − x̂‖² = Σ_j ‖y^{(j)} − x̂^{(j)}‖²`. (2) Each `x̂^{(j)}` is one of `K` values, so the term takes at most `K` values; precompute them (cost `K · (m/M) · M = K m` multiply–adds), after which each database vector costs `M` additions. (3) Bits: `M` indices of `b` bits each, so `M b/8` bytes; at `m = 1,024`, `M = 64`, `b = 8`: 64 bytes versus `4 m = 4,096` bytes for float32, a 64× reduction; codebooks `256 × 1,024 × 4 B = 1 MiB`. (4) Triangle inequality: `|‖y − x‖ − ‖y − x̂‖| ≤ ‖x − x̂‖ = ‖e‖`. (5) For squares: `‖y−x‖² − ‖y−x̂‖² = (‖y−x‖ − ‖y−x̂‖)(‖y−x‖ + ‖y−x̂‖)`, bounded in absolute value by `‖e‖ (2 d_A + ‖e‖)`. (6) At a Lloyd fixed point each centroid equals the mean of its cell, so `E[e^{(j)} | cell] = 0`; therefore `E[yᵀe] = Σ_j y^{(j)ᵀ} E[e^{(j)}] = 0`. (7) Variance: `Var(yᵀe) = yᵀ Cov(e) y ≤ λ_max(Cov(e)) ‖y‖² ≤ tr(Cov(e)) ‖y‖² = ‖y‖² E‖e‖²` (the mean of `e` is zero). (8) High-rate heuristic (flagged as an approximation, not a bound): `D_j` falls roughly like `K^{−2/(m/M)}`, so each extra bit per sub-vector helps less as the sub-vector dimension `m/M` grows.
*Check cell.* Data: 20,000 random vectors from a 16-component Gaussian mixture in `R^{64}` (seed 0); `M = 8`, `K = 256`; train with `d2l.kmeans` (20 iterations) and encode. Verify (a) `adc_search` distances equal explicit `‖y − x̂‖²` from reconstructed vectors, relative tolerance `1e-5` in float32; (b) the bound of step 5 holds for 100% of 10⁵ random `(y, x)` pairs; (c) the mean of `yᵀe` over the dataset is below `3σ/√n` in absolute value (σ the standard deviation of `yᵀe`); (d) print the empirical `Var(yᵀe)` next to `‖y‖² E‖e‖²`; (e) memory: `M b/8 = 8` bytes per vector.

**B. IVF probing cost (short).** Cost per query `≈ nlist·m + nprobe·(N/nlist)·m` multiply–adds; minimizing over `nlist` for fixed `nprobe` gives `nlist* = √(nprobe·N)`; check against measured time on the stress corpus for `nlist ∈ {64, 256, 1,024, 4,096}` (reconciliation, not pass/fail).

#### Pitfalls and misconceptions

- Tuning BM25 parameters or the fusion weight on the dev questions; use `train` (the notebook enforces this by passing only `book_qa('train')` to the tuners).
- Comparing recall at different chunk sizes: larger chunks raise Recall@k trivially (2.5 Part 2 fixes this by equalizing token budgets).
- ANN recall versus exact search is not task recall: a dense model whose exact top-10 is wrong cannot be repaired by a perfect index.
- Using ADC tables built from unnormalized or unrotated data; PQ assumes the sub-vectors have comparable variance (rotations such as OPQ address this; named only).
- Hybrid fusion of raw scores without normalization; BM25 and cosine scores live on different scales (RRF avoids this by using ranks).
- Reranking the top-10 and calling it a Recall@100 gain: the reranker can only reorder what the first stage returned.
- HNSW memory is dominated by the vectors, not the graph; building cost and the lack of cheap deletes matter operationally.
- "Dense beats BM25" is a claim about a corpus, a query set and a model; on exact identifiers BM25 often wins (BEIR's own results show BM25 is a strong zero-shot baseline).

#### Summary (draft bullets)

- R3 is a deterministic, versioned chunking of the book with ids that map back to character spans, split by document into train, dev and test.
- BM25 is the baseline a dense retriever must beat; its errors and a dense retriever's are complementary, which fusion exploits.
- Flat search is exact and, at thousands of vectors, fastest; IVF, PQ and HNSW trade recall for memory and latency at larger scale.
- ADC keeps the query exact and quantizes only the database; its distance error is bounded by the quantization error `‖e‖`.
- Rerankers re-score a short list with joint attention over query and document; their gain is limited by the first stage's recall.
- Report recall versus exact search and end-task recall separately.

#### The `d2l.book_qa` artifact: construction, checking and leakage control

This subsection is Part 1's core and is specified in full here.

**Item schema** (`qa_{split}.jsonl`; one JSON object per line): `qid` (`"{split}-{nnnn}"`), `split`, `type` (`single`, `explain`, `numeric`, `code`, `multihop`, `unanswerable`), `question`, `reference` (1–3 sentences), `key_facts` (1–3 atomic strings the answer must contain), `answerable` (bool), `gold_spans` (list of `{doc_id, char_start, char_end}` into the cleaned `docs[doc_id]['text']`; empty if unanswerable), `corpus_version` (`"r3.1"`), `drafter` (model name and version string), `human_verified` (bool), `checks` (the checklist result vector), `closed_book_correct` (bool, from the generator's no-context probe, Qwen3.5-4B), `flags` (for example `general_knowledge`).

**Targets (design numbers, not results).** `dev`: 240 items, `test`: 240 items, both human-verified: 120 `single`, 40 `explain`, 20 `numeric`, 20 `code`, 20 `multihop` (two spans in two documents of the same split), 20 `unanswerable`. `train`: about 4,000 items passing the automatic filters only (`human_verified = false`), the same type mix without a quota; the final counts after filtering are printed by `BookQA.stats()`. At 240 items a proportion near 0.5 has a bootstrap interval of about ±6 points when unpaired and about ±3 points when paired across systems (to be confirmed by the notebook's own bootstrap); differences smaller than that are not interpreted.

**Pipeline (`tools/build_book_qa.py`, run once per release).**
1. **Anchors.** From documents of the target split (`manifest['doc_split']`), take `sec512` chunks of kind `prose` or `mixed` with at least 80 tokens; stratify by chapter with an equal quota per chapter (up to availability); fixed seed; oversample by 1.8× to cover rejections (the rejection rate is measured and reported). Few-shot examples shown to the drafter come **from `train` documents only**.
2. **Drafting.** A frontier model that is **not** in the Qwen3 family (its name and version are written to the manifest as `drafter`) receives the anchor chunk, its heading path and the document title, and returns two candidates: `question`, `reference`, `key_facts`, `type`, and an `evidence` quote that must be a verbatim substring of the anchor. Rules in the prompt: the question must be understandable without seeing the passage (no "in this section", "above", "the passage"); it must ask for something specific to this book (a number, a design choice, a stated result, what a code cell prints or returns), not general knowledge; it must not reuse four or more consecutive words from the evidence except named identifiers; one correct answer. *Multi-hop:* pairs of anchors from two documents of the same split linked by a cross-reference are drafted together. *Unanswerable:* the drafter is shown a section and asked for plausible reader questions on adjacent topics that the section does not answer.
3. **Automatic filters** (all must pass): (a) the evidence is a verbatim substring of the anchor, which yields the gold span (character offsets, expanded to the enclosing sentence or, for code and math, the enclosing block); (b) self-containedness: a regex blacklist plus a yes/no check by an LLM; (c) lexical overlap between question and anchor: Jaccard of non-stopword word types at most 0.5, and no common token 5-gram outside identifiers (histogram `fig_lm_rag_overlap`); (d) answer check: `Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B` (authoring time: tensor-parallel on a T2 pair, or through an API (1.7); responses are cached, so the build replays them at T0), given the anchor and the question, must produce an answer that the rubric judge marks equivalent to `reference`; it is neither the drafter nor the judge, so neither decides which items survive; (e) near-duplicate questions (embedding cosine above 0.9) keep one; (f) *closed-book probe*: the generator, Qwen3.5-4B, answers with no context (it must be the generator: the probe measures what the generator already knows); if the judge marks it correct, set `closed_book_correct = true` (kept, not removed: removing them would bias the set toward obscure facts; the flag is reported). *Unanswerable* candidates additionally require that BM25 top-20 and dense top-20 over all of R3 contain no chunk that an LLM judges to answer the question.
4. **Human verification (`dev`, `test` only).** One annotator per item from a pool of three, with a checklist of yes/no answers: (1) understandable alone; (2) the gold span fully supports the reference (both spans for multi-hop); (3) the reference is correct and the `key_facts` are complete and minimal; (4) after three searches over R3 no other passage answers the question better or contradicts it (guards against a wrong gold id); (5) not answerable from general knowledge alone (sets `flags: general_knowledge`, does not reject); (6) for unanswerable items, no passage answers it. Any "no" on 1–4 or 6 rejects the item; reserve items replace rejections. A random 10% of items are double-annotated; the notebook reports Cohen's κ per checklist item (to be measured; guidelines are revised if κ is below 0.6). Effort estimate: 1–2 minutes per item, so 8–16 annotator-hours for 480 items.
5. **Gold at chunk level.** `gold_spans` are character spans, **independent of chunking**. `d2l.gold_chunk_ids` derives the relevant chunks for any chunking, so Part 2 can compare chunkers fairly. The builder requires that every answerable item has at least one relevant chunk under each shipped chunking, and drops the item otherwise.
6. **Judge calibration set.** 100 `(question, answer)` pairs from the Part 4 baseline on `dev`, each labeled `correct` and `faithful` by two humans (disagreements discussed); shipped as `dev_judge_labels.jsonl`. Part 4 reports the judge's agreement with them.
7. **Versioning and re-anchoring.** Items name `corpus_version`. `tools/reanchor_book_qa.py` maps each gold span into a new R3 version by exact match of the span text, else a fuzzy match of at least 0.9 similarity, and flags or drops failures; the result is published as `d2l-book-qa-v2`. The artifact is immutable per version.
8. **Private split.** Staff regenerate `private` each term with a new seed over `dev`/`test` documents, excluding every anchor that overlaps a public gold span; it is never shipped (assignment A2).

**Held-out discipline and leakage prevention (stated once, enforced by construction).**
- *Documents, not questions, are split.* No `dev` or `test` passage is the source of a `train` question; any fine-tuning data (embedder pairs, Exercise 2.3.5) come from `train` documents, and hard negatives are drawn from `train` documents only.
- *Tuning uses `train`.* BM25 `k1`, `b`, fusion weights, `k`, reranker depth, abstention threshold and prompt variants are chosen on `train` questions. `dev` is used for the stage-by-stage analysis and for choosing *which* fix to apply in Part 5; `test` is evaluated once per notebook, in the last cell of Parts 5 and 6, and never used for a decision.
- *Lexical copying is filtered* (filter c), so BM25 does not win through the question's words; the overlap histogram is shown.
- *Model contamination is measured, not assumed away.* The generator, judge and embedders may have seen the book online. The closed-book arm and the `closed_book_correct` flag quantify what the generator knows without retrieval; results are reported with and without those items; the text says that the book's public status makes some contamination unavoidable.
- *Judge independence.* The drafter (a different family) writes references; the judge is reference-based; its agreement with humans is measured on the calibration set.
- *No self-retrieval.* R3 excludes the Language Models part (2.4), so the notebooks' own examples and result tables cannot be retrieved.

**The RAG prompt (fixed; Parts 3–6 vary only the context and the query).** System message: "You answer questions about the textbook *Dive into Deep Learning* using only the numbered passages. After each statement, cite the passage numbers that support it, for example [2]. If the passages do not contain the answer, reply exactly: I cannot answer this from the provided passages." User message: "Passages:\n[1] {heading_path}\n{text}\n\n[2] …\n\nQuestion: {question}\nAnswer in at most four sentences." Qwen3.5-4B runs with `enable_thinking=False`, greedy decoding, `max_new_tokens=300`, bf16, left padding.

**Metrics.** *Retrieval:* Recall@k and nDCG@10 (2.3, 2.4) and **span coverage at a token budget** `cov@T`: the fraction of items for which the retrieved chunks, taken in rank order until `T` tokens are used, cover at least 50% of some gold span's characters (fair across chunk sizes). *Answers:* **accuracy** = judge verdict `correct` (all `key_facts` present and nothing contradicted, given `reference`; for `unanswerable` items, correct iff the answer is `ABSTAIN`); **faithfulness** = judge verdict that every factual statement is supported by the passages given (without the reference); **citation precision** = (cited ∩ relevant)/cited (relevance by `d2l.gold_chunk_ids`); **citation recall** = (cited ∩ relevant)/(relevant ∩ context), over items with a relevant chunk in context; **citation support** = judge verdict that the cited passages alone support the answer (the ALCE-style check, in simplified form); **abstention rate**, and abstention precision and recall on unanswerable items. *Uncertainty:* every comparison uses `d2l.paired_bootstrap` with 10,000 resamples and `cluster = doc_id` of the first gold span (questions from one document are correlated), so intervals are wider than question-level ones and honest.

#### Derivations

**RAG-Sequence, RAG-Token, the top-k approximation and the gradient to the retriever.**
*Statement.* Let `p_η(z | x) = exp(s_η(x, z)) / Σ_{z'} exp(s_η(x, z'))` be the retriever over a corpus `Z`, with score `s_η(x, z) = q_η(x)ᵀ d_η(z)`, and `p_θ(y | x, z)` the generator. (1) RAG-Sequence: `p_S(y | x) = Σ_z p_η(z | x) Π_t p_θ(y_t | x, z, y_{<t})`. (2) RAG-Token: `p_T(y | x) = Π_t Σ_z p_η(z | x) p_θ(y_t | x, z, y_{<t})`. (3) With `Z_k(x)` the `k` highest-probability documents and `m_k = Σ_{z∈Z_k} p_η(z | x)`, the truncated sum `p_S^{(k)}` satisfies `0 ≤ p_S − p_S^{(k)} ≤ (1 − m_k) · max_z p_θ(y | x, z)`. (4) For RAG-Sequence, `∇_η log p_S(y | x) = E_{z ∼ p(z | x, y)}[∇_η s_η(x, z)] − E_{z ∼ p_η(z | x)}[∇_η s_η(x, z)]` with posterior `p(z | x, y) ∝ p_η(z | x) p_θ(y | x, z)`; in particular, for free scores, `∂ log p_S/∂s_z = p(z | x, y) − p_η(z | x)`.
*Assumptions.* The corpus is finite and `p_η` is a softmax over scores; the generator's likelihood does not depend on `η`; `y` has a fixed length `T` for the Token form (the length-normalization question is out of scope).
*Steps.* (1) *Marginalize the latent document.* Treat `z` as a latent variable with prior `p_η(z | x)`; the sequence model picks one `z` for the whole answer, giving (1) by the law of total probability. (2) *Token form.* Re-sample `z` at every token: the product moves outside the sum, giving (2); the two coincide when `Z` has one document, and they differ otherwise because a product of sums is not a sum of products. (3) *Top-k bound.* Since `0 ≤ p_θ(y | x, z) ≤ max_z p_θ(y | x, z) =: L_max`, `p_S − p_S^{(k)} = Σ_{z ∉ Z_k} p_η(z | x) p_θ(y | x, z) ≤ (1 − m_k) L_max`, and `≥ 0`. In practice `m_k` is close to 1 when the retriever is peaked, which is the justification for retrieving only `k` documents. (4) *Gradient.* `∇_η log p_S = (1/p_S) Σ_z p_θ(y | x, z) ∇_η p_η(z | x)`. Use `∇ p_η = p_η ∇ log p_η` and `∇_η log p_η(z | x) = ∇_η s_η(x, z) − Σ_{z'} p_η(z' | x) ∇_η s_η(x, z')`. Then `∇_η log p_S = Σ_z [p_η(z | x) p_θ(y | x, z)/p_S] (∇ s_z − E_{p_η}[∇ s])`, and the bracket is the posterior `p(z | x, y)`, so the sum is `E_{p(z|x,y)}[∇ s_z] − E_{p_η}[∇ s]` because the posterior sums to 1. (5) *Reading the result.* The retriever is pushed toward documents that make the answer likely (posterior) and away from documents that it currently favors (prior); the gradient vanishes when `p_θ(y | x, z)` does not depend on `z` (the posterior equals the prior), i.e. when the generator ignores the documents. (6) *Cost.* The exact gradient needs `p_θ(y | x, z)` for all `z`; with the top-`k` approximation it needs `k` generator passes per example and ignores documents outside `Z_k`, so the retriever receives no signal for relevant documents it fails to retrieve (the training analogue of the `not_retrieved` failure class). REALM and RAG address this with periodically refreshed document embeddings and a fixed or slowly updated document encoder.
*Check cell (float64, CPU).* A toy with `|Z| = 8` documents, random scores `s ∈ R^8` (seed 0, `requires_grad=True`) and a random likelihood table `L_z = p_θ(y | x, z) ∈ (0, 1)`. (a) `torch.autograd` gradient of `log Σ_z softmax(s)_z L_z` equals `posterior − prior` to `1e-12`. (b) For `k = 1…8`, the truncated sum satisfies `0 ≤ p − p_k ≤ (1 − m_k) max L` for every `k`. (c) A 3-token toy (token likelihood tables `L_{t,z,v}` over a vocabulary of 5) shows `p_S ≠ p_T` in general and `p_S = p_T` when `|Z| = 1`; the check prints both values and asserts equality for `|Z| = 1` to `1e-12`. (d) Setting `L_z` constant makes the gradient exactly 0 to `1e-12`.

#### Pitfalls and misconceptions

- Tuning on `dev` and then reporting `dev` (or reading `test` repeatedly); the notebook's discipline is `train` for tuning, `dev` for analysis, `test` once.
- Recall@k comparisons across chunk sizes without equalizing tokens (Part 2 uses `cov@T`).
- Using an LLM judge from the same family as the generator without checking its agreement with humans; self-preference and verbosity biases are common (1.5 gives the position-bias protocol for pairwise judges; this pointwise judge is reference-based and calibrated).
- Calling an answer faithful because it is correct (or correct because it is faithful): the two are separate metrics, and `ignored` and `unsupported` failures differ.
- Treating "not retrieved" and "ranked too low" as one thing; the first needs a better first stage, the second a better reranker or a bigger context.
- Citation precision from gold ids penalizes valid citations of non-gold but supporting chunks; `citation support` (judge) is the check against that.
- Closed-book accuracy is not zero for a public book; ignoring it overstates retrieval's contribution.
- Long-context claims from needle-in-a-haystack tests do not carry over to multi-passage questions; compare on the task.
- Prompt-injection and conflicting passages in the corpus (the book's own text sometimes states a convention that a later section changes); outside the measured experiments, one paragraph.

#### Summary (draft bullets)

- A RAG system is a chain of stages; measure each with its own metric and keep paired, clustered error bars.
- The evaluation set is built from character-level gold spans, split by document, drafted by a model from another family, filtered automatically, and verified by hand for `dev` and `test`.
- Chunking changes both what can be found and what the generator reads; compare chunkers at equal token budgets.
- Query-side LLM stages and rerankers pay for themselves only on some question types; the notebook shows which, with intervals.
- Failures fall into four checkable classes (not retrieved, ranked too low, ignored, unsupported); fix the largest class and remeasure on a split you did not analyze.
- Long context and retrieval trade latency for coverage; at equal latency retrieval often wins on few-passage questions (to be measured here), while whole documents help when evidence is spread.
- RAG-Sequence and RAG-Token marginalize over retrieved documents; the retriever's gradient is posterior minus prior, and it vanishes when the generator ignores the documents.

#### Risks and open decisions

- **Time budget.** The full notebook is about 39 min against a stated "about twice the usual" 30 min; cut order: the 28k-token arm, the position sweep, the `A4` arm, the `explain` and `code` types from the Part 4 sample, then V1/V3a controls in Part 2. `QUICK` keeps every part but uses 80 questions and no 28k arm. All minute counts are targets, not measurements.
- **Judge quality.** A Qwen3.5-9B judge may disagree with humans more than a frontier model would. Fallbacks, in order: enable thinking on disputed items; where no whole 24 GB GPU is available, use `Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B` in bf16 rather than a 4-bit 9B; keep the judge fixed and report its agreement. The judge (Qwen3.5-9B) and the generator (Qwen3.5-4B) are different models of the same family; `rag_judge` grades against a gold span, which limits self-preference, and the human calibration set measures what remains. The calibration set exists to make the choice visible.
- **Human annotation load.** Approved 2026-10-29: `dev` and `test` stay at 240 human-verified items each, annotated by the pool of three. 480 items at 1–2 minutes is 8–16 annotator-hours plus 10% double annotation, about 3–6 hours per annotator.
- **Contamination of the generator and embedders** with D2L text is unavoidable; the closed-book arm is the mitigation, not a cure.
- **Memory.** The 28k-token arm needs 7.8 GiB of text-only weights plus 0.85 GiB of KV cache (32,768 B × 28,000) and about 50 MB of linear-attention state, plus prefill activations: well inside the 24 GB GPU; use `logits_to_keep=1` for prefill and chunked prefill if needed; `d2l.config_memory` is the pre-flight check. Fallback: cap at 20k tokens.
- **Editor decisions.** (1) Whether `test` should be hidden behind hashes (it is public text, so the blueprint relies on the private split for grading). (2) Decided 2026-10-29: A2's judge is Qwen3.5-9B in bf16, reproducible offline; an API judge (stronger, not reproducible offline) was the rejected alternative. (3) The drafting model must be recorded with its version; if licensing forbids shipping its outputs, ship only the filtered items' questions and references written by humans, which is slower.
