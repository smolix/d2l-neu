# Spanish, French and Brazilian Portuguese terminology

Each locale contains all 1,184 current concepts. As of this review: Spanish has 69 `verified_external` and 1,115 `proposed`; French has 91 `verified_external` and 1,093 `proposed`; Brazilian Portuguese has 17 `verified_d2l`, 67 `verified_external`, and 1,100 `proposed`. There are no unresolved rows. A filled proposal is an editorial suggestion, not an attested, native-speaker-reviewed or translation-ready constraint. The names of algorithms and acronyms retained in English are also proposed rather than automatically treated as verified invariants.

Evidence comes from localized Google for Developers topic glossaries, numbered bilingual translated Stanford CS229/CS230 teaching notes, and the pinned Portuguese D2L repository. The complete French and Portuguese Google glossary endpoints returned English fallback; topic pages served localized text and were checked instead. Google localized content may contain automatically translated or awkward wording; its appearance does not establish human review. The paired teaching-note repository contains English statements followed by translations and is pinned to commit `99f45b6d65b8e60f637d8ac0db15f76327197c23`; Portuguese D2L is pinned to `ee8a9f0a88a4791f277a55e32cb6c83fd00a7cac`. Evidence locators point to the original target-language line or glossary anchor; English alignment lines appear in evidence context. Source spelling is retained in evidence, with initial capitalization normalized in preferred forms. Portuguese language and regional variation require further review.

The English word *vocabulary* exposed a systematic alignment error: the Stanford teaching page's heading means terminology for an evaluation discussion, not an NLP token inventory. Spanish and French matches were downgraded to proposed, and Brazilian Portuguese now cites a D2L section defining an NLP vocabulary. Other ambiguous common words were kept provisional unless a source establishes the intended sense: model intercept versus statistical bias, convolution kernel versus covariance kernel, density score versus evaluation score, RL return, and classification precision versus accuracy. Other candidate headings with truncated wording, misleading language fallback, mismatched sense, or typographical errors were not promoted.
