# The Cost of Attention

Source: `chapter_attention/attention-at-scale.md`. About 4,600 words of prose, 2,050 words of exercises, 21 code cells per framework (PyTorch and JAX only; no TensorFlow or MXNet material).

## Verdict

This section teaches; it is not a survey. Each of its three remedies (exact attention in blocks, a window, a factorizing kernel) is derived, implemented in a dozen lines, and measured, and the lists of released models are short. The arithmetic is right everywhere I recomputed it. Against the bar it is competent and useful but not yet memorable, for three reasons. It is three sections in one file, with one idea each and no room for any of them to breathe. Its rhythm is one formula (implement, then "maximum deviation" against a reference) repeated six times. And it ends on a timing figure that compares a hand-tuned fused kernel with two unfused Python implementations, tells a different story in each framework tab, and leaves out the only question a reader will ask at that point: these three mechanisms compute different functions, so which one do people use, and what does the cheaper one cost in quality? The most important fix is the ending: make the final comparison fair, and close with a judgment.

## What works

- The opening motivates from the previous section's context of 128 bytes and gives one number the reader can hold: "storing their scores in single precision takes 256 MiB for one head of one sequence" (correct).
- The crossover $n > 2d$ under "Counting FLOPs", with GPT-2 small below it and a wide model at 131,072 tokens sixteen times above it. One equation turned into a rule of thumb with two cases. Keep.
- "Counting Memory": a prediction ($8n^2$ bytes) followed by a measurement that matches to a few megabytes. This is a prediction that could have failed (allocator overhead, extra temporaries) and the reader sees it hold.
- The online softmax, :eqref:`eq_online-softmax`, with the sentence "Chunking changes the order of evaluation, not the definition of attention." The reader finishes knowing what FlashAttention is, exactly, in 25 lines of code.
- The tile count under "A Linear-Cost Implementation": "A fused kernel cannot skip single scores." followed by 53.1% and 12.1% of tiles. This is the kind of detail that matters in practice and that papers do not explain; the consequence ("narrowing the window below the tile size saves nothing more") is stated.
- The cache fraction for interleaved windowed and global layers, with Gemma 3's numbers worked (a quarter at 8,192, tending to one sixth; correct).
- Linear attention read as an associative memory with "$\phi(\mathbf{k}_t)$ the address and $\mathbf{v}_t$ the content", and the honest paragraph that follows: "The state $\mathbf{S}_t$ is a lossy summary."
- The comparison of the parallel form's memory with the score matrix it replaces ("more than the score matrix it replaces until $n$ exceeds $d\, d_v = 4096$"; correct). It motivates chunking by a number.

## Structural findings

1. **Three sections in one; split in two.** The file holds (a) the cost count and its measurement, (b) exact attention without the matrix, (c) windows, (d) linear attention as a recurrence, (e) a timing comparison. Parts (a) and (b) answer "what does dense attention cost, and how much of that cost is avoidable without changing the result". Parts (c) and (d) answer "what do we give up to make the cost linear". The seam is before the heading "Windowed and Sparse Attention". Proposed files: "The Cost of Attention" (Quadratic Time and Memory; Exact Attention Without the Matrix; about 2,000 words of prose and Exercises 1, 2, 3, 4, 7) and "Windowed and Linear Attention" (the rest; about 2,600 words and Exercises 5, 6, 8, 9, 10). The timing section then belongs to the second file and compares like with like (see COST-17). The current section title describes only the first half.
2. **The section has no closing judgment.** The body ends on "linear attention is also faster during generation, where its state stays the size of one matrix", which reads as a recommendation for linear attention. Eighty lines earlier the text said that it "did not replace softmax attention in language models" and "models language less well". A reader should finish knowing: exact attention in a fused kernel is what nearly every model uses and is free; windows are used in some released models, usually interleaved with global layers; plain linear attention is not used as is, and its descendants are the subject of :numref:`chap_modern_rnn`. Write that paragraph as the ending of the body.
3. **One rhythm.** Six cells have the form "the X form should agree with the reference; maximum deviation: 3e-07" (chunked softmax, windowed, FlexAttention or the JAX window argument, linear parallel against recurrent, linear chunked against recurrent), plus one that checks the receptive-field formula. Each is a legitimate test of an implementation (it can fail), unlike a sampling check of a theorem. But six in a row with the same sentence shape is the mechanical formula the author asked to remove. Keep the check for `chunked_attention` (it is the point of the online softmax). Fold the others into the cell that defines the function as an `assert` or a one-line print with no prose paragraph, or combine the three linear-attention forms into one check.
4. **Released-model facts duplicate the further-reading notebook.** `further-reading.md` has "Windows in Released Models", "Sparsity Learned from Content", "FlashAttention-3 and FlexAttention in Detail" and "Gated Recurrences and Hybrids". The section itself names Mistral 7B, Gemma 3, gpt-oss, Llama 4, natively sparse attention, FlashAttention-2 and -3, and MiniMax-01. These are short and accurate as far as I can tell, so this is not a survey problem, but each is one more name at equal weight. Keep Mistral 7B (it illustrates the reach formula) and Gemma 3 (it illustrates the cache fraction); move gpt-oss, Llama 4 and the FlashAttention-2 and -3 paragraph to the notebook.
5. **Dependence on chapter 12.** Down from the six forward references the October review counted to two (`sec_ssm`, `sec_matrix-state`), both in the last paragraph of "Linear Attention in Practice", neither load-bearing. Good.

## Passage-level findings

**COST-1** (minor, code). Imports. Quote: "from torch.nn.attention import SDPBackend, sdpa_kernel". The PyTorch tab has two imports cells. House rule: one imports cell.

**COST-2** (minor, explanation). Under "CNNs, RNNs, and Self-Attention". Quote: "A short path avoids multiplying many". The paragraph defines path length and then qualifies it in the same breath with "recurrent Jacobians", a term from the RNN chapter that is not recalled. One clause would do: "a signal that crosses $n$ recurrent steps is multiplied by $n$ Jacobians and tends to vanish (:numref:`sec_rnn`)". The table and figure are standard and clear; this subsection is the one inherited from the first edition and reads more like a recap than the rest. It could lose a third of its length.

**COST-3** (minor, language and explanation). Throughout. Quote: "receives $n$ queries, keys, and values of dimension $d$ and computes". Here $d$ is the model width when counting a multi-head layer ($4n^2d$, $8nd^2$) and silently the head width in the single-head experiments ("a head dimension of $d_h = 64$", then `d` inside the functions and "a fixed-size state of $d \times d_v$ numbers"). State once that the counts are per layer in terms of the model width $d$, that one head of width $d_h$ costs $4n^2 d_h$, and use $d_h$ in the linear-attention state.

**COST-4** (minor, correctness of reading). Under "Counting Memory". Quote: "The measurement matches $8n^2$ bytes up to a small extra workspace, and". At $n = 2048$ the PyTorch measurement is 40.6 MiB against 32 predicted, a quarter more. "Small" is true from 4,096 on. Say "up to a few megabytes of workspace".

**COST-5** (minor, correctness, unverified). Under "Counting Memory". Quote: "still fit in the GPU's on-chip cache. Beyond a few thousand tokens, each". The stored timings are 0.07, 0.46, 1.84, 7.37 ms (PyTorch) and 0.15, 0.25, 1.21, 4.66 ms (JAX): the first step is far from fourfold in both tabs and the later steps are about fourfold. The three causes offered for the first step (unsaturated GPU, launch overhead, cache) are not tested, and I cannot confirm the cache explanation. Offer them as "likely" or drop the cache clause. The two tab notes above this passage (PyTorch allocator, XLA arena and `memory_analysis`) are framework plumbing of the kind guide section 9 sends to code comments or to :numref:`sec_use_gpu`, where `d2l.peak_memory` is defined; the JAX note is eight lines long.

**COST-6** (moderate, explanation). Under "Blockwise Online Softmax". Quote: "*online*, the way one computes a running mean". The update :eqref:`eq_online-softmax` is given in full generality at once. A two-block numeric instance would make it land: scores $(1, 3)$ then $(5, 2)$, show $m$, $s$ after each block and that $e^{m - m'}$ rescales the first block's sum. Four numbers, in the Illustrated-Transformer manner. As it stands the reader must trust the algebra until the deviation check. Also say why the maximum is carried at all (overflow of $e^{a}$ in half precision) in the text, not only in Exercise 2.

**COST-7** (minor, thought). Under "A Chunked Implementation". Quote: "The chunked version is meant to be exact, so we check it against the". Fine as the one check to keep in prose (structural finding 3). The following paragraph has a stray recap: "The result and the $4n^2d$ FLOP count are unchanged. Only the summation order and the memory schedule differ." repeats "Chunking changes the order of evaluation, not the definition of attention." from fifteen lines earlier. Keep the earlier one.

**COST-8** (minor, correctness, fairly sure). Under "Memory Traffic as the Computational Bottleneck". Quote: "hundreds of arithmetic operations in the time it takes to fetch one float". Roughly right for half-precision tensor-core arithmetic against memory bandwidth on current data-center GPUs (my estimate: a few hundred), but stated as a universal. Give the two numbers for one named GPU or point to :numref:`chap_performance`, which owns this argument; Exercise 4 asks the reader to look them up, so the text can simply quote the result. "Off-chip memory" and "on-chip memory" are not defined; say "the GPU's main memory" and "the small fast memory next to the arithmetic units" once.

**COST-9** (minor, correctness, as far as I know accurate). Same subsection. FlashAttention (tiling to on-chip memory, recomputation in the backward pass), FlashAttention-2 (fewer non-matmul operations, parallelism over query blocks) and FlashAttention-3 (overlap of data movement and arithmetic on newer GPUs, FP8) are described consistently with the papers as I know them. "divides by the normalizer only once at the end" is my understanding of one of the FlashAttention-2 changes. Quote: "In 16-bit precision every version". This sentence is hard to parse on first reading (it is contrasting with the 8-bit mode); write "Except in the 8-bit mode, every version computes exact attention."

**COST-10** (minor, correctness of reading). Same subsection. Quote: "The fused kernel is several times faster than the naive implementation, and". Stored: 19.95 ms against 1.06 ms in PyTorch (about 19 times), 9.32 against 1.34 in JAX (about 7 times). "Several times" undersells one tab; "about ten times, depending on the framework" is within what survives re-execution. Also "runs in a fused kernel" for "sequences beyond a few hundred tokens" is asserted, not measured (the cell runs only $n = 8192$).

**COST-11** (minor, correctness, unverified). Tab notes after the fused-kernel cell and the window cell. I believe `jax.nn.dot_product_attention` defaults to the XLA implementation and accepts `implementation='cudnn'` and `local_window_size`, and that `flex_attention` without `torch.compile` falls back to a dense computation; I could not run either here. The claim that the cuDNN path takes the window "as a parameter" I cannot confirm.

**COST-12** (moderate, explanation). Under "Receptive Field Growth with Depth". Quote: "multiplication, and its $L$-th power says which positions can influence". The cell confirms $1 + L(w-1)$ by powering a band matrix, with a three-panel heatmap. The formula is obvious from the sentence before it, the check cannot fail, and the heatmaps draw a widening band. This is the one cell of the section I would cut outright. What the reader does not get, and needs, is the caveat that reach is not use: a dependency that needs $n/w$ hops must survive $n/w$ attention layers, each of which mixes it with everything else in the window. The text says "it assumes that few dependencies need more than a small number of hops", which is the right sentence; give it the space the cell now takes.

**COST-13** (minor, correctness, mostly confirmed). Same subsection. Mistral 7B (window 4,096, 32 layers, reach about 131,000; $1 + 32 \cdot 4095 = 131{,}041$) and Gemma 3 (five local layers of span 1,024 per global layer) match what I know. gpt-oss alternating dense layers and 128-token windows matches my recollection of the model card. Llama 4 dropping RoPE in periodic global layers and chunked local attention matches my recollection of the released configuration, but I cannot confirm the stated aim; mark unverified. Quote: "the sequence and therefore see only short offsets" (the sentence after it gives the aim).

**COST-14** (minor, explanation). Under "Kernelizing the Score". Quote: "some elementwise feature map $\phi$. Causal attention with this kernel is". The feature map need not be elementwise (the exact map of Exercise 8 is not), and the equation writes outputs as row vectors $\mathbf{o}_t^\top$ without saying so. Drop "elementwise" here and say it when $\mathrm{elu} + 1$ is chosen. Before the display, say in words what is about to happen: "because the kernel is an inner product, the sum over keys can be done before the query arrives".

**COST-15** (moderate, pedagogy). Under "Kernelizing the Score". Quote: "become nearly one-hot in the same way: scaling up a query sharpens the". This is the key limitation of linear attention and the reason for everything in chapter 12, and it is argued in one sentence and an exercise. A four-line cell would show it: the largest attention weight of a softmax row and of an $\mathrm{elu} + 1$ row as the query is scaled by 1, 4, 16. That experiment teaches something no derivation in the section does, and it would replace one of the equivalence checks.

**COST-16** (minor, correctness, as far as I know accurate). Under "Linear Attention in Practice". The attributions (Katharopoulos et al. for $\mathrm{elu} + 1$; Schlag et al. for the fast-weight view; Yang et al. for chunked training and gating; Arora et al. for the recall weakness and the Taylor feature map; MiniMax-01's one softmax layer after seven linear ones) match what I know. Quote: "made the recurrent form competitive. First, the state learns to forget: a". "Two changes made the recurrent form competitive" is stronger than the evidence: competitive on what, at what scale? Write "brought it close to softmax attention on language-modeling benchmarks" or name the claim's scope.

**COST-17** (major, pedagogy and thought; experiment fairness). Under "Measured Running Time". Quote: "We now time the three strategies of this section on equal terms, as the". The terms are not equal. Dense attention runs in a fused vendor kernel; the windowed and linear mechanisms run as the unfused teaching functions under `vmap`. The stored PyTorch figure shows the consequence: the two linear-cost curves are flat at about 0.65 ms up to 2,048 to 4,096 tokens, windowed attention with $w = 128$ is slower than dense attention up to about 10,000 tokens, and at 32,768 it is about 10 ms against 28 ms. In the JAX figure the three curves start together and the linear-cost ones are about eight times faster at 32,768. So the shared sentence "each of them therefore needs well under half the time of dense attention" is the weakest statement true of both tabs, and two tab notes are needed to explain why the figures differ. This is the residue of the October finding that the tabs drew opposite conclusions: the sign now agrees, the story still does not. The section has already built the fair comparison: `flex_attention` with the band `BlockMask` in PyTorch and `local_window_size` with the cuDNN kernel in JAX. Time those against dense attention (the tile count predicts the ratio: 12% against 53% of tiles), and time linear attention separately or drop it from the figure, since its teaching implementation says little about a tuned one. Also remove "Enough work per call keeps the fixed cost of launching kernels from hiding the growth with $n$", which the PyTorch figure contradicts for half of its range.

**COST-18** (moderate, thought). Under "Measured Running Time". Quote: "mechanisms grow about twofold. At 32,768 tokens each of them therefore". Beyond fairness, the comparison is of speed alone among mechanisms that compute different things. A reader could conclude that windows or linear attention are simply better at long contexts. One sentence must say that the figure measures cost only and that Exercise 9 measures what is lost; better, bring one number from that exercise into the text.

**COST-19** (minor, language). Summary. Quote: "dense time grows fourfold per doubling of the length and the time of the". "Measured," as a sentence opener (it ends the line before) is a participle tag; write "In our measurements". The Summary is a list of everything mentioned (seven results in six sentences); after the split each half gets a summary of three or four sentences.

## For the chapter review

- Order in the chapter is right: positions, then cost, then circuits. The section assumes the masks of :numref:`sec_attention-scoring-functions`, the FLOP count :eqref:`eq_multihead-flops` and the KV-cache remark of :numref:`sec_multihead-attention`, and `d2l.Benchmark` and `d2l.peak_memory` from the Basics.
- It sets up chapter 12 (linear attention as a matrix-state recurrence; `linear_attention_chunked` is extended in :numref:`sec_matrix-state`) and chapter 13 (memory traffic against arithmetic). The memory-traffic argument is made here in one paragraph and owned by chapter 13; check that the two do not both derive it.
- Recommend the split in structural finding 1. The chapter would then have seven teaching sections, which its introduction's one-sentence-per-section route paragraph ("asks how ... asks which ... asks what ...", six in a row) already strains to list; that paragraph is the pattern the writing guide section 12.1 names as a defect.
- Pattern I suspect holds chapter-wide: each new implementation is followed by a "maximum deviation" cell introduced by the same sentence shape; and $d$ against $d_h$ is not used consistently across sections.
