# Gated Recurrent Networks

Source: `chapter_recurrent-modern/lstm.md` (lines 1 to 1059, slides ignored). About 5,000 words of prose and exercises, of which about 1,100 are exercises; 19 code cells per tab.

## Verdict

The first third of this section is close to the bar. It goes from the scalar Jacobian product to a running sum to a gated sum in three short steps, each with numbers the reader can check (4% after 30 steps at $w = 0.9$, half-lives of 7, 69 and 693 steps), and it answers the obvious objection to the LSTM's "constant error" story honestly. The LSTM and GRU are then stated compactly and correctly. From "Delayed Recall" on, the section stops being about gates and becomes the place where the chapter stores its shared harness: seven cells and about 290 source lines separate the statement of the task from its result, including a paragraph on initialization that is addressed to the authors. The single most important thing to fix is that the experiment does not show what the section says it shows. In the stored PyTorch output the plain RNN recalls the symbol at every delay up to 100 steps, while the LSTM with default gates fails from 20 and the GRU from 50. The opening claim ("plain recurrent networks learn dependencies over a few dozen steps at best") and the Summary ("Both remember over spans where a plain network fails") are contradicted by the section's own table, and the prose calls the RNN "erratic" without explaining why it works at all. The explanation is one sentence away (the LeCun normal recurrent matrix has spectral radius about 1), and the section should give it.

## What works

- The opening example. "the keys to the cabinet in the hall" with the verb 30 characters after the subject is concrete, countable, and reused later ("for instance a plural subject that matters only when a verb arrives") to motivate the output gate. Keep it.
- The three-step build under "Gates": "The simplest state whose Jacobian is exactly one is a running sum", then "But a running sum cannot forget", then the gated sum. Each step has one job and the reader feels the need for the next. This is the best explanation in the three sections I reviewed.
- Half-life as the unit of memory ("about 7 steps for $f = 0.9$, 69 for $f = 0.99$, and 693 for $f = 0.999$"). It is used consistently through `ssm.md` and `mamba.md` and gives the chapter a common scale.
- The objection paragraph: "A reader may object that the gates are not fixed ... That is correct. The cell does not guarantee that every gradient survives; it provides one path along which gradients can survive". This is the candid voice the author asked for, and it is more accurate than most textbook treatments.
- "A gated network can learn long memories, but it does not start with them." A sharp sentence that the experiment then tests with the keep-gate rows. The two-pairs-of-rows design (same architecture, different bias) is a good experiment.
- The GRU is tied back to the gated sum in one sentence ("the gated sum ... with the input gate tied to the forget gate, $i_t = 1 - f_t$"), and its from-scratch code is left to an exercise instead of repeating `LSTMScratch`.
- The closing paragraph on character-level perplexity is honest about what the task measures: "Character-level language modeling at this scale rewards long memory little".
- The exercises are strong and graded (see below).

## Structural findings

1. **The section is two things: a lesson on gates and the chapter's shared harness.** `init_lecun`, `Recurrent`, `RecallModel`, `init_keep_gate`, `train_queries`, `query_accuracy`, `train_delayed_recall`, `SeqLM`, `PennTreebank` and `fit_lm` are all defined here because this is the first section, not because the lesson needs them here. The cost is paid by the reader of this section: the delayed recall task is stated at source line 379 and its result is read at line 673. Proposed seam: keep "Gates", "Long Short-Term Memory", "Gated Recurrent Units" and a short "Delayed Recall" (task, one model cell, table, reading) as the section. Move the harness into one clearly marked subsection at the end of the section ("The Chapter's Test Bench": `SeqLM`, Penn Treebank, `fit_lm`, the first three perplexities), or shorten it by putting `init_lecun`, `train_queries` and `query_accuracy` into one cell introduced by three sentences. Either way, the reader should reach the recall table within one screen of the task.
2. **The experiment and the thesis disagree** (LSTM-9, LSTM-10, LSTM-15). The fix is in the prose, not the experiment: say what the table shows, including that an ungated network with a well-scaled recurrent matrix carries a symbol 100 steps, and restate what gates buy (a memory that is robust to the recurrent matrix, per-unit, and controllable), instead of "plain networks fail".
3. **"Deep Recurrent Networks" is a mislabelled subsection.** It defines the residual stack every later section uses (`SeqLM`, "sequence mixer") and then appends a paragraph on bidirectional networks that no later text or experiment uses, only exercise 8. Rename it for what it holds, and either cut the bidirectional paragraph to two sentences or move it into the exercise's statement.
4. **The "speed problem" is stated four times**: in the introduction ("so training must still process a sequence one step at a time"), after the library check ("It is still a loop over time steps, each waiting for the one before"), in the closing paragraph ("The gates solved the memory problem, but not the speed problem"), and in the Summary. `ssm.md` then opens with it again at length. Keep the introduction's sentence and the Summary's; the closing paragraph before the Summary can go (decision 6, one ending), with its last sentence ("what remains of a gated network when the gates read only the input") moved into the Summary.
5. **Overlap with Basics.** "Vanishing Gradients" re-derives the Jacobian product that `chapter_recurrent-neural-networks/bptt.md` owns (with an experiment at three spectral radii, `subsec_vanishing-exploding`). The re-derivation is short and the scalar reading is new, so it may stay under the author's rule, but it should open by citing the Basics result and go straight to the scalar case, which would cut about a third of the subsection.
6. **Missing: what practice is in 2026.** The section says gated networks carried speech and translation "until transformers took their place" and stops. One or two sentences on where LSTMs are still the right tool (small streaming models, on-device, time series, reinforcement-learning agents) and that the standard default is a forget-gate bias of 1 would tell the reader what to do with this section. The forget-bias-of-1 fact is currently only in exercise 3.

## Passage-level findings

**LSTM-1** (minor, correctness). Heading: introduction. Quote: "so plain recurrent networks learn dependencies over a few". The full claim, "a few dozen steps at best", is contradicted by the section's own table (RNN 1.00 at 100 steps in PyTorch, 1.00 at 50 and 0.75 at 200 in JAX). Fix: "so plain recurrent networks learn long dependencies only when their recurrent matrix happens to keep these factors near one, and then unreliably".

**LSTM-2** (minor, explanation). Heading: Vanishing Gradients. Quote: "same holds with the largest singular value of". For matrices the shrinking half of the statement is right (largest singular value below 1 forces decay), but "the same holds" also covers the growing half, where the relevant quantity is the spectral radius, and a matrix can have largest singular value 2 and spectral radius 1 (the LeCun normal matrix used in this section's experiment has exactly these, checked numerically at $n = 64$). Fix: state only the sufficient condition for decay, and add that the borderline case, spectral radius near one, is where a plain network can carry a signal a long way; the experiment below meets it.

**LSTM-3** (minor, language). Heading: Vanishing Gradients. Quote: "and then the factor $1 - h_s^2$ drives it toward zero again. For matrices the". This source line runs past the wrap and the paragraph has ragged lines ("place of $|w|$. To"), a sign of a patch. Reflow; also split the paragraph before "To carry information over hundreds of steps", which is the conclusion.

**LSTM-4** (minor, explanation). Heading: Gates as Learned Decay. Quote: "the previous output of the network. For the fraction kept, one per unit,". "The previous output of the network" is $\mathbf{h}_{t-1}$, but the reader has just been working with a scalar memory $c_t$ and has not been told that the layer will have two vectors. The equation that follows uses $\mathbf{h}_{t-1}$ and vector $\mathbf{f}_t$ without a bridge from the scalar $f_t$, $c_t$. Fix: one sentence, "From here on the memory is a vector, one $c$ per unit, and the layer also has an output $\mathbf{h}_t$, defined below; the gates read that output."

**LSTM-5** (minor, thought). Heading: Gates as Learned Decay. Quote: "product of numbers below one cannot grow, so the memory path cannot". True for the product of decays, but the cell itself can grow: with $f_t$ near one it accumulates up to one unit per step (exercise 2 proves the bound $t$). A reader who remembers "a running sum ... grows with the length of the sequence" two paragraphs earlier will ask whether the gated sum still does. Fix: add "the gradient along the path cannot explode; the cell's value can still grow while the forget gate stays near one, which is why the output passes through a $\tanh$".

**LSTM-6** (moderate, explanation). Heading: The Memory Cell. Quote: "contrasts two kinds of memory: the weights of a network change slowly and". The gloss on the name is correct and nice, but the paragraph never says why the LSTM needs two vectors, the question a reader coming from the simple RNN has. The answer arrives a paragraph later, spread over two sentences. Put it first: the cell is protected (nothing but a gate multiplies it), and the output is a gated, squashed view of it, so that what is stored and what is shown can differ.

**LSTM-7** (minor, pedagogy). Heading: The Memory Cell. No worked number accompanies the update. After :eqref:`lstm_update`, one scalar step would help: with $c_{t-1} = 0.8$, $f_t = 0.95$, $i_t = 0.1$, $\tilde c_t = -0.5$, $o_t = 0.2$, the cell becomes $0.71$ and the output $0.2 \tanh(0.71) = 0.12$: stored, barely shown. The scan section does this for the combine rule and it works there.

**LSTM-8** (minor, correctness). Heading: Reset and Update Gates. Quote: "Writing the GRU from scratch would repeat `LSTMScratch` with three gated". The experiments use the library GRU, which applies the reset gate after the recurrent product, not as in :eqref:`gru_tilde_H`. The text discloses this only in exercise 4. Add one clause here: "the library layers used below compute a slightly different candidate (exercise 4)". Also, "three gated layers" is loose: the GRU has two gates and a candidate.

**LSTM-9** (major, thought). Heading: Delayed Recall. Quote: "The chapter's models initialize their own linear and convolutional layers". This 14-line paragraph is written for the authors (guide 4.5). It forward-references "gated linear attention ... of :numref:`sec_hybrids`" five sections ahead, reports that a later result "depends on the random seed", and describes PyTorch's default bias distribution, all between the description of a three-line model and its code. It also hides the one fact the reader of this section needs: the recurrent matrices get LeCun normal weights, which gives the plain RNN a spectral radius near 1 (PyTorch's default would give about 0.6). Fix: cut to two sentences here ("All layers in this chapter start with LeCun normal weights and zero biases, :numref:`sec_numerical_stability`; `init_lecun` applies this to PyTorch layers, whose defaults differ"), and move the consequence for the RNN into the reading of the table. The same applies to the JAX note "atomic operations, which wait on one another when a few filler tokens fill", which is an implementation footnote about `d2l.Embed` that belongs in :numref:`subsec_utils_embed`.

**LSTM-10** (major, pedagogy). Heading: Delayed Recall. Quote: "Besides the three networks with their gates starting near one half, we train an". Two problems. First, "three networks with their gates" counts the plain RNN, which has no gates. Second, the keep-gate setting is introduced as a second thought, with the input gate set to $-5$ and no reason given. The reader wants to know why the input gate must be closed (so that fillers are not written over the symbol; at $\sigma(-5) = 0.007$ the first symbol is also written at only 0.7% strength, and the network must learn to open the gate for it). Fix: state the prediction before the cell ("a gate at one half halves the gradient at every step, so we expect the default LSTM to fail somewhere between 10 and 50 steps; with the forget gate at 0.993 and the input gate nearly closed, a stored value has a half-life of 100 steps"), and say that this is the idea behind the forget-bias-of-1 default and chrono initialization.

**LSTM-11** (major, correctness). Heading: Delayed Recall. Quote: "from 50 steps on, it recalls anywhere from none to all of the symbols, and which it is". The stored outputs: PyTorch RNN 1.00, 1.00, 1.00, 1.00, 1.00, 0.13 at delays 5 to 200; JAX 1.00, 1.00, 1.00, 1.00, 0.12, 0.75. So the ungated network beats both default-gated networks at 20 to 100 steps. The prose labels this "erratic" and moves on, and the paragraph's conclusion ("Gates make a long memory possible and learnable") skips the fact that the network without gates already had one. Explain it: the LeCun normal recurrent matrix has spectral radius about 1 and the hidden state starts small, so $\tanh$ is near-linear and the Jacobian product neither vanishes nor explodes at initialization for some directions; whether training keeps it there is luck, hence the run-to-run variation. Then state what the gates add: the keep-gate rows are at 1.00 at every delay in both frameworks, with no dependence on a matrix's spectrum. The paragraph is also unreflowed (two lines over 90 characters).

**LSTM-12** (moderate, thought). Heading: Delayed Recall. Quote: "shrinks like $2^{-k}$ over $k$ steps, and training never finds the setting". "Never" is too strong for 1,500 updates at one learning rate, and the claim that the gradient "shrinks like $2^{-k}$" describes only the direct path at initialization; the LSTM also has the paths through $\mathbf{h}$ that the section took care to mention earlier. Fix: "at initialization the direct path passes $2^{-k}$ of the gradient, about one part in a million at 20 steps, and within our 1,500 updates training does not find the gates that would keep the symbol".

**LSTM-13** (moderate, correctness). Heading: Deep Recurrent Networks. Quote: "are trained with the same techniques as stacked transformer blocks". As a statement about recurrent networks this is anachronistic: stacked LSTMs in the systems the introduction refers to (speech, translation) had residual connections at most, no pre-normalization and no position-wise MLP. What follows is this book's design choice, made so that every mixer in the chapter sits in one stack. Say so: "We stack them the way :numref:`sec_transformer-block` stacks attention layers ..., which is not how the LSTM systems of the 2010s were built but lets every layer of this chapter be compared in one model."

**LSTM-14** (minor, focus). Heading: Deep Recurrent Networks. Quote: "A recurrent layer can also read the sequence backward. A *bidirectional*". The paragraph is correct (and the remark that "the training loss falls without the model learning to predict anything" is a good one), but nothing in the chapter uses bidirectional layers. It sits after the `SeqLM` code as an afterthought. Cut to two sentences or move to exercise 8.

**LSTM-15** (moderate, pedagogy). Heading: Character-Level Language Modeling. Quote: "mixers are a plain recurrent layer, an LSTM, and a GRU, and count their". The cell prints parameter counts (344k, 542k, 476k in PyTorch) and the prose never reads them. The LSTM model has 58% more parameters than the RNN model, so "The gated networks are a few percent better than the plain one" is a comparison at unequal size. Either read the counts ("with 60% and 40% more parameters") or drop them from the printout. `mamba.md` later quotes "540,000" for the LSTM from this cell, so the count should be read here.

**LSTM-16** (minor, thought). Heading: Character-Level Language Modeling. Quote: "the validation perplexity of every model in this chapter". No cell shows a validation curve, so "is still falling at the last step" is a claim no output supports. Either plot one curve (it would also teach what 3,000 steps buy) or soften to "we have checked that".

**LSTM-17** (moderate, pedagogy). Heading: Character-Level Language Modeling. Quote: "the language modeling task serves mainly to check that a new model learns". This is a frank admission that the chapter's main benchmark does not discriminate between its models (all between 2.95 and 3.4 across three sections). The reader is owed the consequence: say here what would discriminate (longer windows, word-level or subword modeling, larger models), and that the book cannot run it at this scale. Otherwise six sections of perplexities read as evidence when they are sanity checks.

**LSTM-18** (minor, language). Heading: Character-Level Language Modeling. Quote: "The gates solved the memory problem, but not the speed problem." Second ending before the Summary (decision 6), and the fourth statement of the same point. Delete the paragraph; keep its question as the Summary's last sentence.

**LSTM-19** (minor, correctness). Heading: Summary. Quote: "Both remember over spans where a plain network fails, and both". Not what the table shows unless "with gates initialized to keep" is added. Fix: "With gates that start near one, both recall a symbol after 200 steps, where the default-initialized gated networks fail by 50 and the plain network is unreliable."

## Code and experiments

- `lstm-gated-recurrent-networks-1`: a second imports cell (`import os`; in JAX also `import random`). House rule is one imports cell. Merge.
- `lstm-implementation-1`: `scale = 1 / math.sqrt(num_hiddens)` is applied to `W_x`, whose fan-in is `num_inputs`. Harmless (the weights are overwritten) but inconsistent with the LeCun paragraph that follows.
- `lstm-implementation-2`: an implementation check against the library layer, not a confirmation of a theorem. It can fail, and it teaches the stacking order of the library's weights. Keep. Stored outputs 6.0e-08 (PyTorch) and 0.0e+00 (JAX) match "agree to the rounding error".
- `lstm-delayed-recall-5`: stored outputs match the prose for the gated rows. The RNN row is discussed in LSTM-11. The JAX cell seeds models with `random.getrandbits(32)`, unseeded, so its table changes on every capture; the prose is written as ranges, which is right, but the reviser should know that the JAX RNN row in particular is not reproducible.
- The experiment trains 30 models (5 rows by 6 delays, 1,500 updates each, the longest on sequences of 200). Cheap on a GPU; on a laptop CPU the 200-step column of five recurrent models will take minutes each. Consider dropping the 5 and 10 columns, which every row passes.
- `lstm-character-level-language-modeling-6`: prints parameter counts the prose ignores (LSTM-15). Perplexities 3.12, 2.96, 3.01 (PyTorch) and 3.14, 2.98, 2.99 (JAX) agree with "between about 2.95 and 3.15" and with the unordered "differ by one or two percent".
- No cell demonstrates the section's central mechanism, a gate holding its value. A three-line readout after training (the mean forget-gate value of the keep-gate LSTM at filler positions against the first position) would show it; exercise 6.3 asks for something harder. `ssm.md` does this for the minGRU and it is one of that section's best moments.

- Pattern to check in every section: the character-level perplexities span 2.95 to 3.4 across all models, and this section admits the task "serves mainly to check". Conclusions built on perplexity differences (and on unequal parameter counts) recur in `ssm.md` and `mamba.md`.
