# Selective State Space Models

Source: `chapter_recurrent-modern/mamba.md` (lines 1 to 830, slides ignored). About 3,600 words of prose and exercises, of which about 650 are exercises; 15 code cells per tab.

## Verdict

A competent, honest and compact account of Mamba that describes the model correctly (I checked the layer against the reference implementation from memory and found one small discrepancy, the rank of the step-size projection) and has one very good idea of its own: measuring where the state comes from. It is not yet at the bar, for two reasons. First, the step from S4D to the selective model, the step this chapter exists to teach, is made in one dense paragraph and one equation with three new index conventions, while the identity that makes it obvious (with $\lambda = -1$ and the exact hold, $\Delta_t = \textrm{softplus}(s_t)$ gives exactly a GRU blend with gate $\sigma(-s_t)$) is left to exercise 1. Second, the experiments do not separate Mamba from the minGRU. On selective copying the minGRU scores about 0.97 and Mamba 1.00; on character-level text Mamba's 3.0 against the minGRU's 3.2 comes with about 50% more parameters, which the text does not mention. The section's thesis, that Mamba needs both selectivity and multi-timescale states, is therefore asserted, not shown; only the need for selectivity is shown (S4D fails). The most important fix is to put the gate identity in the main text as the bridge from the GRU, and to say candidly what the experiments do and do not establish about the second ingredient. The reader also finishes without a statement of what a Mamba layer costs against an attention layer.

## What works

- The opening paragraph is the best opening of the three sections: "Whether the token ten steps ago was the key fact of a paragraph or a comma, its influence on the present is the same", then "A memory that keeps everything for the same time fills up with commas; a memory that forgets quickly loses the name." Problem, stakes and question in 120 words. Keep every word.
- "Two models of the previous section each have half of a solution." The second paragraph positions the new model against two things the reader has built and run. This is how a family of methods should be introduced.
- The task is chosen to isolate the idea, and the argument for why position-based weights must fail is given before the experiment: "the same position holds filler in one sequence and a symbol in the next".
- "A small step keeps the state and admits little of the input; a large step forgets the state and admits much. The step size acts as a gate." Clear, and prepared by `ssm.md`.
- The adjoint derivation: $\mathbf{g}_t$ obeys "again a linear recurrence, run backward in time, and a second scan computes it". Four lines, correct (checked against the code's index shift), and it explains something practitioners treat as kernel magic. The remark on Mamba's fused kernel agrees with the paper's description.
- "Contributions to the State" is the section's best experiment: it turns "Mamba keeps the symbols" into a measured curve with spikes at the symbols, and the prose reads the plot candidly, including what it does not determine: "Runs that copy equally well find different solutions ... so the printed share changes widely from run to run." This is exactly the treatment of a noisy experiment the author asked for.
- Design departures from the paper are declared: "we keep the MLP of `d2l.SeqLM`, so that every mixer of the chapter sits in the same stack", "the approximation $\Delta$ of the zero-order hold, which Mamba uses".
- Exercises 5, 6 and 8 are real investigations (see below).

## Structural findings

1. **The bridge from gates to step sizes should be an equation in the main text.** The chapter's arc is LSTM gate, then decay of a linear recurrence, then step size of a discretized ODE, then input-dependent step size. The last link is stated in words three times ("The step size acts as a gate", "the step size acts as the gate of a gated recurrent network") and proved only in exercise 1. Three lines under "Input-Dependent Step Sizes" would close the loop for a reader who knows only the GRU: $e^{-\textrm{softplus}(s)} = 1/(1 + e^{s}) = \sigma(-s)$, so one state with $\lambda = -1$ is $h_t = z_t h_{t-1} + (1 - z_t) x_t$ with $z_t = \sigma(-s_t)$: the minGRU's update, with the pre-activation's sign flipped. Then: what Mamba adds to this is $N$ rates per channel and the write and read vectors. Exercise 1 can keep part 2.
2. **The experiments support half the thesis.** See MAMBA-5, MAMBA-12 and MAMBA-15. Either add a setting where the minGRU fails and Mamba does not (more symbols, 4,096 fillers as in exercise 4, or the minGRU at equal parameter count on text), or say that at this scale the cheaper model is nearly as good and that the case for the second ingredient rests on larger experiments in the literature.
3. **No cost comparison with attention, and no transformer baseline.** The reader arrives from the transformer chapter. This section says Mamba "matched or exceeded transformers of the same size" (a claim from the paper) and that it generates "with a state of fixed size", but never states per-layer training work ($O(T H N)$ against $O(T^2 d)$), generation memory ($HN$ numbers plus three convolution inputs per layer against a cache of $T$ keys and values), or the constant: with $H = 2d$ and $N = 16$ the state is $32d$ numbers, the equivalent of a cache of 16 tokens. `hybrids.md` trains the transformer `d2l.TinyLM` on the same data (between about 3.25 and 3.4 by its text); one sentence here pointing to that number would let the reader place Mamba's 3.0. Without it, "it is a strong language model at this scale" in the Summary has nothing to stand on but the LSTM.
4. **Where the field stands is missing.** For a book published in 2026 to 2027 the section should say in two sentences what became of the model: the scan-based layer taught here was followed by Mamba-2, which restricts the decay to a scalar per head so that training uses matrix products (the subject of :numref:`sec_matrix-state`), and pure SSM language models did not displace transformers; the forms in use are hybrids (:numref:`sec_hybrids`). The chapter index promises mechanisms over rankings, which is right, but the reader should not leave thinking the layer built here is what ships.
5. **Order is right; the middle sags.** Problem, failure of the two half-solutions, mechanism, training, layer, success, inspection, text: this is the correct sequence. "Training with a Scan" interrupts the mechanism with a memory problem the reader does not yet have (no selective model has been trained) and could follow "The Layer", or be motivated by a failed attempt ("with `linear_scan` this model needs N GB").
6. **Length.** At 3,600 words this is the shortest of the three sections and the one whose subject is hardest. It can afford the 300 words that findings 1, 3 and 4 ask for.

## Passage-level findings

**MAMBA-1** (minor, correctness). Heading: introduction. Quote: "transformers of the same size in language modeling. The convolution of S4D". "Mamba matched or exceeded transformers of the same size" is what :citet:`Gu.Dao.2023` report at the scales they trained (up to about a billion parameters, by my recollection); it has not held as a general statement since. Attribute and bound it: "In the experiments of :citet:`Gu.Dao.2023`, at up to about a billion parameters, Mamba matched ...". Unverified from the paper's text; I am confident of the direction of the claim, less of the exact scale.

An unreflowed line that appends a preview of the next section to the roadmap. The same preview closes the section ("This is a memory of key–value pairs"). Keep one; the closing one is better placed.

**MAMBA-3** (minor, explanation). Heading: Selective Copying. Quote: "must output the next symbol, in order. Chance accuracy is 1/8. A solution". The figure caption says it correctly.

**MAMBA-4** (minor, pedagogy). Heading: Selective Copying. Quote: "We train every model as `d2l.SeqLM`, the residual stack of". The reader is told the setup of the experiment but not asked for a prediction, though the second paragraph of the introduction has already given one for each model (minGRU: selective but short memory; S4D: long memory but not selective). Use it: "By the argument above, S4D should fail outright. The minGRU can select, but its gates start with a memory of one step and the symbols lie up to a thousand steps back; will it learn?" The surprise of 0.97 then has something to land on.

**MAMBA-5** (moderate, thought). Heading: Selective Copying. Quote: "sometimes sits close to a query, and the short-range gradients from such". The explanation for why the minGRU succeeds here though it failed at delayed recall over 1,000 steps (a curriculum supplied by symbols near the queries) is stated as fact. No cell tests it; exercise 8 asks the reader to test the analogous claim for Mamba. It is a good hypothesis; mark it as one ("a likely reason is"), or support it with the gate statistics on filler tokens before and after training, a three-line cell of the kind `ssm.md` already has.

**MAMBA-6** (moderate, explanation). Heading: Input-Dependent Step Sizes. Quote: "Let the input of the layer have $H$ channels, $\mathbf{x}_t \in". This paragraph and the equation after it are where a first-time reader is lost. Within ten lines: channels are renamed from $d$ (S4D) to $H$; a superscript $(c)$ for the channel is introduced; $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_t \in \mathbb{R}^H$ is per channel while $\mathbf{B}_t, \mathbf{C}_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ are "shared by all channels" (dimensions never stated); and $\mathbf{B}$, $\mathbf{C}$ change from fixed parameters to functions of the whole input vector. Missing: the shapes of $\mathbf{W}_\Delta$, $\mathbf{W}_B$, $\mathbf{W}_C$; a statement that this is the first place where channels interact inside the recurrence (the step size of channel $c$ depends on all channels of $\mathbf{x}_t$); and a concrete case. Fix: build it in two steps. First one channel with one state and an input-dependent $\Delta_t$ (the GRU identity of structural finding 1). Then "now give each channel $N$ states and let the token also choose a direction $\mathbf{B}_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ to write along and a direction $\mathbf{C}_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ to read". A small table of shapes ($\boldsymbol{\Delta}_t$: $H$; $\mathbf{B}_t$, $\mathbf{C}_t$: $N$; state: $H \times N$) would replace three sentences.

**MAMBA-7** (minor, explanation). Heading: Input-Dependent Step Sizes. Quote: "$N$ states, and the input weight is the approximation $\Delta$ of the". The reader is told that Mamba replaces $(e^{\Delta\lambda} - 1)/\lambda$ by $\Delta$ but not what that changes. Exercise 1.2 asks. One sentence: with the exact weight a state with a large step is overwritten by the input (a convex blend); with weight $\Delta$ the write is unbounded in $\Delta$ and the state is no longer a weighted average, so the layer relies on normalization around it.

**MAMBA-8** (minor, explanation). Heading: Input-Dependent Step Sizes. Quote: "with memories from a few steps to a thousand". Within one channel the $N$ rates $-1, \ldots, -N$ span only a factor of $N = 16$ in timescale; the range "from a few steps to a thousand" comes from the spread of step sizes across channels, as in S4D. Say which axis supplies which range, since exercise 4.2 depends on the reader knowing that the slowest state of a channel is the one with $\lambda = -1$.

**MAMBA-9** (moderate, explanation). Heading: Training with a Scan. Quote: "position now has $H \times N$ numbers, 1,536 for 96 channels with 16 states". The worked number mixes two models. The copying model has $H = 96$ channels (width 48, expansion 2) but 4 states per channel ("We train a Mamba model with four states per channel"), which gives 384 numbers and about 100 MB; the language model has 16 states but $H = 256$ and windows of 128. The parenthesis "(the language model below has 16 states per channel)" tries to patch this and makes it worse. Use the copying model as trained: $96 \times 4 = 384$ numbers per position, $64 \times 1{,}028 \times 384 \approx 25$ million, 100 MB per stored tensor. The arithmetic for the numbers as printed is right ($64 \times 1{,}024 \times 1{,}536 \approx 10^8$, 400 MB).

**MAMBA-10** (minor, correctness, medium confidence). Heading: Training with a Scan. Quote: "tensors of the size of the states instead of ten, and in PyTorch it lets the". Backpropagation through the doubling scan keeps, for each of the ten rounds, both components of the pair (the products of decays and the partial states), so about twenty tensors of this size, not ten; the earlier sentence "which takes several gigabytes per layer" is consistent with twenty at 400 MB. Exercise 2 asks for the count, so the main text should not be loose about it. Also, "in a few gigabytes" is a number no cell prints.

**MAMBA-11** (minor, pedagogy). Heading: Training with a Scan. Quote: "backward function, and check it against the gradient that automatic". A check of a hand-written backward pass against automatic differentiation. It can fail and it is the right test; keep. The sentence after the cell, "The two gradients agree to rounding error", is the fourth instance of this formula in three sections.

**MAMBA-12** (minor, correctness). Heading: The Layer. Quote: "projection of low rank $R = \lceil H/16 \rceil$, down to $R$ numbers and". The text says "As in :citet:`Gu.Dao.2023`". In the reference implementation the rank is the ceiling of the model width over 16, that is $\lceil d/16 \rceil = \lceil H/32 \rceil$ at expansion 2, not $\lceil H/16 \rceil$. I am confident of this from the released code (`dt_rank = ceil(d_model / 16)`). Either change the code and the parameter arithmetic in "Language Modeling", or write "a rank of $H/16$, twice that of the reference implementation". The initialization described (uniform in $\pm R^{-1/2}$, bias as inverse softplus of a log-uniform step size in $[0.001, 0.1]$) matches the reference, and the inverse-softplus expression in the code is correct.

**MAMBA-13** (minor, explanation). Heading: The Layer. Quote: "short causal convolution over the last four tokens lets each channel combine". The paragraph lists five components in five clauses. Two deserve a reason. The convolution: why does a model with a recurrent state need it? (The step size and the write are computed from the current token alone; the convolution lets them depend on the last few tokens, for instance to recognize a symbol by what precedes it.) The gate branch: what does multiplying by $\textrm{SiLU}(\mathbf{z})$ add? :numref:`fig_mamba_block` (rendered) shows a LayerNorm, a residual connection and "$\times L$", which belong to `SeqLM`, not to the `Mamba` class, while the caption says "The Mamba layer"; and the figure omits the MLP that this book's stack adds. Align the caption with what is drawn.

**MAMBA-14** (minor, language). Heading: The Mamba Layer. Quote: "### The Layer". A heading "The Layer" under "The Mamba Layer" says nothing. "Projections, Convolution, and Gate" or fold the heading.

**MAMBA-15** (moderate, thought). Heading: Selective Copying with Mamba. Quote: "The minGRU, which has only the first, nearly solves the task; S4D, which". The paragraph claims that Mamba "combines the two properties" and the evidence is 1.00 against 0.97 (both frameworks). The second property, "memories of many lengths from the start", is therefore worth three points here, on a task where the previous paragraph explained why short initial memories suffice. The prose is honest about the numbers, but the reader is led to conclude more than the table shows. Fix: say so ("selectivity is what this task needs; the minGRU has it and nearly solves the task. What the $N$ states and the initialization add shows on text, below, and on harder versions of this task, exercise 4"), or strengthen the experiment (structural finding 2).

**MAMBA-16** (minor, explanation). Heading: Contributions to the State. Quote: "starting at each symbol, which cover less than 2% of the sequence: the". Good that the window of four is explained by the convolution. The measure itself needs one more sentence: it is the norm of each position's decayed write in the first layer's state, so it shows what the state is made of, not what the readout uses; a small contribution can still decide the answer (as S4D's 100-step recall with 10-step time constants showed in `ssm.md`). The plot's legend covers part of the curves in the PyTorch output.

**MAMBA-17** (moderate, correctness). Heading: Language Modeling. Quote: "model, about 520,000 against 540,000. The low-rank step sizes make the". Three problems. (a) No cell prints Mamba's parameter count; my count from the code is 518,963, so "about 520,000" is right, but it should be printed with `d2l.num_params` as in `lstm.md`. (b) The comparison chosen is the LSTM. Against the models this section is about, Mamba has about 50% more parameters than the minGRU model (about 344,000 by my count) and 80% more than S4D (about 286,000), and the sentence "well below the minGRU and S4D" does not say so. (c) "The low-rank step sizes make the difference" reads as if low rank explained the perplexity; it explains only why the count stays below the LSTM's. The 115,000 is right for two layers ($2(H^2 - 2HR) = 114{,}688$) but the text says "would add about 115,000" as if per model without saying so, and it depends on the rank chosen in MAMBA-12.

**MAMBA-18** (minor, thought). Heading: Language Modeling. Quote: "fixed size, models text as well as the gated networks that dominated before". The conclusion is fair at this scale. It is weaker than it sounds: the section `lstm.md` said that this task "rewards long memory little" and "serves mainly to check that a new model learns ordinary text". A reader who remembers that will not be impressed by a tie with the LSTM at 128 characters. **MAMBA-19** (minor, pedagogy). Heading: Language Modeling. Quote: "This is a memory of key–value pairs, with $\mathbf{B}_t$ as key and". The identification is correct (the write is the outer product of the step-scaled input, the value, with $\mathbf{B}_t$, the key; the read multiplies the state by $\mathbf{C}_t$, the query) and it is the hinge to the next section. It arrives as the last paragraph of a subsection on language modeling, with terms (key, value, query) the section has not used. Give it its own short subsection or move it into the Summary's last sentences; and say what does not fit the analogy (each entry of the matrix has its own decay).

**MAMBA-20** (minor, language). Heading: Summary. Quote: "a strong language model at this scale". "Strong" is not earned by a tie with a 1997 architecture on character-level Penn Treebank. "and it models character-level text as well as the LSTM" is what was shown.

## For the chapter review

- **Can the reader follow LSTM to linear SSM to Mamba?** Up to the minGRU and the scan, yes. From S4D to the selective model the reader must absorb per-channel states, a per-channel step size and shared write and read vectors in one paragraph (MAMBA-6). The cheapest repair is the gate identity: it tells the GRU reader that the new object is the old one, and then only $N$, $\mathbf{B}_t$ and $\mathbf{C}_t$ are new.
- **Fit.** The section uses `linear_scan`, `S4D`, `MinGRU`, `SeqLM`, `train_queries` and `first_mixer_input` from the two sections before it; nothing is re-explained at length. It hands :numref:`sec_matrix-state` the matrix state and the key–value reading in its last paragraph. That hand-off deserves more room than the Penn Treebank result that precedes it.
- **Appendix.** No reference to the appendix, and none needed; the zero-order hold is recalled from `ssm.md` in one sentence.
- **Pattern across the three sections.** (a) Perplexities from 2.95 to 3.4 on a task the chapter itself says discriminates little, compared across models of unequal and mostly unprinted size. (b) A selection of honest readings of experiments that then stop short of drawing the uncomfortable conclusion (the plain RNN beating default LSTMs; a bias initialization matching S4D; the minGRU nearly matching Mamba). In each case the cheaper, older idea does nearly as well at this scale. That is a finding worth stating once, in the chapter's voice, instead of leaving three sections to step around it.
- **Missing for the chapter.** A statement of cost against attention in the reader's terms (work, sequential depth, memory at generation), a transformer row in the comparisons before `hybrids.md`, and two sentences on what is used in practice in 2026.
