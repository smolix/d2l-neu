# Linear Recurrences and State Space Models

Source: `chapter_recurrent-modern/ssm.md` (lines 1 to 1144, slides ignored). About 6,000 words of prose and exercises, of which about 950 are exercises; 15 code cells per tab.

## Verdict

The first half of this section (linear recurrences, the minGRU, the parallel scan) is the best teaching in the chapter and close to the bar: one scalar recurrence, unrolled; one worked composition with numbers; one figure that carries the algorithm; code that is fifteen lines. A reader who knows only the simple RNN can follow every step. The second half (continuous time, zero-order hold, the convolutional form, S4D) is correct and compact, but its argument has a hole that the section's own experiment exposes. The state space view is sold three times on stability ("stable for every value of its parameters") and on multi-timescale initialization. Stability is not an advantage over the minGRU, whose sigmoid decays also lie in $(0, 1)$ for every parameter value, as the first half says. And the control experiment shows that a minGRU with a bias initialization recalls as well as S4D. The section reports this candidly ("the initialization decides") and then does not say what the differential equation is still good for, so the reader finishes the second half unsure why it was taught. The most important fix is to state plainly what the continuous-time view contributes that a gate bias does not (the step size as a single interpretable knob that the next section makes input-dependent, $N$ states per channel, the time-invariant convolution), and to stop claiming stability as a distinguishing property. The section is also two sections; the seam is at "## State Space Models".

## What works

- The opening problem is concrete and felt: "A gated network performs 4,096 updates in a row ... it cannot start step 2,000 until step 1,999 has finished, so most of it waits", answered by "$\log_2 4096 = 12$ rounds". The reader knows the stakes and the payoff before any machinery.
- "A Single Decaying Number" follows the guide's own example (5.1): the state "reads $1, 0.9, 0.81, 0.729, \ldots$", then the unrolled sum, then two consequences. The sentence "The decay that forgets an input also weakens the signal that would teach the model to use it" ties memory and gradient together in one line.
- "Nothing new happens here." A short, confident sentence that tells the reader the vector case is $d$ copies of the scalar one. This is the voice asked for.
- The composition rule is introduced through prefix sums, given a name ("this step, then that one"), and checked on numbers: "$(0.25,\ 0.5 \cdot 1 + 2) = (0.25, 2.5)$". The remark "This matters as much as associativity: a block of a thousand steps costs no more to store or to combine than a single step" is the insight most treatments omit, and exercise 1 tests it with dense matrices and with $\tanh$.
- :numref:`fig_scan_tree` (rendered and checked) carries the doubling schedule without help from the prose: boxes labelled $(2{:}5)$, strides, complete prefixes in teal.
- The minGRU is derived from the GRU by naming "the three places where $\mathbf{h}_{t-1}$ enters a gate or the candidate", and the loss is stated: "The minGRU gives something up."
- Looking inside the trained minGRU (state reset, gate statistics) is the kind of cell the house rule wants: it computes something the prose then reads.
- The zero-order hold paragraph translates $\Delta$ into gate values and half-lives ("With $\Delta = 0.01$ the state keeps $\bar{a} = 0.990$ of its value, a half-life of 69 steps"), all correct, and lands on "A small step size means a long memory", the sentence `mamba.md` depends on.
- The long-range recall experiment has two controls that isolate the cause, and the prose accepts the answer even though it deflates the section's subject: "the convolution makes training fast but is not needed for the result".
- The closing image of S4D's limit, "Whether the character four steps back ended a word or sat in its middle, its influence on the present is the same fixed number $K_4$", sets up the next section exactly.

## Structural findings

1. **Split the section at "## State Space Models" (source line 577).** The introduction already says "The second half of the section borrows an answer from physics". Part one, "Linear Recurrences and Parallel Scans" (introduction, Linear Recurrences, Parallel Scans including the minGRU language model; about 3,000 words with exercises 1 to 6), answers "can a recurrent network be trained on all positions at once?". Part two, "State Space Models" (continuous time, discretization, convolutional form, S4D, long-range recall, language modeling; about 3,000 words with exercises 5, 7 and 8), answers "how fast should a state forget?". The chapter index already states these as two questions joined by "and" in one sentence. The bridge paragraph "The minGRU shows that a gated recurrence can be linear in its state" becomes the end of part one's Summary; the paragraph that follows the next heading, which repeats it, becomes part two's opening.
2. **What the differential equation buys is never stated after the experiment undercuts it.** See SSM-12 and SSM-19. A reader who follows the recall table concludes: set the gate biases and skip the ODE. The text should answer that conclusion directly.
3. **The motivation for part two is given three times in a row.** The introduction's third paragraph ("a gate learned from scratch starts with a memory of about one step"), the bridge paragraph at the end of the minGRU subsection ("Its gates start near $\sigma(0) = 1/2$, a half-life of one step ..."), and the first paragraph under "State Space Models" ("it must learn from scratch how long to remember ... from gradients that, at the start, have been halved a thousand times"). After the split, each part needs it once.
4. **Cost against attention is absent.** The opening compares training speed with a transformer in words. Nowhere in this section or in `mamba.md` is the comparison made in the terms the reader of chapters 10 and 11 knows: per layer, attention does $O(T^2 d)$ work and keeps a cache of $T$ keys and values; a linear recurrence does $O(Td)$ work (or $O(T \log T\, d)$ with the doubling scan) and keeps $d$ numbers ($dN$ for an SSM). `hybrids.md` has the memory half. One small table at the end of "Parallel Scans" (rows: plain RNN or LSTM, attention, linear recurrence with a scan; columns: work, sequential depth, memory at generation) would give this section its conclusion.
5. **The appendix is not cited, and should be, once.** See "Appendix" under "For the chapter review".
6. **Missing detail a builder needs.** (a) Memory of backpropagation through the doubling scan ($\lceil \log_2 T \rceil$ stored rounds) is raised only in `mamba.md`; one sentence belongs with the implementation here. (b) What is lost by a real diagonal $\mathbf{A}$ (no oscillating modes; a complex diagonal loses almost nothing since almost every matrix is diagonalizable over the complex numbers) is not said; the section only says "We take" it diagonal.

## Passage-level findings

**SSM-1** (minor, thought). Heading: introduction. Quote: "Such a recurrence". The full sentence is "Such a recurrence is stable for every value of its parameters, and it starts with memories that range from a step to a thousand steps." The first property is shared by the gated recurrence of the first half ("the products can shrink but never grow"); see SSM-12. Fix here by dropping the stability clause or by saying what it is stable relative to: "unlike a recurrence with a free matrix".

**SSM-2** (minor, correctness). Heading: introduction. Quote: "was a main reason why". "This difference in training speed, more than any difference in what the two kinds of model can represent, was a main reason why transformers replaced recurrent networks". Defensible, but "more than any difference in what the two kinds of model can represent" passes over direct access to every past token, which the chapter itself treats as the thing no fixed state can do (`hybrids.md`). Soften to "was one main reason; the other, direct access to every earlier token, returns in :numref:`sec_hybrids`".

**SSM-3** (minor, explanation). Heading: Input-Dependent Decay. Quote: "have also renamed the input term $\mathbf{b}_t$, because it will soon be a". Good that the renaming is announced. But $\mathbf{b}$ was the bias in every equation of `lstm.md` ($\mathbf{b}_\textrm{f}$, $\mathbf{b}_\textrm{h}$) and is again a bias eleven lines later in the GRU equations ($\mathbf{b}_\textrm{z}$), and in the SSM half $\bar b$ is an input weight and $\mathbf{B}$ the input matrix. Four meanings of one letter in one section. The pair $(\mathbf{a}_t, \mathbf{b}_t)$ is used through `mamba.md` and the code, so keep it, but call $\mathbf{b}_t$ "the write" from its first appearance (the text starts doing so at "and write $\mathbf{b}_t = (1 - \mathbf{z}_t) \odot$") and say once that it is unrelated to the biases.

**SSM-4** (minor, language). Heading: The Minimal GRU. Quote: "The GRU of :numref:`sec_gru` almost has this form. Its update gate". The four GRU equations are reprinted in full from `lstm.md`. This is a small duplication and it serves the "three places" argument, so it may stay; the three occurrences of $\mathbf{h}_{t-1}$ could be marked (colour or underbrace) so that the reader sees the three places instead of hunting for them.

**SSM-5** (moderate, correctness). Heading: The Minimal GRU. Quote: "a GRU layer has $6d^2$ weights, three input matrices and three state". Correct. But the conclusion drawn 300 lines later, "with a third of the GRU's weights per layer", is then used to frame the perplexity gap (3.2 against 3.0) as cheap. In the `SeqLM` stack the mixer is a small part of the model: by the counts printed in `lstm.md`, the GRU model has about 476,000 parameters and the minGRU model about 344,000 (my count: the same as the plain RNN model), so the models differ by 28%, not by a factor of three. Say which.

**SSM-6** (minor, thought). Heading: The Minimal GRU. Quote: "between tokens its state can only be scaled and added to. Stacking layers". The paragraph says what is lost and that stacking "gives some of this back". The reader would like one concrete thing a single minGRU layer cannot do that a GRU can (for example: close its gate once it has stored a symbol, so that a second symbol does not overwrite the first; that needs the state). The selective copying task in the next section is where this bites.

**SSM-7** (minor, explanation). Heading: Composing Affine Steps. Quote: "of the pairs under $\bullet$. Computing all prefixes of a". Clear. One step is skipped: that the $\mathbf{a}$-component of the prefix is the running product of decays and is needed only as a carrier. Readers implementing the scan often ask why they must carry $\mathbf{a}$ when they want only $\mathbf{h}$. One clause.

**SSM-8** (minor, correctness). Heading: The Doubling Schedule. Quote: "performs about". "$T \log_2 T$ combines" is right for Hillis and Steele, and "about $2T$ combines in about $2 \log_2 T$ rounds" is right for Blelloch's schedule. The implementation paragraph then says JAX "uses a schedule with linear total work" without connecting it to the Blelloch sentence two paragraphs earlier, and the PyTorch tab uses the doubling schedule, so the two tabs run different algorithms. Say so in the shared prose, because the timing plot's two shapes depend on it (SSM-10).

**SSM-9** (minor, pedagogy). Heading: Implementation. Quote: "We check the scan against a loop that applies the recurrence one step at a". An implementation check that can fail (a wrong combine order fails it), on "a length that is not a power of two", which is the case that breaks naive scans. Keep. It is one of four "max deviation" cells in the three sections (`lstm-implementation-2`, `ssm-implementation-2`, `ssm-the-s4d-layer-4`, `mamba-training-with-a-scan-2`); all are checks of code, not of theorems, and each tests something that can go wrong, so none falls under decision 4, but the identical "The two agree to the rounding error" sentence after each is a formula.

**SSM-10** (moderate, pedagogy). Heading: Implementation. Quote: "faster at every length, by a factor between a few and about a hundred,". The stored plots (rendered): in PyTorch the loop goes from 5 ms to 330 ms and the scan from 0.7 ms to 48 ms, a factor of 7 to 20; in JAX the loop goes from 3.5 ms to 180 ms and the scan from 0.1 ms to 2.7 ms, a factor of about 8 to 70. The two scan curves have opposite shapes: PyTorch is flat to 1,024 and then rises faster than the loop; JAX rises fifteenfold from 256 to 1,024 and is then flat. The prose covers both with "Its time does not follow the length ... Where this happens depends on how the scan is compiled", which is true but teaches nothing, and the reader cannot predict either curve from $T \log_2 T$ or $2T$. Two further points. The loop baseline is an interpreted Python loop (about 20 microseconds per step, which is dispatch overhead, not arithmetic), so the factor overstates the gain over a fused recurrent kernel; say that the comparison is with a step-by-step loop in the framework, not with cuDNN. And the plot would teach more with a reference line of slope 1. Fix: per-tab sentences that read the actual curve, and one sentence on the baseline.

**SSM-11** (minor, thought). Heading: A Linear Recurrent Language Model. Quote: "Resetting the state raises the perplexity from about 3.2 to more than 10, so". The first of the "two questions" ("does the model use its memory at all?") cannot have the answer no for a character model, so as posed the check cannot fail. What the number does teach is the size of the gap between a one-character model and a model with memory. Pose it that way: "How much of the model's skill is memory? Without it, the model is a one-character predictor." Note also that the model was not trained in the reset condition, so 10.8 is an upper bound on what a trained one-character model would reach.

**SSM-12** (major, thought). Heading: Discretization. Quote: "The same formula guarantees stability. For every $\lambda < 0$ and every". Everything in the paragraph is true. The implied contrast is not: the reader has just spent the first half with a recurrence whose decays are sigmoids, which also lie in $(0, 1)$ for every value of the parameters, as the text said ("no past input is ever amplified, however long the sequence"). Stability is presented as the first gift of the continuous-time view in the introduction, here, at the head of "State Space Models" ("decays are stable for every value of the parameters"), in the reading of the recall experiment and in the Summary: five times. Against a free recurrent matrix it is a real property; against the minGRU it is none. Fix: say once, here, "Like a sigmoid gate, this parameterization cannot produce a decay outside $(0, 1)$; what is new is that the parameter is a rate, so that memory length is $1/(\Delta|\lambda|)$ steps and can be set, spread and read off directly." Remove the other four.

**SSM-13** (moderate, explanation). Heading: Discretization. Quote: "one step, and the result is a linear recurrence:". The reader is told that :eqref:`eq_ssm_scalar` "can be solved exactly over one step" and is given the answer; the next paragraph justifies $\bar b$ by an integral whose origin (variation of constants) is not stated. For a reader without ODEs, two lines suffice and should be in the main text: with $x$ held at $x_t$, the function $h(t) + x_t/\lambda$ obeys the input-free equation, so it decays by $e^{\Delta\lambda}$ over the step, which gives $h_t = e^{\Delta\lambda} h_{t-1} + (e^{\Delta\lambda} - 1)x_t/\lambda$. Then cite the appendix for the matrix case (see "Appendix" below). Also say which value is held: the equation holds the input at $x_t$ over the step that ends at $t$.

**SSM-14** (minor, explanation). Heading: Discretization. Quote: "Rates may also be complex, $\lambda = -\alpha + i\omega$, in which case the". The sentence is fine as an aside. :numref:`fig_ssm_discretization` (rendered) gives two of its three panels to complex rates, with the curves for "$\lambda = -1 \pm 4i$" and floating labels "0.3" and "1" that are hard to attach to their dots, while "the models of this chapter use real rates". The right panel, decay against $\Delta|\lambda|$ with half-lives 693, 69, 6.9, 0.69, is the one the text needs and it is small. Either redraw with the right panel dominant and the complex plane as a small inset, or reduce to the right panel and move the complex picture to the appendix or further reading.

**SSM-15** (minor, correctness, medium confidence). Heading: Discretization. Quote: "approximate that design". S4D-Real takes the diagonal of the HiPPO matrix, $-(n+1)$. In :citet:`Gu.Gupta.Goel.Re.2022` as I recall it, the variants shown to approximate the HiPPO kernels are the ones built from the eigenvalues of the normal part (complex rates), and S4D-Real is an ablation that keeps only the real parts. "the diagonal rates above approximate that design" therefore claims more than the paper shows. Unverified from the text of the paper. Safer: "the rates above keep the diagonal of that matrix". The claim that the paper "fixes $\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{1}$" is also from memory plausible (only the product of $\mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{C}$ enters the kernel) but unverified.

**SSM-16** (minor, explanation). Heading: Continuous-Time Dynamics. Quote: "We take $\mathbf{A} = \textrm{diag}(\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_N)$". The general matrix system is introduced with four named matrices and reduced to a diagonal one in the next sentence, without a reason. Give it: a full $\mathbf{A}$ needs a matrix exponential per step and an $N^3$ combine in a scan (exercise 1 computes this), while a diagonal one is $N$ scalar recurrences; the price of a real diagonal is that no state can oscillate. The sentence "the name says that the system is described by the space of its states" explains nothing and can go.

**SSM-17** (minor, explanation). Heading: Continuous-Time Dynamics. Quote: "coordinate, or *channel*, so the state of a layer holds $d \times N$". This is where a reader can get lost: until now the state had one number per coordinate, and now each coordinate has its own $N$-dimensional state that never mixes with the others. A two-line picture in words helps: "channel 7 of the input drives its own 16 leaky integrators with 16 different rates; the layer's output in channel 7 is a learned combination of those 16". `mamba.md` relies on this picture.

**SSM-18** (minor, explanation). Heading: Long-Range Recall. Quote: "step sizes start between 0.1 and 1, so that its time constants are at most". With time constants of at most 10 steps this control still recalls perfectly at 100 steps (stored: 1.00 and 0.13/0.12), ten time constants out, where the symbol's weight is $e^{-10} \approx 5 \times 10^{-5}$. A careful reader will find that surprising after being taught to read half-lives as memory length. The reason should be stated: with identical fillers nothing else in the state varies, so a linear readout can amplify a tiny difference; the limit is reached only when the difference falls below rounding error or the gradient is too small to train on. This also qualifies "identical ones add only a constant to its state".

**SSM-19** (major, thought). Heading: Long-Range Recall. Quote: "the initialization decides: memories of many lengths, present from the first". This is the honest reading, and it is the right one. But the next sentence ("The continuous-time view supplies them, together with decays that are stable ...") is all the defence the second half gets, and the first control has just shown that a gate bias supplies them too. Tell the reader what remains: (1) the rate parameterization makes timescale a quantity one sets and reads, where a gate bias must be reverse-engineered from it (`init_timescales` does exactly that, with `-log(expm1(dt))`); (2) $N$ states per channel at different rates, read by a learned $\mathbf{C}$, give each channel a learnable kernel, not a single exponential; (3) the step size $\Delta$ is the handle that the next section makes a function of the input. Also say that the keep-gate LSTM of :numref:`sec_lstm` would be the natural third control and what it would show; the reader remembers that it reached 200 steps.

**SSM-20** (minor, correctness). Heading: Long-Range Recall. Quote: "network with gates that start near one half kept the symbol for 50 steps". True as worded, but it steps around the plain RNN, which did (see the review of `lstm.md`, LSTM-11). Once `lstm.md` explains the RNN row this sentence is fine.

**SSM-21** (moderate, thought). Heading: Language Modeling. Quote: "S4D reaches a perplexity of about 3.35, behind the minGRU's 3.2 and the". The explanation offered (fixed dynamics cannot treat a word-final character differently) is plausible and is the hand-off to the next section, but nothing here tests it, and there are other candidate causes the reader can see: S4D has no nonlinearity and no mixing across channels inside the layer, and by my count its model has fewer parameters (about 286,000 against 344,000). State the explanation as the hypothesis that the next section tests, which it does with selective copying.

**SSM-22** (minor, language). Heading: end of file. Quote: "[Discussions](https://d2l.discourse.group/)". Two Discussions links without tab markers, the first to the forum root. `mamba.md` has the same. Fix the pair.

**SSM-23** (minor, language). Heading: A Linear Recurrent Language Model. Quote: "quarter exceed 0.9, which keeps the state with a half-life of 7 steps or". This paragraph and the timing paragraph ("The scan / changes how we train, / not how we generate") have ragged, patched line breaks. Reflow.

## For the chapter review

- **Arc.** `lstm.md` ends on "gates read the previous state"; this section removes that and gets the scan; `mamba.md` needs from here exactly three things: the pair $(\mathbf{a}_t, \mathbf{b}_t)$ with `linear_scan`, "a small step size means a long memory", and the picture of $N$ states per channel. All three are present; the third is the weakest (SSM-17).
- **Can the Basics reader follow LSTM to linear SSM?** Yes for the recurrence and the scan. For the differential equation, yes if two lines of derivation are added (SSM-13); the reader needs only $e^{\lambda t}$.
- **Appendix.** :numref:`subsec_mdl-ode-driven-linear` derives the variation-of-constants formula and the exact zero-order-hold recurrence :eqref:`eq_mdl-ode-zoh` for a general matrix $\mathbf{A}$, and already points back to `subsec_zoh` here ("there the step is $\Delta$ and the state is $h$"). This section does not cite it. It should, once, at :eqref:`eq_zoh`: "the matrix case, $\bar{\mathbf{A}} = e^{\Delta\mathbf{A}}$, is derived in :numref:`subsec_mdl-ode-driven-linear`". The scalar derivation is small and should stay in the main text, so the section still reads without the appendix. Notation agrees in substance and differs in letters: the appendix writes the state $\mathbf{x}$, the input $\mathbf{u}$, the step $h$ and the index $n$; this section writes the state $h$, the input $x$, the step $\Delta$ and the index $t$, so $h$ and $x$ swap roles between the two texts. The appendix flags this; the citation here should too. One real difference: the appendix holds the input at $\mathbf{u}_n$ over $[t_n, t_n + h)$ and writes $\mathbf{x}_{n+1} = \bar{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{x}_n + \bar{\mathbf{B}}\mathbf{u}_n$, while this section writes $h_t = \bar a h_{t-1} + \bar b x_t$, the input indexed by the step it ends. Both are right; one clause should say that the index is shifted by one. The coefficient formulas are identical, including the scalar $\bar b = (e^{h\lambda} - 1)b/\lambda$, and both texts make the same stability remark.
- **Pattern for the chapter.** Perplexity differences of 0.15 to 0.35 between models of unequal size are read as architectural effects (SSM-5, SSM-21). Parameter counts should be printed and read wherever a perplexity is compared.
- **Pattern.** Unreflowed patched paragraphs (three in this file) and a doubled Discussions link.
- **Missing for the chapter.** A work/depth/memory comparison with attention (structural finding 4). If the chapter review places it elsewhere, this section should point to it.
