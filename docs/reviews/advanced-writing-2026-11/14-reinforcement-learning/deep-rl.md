# Function Approximation in Reinforcement Learning

Source: `chapter_reinforcement-learning/deep-rl.md` (lines 1 to 848, slides ignored). About 5,600 words of body prose (including a 23-row table) and 1,900 words of exercises; 16 code cells per tab. PyTorch and JAX tabs with stored outputs for both.

## Verdict

This is a real section, not a bridge: about four fifths of it teaches things the reader needs and has not seen (the table as a linear network on one-hot states, the tangent kernel as the measure of how far one update spreads, value regression, the time-limit correction, the Gaussian policy). Its best passages are at the bar: the opening on the cart that "will never see the same four numbers twice", the worked time-limit example with a weight of $-90$ that the correction returns to zero, and the admission that the loop it trains "is not the gradient of the objective $J(\theta)$", with the four reasons listed. All formulas and exercise results I checked are correct. The fault is focus. The section has six subjects (networks for tables, time limits, the surrogate loss, continuous actions, pathwise gradients, interference) and then turns into a bridge: a "limits" paragraph, a 23-row map of two chapters of which more than half the rows name methods the reader has not met, and a summary sentence, all three restated at once by the opening of the next chapter. Several empirical claims rest on runs the notebook does not contain, and the Pendulum result is read more favourably than three seeds show. The most important fix: cut the section to its own question (what changes when a network replaces the table), move the pathwise subsection to `sac.md` and the map to the end of the next chapter, and bring "Generalization across States" forward to where its equation is introduced.

## What works

- The opening paragraph. "in a thousand episodes the agent will never see the same four numbers twice. A table over such states would need a new row for every visit, and what it learned at one visit would say nothing about the next." The problem is felt before any machinery, and the second paragraph's history (Bellman, Samuel's checkers in 1959, 162 hand-made boxes for this very cart) is short and true.
- "A table is therefore a linear network whose input features are fixed one-hot vectors." This makes the step to networks continuous instead of a jump, and :eqref:`eq_deeprl_kernel` then says in one line what changes: $k(s, s_0)$ is an indicator for a table and a smooth function for a network. Bringing the tangent kernel into a first treatment of function approximation is a good, unusual choice.
- "Time Limits". The failure is shown with numbers the reader can redo: at $t = 490$, $\hat G_t \approx 9.6$ against a prediction of 100, "a weight near $-90$", and "the update pushes down the very actions that kept the pole up". The correction adds $0.99^{10}\cdot 100 \approx 90.4$. Formula, code (`truncation_bootstrap`, which I checked gives $\gamma^{T-t}$) and example agree. The remark that the limit is "a convenience of training, not ... part of the task; if the limit were part of the task, the remaining time would belong in the state" is the sentence most texts omit.
- "The update that results is not the gradient of the objective $J(\theta)$ ... No stochastic-approximation theorem applies to this combination directly, so the evidence that the loop works comes from runs, not from a convergence proof." Candour about limits, in the voice the guide asks for.
- The value-regression paragraph reasons about scale ("targets ... of order $100$", "an Adam step moves each parameter by roughly its learning rate, $0.01$") to explain why one step per batch is not enough. This is the kind of practical detail a builder needs.
- The zero-initialized mean layer is motivated by the failure it prevents (a mean outside $[-2, 2]$ makes "the score ... multiplied by weights that carry no information about the action").
- The coupling probe: one state nudged, 256 probes sorted by distance; stored output `+0.49`, nearest ten `+0.43`, farthest ten `-0.06`, table `+0.50` and `0.000000` elsewhere (JAX: 0.55, 0.49, -0.01). The prose reads it at the right precision and says what depends on the initialization and what does not. :numref:`fig_rl_table_vs_network` (rendered) shows the same contrast clearly.
- Exercises 4 (the value of the surrogate loss is $-s\,c$) and 6 (exact variances $15\sigma^2 + 14 + 1/\sigma^2$ and $4\sigma^2$, optimal baseline $-(1+3\sigma^2)$; all verified, including 281.75 for $c = 10$) are excellent.

## Structural findings

1. **Section or bridge?** A section with a bridge attached. The bridge is "Limits of the Monte Carlo Agent" (second paragraph), "A Map of the Methods" and the last sentence of the Summary. The three limits (waits for whole episodes, uses each batch once, does not limit its step) are stated in the "limits" paragraph, again in the Summary, again in the first paragraph of `chapter_deep-reinforcement-learning/index.md` ("The agent waited for every episode to end, used each batch once and never limited how far an update moved its policy") and, for the first limit, again in the opening of `actor-critic.md`. The comparison "a few hundred thousand steps" against soft actor-critic appears at the end of "Pendulum", in the "limits" paragraph, and in the next chapter's index, `sac.md` and `rl-sequences.md`. The next chapter's introduction must be able to open on its own, so it keeps the statement; here, end the body on the Pendulum cost and one sentence that names the three limits, and take them out of the Summary.
2. **The map belongs elsewhere.** "A Map of the Methods" is a figure and a 23-row table covering both chapters. At this point the reader knows 11 of the rows; the other 12 hold sixteen method names (A2C, PPO, KL-regularized optimization, DQN, Rainbow, DDPG, TD3, SAC, MPC, Dyna, MBPO, CQL, Decision Transformer, MuZero, Dreamer, DPO) are names, five rows of them "named in" another section only. The guide says sections do not end with surveys and introductions carry no lists of model names. The October review flagged the same table when it stood in the chapter index; it has moved and grown from 21 rows to 23. Recommended: keep :numref:`fig_rl_roadmap` (rendered; it is clear and its two axes are the right two questions) with this chapter's solid boxes only as the closing figure of the chapter, and move the full figure and the table to the end of the Deep Reinforcement Learning chapter (its `further-reading.md` or a closing page), where every row has been taught. The next chapter's index cites both by label, so the references survive the move.
3. **Order.** :eqref:`eq_deeprl_kernel` is introduced in the first subsection and promises that ":numref:`subsec_deeprl_coupling` measures the shape of $k$ for the network we train". The measurement arrives 480 lines later, after CartPole, the surrogate loss, the Gaussian policy, Pendulum and pathwise gradients. Move "A Probe of State Coupling" to follow "The Policy and Value Networks" (it needs only `ActorCritic` and `fit_value`), so that the section's main idea is stated, measured and then used.
4. **The pathwise subsection has no use here.** "Score-Function and Pathwise Gradients" introduces an estimator that nothing in this section or chapter uses; its first use is `sac.md` ("Clipping and the Pathwise Gradient"). It also repeats, with a different quadratic, the comparison that the appendix makes in `subsec_mdl-stochastic-gradient-estimators` (same two estimators, the effect of a baseline, a variance ratio), without citing it. Move the subsection, its figure and exercises 3 and 6 to `sac.md`, and leave one sentence here: a continuous action also allows differentiating through the sample, which :numref:`sec_sac` uses. See DR-11.
5. **The surrogate loss is explained twice.** `policy-gradient.md` already says of `policy_step` that it minimizes "a *surrogate loss*, whose value says nothing about how well the policy performs; only its gradient at the current $\theta$, the estimate, matters". "The Surrogate Loss" here spends about 250 words, an equation, a plot and exercise 4 on the same point. Keep the identity ($L$ is minus the standard deviation of the log-probabilities times their correlation with the weights), which is new and neat, in two sentences after the CartPole curve; drop the subsection heading and the plot of a quantity the text says to ignore.
6. **With 2 to 5 applied, no split is needed.** The section becomes: networks in place of tables (with the probe), value regression and time limits, CartPole, the Gaussian policy and Pendulum; about 4,000 words. If the author prefers a split instead, the seam is at "## Continuous Actions".
7. **Appendix.** No pointer to the appendix anywhere in the file. The section reads without it. The only one owed is at the pathwise identity (finding 4).

## Passage-level findings

**DR-1** (minor, language). Heading: introduction. Quote: "For seven sections every quantity we learned lived in a table". A hardcoded count of sections, which rots at the next reordering in the same way a hardcoded section number does. "Until now every quantity we learned lived in a table".

**DR-2** (minor, explanation). Heading: Tables as Linear Networks on One-Hot States. Quote: "(Adam, the default optimizer of `Policy`, can still move an entry whose gradient is zero". Optimizer plumbing in parentheses in the middle of the section's central explanation; the reader cannot skip it, so it is not an aside. The probe later replaces Adam by plain SGD and says why; that is the place for the remark.

**DR-3** (minor, focus). Heading: The Policy and Value Networks (JAX tab). Quote: "In the DQN of :numref:`sec_dqn`, run for 50 seeds with each initialization, it solved CartPole on about two thirds". A tab note that reports an experiment from a section in the next chapter, with numbers no cell prints. The first two sentences (which initialization, and that it differs from Flax's default) are what the tab needs; the evidence belongs in `dqn.md` or a code comment.

**DR-4** (moderate, correctness). Heading: Value Regression. Quote: "then climbs only to between $0.4$ and $0.8$ by the end of a run on most seeds". The choice of forty regression steps is justified by an explained-variance measurement (0.4 to 0.8 with one step, "about $0.99$" with forty) that no cell computes. Since this number decides a hyperparameter, show it: let `train_reinforce` yield $1 - \textrm{Var}(\hat G - \hat V)/\textrm{Var}(\hat G)$ and print its final value for `value_steps` 1 and 40, or state the claim qualitatively.

**DR-5** (minor, explanation). Heading: The Training Loop. Quote: "Four things separate $\hat{g}$ from $\nabla_\theta J$." Good content. It follows, one paragraph apart, "four things differ from the learned-baseline runs", and the two lists overlap (normalization, truncation). Merge them into one list that says, for each change from `baselines.md`, what it does to the estimator.

**DR-6** (moderate, correctness). Heading: CartPole. Quote: "On this task the time-limit correction barely changed the average return in our runs". Together with "without the correction only about half of the seeds in our runs reached $-200$ within the same budget" under Pendulum, this is the section's evidence that the correction matters, and neither ablation is in the notebook. The Pendulum ablation is one extra call (a flag that zeroes the bootstrap) on a run the notebook already makes; with policy-gradient seed variance, "about half of the seeds" from an unstated number of seeds is the kind of claim the reader should be able to reproduce. Add the arm, or cut the sentences to what exercise 1d argues analytically.

**DR-7** (minor, thought). Heading: CartPole. Quote: "Every seed's batch mean reaches the 500-step ceiling by about update 50 and mostly stays there". Consistent with the stored plot (median at 500 from about update 37, band dipping to about 450 afterwards). Three seeds; the wording is suitably modest. No change needed beyond noting that the JAX tab should be checked to show the same.

**DR-8** (moderate, thought). Heading: Pendulum. Quote: "The discount is our choice here, not part of the task." `mdp.md` told the reader "The discount is part of the task, chosen by its designer". Both are defensible, but the reader has been given a principle and now sees it set aside in a clause. Say what is going on: the task reports the undiscounted 200-step return; the algorithm uses $\gamma$ as a parameter of the estimator that trades variance for bias with respect to that objective. That is how practitioners treat $\gamma$, and it is worth one explicit sentence that revises the earlier statement.

**DR-9** (moderate, correctness). Heading: Pendulum. Quote: "Every run learns to swing the pendulum up and balance it." Three seeds. In the stored plot the median of the ten-update average first touches $-200$ near update 180, then sits between about $-220$ and $-300$ until update 250, and the band falls to about $-600$ near update 295; $-200$ was defined two paragraphs earlier as what "a controller that swings it up and balances it" collects. So the runs reach the threshold and hover at or below it; "learns to ... balance it" claims more. Suggested: "All three runs climb from $-1200$ and reach $-200$ at some point between update 150 and 300, but they do not hold it: the average falls back by a hundred or more, and single batches by several hundred." This also makes the comparison with soft actor-critic fair, since the next chapter counts steps to the same threshold.

**DR-10** (minor, explanation). Heading: Pendulum. Quote: "Over the run $\sigma$ falls to about half its initial value". No cell prints $\sigma$. One `print` of `log_std.exp()` per seed after the run would support it.

**DR-11** (moderate, thought). Heading: Score-Function and Pathwise Gradients. Quote: "The price of the lower variance is access to $Q$ as a differentiable function of the action." The comparison is one quadratic at one $\sigma$, and "the lower variance" turns it into a general property. The text does say "the ratio depends on $Q$, on $\sigma$ and on the offset", and exercise 3 gives the counter-case, but the appendix is more careful in its main text ("It would be wrong to conclude that the pathwise estimator always has the lower variance", with a high-frequency $g$ where it loses). If the subsection stays, add that sentence's content and cite `subsec_mdl-stochastic-gradient-estimators`; the cell here then duplicates the appendix's and can be replaced by the three exact variances (21.75, 1, 281.75), which exercise 6 derives. The stored output (21.5, 1.00, 281) agrees with them.

**DR-12** (minor, correctness). Heading: A Probe of State Coupling. Quote: "Such changes can make a training curve dip even when every update fits its own batch well." Plausible, but nothing in the section connects the dips in the CartPole or Pendulum curves to interference; the probe is on a freshly initialized network at random states. Keep the mechanism ("fitting them moves the estimates at large pole angles that the batch never visits") and drop the causal link to the curves, or mark it as a possibility the section does not test.

**DR-13** (minor, correctness). Heading: Limits of the Monte Carlo Agent. Quote: "with nonlinear approximators or data from another policy the iteration can diverge". The citation (:cite:`Tsitsiklis.VanRoy.1997`) covers the nonlinear counterexample and the on-policy linear convergence result. Divergence under off-policy data with linear features is Baird's counterexample (1995); cite it for that half, or cite Sutton and Barto's chapter 11, which the next chapter's resources already name. This paragraph also previews the deadly triad that `dqn.md` owns; one sentence is enough here.

**DR-14** (moderate, language). Heading: Limits of the Monte Carlo Agent. Quote: "We can now train a policy network on continuous states and continuous actions". The "We can now" recap, followed by the three limits that the Summary and the next chapter repeat (structural finding 1).

**DR-15** (moderate, focus). Heading: A Map of the Methods. Quote: "This chapter and the next present many methods." See structural finding 2. Two rows also carry prose that is not a table entry ("RLHF at scale is left to the Language Models part, which takes it up in more depth", twice); those chapters are skeletons, so the pointer is to text that does not exist yet.

**DR-16** (minor, explanation). Heading: The Gaussian Policy. Quote: "With `state_std=True` it becomes a second linear layer on the features, clamped to $[-5, 2]$". The paragraph documents two constructor options (`trunk`, `state_std`) that this section does not use; one serves an exercise, the other a later chapter. Describe the network that is trained here (one tanh layer, a linear mean started at zero, one learned $\log\sigma$) and leave the options to the docstring, which already states them.

It depends on `Batch`, `rollout`, `policy_step` and `standardize` from the two sections before it, and on `mdp.md` for truncation.
- As a chapter ending it does double duty with the next chapter's introduction. Decide once where the hand-over lives (recommended: there) and where the map lives (recommended: end of the next chapter).
- It sets up `actor-critic.md` (the learned $\hat V$, `ActorCritic`, `fit_value`, `truncation_bootstrap`) and `sac.md` (the Gaussian head, Pendulum, the pathwise gradient).
- Across my three sections, the arc is sound: one estimator derived, its noise reduced, its tables replaced. Length is roughly proportionate, but `baselines.md` and this file each carry material that belongs to the next chapter (GRPO bookkeeping; the pathwise gradient and the map).
