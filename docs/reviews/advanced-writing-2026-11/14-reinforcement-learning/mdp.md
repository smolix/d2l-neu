# Markov Decision Processes

Source: `chapter_reinforcement-learning/mdp.md` (to the `<!-- slides -->` marker). About 5,200 words of body prose including the 44-row symbol table, plus 1,500 words of exercises; 9 code cells.

## Verdict

A sound and concrete first section, better than the corresponding pages of most deep-learning textbooks and exact where Sutton and Barto are loose (termination against truncation, the effective horizon, the loop arithmetic of a bad bonus). Every number I recomputed is right. It falls short of the bar because the section does several jobs for later sections at the expense of its own reader: a 44-row symbol table before the first example, a 35-line `VerifierTask` class that is not used until two sections later, value targets $y_t$ and $V(s)$ in the termination discussion before either is defined, and a statement about optimal policies before "optimal" has a meaning. The most important thing to fix is this forward loading: the section should define the model, the return (including the return from step $t$, which is missing and which every later section needs), the state and the reward, and stop.

## What works

- **The question at cell 9.** "Is the safety of *down* worth the steps it loses sliding sideways?" (under "The Model") is a problem the reader can feel before any machinery, it is answerable only with values, and the next section answers it with $Q^*(9, \cdot)$. Keep it and keep it open at the end of this section.
- **Reading the kernel out of the simulator** (`env.unwrapped.P[9][DOWN]`) and then naming it. The abstraction arrives after its instance, as the guide asks.
- **The transcript of one random episode**, read line by line ("At $t=2$ it commands *left* and slides *down* to cell 4"). This is the Illustrated-Transformer habit: a small concrete case for every definition.
- **Termination against truncation** (`subsec_mdp-termination`). The distinction is stated correctly and operationally ("`truncated=True` means that recording stopped"), and it is the detail that most implementations get wrong. Sutton and Barto do not have it. The absorbing-state remark ("so that every row of $P$ is a distribution") prepares the proofs of the next section.
- **The loop arithmetic of the approach bonus.** $0.3\gamma/(1-\gamma^2) \approx 2.9$ against $1.3$, then $0.315$ and $-0.27$ for the potential. Four numbers make reward hacking concrete. All four check.
- **Text generation as an MDP.** Deterministic transitions, a tree of prefixes, collapse to one step. Short, correct (with one missing hypothesis, MDP-13), and it tells the reader why this chapter matters for language models.
- **Exercises** 1, 3, 4b, 5 and 7 are real problems (see below).
- The `d2l.TabularMDP` class is twelve lines and is the right abstraction for the whole chapter.

## Structural findings

1. **The section works for later sections before it works for its reader.** Four items are advance payments: the chapter-wide symbol table (MDP-1), the claim about optimal policies (MDP-4), the targets $y_7$ and $y_{99}$ in the termination figure (MDP-8), and `VerifierTask` with its API description (MDP-14). Each is correct. Together they make the first section of the chapter feel like a setup file. Move each to its first use and the section becomes a clean 4,000 words with one line of argument: model, return, state, reward.

2. **The return from step $t$ is never defined.** The section defines $R(\tau)$, the return of a whole trajectory. Later sections need the return from step $t$: the symbol table already lists $\hat{G}_t$ ("the discounted sum of the rewards from step $t$ on", owned by `sec_baselines`) and $y_t$ ("an estimate of the return from step $t$"), and the termination figure speaks of "the return counted from that step". Sutton and Barto define $G_t$ and its recursion $G_t = r_t + \gamma G_{t+1}$ in their chapter 3, and that recursion is the Bellman equation before the expectation is taken. Define it here, in "Discounting and the Effective Horizon", in two lines. Then `sec_valueiter` can derive the expectation equation by taking expectations of the recursion, and `sec_baselines` does not need to introduce a second symbol for a return. This is the main notational gap between this section and what the two chapters go on to use.

3. **"The Model as Arrays" sits under the wrong heading.** It is a `###` of "Return, Discount and Horizon" but its subject is $P$ and $r$ (its cell IDs still read `mdp-states-actions-and-the-transition-kernel-2` to `-4`). It is there because `TabularMDP` stores `gamma`. Move it to the end of "The Model" and let the constructor take the discount when "Discounting" has introduced it, or leave the order and promote it to its own `##`. As it stands it interrupts the argument between the discount and episodes.

4. **Reward shaping is told in two halves.** This section gives the bad bonus, the potential form and the terminal-state condition; `sec_valueiter` repeats the equation, the loop and the condition and adds the proof. Under guide 7.7 one section owns each fact. Proposed split: this section owns the approach bonus, the loop arithmetic and the *definition* of the potential form with its two numbers; `subsec_vi_shaping` owns the proof and the terminal-state condition, which cannot be understood here because the telescoping sum has not been shown (MDP-11).

5. **Two endings.** The paragraph "We can now write a decision problem down ..." is the recap that the author has asked to remove, and it introduces two new terms (MDP-15).

6. **Not missing, and rightly so:** the average-reward criterion, finite-horizon problems, continuous states. The one-clause dismissals are the right weight.

## Passage-level findings

**MDP-1** (moderate, pedagogy). Opening. Quote: "The chapter uses the symbols of :numref:`tab_rl_notation`. Those of this section are defined below; the others belong to later sections". A 44-row table, 30 rows of which are forward references ($\rho_t$, $\Delta_{\textrm{KL}}$, $\nu$, $\hat{f}_\phi$), stands between the roadmap and the first sentence about the lake. A reader meets "Polyak rate of a target network" before the word "state". Fix: keep in this section only the rows it defines (the first fourteen), placed at the end as a short table, or drop the table from the section entirely. Put the full table where a reference belongs: the end of the chapter introduction or a notation page for the two chapters. (The LM-part plan in `chapter_lm-rl/index.md` cites `tab_rl_notation` with "owner `sec_mdp`"; update that pointer if the table moves.)

**MDP-2** (minor, thought). Table caption. Quote: "The exploration rate $\epsilon$ and the per-step disagreement $\varepsilon$ are different symbols, and so are a response $y$ and a value target $y_t$." A caption that has to warn about collisions is reporting a notation problem, not solving it. There are more than it lists: $r(s,a)$, $r_t$ and $r(x,y)$; $T$ (steps) and $\mathcal{T}$ (operator); $\beta$ is the KL weight in the table and the belief $\beta_t(s)$ in exercise 7 of this section; $R(\tau)$ is the return and `R` is the verifier's reward table in `VerifierTask`; `sec_valueiter` uses $r_0$ for a radius in a figure caption. Fix the ones that cost nothing: rename the belief in exercise 7 to a letter not in the table, rename the attribute `R` (for example `scores`), and give the value target a letter other than $y$ now that $y$ is a response. $\epsilon$ against $\varepsilon$ should become two different letters.

**MDP-3** (minor, correctness). Under "States, Actions and the Transition Kernel". Quote: "nothing in this chapter loses generality from the simpler form". True for expected returns only. Exercise 3b of this very section shows that the distribution of the return changes (under $r(s,a)$ an episode is paid $1/3$ at each visit of cell 14 whether or not it enters the goal), and `sec_baselines` is about the variance of returns. Write: "expected returns, which are all that the objectives of this chapter use, are unchanged (exercise ...); the distribution of the return is not."

**MDP-4** (moderate, explanation). Under "Discounting and the Effective Horizon". Quote: "There is one policy that is best from every start state at once, and it can be chosen deterministic and independent of time: one command per cell." At this point neither "best from a state" nor a value has been defined, so the sentence cannot be understood, and `subsec_vi_optimality` states the same fact as a proposition and says "as :numref:`sec_mdp` stated". The sentence is here only to excuse the point-mass $\mu_0$. Fix: cut the three sentences from "For a finite MDP with $\gamma < 1$" to "loses nothing", and say at the proposition in `sec_valueiter` that the optimal policy does not depend on the start state. If a pointer is wanted here, one clause: "(the next section shows that the best policy does not depend on where the agent starts)".

**MDP-5** (minor, language/voice). Same paragraph. Quote: "We maximize this *expected return* because it is what many independent attempts collect on average." This restates the definition of an expectation. An author who judges would say what the choice gives up: the expectation is indifferent to risk, and the next section's optimal policy falls into a hole in 22 percent of its episodes while being optimal. One candid sentence here ("it ignores how the returns are spread; an agent that must never fall needs a different objective") is the Learning-with-Kernels register and costs twenty words.

**MDP-6** (moderate, pedagogy). Under "Return, Discount and Horizon". Quote: "No step entered the goal, so every reward is zero. To score a trajectory that does reach the goal, we must first decide how much a late reward counts." The section then never scores a trajectory. The only episode shown has return zero, and the formula :eqref:`eq_mdp_discounted_return` is never applied to a concrete sequence of rewards. Fix: after the definition, compute one return by hand ("an episode that enters the goal on step $t = 11$ has return $\gamma^{11} \approx 0.57$; the episode above has return 0"), or choose a seed whose episode reaches the goal and print its discounted return. With the return from step $t$ defined (structural finding 2), the same episode gives a worked $G_t$ for each $t$.

**MDP-7** (minor, explanation). Under "Discounting and the Effective Horizon". Quote: "about three horizons". The preceding sentence carries the qualifier "For $\gamma$ near one"; this one does not, and the table printed next includes $\gamma = 0.5$, where the count is 5 steps against a horizon of 2. Add "for $\gamma$ near one, $\ln 20 \approx 3$ horizons" so the reader sees where the three comes from; exercise 4a then has less to do, which is fine.

**MDP-8** (moderate, explanation). Under "Episodes, Termination and Truncation". Quote: "the value is zero after termination, and after truncation it is whatever the last state is worth, its *value* $V(s)$, the expected return from $s$ on, which :numref:`sec_valueiter` defines precisely." The paragraph and the caption of :numref:`fig_rl_termination` teach the bootstrapped target ("$y_{99} = r_{99} + \gamma V(s_{100})$", "the target for the last step, $y_7$") one section before values and two sections before targets exist. The distinction itself needs none of this: a truncated episode's sum of rewards is not its return because the rewards after the cut are missing. Fix: keep the two definitions, the 100-step limit and the sentence "The sum of rewards of a truncated episode is therefore not its return"; move the sentence quoted above, the target formulas in the caption and the rule "sets it to zero on `terminated` alone" to `sec_qlearning`, where the first target is formed, with a reference back to this subsection. In the figure, the terminal state of row (a) is labelled $s_8$ and drawn as a hole; on a lake whose cells are numbered, "the hole $s_8$" reads as cell 8, which is frozen. Label the time index differently (for example $s_T$ with $T = 8$) or say "the state at step 8".

**MDP-9** (minor, explanation). Under "State Augmentation". Quote: "The enlarged state can be large, and the information it needs can be unknown, too large to store, or impossible to observe." Three failure modes in one sentence and only the third gets a subsection. Either give each a clause of example (unknown: a user's mood; too large: the full history of a dialogue) or cut to the one that follows.

**MDP-10** (minor, language). Under "Reward Design". Quote: "the shaped reward keeps the goal ahead of the loop by exactly the one that the original reward pays". Hard to parse ("the one" is the number 1). Write: "the goal is still worth more than the loop, by 1, the original reward". Also under "States, Actions and the Transition Kernel": "a difference that the section on reward design turns on" (write "the section on reward design depends on this difference"), and under "The Verifier Task": "the problem of reward design in another form" is a trailing fragment; make it a sentence.

**MDP-11** (moderate, thought). Under "Reward Design". Quote: "One condition remains: the potential must be zero at the terminal states." The justification that follows ("a nonzero potential there would leave a final term that depends on which terminal state the episode ends in, and when") refers to a telescoping sum this section has not shown; the reader cannot see what "final term" means. The same condition is derived properly in `subsec_vi_shaping`, which then says "This is the condition that :numref:`sec_mdp` stated in words." Fix: delete this paragraph here and end the subsection on the forward reference ("... :numref:`sec_valueiter` computes what the approach bonus does on slippery ice, proves that the potential form leaves the best policy unchanged, and states the one condition the proof needs").

**MDP-12** (moderate, correctness; certain of the arithmetic, the judgment about usefulness is mine). Under "Reward Design". Quote: "Our $\Phi = -0.3\,\ell$ is zero at the goal but not at the holes, so it must be set to zero there." and, one paragraph earlier, "a potential spreads the goal's reward over the route, which gives a learner a signal long before it first reaches the goal". With $\Phi$ set to zero at the holes, the shaped reward for stepping into a hole is $\gamma \cdot 0 - \Phi(s) = +0.3\,\ell(s)$: $+1.5$ from cell 4 into hole 5, $+1.2$ from cell 8 into hole 12, $+0.9$ from cell 6, $+0.6$ from cell 10. The largest "signals" this potential gives a learner are for falling in, and the bonus for entering the goal is $1.3$. The invariance theorem still holds (the shaped values of the frozen cells are higher by the same amounts), but the sentence about a helpful signal misdescribes this potential, and neither this section nor the next tests a learner with it; the claim is repeated in `subsec_vi_shaping` and never shown anywhere in the chapter. Fix: either choose a potential for which the sentence is true and say what a good one is (a potential is a guess at $V^*$; shaping with $\Phi$ acts like starting the learner's value table at $\Phi$, a result I recall as Wiewiora 2003, to be checked before citing), or keep $-0.3\,\ell$ and say candidly that it rates every frozen cell below a hole and is therefore a poor guide, which is a better lesson about shaping than the current reassurance. Exercise 11c of `sec_valueiter` uses the same potential and should follow whatever is decided.

**MDP-13** (moderate, correctness; certain). Under "Text Generation as an MDP". Quote: "With deterministic transitions, a single reward at the end, and no revisits, the expected return depends only on the probability that the policy gives to each complete response." This needs $\gamma = 1$. With the chapter's $\gamma = 0.95$ a reward at the end of a response of $n$ tokens is weighted $\gamma^{n-1}$, so the return also depends on the response's length and the collapse to $r(x, y)$ fails. The section set $\gamma = 0.95$ "throughout this chapter" two pages earlier, so the reader has no reason to assume otherwise. Add: "Responses end, so no discount is needed and we take $\gamma = 1$ (:numref:`subsec_mdp-termination`)." Also "no revisits" is not a third hypothesis: it follows from the state being the prefix. Say "and, since the state is the prefix, exactly one path leads to each complete response".

**MDP-14** (moderate, focus). Under "The Verifier Task". Quote: "Its method `verify(x, y)` is the checker, and its attribute `R` holds the checker's rewards as a table, one row per prompt and one column per response." This paragraph is a 230-word API description of a class whose only use in this section is to print one number, $0.062$; `VerifierTask` is first trained in `sec_exploration` (`exploration.md:619`), two sections on, and `theta_ref`, the hedge column at $-4$ and the optional `reward` argument have no role here. A reader finishes a section on MDPs reading about a softmax over a preference table. Fix: keep "Text Generation as an MDP" and the first paragraph of "The Verifier Task" (four prompts, nine candidates, exact match, the hedge and the lenient checker, the ceiling of $0.5$), which make the one-step view and the reward-design point concrete. Move the class, the reference policy and the API paragraph to the section that first trains on it, or keep the class here as a saved cell with two sentences and let the later section explain `policy`, `success` and `reward` when it calls them. The hedge is a reward-design example; a clause pointing back to "Reward Design" would connect it.

**MDP-15** (moderate, pedagogy). Closing paragraph. Quote: "We can now write a decision problem down: the lake as two arrays and a discount, the verifier task as a table of scores". This is the "We can now ..." recap before the Summary that the author asked to remove, and it defines *model-based* and *model-free* inside a recap, where no reader will look for a definition. Fix: delete the first sentence; move the model-based and model-free sentence to the closing paragraph of `sec_valueiter` or the opening of `sec_qlearning`, where the contrast is the subject; end the body on the cell 9 question ("Comparing *down* with *right* requires ... :numref:`sec_valueiter` computes these values for all cells at once"), which is a real ending.

**MDP-16** (minor, language). Summary. Quote: "FrozenLake and the four-prompt verifier task, a token MDP collapsed to one step, are the running examples of the chapter." A summary sentence about the chapter's logistics. If MDP-14 is applied, cut it.

**Pattern: voice.** The prose is clean and almost free of the banned patterns of guide 10.3 (I found no stagecraft, no slogans, two uses of "exactly"). It is also even: the authors rarely judge. Places where a judgment would help the reader rank the material: which of the section's ideas they will use daily (termination against truncation, in my view), that the Markov property is "a property of how we represent the state" (already said, and the best sentence in "The Choice of State"), and that the discount is as much a computational device as a preference. The loop paragraph has voice ("pays for moving closer but charges nothing for moving away"); protect it.

## For the chapter review

- **Fit.** The section sets up `sec_valueiter` well: the cell 9 question, `TabularMDP`, the absorbing terminal states and $\gamma = 0.95$ are all used at once. The handover is the best in the two sections I read.
- **Notation to settle chapter-wide.** (a) Define the return from step $t$ here and use one symbol for it through both chapters; at present $R(\tau)$ (here and in `policy-gradient.md`), $\hat{G}_t$ and $G_t$ (`baselines.md`, `deep-rl.md`) and the target $y_t$ coexist. (b) $r(s,a)$ is the expected reward and $r_t$ the realized one; the next section defines values with $r(s_t, a_t)$ and calls the sum "the discounted return of `sec_mdp`", which it is not (see VI-1 in the value-iteration review). (c) The collisions listed in MDP-2.
- **Overlap.** Reward shaping is split between this section and `subsec_vi_shaping` with the equation, the loop and the terminal condition told twice; the optimal-policy existence statement appears here and as a proposition there.
- **Against Sutton and Barto.** This section is tighter and more exact than their chapter 3 on what an implementer needs (truncation, absorbing states, horizon arithmetic, a reward that can be gamed, the language-model case), and it reads a real simulator. It is thinner on what a first-time reader needs to form the concept: Sutton and Barto give several small MDPs (recycling robot, gridworld, pole balancing), discuss where the agent ends and the environment begins, and define $G_t$ with its recursion. One lake and one LM example are enough if the return is worked on a trajectory (MDP-6).
- **Suspected pattern for the chapter:** sections that carry classes and definitions for sections two or three ahead, and "We can now ..." closers; the index's route paragraph is seven consecutive "asks how/what" sentences, the shape guide 12.1 names as a defect.
