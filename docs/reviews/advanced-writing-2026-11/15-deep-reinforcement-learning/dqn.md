# Deep Q-Networks

Source: `chapter_deep-reinforcement-learning/dqn.md` (to the slides marker). About 4,600 words of prose, 2,200 words of exercises, 28 code cells (PyTorch and JAX counted separately). Stored outputs read for both frameworks; all five figures and the three stored plots rendered and inspected; Baird's numbers, the synchronization bound and the closed forms in the exercises recomputed with NumPy.

## Verdict

A good section with three ideas of its own that a reader will remember: Baird's seven states run in ten lines, the ceiling $1/(1-\gamma) = 100$ that turns "overestimation" from a matter of opinion into a fact, and the nine-fold duplicated actions that make the maximization bias visible on CartPole. The mathematics is correct; I found no wrong equation, and every number I could recompute (77, 335, 677, the growth factor, 86, 99.3, $1/\sqrt{\pi}$, the exercise formulas) is right. What keeps it from the bar is the argument's joints, not its parts. The section opens with the deadly triad and Baird's divergence, then repairs two different problems (correlated data, moving targets), and says only in one sentence that the repairs do not touch the triad; the reader is never told what, if anything, protects DQN from Baird's kind of divergence, although exercise 9 holds the answer. Of the two devices only the target network is tested, in a configuration where its removal is catastrophic on every seed, with no word that this is the extreme case. And the introduction promises that replay gives sample efficiency, while the section's own result is that DQN needs more CartPole steps than PPO, stated in one sentence and left unexplained. The single most important fix: make the first half one argument (which failure each device addresses, which it does not, and what the CartPole ablation is an instance of), and bring the one-paragraph result of exercise 9 into the text.

## What works

- **The opening paragraph** poses the question precisely and in the reader's terms: "For Q-learning the table sits inside the target". The closing question, "Can a network keep the table's licence to learn from any transition?", is the right one. Keep both.
- **Baird in ten lines of NumPy** (cell `dqn-the-deadly-triad`). No sampling, true values representable, and the weights still grow. The prose after it rules out the three excuses a reader would reach for (noise, step size, approximation error) in three sentences. This is the best passage of the section.
- **The ceiling.** "A policy that balances the pole forever earns $\sum_t \gamma^t = 1/(1-\gamma) = 100$ from the start, and no policy earns more". Because the reader can say in advance where the estimate must end, $10^8$ and 117 are both readable without a second experiment. This is the method the Basics review asked for: predict a number, run, compare.
- **The synchronization bound** $(1-\gamma^n)/(1-\gamma)$, evaluated to 86 and compared with the plot. A short argument that produces a number the run then exceeds, with a reason offered. (See DQN-9 for the reason.)
- **Duplicated actions.** Offering each action nine times changes neither $Q^*$ nor the ceiling and gives the maximum something to choose among. It is the only way I have seen to show the bias on a task this small, and the stored outputs of both frameworks support the stated result (DQN ends at 113 to 118 on six of six stored seeds, Double DQN at 92 to 106).
- **Candour about what is not shown**: "On CartPole the double estimator makes the values more accurate without changing how well the greedy policy balances the pole." "A good check is not final". The paragraph on why the return curve is a poor summary ("its best value is chosen after looking at the whole curve, which flatters every method") is a judgment the reader can carry to every RL paper.
- **Sentences worth protecting**: "A falling loss therefore shows that the network fits its current targets, not that the targets are right." "Predictions feed back into targets once per synchronization instead of once per gradient step."
- **Exercises 1, 3, 5, 6 and 9** are of the quality the appendix review praised: each has a closed form to derive and a run to check it against.

## Structural findings

1. **The first half is two arguments that are not joined.** "Sources of Instability" has three subsections. The first and third (semi-gradient, triad and Baird) argue that function approximation, bootstrapping and off-policy data can diverge even in expectation. The second (correlated data, moving targets) names two practical couplings. "Replay and Target Networks" then repairs the two couplings. The only bridge is the two-sentence paragraph beginning "Replay and target networks do not remove an ingredient of the triad", which sits between Baird's result and the figure. A reader finishes the first `##` section believing the triad is the problem and starts the second one being told the devices answer something else. Proposed order: (a) semi-gradient update; (b) triad and Baird, ending on the question "DQN keeps all three ingredients; what does it do instead?"; (c) the two couplings as the practical form the instability takes in a network; (d) the devices, each with one sentence on what it does to Baird's example (see DQN-6). The figure `fig_rl_deadly_triad` should sit where it is first cited, before Baird, not after.

2. **Only one of the two devices is tested.** The target network gets an ablation, a bound, a plot and an audit. Replay gets a paragraph of assertion ("A minibatch then combines transitions collected at different times") and exercise 5. The introduction calls them "Two devices". Either add the one-arm experiment of exercise 5(d) (consecutive minibatches at full capacity) to the ablation cell on the development machine, or say plainly in the text that replay's effect is left to the exercise and why the target network was chosen for the main experiment.

3. **The introduction's promise about data efficiency is reversed by the result and not explained.** See DQN-2 and DQN-12. The section has the material to explain it (the synchronization bound says the value needs most of the budget just to climb) and does not connect the two.

4. **The setup block is 250 lines ahead of its use.** The hyperparameter paragraph ("We again use CartPole with discount $0.99$ ...") and the cells `dqn-deep-q-networks-2` and `-3` come before "Sources of Instability", and introduce the argument `copies`, which "serves the last experiment of the section". The reader meets `TransformAction` and a duplicated-action wrapper before knowing what a target network is. The only thing the first `##` section needs from this block is `gamma`, which the Baird cell reads as a global. Move the block to the start of "Replay and Target Networks", give the Baird cell its own `gamma = 0.99`, and introduce `copies` in "Duplicated Actions" (a default argument can stay in the code; the prose need not mention it early).

5. **The audit belongs to "Overestimation".** The last two paragraphs of "Evaluation of the Greedy Policy" (from ":citet:`Hasselt.Guez.Silver.2016` test a value estimate") introduce `audit` and conclude that with two actions the effect cannot be measured. That is the motivation of the next `##` section and reads as its opening. As placed, a subsection titled for policy evaluation ends on a negative result about value calibration.

6. **Length and what could go.** The prose is not long for the content. What could go without loss: the library plumbing paragraph on `sample` (DQN-7), about half of the `train_checked` paragraph (DQN-10), the max-bias simulation cell or the left panel of the figure (they show the same 1.03; see Code and experiments), and the language-model aside (DQN-14). About 400 words.

7. **What a reader would look for and not find.** The successors of DQN (dueling, distributional, Rainbow, prioritized replay) are in `further-reading.md`, which is the right place; but nothing in this section points there. One sentence at the end of "Double DQN" or in the bridge should say that Double DQN is one of several repairs, that the Rainbow ablation ranked it among the least important on Atari (as `further-reading.md` reports), and where to read on. Without it the section gives Double DQN more weight than the field does.

## Passage-level findings

**DQN-1** (minor, correctness, confident). Introduction. Anchor: "In 2013 a group at DeepMind made the swap work: one network, with one set of hyperparameters, learned to play dozens of Atari games".
The 2013 workshop paper reported seven games and had replay but no target network; the 49 games, the human-tester comparison and the target network are from the 2015 Nature paper. The sentence merges the two, and the next one ("Two devices kept it stable") then credits both devices to "it". Fix: "In 2013 a group at DeepMind made the swap work on seven Atari games, and by 2015 one network with one set of hyperparameters played 49 of them from the screen pixels, many about as well as a human tester." Then "Two devices kept the 2015 agent stable".

**DQN-2** (moderate, thought). Introduction. Anchor: "They also give the agent what the preceding sections could not: actor-critic used each batch once and PPO for a few epochs, while DQN trains on every transition it has stored, many times over."
This sets up data efficiency as the payoff. The section never returns to it except to report the opposite (DQN-12). Either drop the promise here, or keep it and make it a thread: state how many times a transition is used (exercise 5 gives it: total samples are $24{,}500 \times 128$ over 50,000 transitions, about 60 uses each on average, about 250 for the earliest), and say in advance that reuse alone will turn out not to be enough on CartPole.

**DQN-3** (minor, explanation). "The Semi-Gradient Update". Anchor: "\"Closest\" is measured by least squares over the training data, in which the error at a state counts in proportion to how often the data visit it; for linear features this fit is a projection."
This paragraph is the conceptual core (max norm against weighted norm) and is correct, but it is abstract for five sentences in a row with no object to look at, and Baird's example, which is the instance, comes two subsections later. After reordering (structural finding 1) the Baird paragraph can follow directly and say which states the uniform weighting over-counts: the policy being evaluated spends all its time in state 7, the data weight it at 1/7.

**DQN-4** (minor, language). "The Deadly Triad and Baird's Counterexample". Anchor: "These are sufficient conditions, not a theorem about pairs".
The same caveat appears three times within forty lines: here, in "does not restore a general convergence guarantee", and in the figure caption ("the triad is a warning pattern, not an if-and-only-if theorem"). Once in the text is enough; cut it from the caption, which is also carrying "Each circle is one ingredient" and can lose a third of its length.

**DQN-5** (minor, explanation). Same subsection. Anchor: "After a transient, the weights grow by a factor per sweep that settles to a constant".
True (I measure 1.0027 at sweep 1,000, tending to $1 + 0.01\lambda_{\max} = 1.0024$), but the reader is not told the factor, and "335" after a thousand sweeps does not look like an explosion. Give the number and its consequence: "by about 0.25% per sweep, a doubling every 290 sweeps, for ever". Exercise 1(a) then has something to check against.

**DQN-6** (major, thought). Same subsection. Anchor: "Replay and target networks do not remove an ingredient of the triad. They reduce temporal correlation and slow the changes of the targets, which makes training much more stable in practice but does not restore a general convergence guarantee."
This is the hinge of the section and it is two sentences placed before the devices have been introduced. It also undersells what the section knows. Exercise 9 shows that a target network synchronized slowly enough makes Baird's iteration converge (because the features can represent every target, each phase is an exact backup and the composition is a contraction), and that in the Tsitsiklis and Van Roy two-state example it cannot (the projection itself expands). That is the precise answer to "what does the target network fix": it turns the coupled update into a sequence of regressions, which removes the divergence caused by chasing a moving target, and leaves the divergence caused by projecting onto a class that cannot hold the backup. Move the paragraph to the end of "The Target Network", state this in three or four sentences with the two examples named, and leave the derivation in exercise 9. As written, the best idea about target networks in the file is behind two difficulty dots.

**DQN-7** (moderate, language). "Experience Replay". Anchor: "On such a batch `reward_to_go` is the reward itself, and the `truncation_bootstrap` of :numref:`sec_deeprl` adds $\gamma (1 - \mathrm{term})\, \hat{V}(s')$ for a given value estimate $\hat V$, so their sum is the one-step target that `td_target` from :numref:`sec_actorcritic` computes."
This explains how the book's `Batch` class happens to produce the DQN target. It serves the maintainers of the library, not the reader, who needs only "`batch.td_target(V, gamma)` returns $r + \gamma(1-\mathrm{term})V(s')$". The following sentence, "Replay leaves only the one-step target, which is why it pairs with one-step bootstrapping", is circular. Replace the paragraph's second half by: "A uniform draw has no episode order, so only the one-step target can be formed from it. An $n$-step target needs consecutive transitions (exercise ...)."

**DQN-8** (moderate, correctness, believed from memory, not checked against the papers). "The Target-Network Ablation". Anchor: "Without a target network the return never rises above the random policy's and soon falls to about 9".
The stored outputs support this for this configuration (six of six stored seeds end near $10^8$). The prose presents it as the answer to "Is the frozen copy needed?" without saying how typical it is. It is the extreme case: the Nature paper's own ablation found that with replay and no target network DQN still learned, with lower scores, and `further-reading.md` reports from van Hasselt et al. (2018) that "Unbounded divergence was rare". A reader will leave believing that Q-learning with a network always blows up without a frozen copy. Add two sentences after the explanation of the runaway: this task is close to a worst case (reward 1 at every step, $\gamma = 0.99$, a step size of $10^{-3}$), in published ablations removing the target network more often costs score than causes divergence, and soft divergence (estimates above any achievable return for a while) is the common form. The PQN paragraph that follows then reads as part of the same thought. If the authors have the run, the outcome at a ten times smaller step size would make the point with a number.

**DQN-9** (moderate, thought). Same subsection. Anchor: "because a network generalizes: a step that lifts the value of one state lifts its neighbors too, so value spreads faster than one backup per synchronization".
The bound assumes zero initial values and exact fits. An estimate above the bound means some target-network value exceeded it, that is, the regression overshot its targets somewhere or the maximum picked a positive error. "Generalization" is one candidate; the maximization bias of the next section is another, and nothing in the section separates them (the estimate ends 5 to 12 points above 86 with two actions and about 30 above with eighteen, which suggests the maximum matters). The sentence states a cause as fact. Say "The bound assumes exact fits; the run exceeds it by 5 to 12 points, so the fitted values overshoot their targets somewhere. Exercise ... finds where." and return to it in "Duplicated Actions", where the excess grows with the number of actions.

**DQN-10** (moderate, language). Same subsection. Anchor: "It collects the per-episode records and, at the first episode end past every multiple of 5,000 environment steps, runs the *greedy policy*, which always takes $\mathrm{argmax}_a Q_w(s, a)$, for ten episodes on a separate copy of the environment, and once more at the end of the budget;"
One sentence of about 80 words that defines the greedy policy, describes a schedule, and (after the semicolon) introduces two library functions. Then: "These checks do not change training; we read them once the value estimates are understood." The reader is asked to hold a measurement for two subsections. Simpler: define the greedy policy in a sentence of its own; run the plain `train_dqn` here; introduce the periodic checks in "Evaluation of the Greedy Policy" where they are read. If one training run must serve both (it should), say only "the loop also records a greedy evaluation every 5,000 steps, which we read below".

**DQN-11** (minor, explanation). Same subsection. Anchor: "The budget holds about 196 synchronizations after the first gradient step, which bounds the start-state value by about 86."
Correct ($100\,(1 - 0.99^{196}) = 86.05$). The consequence is not drawn: the frozen copy limits how fast value can travel, so the stability is paid for in speed, and at this sync period the estimate cannot reach the ceiling within the budget whatever the data. That is the explanation the reader needs at DQN-12 and the motive for Polyak averaging in the next section.

**DQN-12** (moderate, pedagogy). "Evaluation of the Greedy Policy". Anchor: "Although DQN trains on each stored transition many times, it needs more environment steps to balance the pole for the full episode than PPO with four epochs per batch needed in :numref:`sec_ppo`."
This is the most surprising result in the section (off-policy reuse of about 60 times per transition loses to four epochs of PPO, roughly 35,000 to 45,000 steps against 25,000) and it gets one concessive sentence in the middle of a paragraph about something else. A reader who came for "learn from any transition, many times over" deserves a paragraph: one-step targets move value one synchronization at a time (DQN-11), early transitions come from a nearly random policy and dominate the buffer (exercise 5a), and CartPole is a task where on-policy methods are strong. Say also that the comparison is of different quantities (greedy return at checks against the batch mean return in the PPO section). This is where an author's judgment ("unfortunately", "on this task replay does not pay") belongs.

**DQN-13** (minor, thought). Same subsection. Anchor: "Where the greedy policy fails, which happened on about two seeds in five, the estimate is above what the policy earns, by more than 20 points on about half of such runs."
$Q_w$ is trained toward $Q^*$, whose start-state value is 100, not toward the value of the greedy policy. An estimate of 98 beside a policy that earns 62 is evidence of a bad policy at least as much as of an inflated value. The text half-says this ("too mixed with other errors"). Say it directly, and note that the ceiling is the cleaner test because it needs no policy at all; that is what the next section uses. In the JAX store no seed fails, so this sentence has no instance in that tab's printout.

**DQN-14** (moderate, correctness, unsupported claim). "The Maximization Bias in Numbers". Anchor: "A language model choosing its next token among $10^5$ actions would face a far larger bias from a maximum over noisy estimates, one reason why language-model post-training uses policy gradients rather than a Q-network."
The first half extrapolates the independent-noise model to a setting where it does not hold (errors of one network's outputs are strongly correlated; the section itself says so in exercise 4b), and even under it the bias is at most $\sqrt{2\ln m}$, under 4.8 noise units at $10^5$ against 1.8 at 16, which is larger but not "far larger". The second half is a historical claim I do not believe can be supported: post-training starts from a pretrained policy, which is reason enough. Cut the sentence, or reduce it to the true part: the bias grows slowly, no faster than $\sqrt{2 \ln m}$ for independent unit noise.

**DQN-15** (minor, explanation). Same subsection. Anchor: "with strict inequality when the noise can change which action is largest".
The inequality is asserted here; `qlearning.md` gave "Since the maximum is convex". Neither gives the one-line reason a reader can check: $\max_a \hat Q(a) \geq \hat Q(a^\star)$ for the truly best action $a^\star$, so the expectation of the left side is at least $\mathbb{E}[\hat Q(a^\star)] = \max_a \mathbb{E}[\hat Q(a)]$. One sentence, and the bias is derived and not only simulated. The clause about :numref:`sec_regularized` in the same paragraph ("showed that the hard maximum is the $\beta\to0$ limit of the soft backup with a uniform reference") has no job here and can go.

**DQN-16** (minor, correctness). "Double DQN". Anchor: "If the evaluating estimate is correlated with the selecting one with coefficient $\rho$, the bias that remains is $\rho$ times that of the single estimator".
True for the jointly normal model of exercise 4(c), where the evaluator's error for an action depends on the selector's errors only through the same action. Add "for jointly normal errors" or "in the model of exercise ...".

**DQN-17** (minor, explanation). "Duplicated Actions". Anchor: "and the two-action estimates of the ablation are pulled the other way by the lag of the synchronizations".
The reader must reconstruct that "the lag" is the bound of 86 from two subsections back. Name it: "and the synchronization bound holds the two-action estimates below the ceiling for the whole budget, which hides a bias of this size".

**DQN-18** (moderate, pedagogy). "Duplicated Actions", last paragraphs. Anchor: "We can now train a value network on transitions from any policy, replayed many times, with a frozen copy that keeps the targets still and a second estimate that keeps the maximum from inflating them."
The "We can now" recap before the Summary that the author asked to remove. The rest of the paragraph is a good bridge to SAC (the maximum over a continuous action is "an optimization problem of its own at every step") and should stay, starting at "Both repairs assume that the maximum over actions can be computed".

**DQN-19** (minor, language). "Replay and Target Networks", figure caption. Anchor: "The drawn buffer is full and evicting its oldest entries, the steady state of a long run; in this section's runs the 50,000-step budget never fills the 200,000-slot ring, so nothing is evicted."
The caption is about 130 words and ends by explaining why the drawing does not match the experiment. Either draw the buffer as it is in the runs or drop the eviction arrow's explanation; the text says the same thing two paragraphs later ("so no transition is evicted").

**Pattern: narration of the library** (about 9 instances, moderate in sum). The section spends a sentence or more telling the reader which earlier section each helper came from and what its signature is: "the `linear_schedule` of :numref:`sec_qlearning`", "returns them as the `Batch` of :numref:`sec_policygradient`", "`d2l.evaluate` is the Monte Carlo evaluation of :numref:`sec_valueiter`", "`d2l.on_grid` resamples each run's per-episode records onto a common grid", "The parts of the learner that differ between frameworks come from `make_learner`". Each is accurate; together they make the middle of the section read as documentation. Keep a clause where the name alone would puzzle, and let docstrings carry the rest. The JAX tab notes on `nnx.cached_partial` and `d2l.forward_np` are the October finding about JIT prose inside the algorithm; they are now confined to tab blocks, which is acceptable, but two of the three could be code comments.

**Pattern: statistics from runs the reader does not see** (6 instances). "about half of the twenty did", "which happened on about two seeds in five", "by more than 20 points on about half of such runs", "typically 10 to 30 points above it", "A few of the twenty 18-action DQN runs are still climbing". The disclosure sentence ("were checked over twenty seeds per arm") is right and should stay. But in "Evaluation of the Greedy Policy" the prose is mostly about the twenty runs and only occasionally about the three printed ones, so the reader cannot check most sentences against the output above them. Lead with what the printout shows, then give the twenty-seed frequency once per paragraph.

## Code and experiments

- `dqn-the-deadly-triad`: teaches; numbers confirmed (77, 335, 677). It reads `gamma` from the CartPole configuration cell; give it its own.
- `dqn-the-target-network-ablation-1` and `dqn-the-ablation-2`, `-5`: outputs match the prose in both frameworks. The comparison is fair in budget and seeds. See DQN-8 for what the prose claims beyond it. `use_target=False` is implemented as a sync after every step, which is clean.
- `dqn-the-ablation-3`: prose ("reaches 500 on most seeds between 35,000 and 45,000 steps") matches PyTorch (35k, never, 40k); in JAX two of three seeds first reach 500 at 30k and one at 50k. Within the stated seed variance, but "between 30,000 and 45,000" would cover both stores.
- `dqn-the-ablation-4`: "within about eight points ... usually below it" holds (5.4 to 7.6 below in five of six stored balancing seeds).
- `dqn-the-max-bias-measured` and the left panel of `fig_rl_max_bias` show the same number, 1.03, and `qlearning.md` has already shown the bias on a table. This is the third demonstration. Under the author's decision on cells that confirm, keep one: the figure (it adds the growth with the number of actions) and delete the cell, or keep the cell and make it print the bias for 2, 4 and 18 actions, which the later experiment uses.
- `dqn-double-dqn-in-three-lines-2`, `-3`, `dqn-duplicated-actions-1`: outputs match in both frameworks. The greedy returns in this table (for example 181, 500, 153 against 94, 356, 500 in PyTorch) show no effect on the policy, which the prose says. The Summary's "removes most of that bias" is supported for the 18-action task only; with two actions the two estimators are indistinguishable (93 to 98 against 91 to 95). Say "on the 18-action task" in the Summary.
- Scale: CartPole is large enough to show the target network's effect on values and, with the duplication, the maximization bias on values. It is too small to show either changing the policy, and too small to show replay's benefit over an on-policy method. The prose is honest about the first two and silent on the third (DQN-12).
- Experiments to consider on the development machine: a consecutive-minibatch arm (exercise 5d) in the ablation; the no-target arm at a smaller step size (DQN-8).

## Exercises

Ten exercises, about a third of the section's words, each with three or four parts. Quality is high: every one asks for a derivation and a check, and I confirmed the closed forms in 1, 3, 4, 5 and 9. Two remarks. Exercise 9 carries the section's main conceptual result about target networks (DQN-6); keep the exercise, state the result in the text. Exercise 2 is four parts on Huber loss, clipping and Adam's scale invariance, which is more than the paragraph it supports; parts (c) and (d) could be one. Exercise 10 summarizes a theorem of Gallici et al. ("LayerNorm alone sufficed in every environment except Baird's counterexample"); I believe this matches the paper but could not verify it here. No exercise should be cut.
