Stored outputs checked (identical in both tabs; the section is pure NumPy); `img/mdl-rl-token-mdp.svg` rendered. Arithmetic in the text recomputed by hand, including the two optima quoted for `8*8` at $\beta = 0.5$.

## Verdict

This is the section most readers of a 2026 book will come to the two chapters for, and it is the shortest and the hardest to read of the three. Its mathematics is correct everywhere I could check, and two passages are excellent: the two-digit example that shows a single score pushing a correct tens digit down, and the hedge whose share under the tilted optimum is predicted in closed form and then matched by training. But the section is written as a dense digest for someone who already knows the field. In 2,350 words it names RLHF, RLVR, RLOO, GRPO, VinePPO, GSPO, DAPO, Dr. GRPO, DPO and the $k_3$ estimator; four of its subsections are a single paragraph; DAPO and RLOO are never expanded and GRPO is used four paragraphs before it is defined; and Direct Preference Optimization, the method the brief and the Language Models plan both treat as taught here, gets four sentences, with its derivation and its only experiment in an exercise. Meanwhile half the length of the body again sits in exercises that carry results the text relies on. The most important fix is to decide what this section teaches and give it room: three ideas (per-token credit, a penalty prices a loophole, the GRPO update as an assembly of known parts) worked at the pace of the first, DPO either derived in the text or left to the Language Models part, and the variants reduced to one clause each or moved to Further Reading.

## What works

- **"Credit within a Response".** "The response `13` scores $0$ and receives the weight $r - V(x) = -0.01$ on both tokens, which pushes its correct tens digit down." Then the telescoping identity and the variance split $0.0099 = 0.0009 + 0.0090$, printed and explained ("the second has mean zero given any prefix"). Small, exact, and it changes how the reader sees a terminal reward. This is the level the whole section should hold.
- **The threshold from the closed form.** "its odds against an ordinary candidate rise from $e^{-4}$ to $e^{-4} e^{1/\beta}$: the hedge is the more probable exactly when $\beta < 1/4$". A prediction before the experiment, then a table with the trained policy beside the optimum.
- **"The penalty prices the loophole at every $\beta$; it never closes it."** A judgment in the authors' voice that the reader will carry into practice.
- **Clip against penalty**: "The clip compares with the sampling policy and limits each update; the penalty compares with the frozen reference and changes the optimum." One sentence that separates two things routinely confused.
- **The $k_3$ observation** (expectation equals the reverse divergence, gradient with samples held fixed equals that of the forward one) is correct, current, and matters in practice. I verified both statements and the two optima ($0.017$ and $0.50$ on `8*8`).
- **The scope sentence**: "Everything here rests on one turn and one terminal reward, which make the problem a contextual bandit over whole responses ... tool calls, feedback from an environment and conversations of several turns bring back the transition kernel". Honest about what the section does not cover, and exercise 1 follows it up well.

## Structural findings

1. **Compression.** The opening is 180 words, of which 60 are a citation chain. "One Gradient, Two Views" is one paragraph and an equation. "Necessary and Optional Components" is one paragraph. "Ratios for Stale Samples" and "Asymmetric Clipping Bands" are one paragraph each. "Direct Preference Optimization" is one paragraph. A subsection that is one paragraph is a list item with a heading. The section reads as if each topic had been cut to its conclusion; a reader without :numref:`sec_baselines`, :numref:`sec_ppo` and :numref:`sec_regularized` fresh in mind cannot follow it, and one with them fresh still gets no worked case for five of the nine subsections.
2. **A survey inside a teaching section.** Guide 4.3: teach one representative completely and mention a relative only with the one thing it changes. GRPO is the representative and is taught. GSPO, DAPO, Dr. GRPO, RLOO and VinePPO are named at close to equal weight. Some pass the one-change test in a clause ("DAPO drops the penalty, raises the upper clip, and discards groups whose rewards are all equal"); the two that have their own subsections (GSPO, the asymmetric band) do not earn them, since neither has an example, a cell or a consequence used later. `docs/llm/language_models_part_plan.md` lists "DAPO clip bands, GSPO ratio, Dr. GRPO divisor" as owned by this section, which looks like the reason they are here: material placed for the cross-reference table, not for this reader.
3. **DPO: promised more than delivered.** The previous section points here twice for DPO; the Language Models plan says "The reader derived GRPO and DPO on a toy verifier task in Advanced" and "the DPO derivation is already there". What is here is :eqref:`eq_dpo_reward` and the sentence "Substituting it into the Bradley-Terry likelihood turns reward fitting into a classification loss on the policy itself". The loss is never written. The derivation is exercise 4(a), the consistency result 4(b), and the experiment (including the instructive failure where the hedge never loses a comparison and $\beta$ does not restrain it) 4(c), marked as harder. Either write the loss $-\log\sigma\big(\beta\, h_\theta(y_w) - \beta\, h_\theta(y_l)\big)$ in the text with its three-line derivation and run it once on the verifier task, or cut the subsection to a pointer and let the Language Models chapter own DPO. The present state lets two other places rely on a derivation that the reader has not been given.
4. **The exercises carry the body.** The proof that $k_3$ has the forward divergence's gradient, the forward optimum used for the "$0.50$", the per-token form of the folded penalty ("exercise ...c"), the DPO loss, and the analysis of GSPO's ratio are all in exercises, three of them marked hard. The text cites them as if they were appendices. A result the argument uses should be shown or cited to a source; an exercise is neither.
5. **The loophole costs nothing in the experiment.** See SEQ-7.
6. **Suggested shape** at about 3,300 words: (1) a response as a trajectory, with the two-digit example (keep as is, add two sentences on what a prefix value costs at scale); (2) a checker and its loophole (keep, fix SEQ-7); (3) the GRPO update, with stale samples motivated by a number and the clip and the two penalties compared on the hedge in a printed cell; (4) either DPO done properly or nothing. GSPO, the asymmetric band and Dr. GRPO become one sentence each in (3) or go to Further Reading.

## Passage-level findings

**SEQ-1** (moderate, language). Opening. Quote: "The idea is older than these models." The history formula the writing guide asks not to repeat ("The question is older than ..."), followed by one sentence holding four citations: "fitted a reward to human comparisons ...; ... did so for a large language model ...; ... replaced the value network by a group of samples, and ... the learned reward by a checker". The last clause has lost its verb and its object's referent; a reader who does not know the four papers learns nothing from it. Fix: cut the paragraph. The two that matter (the group baseline, the checker as reward) are introduced where they are used.

**SEQ-2** (moderate, explanation). Opening. Quote: "What does the token-by-token view add when the reward is one number per response, and how far does the penalty of :numref:`sec_regularized` hold back a policy that has found a loophole?" Two good questions, but the paragraph assumes "the group weight of :numref:`sec_baselines`, the ratio and clip of :numref:`sec_ppo`, and that penalty" without a word on what each is. The first paragraph was concrete (a checker, a reader, no target token to copy); the third should stay concrete too. Fix: one sentence each: the group weight is a response's reward minus the mean of its group; the ratio corrects for samples drawn from an older policy; the penalty charges for moving away from the pretrained model.

**SEQ-3** (minor, explanation). Under "One Gradient, Two Views". Quote: "With one reward $r(x, y)$ at the end and no discount, every token's reward-to-go is $r(x, y)$". Correct and essential, and stated in one subordinate clause. This is where the reader should see why the two views are the same estimator: write the REINFORCE estimate once in each view, side by side. Three lines.

**SEQ-4** (minor, explanation). Under "Credit within a Response". Quotes: "so $V(x) = 1/100$, $V(x, 1) = 1/10$, and $V(x, a_1) = 0$ for every other tens digit" and "The response weight has eleven times the variance of the tens digit's advantage." Notation: the state is $s_t$ everywhere else, the advantage is written $A(s_1, a_1)$, but the value is written $V(x)$ and $V(x, a_1)$; use $V(s_1)$ and $V(s_2)$ or say once that $s_2 = (x, a_1)$. And the reading of the variances is one-sided: the units digit's advantage has variance $0.0090$, nearly that of the response weight, so per-token credit helps the early token and hardly helps the last one. That asymmetry is the general fact (a late token's advantage carries almost all the remaining uncertainty) and deserves the sentence. "Every token's score carries the noise of all the other tokens, $T$ terms for $T$ tokens" gestures at it without saying it.

**SEQ-5** (major, focus). Under "Necessary and Optional Components". Quote: "A critic is optional, because the response weight is unbiased and the group mean of :numref:`sec_baselines` is a baseline without a network, as in RLOO :cite:`Ahmadian.Cremer.Galle.ea.2024` and GRPO; a value head on every prefix :cite:`ouyang2022training`, or continuations sampled from the prefix as in VinePPO :cite:`Kazemnejad.Aghajohari.Portelance.ea.2025`, buys per-token credit." One sentence, four methods, two of them (RLOO, GRPO) never expanded at this point; GRPO is defined four subsections later. The paragraph before it compresses "much of the chapter" into one sentence of four clauses. This is the section's thesis (what remains of reinforcement learning when transitions are known and the reward is terminal) and it gets 120 words. Fix: give it a short paragraph per claim. Why $\gamma = 1$; why no model and what "generation can restart from any prefix" buys (Monte Carlo values at a prefix, at the cost of extra generation: say what that cost is at language-model scale); why the triad does not arise; then the choice between the response weight and a prefix value, with the variance numbers just computed as the argument. Name methods only where a clause says what each changes.

**SEQ-6** (minor, explanation). Under "The KL-Penalized Group Step". Quote: "On the verifier task of :numref:`sec_mdp` no ordinary candidate answers `8*8` or `9*9`, and the ninth candidate, the *hedge* "one of 4, 12, 64 or 81", has $e^{-4}$ times the reference probability of each ordinary one." The running example is recalled in one sentence from a section a chapter away. The reader needs three facts restated: four prompts, nine shared candidate responses, a reference policy that is uniform over the eight ordinary ones. Two sentences; the deck of `mdp.md` puts it more clearly ("each ordinary response has probability about 1/8, the hedge about 0.2%").

**SEQ-7** (moderate, pedagogy). Under "The Sweep against the Closed Form". Quotes: "At $\beta = 0$ the policy earns the full lenient score but only $0.50$ under exact evaluation" and "The price is useful learning: at $\beta = 0.5$ the exact score falls to about a quarter." In the stored table the exact score is $0.50, 0.50, 0.48, 0.40, 0.23$ as $\beta$ rises: it is highest with no penalty at all. The hedge wins only on the two prompts where no candidate is correct, so by the section's own measure the loophole is free and the penalty is pure cost. The experiment shows that a penalty prices a loophole; it cannot show why one would pay, and a reader may reasonably conclude that the hacked policy is the best one in the table. Fix, in order of preference: (a) change the instance so the hedge displaces something of value, for example a candidate "I cannot tell" that the exact checker scores above a wrong guess on `8*8` and `9*9`, or a prompt where the hedge competes with a correct answer the lenient checker underpays; (b) add a third column for what is lost (the probability of the hedge is already printed; say that a deployed model answering "one of 4, 12, 64 or 81" to half its prompts is the failure, whatever the exact score says). Option (b) is a text fix; (a) needs `d2l.VerifierTask` changed in :numref:`sec_mdp` and a recapture.

**SEQ-8** (minor, explanation). Same subsection. Quote: "The trained rows lie within about $0.02$ of the optimum, a small systematic gap: near $\pi^\star$ the penalized rewards of a group become nearly equal, and standardization enlarges the step just where the iterate should settle." A diagnosis of a 0.02 discrepancy that no cell tests, in a section that has no room for its main topics. Cut to "The trained rows lie within about $0.02$ of the optimum."

**SEQ-9** (moderate, explanation). Under "Ratios for Stale Samples". Quote: "an inference engine whose kernels and precision differ from the trainer's generates the responses, and several gradient steps share each batch." Two different reasons for a ratio in one clause, one of them (engine mismatch) unexplained and far from anything the reader has seen. Then $W$, $\bar\rho$ and GSPO in four sentences, with the analysis in a hard exercise and no number. If the subsection stays, give it one: with a per-token log-ratio of standard deviation $0.05$ and a response of 1,000 tokens, $\log W$ has standard deviation about $1.6$, so $W$ varies by a factor of five either way at one standard deviation, while $\bar\rho$ stays within a fraction of a percent of one, which is also why its band must be so narrow (exercise 7c). Otherwise reduce GSPO to that one sentence inside the GRPO subsection. The ratio is indexed $t = 1, \ldots, T$ here and $\rho_0 \cdots \rho_t$ in :numref:`sec_ppo`.

**SEQ-10** (minor, focus). Under "Asymmetric Clipping Bands". Quote: "A band of fixed width in ratio space permits different changes in probability: at $\epsilon_{\textrm{clip}} = 0.2$ a token at $0.01$ gains nothing by rising above $0.012$, a token at $0.6$ nothing above $0.72$." A good concrete sentence in a subsection with nothing else. The label is `subsec_ppo_asymmetric` and the exercise `ex_ppo-asymmetric-bands`: this passage was moved from the PPO section and still reads like a note. DAPO is never expanded. Fold the sentence into the GRPO subsection where the clip is discussed, name DAPO once with its three changes (already done two paragraphs later, so the method is currently introduced twice), and drop the heading.

**SEQ-11** (major, pedagogy). Under "Direct Preference Optimization". Quote: "Substituting it into the Bradley-Terry likelihood turns reward fitting into a classification loss on the policy itself, with no sampling and no reward model". See structural finding 3. Also: "Its data are a fixed set of comparisons, so it inherits the questions of :numref:`sec_offline`" is the third of three pointers between the two sections that assert a link and never state it. The link is concrete and takes two sentences: DPO never samples from the policy it trains, so nothing tests the responses that policy comes to prefer outside the comparison set, and the reference in the loss plays the part the behavior policy played in the policy-constraint family; exercise 4(c) shows the consequence (a response that never loses a comparison grows without bound, whatever $\beta$ is). Say that here. "Read backwards, the tilted optimum gives the reward" is a nice turn and should stay.

**SEQ-12** (moderate, explanation). Under "Group Relative Policy Optimization". Quotes: "the gradients of $\rho\,\hat{A}$ and of $-\beta k_3$ are $\rho\,\hat{A}$ and $-\beta(1 - \pi_{\textrm{ref}}/\pi_\theta)$ times the score" and "on `8*8` at $\beta = 0.5$ the reverse optimum keeps the hedge at $0.017$, the forward optimum at $0.50$". Both correct. The first hides the code's central step in a subordinate clause; show the two derivatives on a line each. The second is the section's most surprising number (GRPO's penalty as implemented leaves the hedge with half the probability where the penalty of the previous subsection leaves 2%) and no cell prints either value; the forward optimum is defined only in exercise 5(b). Print them: `task.policy(task.theta_ref + R / beta)` gives the first, and a four-line bisection for the multiplier $m$ gives the second. Better still, run `grpo_step` against `sloppy` at $\beta = 0.5$ beside `kl_group_step` and show the trained hedge probabilities; that experiment is the payoff of the whole section and it is missing.

**SEQ-13** (moderate, correctness). Same subsection. Quotes: "The fraction outside the band then grows, and on almost every group it is larger at the fourth epoch than at the second. Over forty groups, four epochs always ended with a higher exact score than one." The printout shows one group. Both sentences report runs the notebook does not contain. And "four epochs beat one" is read as a gain from reuse, but four epochs take four gradient steps at the same step size and one epoch takes one: the comparison confounds reuse with the total step. The honest control is four fresh groups (four times the samples) or one step at four times the step size. Unverified: I could not run the cell (it needs `d2l`). Fix: print the forty-group tally if it is kept, and state the comparison as what it is ("four steps on one group moved the policy further than one step; whether that is as good as four fresh groups is exercise ...").

**SEQ-14** (minor, pedagogy). End of body. Quote: "The chapter began with a pendulum that needed a few hundred thousand steps." A chapter recap as the last paragraph of a section. Its final sentence ("Each saving rests on an estimate that can be wrong: a critic, a stale batch, a model, a log or a checker") is the best single-sentence summary of the chapter and should survive, but in the chapter's own closing (Further Reading or the introduction), not here; the list of step counts that precedes it repeats the table in `index.md`. The body's real last sentence is the scope sentence about one turn and one terminal reward, which is a good place to stop.

**SEQ-15** (minor, language). Under "Verifiers, Reward Models and Their Loopholes". Quote: "The reward comes from a model fitted to human comparisons (:numref:`sec_regularized`), least reliable where the comparisons never went, or from a check such as a test harness or an exact-match grader." The section's second `##` opens with a sentence that has to be read twice (the participle "least reliable ..." dangles between the two alternatives). Two sentences: one for each source of reward and its weakness. "A checker is exact for the property it checks, which may not be the property intended" is good and should follow directly.

## Code and experiments

- `rl-sequences-credit-within-a-response-2`: the `np.allclose` line confirms an identity proved in the next paragraph (and assigned as exercise 2a). It cannot fail. Keep the table and the variances, which teach; drop the check or replace it by printing the two expected gradients for the digit 1.
- `rl-sequences-reward-hacking-unified`: prose matches the output. The experiment shows no cost of the loophole on the exact score (SEQ-7).
- `rl-sequences-group-relative-policy-optimization-2`: two claims about forty groups that are not printed; the one-against-four comparison is confounded (SEQ-13).
- Numbers no cell prints: "$0.017$" and "$0.50$" (correct by my calculation: $e^{-2}/(8 + e^{-2}) = 0.0166$; multiplier $m \approx 1.0023$ gives $0.502$), the forty-group statements.
- The imports cell loads `torch`/`jax` and friends; nothing in the section uses them. A section about training language models that runs entirely on a $4 \times 9$ table is defensible (exact optima are the point), but the text should say once that the same update at scale is the Language Models part's subject, and the frameworks should not be imported for show.
- `kl_group_step` passes a penalized reward to `d2l.group_step`, which standardizes it; the reader has to remember that from :numref:`sec_baselines`. One comment line.

## Exercises

Seven, at half the length of the body. Individually they are strong: exercise 1 (the tool's output must not receive a score term) is a real bug in real training loops and exactly the kind of exercise a modern reader needs; exercise 5 is a small paper's worth of analysis; exercise 7 on the geometric-mean ratio is elegant. Collectively they hold too much of the section's content (structural finding 4). Promote 4(a) and the statement of 5(a) into the text; keep the rest. Exercise 6 (asymmetric bands) can stay if the subsection becomes a sentence; relabel it (`ex_rl-sequences-...`). Exercise 3(c) asks which property of `d2l.group_step` selects the split at $\beta = 0$, which requires rereading code from another chapter: give a hint or cut.
