# Soft Actor-Critic

Source: `chapter_deep-reinforcement-learning/sac.md` (to the slides marker). About 6,100 words of prose, 1,900 words of exercises, 34 code cells (PyTorch and JAX counted separately). Stored outputs read for both frameworks; the two figures and three stored plots rendered and inspected; the two-action numbers, the stable log-determinant, the Polyak half-life, the temperature prediction and the exercise formulas recomputed.

## Verdict

The derivations are correct and well ordered, and the implementation detail is the best in the chapter: the tanh change of variables, the stable log-determinant with the float32 table that shows what the usual $10^{-6}$ guard hides, the sum-over-coordinates warning, the prediction of the temperature trace from Adam's step size. A reader can build SAC from this section and will know which lines are easy to get wrong. I found no wrong equation. Against the bar, the weakness is that the section explains four mechanisms (entropy in the objective, the squashed policy, twin critics, a learned temperature) and its experiments show that on Pendulum none of the last three changes the return, while the first is never tested at all. The prose says so honestly each time ("it does not change how fast the pendulum is learned", "on Pendulum neither changes the return"), but three honest nulls in a row leave the reader with an algorithm whose parts are justified by citation. The single most important fix: decide what the experiments are for. Promote the reward-scale experiment (now exercise 3e), which is the stated reason for learning the temperature, into the text in place of the comparison at one scale where $\beta = 0.2$ happens to work, and say plainly, once, that Pendulum is too easy to show what the entropy term and the twin critics do for the return. A second, smaller matter is conceptual: the section opens with a policy "paid for staying random" and ends with a learned temperature of 0.03 and an entropy target that makes the policy nearly deterministic, and never reconciles the two.

## What works

- **The opening.** "A simulator makes such steps cheap. A robot arm does not: every step takes real time, wears the hardware and risks breaking something." The October complaint that the motivating result came at the end is fixed: the first paragraph states the gap (a few hundred thousand steps against a few thousand) and the question.
- **"Two Actions by Hand".** $Q = (1, 0)$, $\beta = 0.5$, $\pi^\star = (0.88, 0.12)$, soft value 1.06. All numbers check. The observation that "The bracket $Q(a) - \beta \log \pi^\star(a)$ takes the same value for both actions" and so "At the optimum, one sampled action gives the soft value without error" is a small, sharp fact that explains why a single sampled action is a reasonable target. This is the MacKay method.
- **The distinction between soft evaluation and the soft backup**: "It is not the log-sum-exp backup of :numref:`sec_regularized`, which computes the value of the tilted optimum." Many treatments blur this. And: "The buffer supplies what happened; the policy supplies what it would do next."
- **The proposition and its five-line proof** are correct and at the right length. The sentence after it is candid about its reach: "the proposition describes the exact algorithm, not the loop we run".
- **"A Numerically Stable Log-Determinant".** The identity is derived in one line, and the table (naive $-\infty$ from $u = 10$, guarded stuck at $-13.8155$, stable correct) shows a failure that produces no error message. "Summing one and averaging the other gives the right answer for Pendulum's single coordinate and a wrong one for every larger action space" is exactly the kind of detail that matters in practice.
- **The temperature prediction.** "After 6,000 updates that predicts $\beta \approx e^{-1.8} \approx 0.17$, which we will compare with the measured trace." The stored medians are 0.74 at 2,000 steps (prediction 0.74) and 0.21 at 7,000 (prediction 0.17). The prediction also tells the reader something uncomfortable and true: for the first half of training the "learned" temperature is a decay schedule set by the optimizer's step size.
- **The floor of the measurement** is stated: "a five-episode average cannot reach $-200$ before step 2,000."
- **The bridge to the next section**: "A person who has pushed a swing a few times can predict where it will go next."

## Structural findings

1. **Four mechanisms, no experiment in which one of them matters for the return.** Learned against fixed temperature: "neither crossed consistently earlier". Twin against single critic: "the return does not tell the two apart". Entropy term against none: not run. The only large effect shown is SAC against REINFORCE, which changes at least three things at once (SAC-12). By the writing guide's own rule ("An experiment must show what the prose says it shows ... Do not keep it and explain what it fails to show") two of the three comparisons are candidates for redesign. Concretely, for the development machine:
   - Temperature: scale the reward by 10 (exercise 3e) and compare fixed $\beta = 0.2$ with the learned temperature. The text motivates the learned temperature with exactly this ("a temperature tuned for one task is wrong for the same task measured in other units") and then tests it where the fixed value is right.
   - Entropy: one arm with a very small fixed $\beta$ (or the deterministic limit of exercise 3d) to show what the bonus does or does not do for exploration on this task.
   - Twin critics: the calibration measurement does show an effect and should stay. If a second task is out of scope, say once that Pendulum is too forgiving for the bias to cost return, and that the update-ratio literature in `further-reading.md` is where it does.
   Until then, the prose pass can state the limitation in one place (the start of "Sample Efficiency and Calibration") and stop repeating it per subsection.

2. **The maximum-entropy story and the learned temperature are not reconciled.** The introduction says "The policy is paid for staying random, so an actor trained against its own critic keeps exploring", and "Entropy as Reward" says the term "changes the optimum, and the best policy stays random wherever several actions are nearly as good". The algorithm that is run then drives $\beta$ from 1 to about 0.03 and holds the entropy at $-1$ nat. An entropy of $-1$ nat on a torque range of width 4 is that of a uniform density on an interval of length $e^{-1} \approx 0.37$, or of a Gaussian with $\sigma \approx 0.09$: nearly deterministic. With the constraint form, entropy is not a reward to be collected but a floor, high early and low late; the objective :eqref:`eq_maxent_objective` with a fixed $\beta$ is no longer what is optimized. The section needs a paragraph in "Automatic Temperature" that says this: what $-1$ nat means in torque units, that the constrained problem is a different problem from the fixed-$\beta$ one, and that in practice the mechanism acts as an exploration schedule. The closing claim of "Entropy and Temperature", "The learned, state-dependent spread keeps the policy random at little cost in return", should be rewritten against that reading (SAC-16).

3. **The dip investigation is a research note inside a teaching section.** About 350 words, two cells and three reference controllers establish that at 2,000 steps the actor damps the pendulum at the bottom. It is original and the damping controller scoring worst is a nice surprise. But it ends on "A likely reason, which we have not tested separately", it is about a transient of this algorithm on this task, and it sits between the headline result and the update-ratio result, which is the one the section's question is about. Recommendation: reduce to three sentences in the text (the returns fall below random; the actor is holding the pendulum still at the bottom, as measuring $\cos\vartheta$ and speed shows; the explanation is conjecture) and move the controllers and the measurement to an exercise. If the authors want to keep it, test the conjecture (for example by the critic's horizon) so that it ends on a finding.

4. **Length.** At about 8,000 words with exercises this is the longest section of the chapter. I would not split it; the derivation, the policy, the algorithm and the measurements are one arc. If a split is wanted, the seam is the heading "Sample Efficiency and Calibration": everything before it builds the agent, everything after measures it, and the runs are created in the last cell before the seam. What can go without loss, about 1,000 words: the dip investigation (finding 3), the second normalization cell (SAC-9), about half of the seven paragraphs that narrate the classes (pattern below), the re-quoted variances (SAC-5), and the two-action cell if its table is given in prose (Code and experiments).

5. **The actor's gradient is never written out.** The section derives the target, the loss and the density, and then hands the loss to automatic differentiation. The reader who wants to know what the term $\beta \log \pi_\theta$ does to $\mu$ and $\sigma$ finds it only in exercise 7. Two lines would do it: with $u = \mu + \sigma z$, $\log \pi = -z^2/2 - \log\sigma - \log(1 - \tanh^2 u) - \log c + \textrm{const}$, so the entropy term pulls $\sigma$ up at the rate $\beta/\sigma$ and, through the Jacobian, pushes $u$ toward zero with force $2\beta\tanh u$, while the critic term pulls $u$ toward higher $Q$. This is the content of "reparameterized policy gradient with the tanh correction" and it explains the paragraph about the clamp ("a mean far past the squash still drives [the entropy] down").

## Passage-level findings

**SAC-1** (minor, thought). Introduction. Anchor: "Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) :cite:`Haarnoja.Zhou.Abbeel.ea.2018,Haarnoja.Zhou.Hartikainen.ea.2018` keeps both repairs and adds a third idea".
"Both repairs" has no clear antecedent: the paragraph has named two ideas (replay, the pathwise gradient), one failure (DDPG) and one repair (TD3's two critics). It also reads as if SAC were built on TD3; the two were concurrent, and the text says later that "The first SAC paper already trained two critics". Write "keeps replay, the pathwise gradient and the two critics, and adds the entropy reward".

**SAC-2** (moderate, explanation). "The running example" paragraph. Anchor: "applies a torque $u \in [-2, 2]$".
Here $u$ is the torque, and the cost is $0.001\,u^2$. From "The Tanh Change of Variables" on, $u$ is the unbounded pre-squash variable and the torque is $a = c\tanh u$. The two meanings meet in the sentence "Draw $u \sim \mathcal{N}\big(\mu_\theta(s), \sigma_\theta(s)^2\big)$ and squash it". Call the torque $a$ from the start.

**SAC-3** (minor, explanation). "Entropy as Reward". Anchor: "it is negative for a density concentrated on an interval shorter than one".
The only preparation the reader gets for an entropy target of $-1$ and entropy plots that go below zero. Add the concrete reading here (uniform on an interval of length $\ell$ has entropy $\log \ell$; a Gaussian has $\tfrac12\log(2\pi e\sigma^2)$, which is zero at $\sigma \approx 0.24$), so that the later numbers can be read as widths.

**SAC-4** (minor, correctness). "Soft Policy Evaluation". Anchor: "That holds for finitely many actions, and for continuous policies whose density stays bounded, which we assume."
A bounded density bounds the entropy from below; the upper bound ($\log 4$) comes from the bounded action range. Say "on a bounded action range, for policies whose density stays bounded". (The squashed Gaussian with clamped $\sigma$ qualifies: its density tends to zero at $\pm c$.)

**SAC-5** (minor, language). "Soft Policy Improvement". Anchor: "and on that section's example its single-sample variance was $1.0$, against $21.75$ for the score function".
Re-quoting two numbers of another section's toy example, to four digits, does not help here; the ratio depends on that example. "an order of magnitude lower variance on the example there" is enough.

**SAC-6** (minor, thought). "Clipping and the Pathwise Gradient". Anchor: "so a sample beyond a boundary gives the actor no gradient, even at states where the boundary torque is the best action".
The concession points the wrong way. Where the boundary is best, a zero gradient does no harm. The damage is at states where the boundary is not best: a mean that has drifted past it gets no signal from the critic to come back. Rewrite: "so a sample beyond a boundary gives the actor no gradient, and a mean that has drifted past the boundary is not pulled back even where an interior torque is better".

**SAC-7** (moderate, correctness, confident about the common implementations). "The Tanh Change of Variables" and "Automatic Temperature". Anchors: "We keep it, so that the reported entropies are those of the actual torque distribution." and "The common choice is $\bar H = -\dim \mathcal{A}$, here $-1$ nat."
The common implementations compute the log-density of the tanh output in $[-1, 1]$ and rescale the action outside the density, so their $-\dim\mathcal{A}$ refers to entropies without the $\log c$ term. This section keeps $\log c = \log 2$, so its target of $-1$ nat corresponds to $-1.69$ in that convention, a somewhat wider policy than the "common choice" gives on this task. The effect on the runs is small, but the text presents the two choices side by side as if they were compatible. Add one sentence: with the $\log c$ term kept, the conventional target is $-\dim\mathcal{A} + \dim\mathcal{A}\,\log c$ if one wants to match those implementations; we use $-1$ in torque units. Also say that $-\dim\mathcal{A}$ is a heuristic with no derivation behind it; the text gives no reason for it at all.

**SAC-8** (minor, explanation). "The Tanh Change of Variables". Anchor: "It does not bound the entropy of the squashed action, which a mean far past the squash still drives down."
Correct and important, but the reader cannot see why until the gradient is written (structural finding 5). After that addition this sentence becomes a consequence and can point to exercise 7.

**SAC-9** (moderate, pedagogy). "A Numerically Stable Log-Determinant". Anchor: "Normalization gives a second, independent check of the change of variables."
The cell re-implements :eqref:`eq_tanh_logdet` in NumPy (`squashed_logp`) and integrates it. It does not call `log_prob_u`, so it checks the formula the text has just derived, not the code the reader has just written, and the figure caption already reports the same 1.13. The sentence "the check finds it without any training" is true of a check that tested the class. Either rewrite the cell to evaluate `SquashedGaussianPolicy.log_prob_u` on the grid (then it is a unit test and earns its place), or delete it and keep the figure. "This error would barely change the return on Pendulum" is an untested assertion; exercise 7(d) tests it, so cite the exercise or cut the sentence.

**SAC-10** (minor, explanation). "Twin Critics and Polyak Targets". Anchor: "and the target needs no extra smoothing of that action, because a stochastic policy averages its own targets over its spread".
"Extra smoothing" refers to TD3's target-policy noise, which the section has not described (it is in `further-reading.md`). A reader has nothing to attach the clause to. Name it in half a sentence ("TD3 adds noise to the target action so that the actor cannot exploit a narrow peak of the critic; SAC's sampled $\tilde a'$ already does this") or cut it.

**SAC-11** (moderate, correctness, confident). "Automatic Temperature". Anchor: "The multiplier is learned by gradient descent on".
Two gaps. (a) The loss shown, with $-\log\beta$ in front, is the form implementations use; the paper's dual objective has $\beta$ itself there. The text's derivative statement is correct for the form shown, and the two share sign and fixed point, but the text presents the $\log\beta$ form as what "treats $\beta$ as the Lagrange multiplier" yields. Say that the dual gives $J(\beta) = \beta\,(\hat H - \bar H)$ and that implementations replace the factor $\beta$ by $\log\beta$, which keeps the sign and makes the step size independent of the scale of $\beta$ (the fact the Adam prediction two sentences later relies on). (b) The reader of this part has not met Lagrange multipliers in the main text. One sentence of meaning is needed: $\beta$ is the price of entropy; raise the price while the policy holds less than $\bar H$, lower it while it holds more, and if the unconstrained optimum already has more entropy than $\bar H$ the price goes to zero. The constraint is also written as "$\mathbb{E}_{s}$" with no distribution named; the paper imposes it per time step under the policy's own visitation.

**SAC-12** (moderate, correctness, claim beyond the evidence). "Return against Environment Steps". Anchor: "Three changes account for the difference."
The comparison is SAC against the REINFORCE of `sec_deeprl`, which differs in replay, bootstrapping, the gradient estimator, the discount (0.95 there, 0.99 here), the network, the entropy term and the optimizer settings. No run separates them, so "account for" is an explanation offered, not shown. Write "Three changes plausibly account for most of it" and, if possible, add the one number the chapter already has that isolates something: how PPO or actor-critic fares on Pendulum (neither section runs it; if no such run exists, say that the comparison is against the simplest on-policy method, not the best).

**SAC-13** (minor, thought). Same subsection. Anchor: "It crosses $-200$ after 2,400 to 4,000 environment steps, against 5,400 to 11,200 at one update per step, about 2.5 times fewer."
The runs at ten updates per step stop at 4,000 steps, so an upper end of 4,000 says that some of the twenty runs crossed only at the end, or would not have been counted had they needed longer. The range is censored and the ratio with it. Also, the lower end of 2,400 is 400 steps above the floor of the metric that the paragraph itself states. Say that the measurement is close to both its floor and its cap, or run the arm to 6,000 steps.

**SAC-14** (minor, language). Same subsection. Anchor: "A higher update ratio exchanges computation for interaction, the exchange a robot arm wants."
Good sentence, and it returns to the opening. Keep. The closing paragraph of the section then says it again ("a higher update ratio exchanges computation for further environment steps"), as does the Summary. Once here and once in the Summary.

**SAC-15** (minor, explanation). "Entropy and Temperature". Anchor: "With the learned temperature the entropy falls steadily from above one nat onto the target and stays near it from about 15,000 steps on."
The stored plot (PyTorch) shows the entropy at about $-0.9$ at 15,000 steps and reaching $-1$ at about 19,000; the printed averages over the last 20 episodes are $-0.95$ to $-1.0$. "approaches the target and is within a tenth of a nat of it from about 15,000 steps on" matches the figure.

**SAC-16** (moderate, thought). Same subsection. Anchor: "The learned, state-dependent spread keeps the policy random at little cost in return."
At $-1$ nat the policy is not random in any sense the reader would recognize (structural finding 2), which is the reason sampling costs little. The measurement supports a different sentence: "By the end of training the policy is narrow enough that sampling from it costs at most about ten points of return." Related: "By lowering $\beta$ as the critic flattens" attributes the fall of $\beta$ to the critic, while the preceding sentences have just shown that until about 10,000 steps the fall is Adam's constant step. Keep the Adam explanation and drop the other.

**SAC-17** (moderate, pedagogy). "Critic Calibration". Anchor: "Over the twenty runs per configuration these signs held as stated, while the sizes of the gaps varied from run to run by tens of points."
The measurement is sound in sign and the caveats about the time limit and the first-step entropy are careful. Two things the reader should be told. First, the prediction is read at the start state, a hanging pendulum, which each episode visits for only a few steps; at ten updates per step the buffer holds 20 episodes, 5 of them random, so the critic is being audited where it has the least data. That is a fair place to look for bias, but it means the gaps (20 to 50 points on values near $-100$) are not the critic's typical error. Second, single and twin are compared only at 4,000 steps; whether the single critic's optimism persists with more data is not shown. The conclusion "The minimum therefore prevents the overestimation that a single critic shows here" is supported; add "at the start state, early in training".

**SAC-18** (minor, pedagogy). End of "Critic Calibration". Anchor: "The pendulum is now learned from a few thousand environment steps, and a higher update ratio exchanges computation for further environment steps."
A recap before the Summary. The paragraph's second half (one number per transition, the swing, the question for `sec_model_based`) is a good bridge and should stay; start it at "Each transition still teaches the critic one number".

**SAC-19** (minor, correctness). Summary. Anchor: "Twin critics with Polyak-averaged copies keep the critic from overestimating".
The section measured that the minimum underestimates, by 15 to 40 points at the start state. "replace the single critic's overestimate by a smaller underestimate" is what was found; whether smaller is true is not uniform across the stored seeds (gaps of $-18$ to $-40$ against $+37$ to $+52$ in PyTorch, $-12$ to $-26$ against $+19$ to $+32$ in JAX), so "by an underestimate" is safest.

**Pattern: paragraphs that narrate the classes** (7 instances, moderate in sum). "`SquashedGaussianPolicy` wraps the head. `sample` draws ...", "`SoftActorCritic` holds the actor, a list of critics ...", "`make_critic` builds one critic ...", "One update follows :numref:`fig_rl_sac_update`, and three functions hold its losses.", "`sac_step` performs the update.", "`train_sac` runs one agent on Pendulum.", "`run_arm` trains one configuration on several seeds". Each lists methods and arguments that the docstrings directly below repeat. The useful sentences are those that say why (for example why the sampler keeps $u$: "so no inverse hyperbolic tangent appears on the differentiable path"; why `make_update` seeds). Keep those, cut the inventories. The paragraph beginning "The `ActorCritic` container of :numref:`sec_deeprl` ... does not fit SAC" explains a library decision to a reader who did not ask.

**Pattern: detail that serves nobody** (4 instances, minor). "published SAC implementations use the same shape with 256 units"; "Common implementations clamp to $[-20, 2]$"; "The clamp never binds in our runs, where $\log \sigma$ stays above about $-3.5$, and removing it does not change their outcome"; "start from $\beta = 1$, the default of common implementations". One sentence collecting the differences from the reference implementations (width, clamp, warm-up) would replace these and be easier to find.

## For the chapter review

- Assumes `sec_regularized` heavily (the tilted optimum, $\beta\log Z$, the KL identity :eqref:`eq_sac_kl_identity`, `subsec_kl_direction`); the two-action example and the restated identity make the section readable without rereading it, which is the right amount of duplication. Assumes `sec_dqn` (replay buffer, target network, `subsec_dqn-max-bias`) and `sec_deeprl` (Pendulum, `GaussianHead`, the pathwise gradient). The only appendix reference is indirect, through `subsec_kl_direction`, which exists in `regularized.md` and holds the cited content. No reference to the Language Models part.
- Order in the chapter is right: after `regularized` and `dqn`, before `model-based`, which the last paragraph sets up well.
- Notation: $\beta$ (temperature) and $\nu$ (Polyak rate) are in `tab_rl_notation`. Changes and collisions to report: $u$ is first the torque and then the pre-squash variable (SAC-2); $c$ is the action scale in the text and `scale` in the class; $H$ is entropy here and a horizon in exercise 3 of `dqn.md`; the entropy target is $\bar H$ in the text and $\bar{\mathcal H}$ in `fig_rl_sac_update`; the target network is "frozen" in the docstrings ("their frozen copies") although the text has just explained that Polyak copies drift.
- The maximization bias figure of $0.56\sigma$ is restated here from `dqn.md`; a citation is enough.
- Pattern for the chapter: mechanisms are explained with care and then tested on a task where they do not change the return, with the null reported honestly each time. One sentence per chapter about what CartPole and Pendulum can and cannot show would be better than one per experiment, and the experiments that remain should be the ones with an effect.
