# Adversarial Losses Beyond GANs

Source: `chapter_generative-adversarial-networks/adversarial-losses.md` (label `sec_gan_beyond`). About 2,950 words of prose, 1,400 words of exercises, 6 code cells per framework. Stored outputs checked in both frameworks.

## Verdict

The first 1,400 words are the best teaching in the two sections I reviewed and close to the bar: a problem the reader feels (the thumbnail), a two-line decomposition, the smallest example that separates the two losses (two bands, with the numbers 1.0025 and 2.0025 worked by hand), an experiment that shows it, and an honest paragraph on what the adversarial student still fails to do. Then the section changes genre. "Adversarial Terms in Current Systems" and "The Objective without the Game" (about 1,300 words) name ten systems (ADD, DMD, DMD2, VQGAN, the Stable Diffusion autoencoder, ViTok-v2, HiFi-GAN, BigVGAN, RAF, DDO) with no code and no experiment, quote one paper's FID ablation to three digits, and explain diffusion distillation to a reader who will not meet a diffusion model until the next chapter. So the answer to "does it teach or survey" is: it teaches, then surveys, and the survey is better organized than most (one principle, "ambiguous output and one pass", predicts each case) but it is still a survey. The most important thing to fix: carry the central claim of the second half, that a one-step student regressed onto a denoiser's output inherits the average and a critic repairs it, on the two bands in the text (exercise 3b already contains the construction), and cut the system list to what that demonstration needs. Second: the chapter's last word on where GANs stand is a list of four limits that omits what GANs still do better and rests one of the four on a single 2026 preprint.

## What works

- **The opening paragraph.** "the same face with its hair falling slightly differently, the same lawn with its blades of grass elsewhere" and "that mean is a blur that matches none of them". October singled this out to keep; I agree.
- **Two bands.** "The conditional mean $\mu(c) = c$ runs through the empty corridor between them" and "Squared error prefers the corridor, where no data point lies." The example is as small as it can be, the two risks are computed by hand before any code, and the figure shows the result at a glance. This is the MacKay move; keep every sentence of the paragraph that follows :eqref:`eq_beyond_twoband`.
- **The noise injection is explained, not hidden**: "so the two distributions differ in where they put their mass, not in how smooth they are". A detail that decides whether the experiment works, with its reason.
- **"Ambiguity and Capacity", first paragraph.** "The adversarial student does not recover the conditional distribution ... The missing ingredient is information." It refuses to oversell the critic and says exactly what is missing. The exercise on a latent bit follows it up.
- **The phase calculation.** "a sine of the same frequency whose amplitude has shrunk by the factor $\sin a / a$ ... With the phase entirely unknown, $a = \pi$, the prediction is silence." One formula carries the vocoder case. It is the only system in the second half that is derived instead of reported.
- **DMD in one sentence**: "the input gradient of the optimal log-loss critic between the noised data $p_t$ and the noised samples $q_t$. DMD therefore follows the direction a perfect critic would give, without a classifier." Correct, and it ties a 2024 method to :eqref:`eq_gan_dstar`.
- **The stored outputs support the prose**: 0.98 and 0.02 for the squared-error student, 0.02 and 0.05 from the band and 2% and 0% off the bands for the adversarial student.

## Structural findings

1. **Teach the second half on the two bands, or shorten it to a guided pointer.** The claim "A one-step student ... trained by squared error toward the teacher's denoised estimate at high noise ... inherits the average" is the reason the section exists in a 2026 book, and it is supported only by a number from the ADD paper (FID 315.6). The reader can check it in forty lines with what the section has already built: the posterior mean of the band given a noised prediction is $c + \tanh((\tilde x - c)/(\tau^2 + 0.05^2))$ (exercise 3b), so a "teacher" is a closed form, and three students (regression onto the teacher's estimate at high noise, the critic alone, both) reproduce the ADD ablation in miniature: the first goes to the corridor, the other two stay on a band. Bring that into the text as the section's second experiment. Then ADD needs two sentences and no FID values.

2. **Too many systems at equal weight.** Ten named systems in 900 words, each with one to three sentences. By the brief's own test (lists of variants at equal weight, paper retelling), cut to the three that each stand for a kind (one-step distillation, autoencoders, vocoders), with one named example each, and move DMD2, RAF, BigVGAN and the ViTok-v2 detail to `further-reading.md`, which already holds most of them.

3. **DDO is out of proportion.** "Likelihood Models as Critics" gives a 2025 method a subsection, a labelled display equation and two exercises (6 and 8, the second the only two-dot exercise of the section). Its idea is neat and correctly described, but it is not something the reader builds on in this book (the diffusion chapter never returns to it; `further-reading.md` already has it), and the reader has not yet met a likelihood-based generator to which it applies. Reduce to a paragraph or move it and exercise 8 to further reading; keep exercise 6, which is short and instructive.

4. **The reader has not met diffusion.** "Denoisers as Conditional Means" must introduce denoiser, noise level, many-step sampler, teacher, student and (in the next subsection) score, in 150 words, one chapter early. It mostly manages, because :eqref:`eq_beyond_condmean` does the work, but none of the three subsections points forward to where the reader will learn this: there is no reference to `sec_diffusion-denoising`, `sec_diffusion-few-step` or `sec_diffusion-latent`, only one to `chap_diffusion` in the last sentence. The diffusion chapter cites this section four times (`denoising.md`, `few-step.md` twice, `latent-diffusion.md`); the links should run both ways.

5. **The chapter's ending.** "Remaining Limits" is the last prose of the chapter's teaching sections. See ADV-17 and ADV-18: it opens with a recap, lists four limits, and stops. It does not tell the reader what GANs are still for, why diffusion displaced them, or how to choose. The chapter introduction says it in one clause ("have since replaced stand-alone GANs for most image synthesis"); the ending should say it in a paragraph.

6. **Section title against content.** The title promises adversarial losses outside GANs; the first half is about why squared error blurs. That is the right content. The index states the question as "Why does squared error blur an ambiguous prediction where a learned comparison does not", which is a better description than the title. No change needed, but the reviser should let that question, not the list of systems, decide what stays.

## Passage-level findings

**ADV-1** (moderate, correctness). Anchor: "for house prices or class probabilities that was the right choice", introduction.
The book did not train class probabilities with squared error; it used cross-entropy from the softmax section on. A reader who remembers that stumbles on the first sentence. Fix: "Almost every regressor in this book was trained with squared error, and for house prices that was the right choice."

**ADV-2** (minor, thought). Anchor: "Pix2pix, in the previous section, had a paired target for every input and still added a critic to its $L_1$ loss", introduction.
The previous section gave pix2pix four sentences and the same explanation ("the $L_1$ term alone blurs it"). One of the two should own it; I recommend this section (see the conditional review, structural finding 4). Note also that the section then argues about squared error while pix2pix uses $L_1$; exercise 2 covers the median, but the text never says in one sentence that $L_1$ replaces the mean by the median and that the median of an ambiguous posterior is also not, in general, a sharp image.

**ADV-3** (minor, explanation). Anchor: "given $c$, the second factor is fixed and the first has mean zero", under "The Conditional-Mean Decomposition".
Fine for scalars. The reader is thinking of images; say once that for a vector $x$ the square is a squared norm and the argument holds coordinate by coordinate.

**ADV-4** (minor, explanation). Anchor: "It is strongly negative where predictions land with no data near them", under "The Conditional-Mean Decomposition".
This is the hinge between the two losses, and it goes by quickly. Say what the critic does in the corridor in the two-band language one paragraph early, or move this paragraph after the two bands are defined, where "an empty region between valid answers" has a picture. As ordered, the abstraction precedes its example by 150 words.

**ADV-5** (minor, pedagogy; author decision 4). Anchor: "so the last steps average out the minibatch noise and the curve settles where :eqref:`eq_beyond_condmean` says it should", under "Two Bands".
The squared-error student is a confirmation of a two-line identity, and its three printed numbers (0.98, 0.02, 100%) could not have come out otherwise. This is acceptable once, because the student is the control for the adversarial one and the plot needs its curve. But do not present it as a finding: shorten the reading ("as :eqref:`eq_beyond_condmean` predicts") to a clause and spend the words on the adversarial student, whose behaviour was not derived.

**ADV-6** (minor, pedagogy). Anchor: "other seeds change which band it follows and where it crosses, not these numbers", under "Two Bands".
Good practice (a range across seeds). One thing a careful reader will ask and the text does not answer: why does the adversarial student ever cross, and why does the critic not push the whole curve onto one band? (A curve on one band matches half the data everywhere; a crossing costs only in a narrow interval; neither is the optimum of the game, which a deterministic student cannot reach.) Two sentences here would connect to the next subsection.

**ADV-7** (moderate, thought). Anchor: "In the second, the input determines the output but the model cannot compute the map in one pass", under "Ambiguity and Capacity".
The section offers two sources of averaging and then uses them inconsistently for the one-step student. Here the student's averaging is a capacity effect: "The noise vector of a one-step student fixes its teacher's image, but a student that cannot reproduce the teacher's long computation sees several inputs as alike." In "Denoisers as Conditional Means" it is an ambiguity effect: the regression target is a denoised estimate, which is itself a conditional mean. These are two different distillation setups (regression onto the teacher's sampled output for the same noise, and regression onto a denoiser's estimate at a noise level, as in ADD), and the reader is not told that. "sees several inputs as alike" is also not an argument: why would a limited network average outputs instead of making some other error? Fix: name the two setups. For the first, give the reason (a network with limited capacity fits a smooth function to a very irregular map from noise to image, and squared error makes the smoothing an average over nearby noise vectors). For the second, say that the target is already an average, so capacity is beside the point. Then state which one ADD's 315.6 belongs to (the second).

**ADV-8** (moderate, correctness). Anchor: "low distortion and outputs distributed like the data cannot both be reached", under "Ambiguity and Capacity".
Overstated. Blau and Michaeli show a tradeoff: the lowest achievable distortion rises as the output distribution is constrained toward the data's, and for squared error the price of a perfect output distribution is at most a factor of two (3 dB), attained at worst by the posterior sampler of the previous sentence. Exercise 1c of this very section exhibits a deterministic predictor whose outputs are distributed like the data at 1.0025 times the minimum error. The conclusion "so a realistic output must be judged by whether it looks like data, not by its squared error" does not follow from the theorem either. Fix: "Blau and Michaeli showed that the conflict is general and bounded: forcing the outputs to be distributed like the data raises the smallest achievable squared error, by at most a factor of two. A system that wants realistic outputs must therefore accept a worse squared error, and squared error alone cannot rank such systems." (The citation key says 2017, the arXiv date; the paper appeared at CVPR 2018.)

**ADV-9** (minor, correctness). Anchor: "A many-step sampler takes only a small step toward that mean, adds fresh noise, and asks again at a lower noise level", under "Denoisers as Conditional Means".
True of ancestral samplers. The flow models cited in the same paragraph, and the deterministic samplers of `sec_diffusion-samplers`, add no noise. Fix: "takes only a small step toward that mean (some samplers then add fresh noise) and asks again at a lower noise level". Add the forward reference to `sec_diffusion-denoising` here. The description of the denoiser as predicting $x_0$ agrees with the diffusion chapter's own framing ("one network that predicts the clean point from a noisy one").

**ADV-10** (minor, explanation). Anchor: "$\sin(\omega t + \vartheta)$ with the phase $\vartheta$ uniform", under "Tokenizers and Vocoders"; and "noise level $t$" two subsections earlier.
$t$ is a noise level in "Denoisers as Conditional Means" and "One-Step Distillation" ($p_t$, $q_t$) and a time index in the waveform; exercise 3b then uses $\tau$ for a noise level and exercise 2 uses $t$ for a threshold. Use $n$ for the sample index of the waveform.

**ADV-11** (moderate, pedagogy, paper retelling; numbers unverified). Anchor: "the regression alone gives FID 315.6, the adversarial loss alone 20.8, and both together 20.6", under "One-Step Distillation".
A paper's ablation table in prose, repeated in exercise 3. I recall these values from the ADD paper as plausible (in particular the distillation-only failure above 300) but cannot confirm the digits from certain knowledge; a reviser should check them against the paper's ablation table. Whatever the digits, the text should not carry them: with the two-band experiment of structural finding 1 the section shows the effect itself, and ADD is then cited as "the same ordering at scale: unusable, good, slightly better". Undefined on first use in this subsection: DINOv2, CLIP score (glossed, good), "frozen". The description of ADD (score-distillation regression onto a frozen teacher's denoised estimate of a noised student sample, plus a critic on frozen DINOv2 features with trainable heads) is correct.

**ADV-12** (minor, correctness and missed connection). Anchor: "Two diffusion models supply the scores on noised copies of $x'$: the frozen teacher for the data", under "One-Step Distillation".
Two points. (i) The teacher supplies the score of the teacher's distribution, not of the data; that is why "DMD2 adds a critic against real images" matters (and why `few-step.md` says a student "can surpass its teacher" only "with real images in the loss"). Say "for the data, as far as the teacher has learned it". (ii) The direction $\nabla \log(p_t/q_t)$ is the gradient of the reverse divergence $D_{\textrm{KL}}(q_t \,\|\, p_t)$, the mode-seeking one that `subsec_gan_mode_dropping` studied; DMD's first version added a regression term on precomputed teacher pairs partly for that reason. One sentence would tie DMD to the chapter's mode-coverage thread, which this section otherwise drops.

**ADV-13** (moderate, correctness of emphasis). Anchor: "add a critic that scores image patches, because the code does not fix the exact texture at each location", under "Tokenizers and Vocoders".
VQGAN and the Stable Diffusion autoencoder train with three terms: a pixel reconstruction loss, a perceptual (LPIPS) loss on fixed features, and the patch critic. The text mentions only the critic and then introduces the perceptual loss as what ViTok-v2 put in its place, which suggests that perceptual losses are a 2026 replacement for critics. They are older than these autoencoders and have always accompanied the critic. Both `further-reading.md` ("VQGAN combined reconstruction and perceptual losses with a patch critic") and `latent-diffusion.md` ("add a patch critic and a perceptual loss") say it correctly. Fix: state the three terms and the job of each in the section's own language (pixels: the conditional mean; perceptual: a conditional mean in a feature space where textures that differ in pixels are close; critic: a learned comparison that rejects the residual blur), then say ViTok-v2 dropped the third. Name the patch critic (PatchGAN) since pix2pix introduced it and the chapter has used pix2pix twice.

**ADV-14** (moderate, correctness, unverified; over-reliance on one source). Anchor: "reporting that adversarial losses prevented stable scaling", under "Tokenizers and Vocoders".
ViTok-v2 (arXiv 2605.05331 in `d2l.bib`) and RAF (Interspeech 2026) are at or past the edge of what I can confirm; I cannot verify the five-billion-parameter figure, the DINOv3 perceptual loss, or the reported reason. More important than the digits: this single preprint is cited four times in the section and carries one of the chapter's four closing limits ("the game can still become unstable at scale, as ViTok-v2 reported"), which the diffusion chapter's introduction then adopts as a premise ("instability at scale"). One paper's remark about its own training runs is thin support for a limit of a method, especially since the same paragraph family (StyleGAN-T, GigaGAN, R3GAN) trained adversarially at scale. Fix: support the limit with the broader record (BigGAN's reported collapse and early stopping, the engineering needed by GigaGAN and StyleGAN-T, and ViTok-v2 as the recent instance), and soften "shows that they can block scaling" to "reports".

**ADV-15** (minor, explanation). Anchor: "A perceptual loss is still squared error, but on features that change little across the textures the code leaves open, so averaging them does not blur the pixels", under "Tokenizers and Vocoders".
The best sentence of the subsection and too compressed. "averaging them" (the features) "does not blur the pixels" skips the step: the decoder is asked for an image whose features equal the mean feature, and many sharp images have that feature, so a sharp one satisfies the loss. Spell it out in two sentences, and note the limit (features that are invariant to texture cannot tell a good texture from a bad one, which is why the critic was kept for so long).

**ADV-16** (moderate, thought). Anchor: "The second constrains the critic, by bounding its slope, by freezing its features as ADD does, or by augmenting both of its inputs", under "Responses to Instability"; and :numref:`fig_gan_exits`.
The taxonomy does not separate its first two classes: a zero-centered gradient penalty (response 1) is a bound on the critic's slope (response 2). The figure adds three problems. (i) Its first row labels RpGAN a "convergent objective", while `sec_gan_relativistic` ends by saying of pairing that "it does not make gradient training converge"; the convergence comes from the penalties. (ii) The arrows inside each row (spectral norm → frozen pretrained features → controlled augmentation) read as a pipeline or a history, and the three are unrelated alternatives. (iii) IMLE and ADA appear in the figure and nowhere in the section's text (IMLE only in exercise 7), and the caption's "corresponding to different sources of instability" promises sources the text never names. Fix: define the classes by what is changed (the update rule's fixed point and its stability; the function class of the critic; the presence of a second player), fix the RpGAN label, replace arrows by a plain grid, and either name the sources or drop the phrase. Consider whether the figure and this subsection are needed at all: the same three-way comparison is the table `tab_gan_games` at the end of `sec_gan_convergence` plus one row.

**ADV-17** (moderate, pedagogy; author decisions 2 and 6). Anchor: "The chapter began with a game whose generator could lose its gradient, whose training could circle without converging", under "Remaining Limits".
Half of this paragraph is a recap of the chapter ("The repairs work. The non-saturating loss keeps a gradient ... With these, a plain convolutional generator learns to draw images"), placed before the Summary, and the paragraph before it ("The adversarial objective thus needs an estimate of $\log(p/q)$ or of its gradient ... Vocoders and ADD keep that game, ViTok-v2 drops the objective, DMD estimates ... and DDO represents ...") is a recap of the section that the Summary's last sentence repeats. The section ends three times. Fix: delete the recap sentences; let "Remaining Limits" start at "Four limits remain".

**ADV-18** (major, thought and currency). Anchor: "Diffusion and flow models, the subject of :numref:`chap_diffusion`, avoid the game", last sentence of "Remaining Limits".
This is where the chapter tells the reader where GANs stand, and it tells half. What it says is accurate and matches the diffusion chapter exactly: `chapter_diffusion-models/index.md` restates the four limits ("no density, two networks and a tuned penalty, no cost for a missing mode, and instability at scale") and `discrete-diffusion.md` closes by answering them one by one, and `chap_diffusion` is a real label. What it omits:
- **What GANs keep.** One network evaluation per sample. The whole middle of this section exists because diffusion is slow to sample and distillation buys the speed back with a critic; `few-step.md` opens with "the generator of a GAN ... needed one". The limits paragraph never states the advantage.
- **Why they were displaced.** Not instability as such: on broad, multi-modal targets (text-to-image) regression-trained models covered the distribution and scaled with data and parameters with little tuning, and guidance gave a simple control of fidelity against diversity; GANs remained competitive on narrow domains and at low step counts. The paragraph gives limits of the method but not this judgment.
- **A caveat on the diffusion side.** "trained by regression against closed-form targets" is presented as pure gain. The price (many evaluations, hence the return of the critic in distillation) is in the figure caption ("Adversarial losses can reappear when non-adversarial models are distilled to a few steps") and not in the text.
- **Guidance for the reader.** When would one train a GAN in 2026? (A decoder or vocoder that must answer in one pass; a one- to four-step student; a small domain with a real-time budget.) One sentence.
Fix: replace the last sentence by a short paragraph in the authors' voice that gives the verdict, for example: "Stand-alone GANs are no longer how images are generated from text: models trained by regression (:numref:`chap_diffusion`) cover broad distributions and scale without a game. What the game still buys is a sample in one pass, which is why a critic remains in decoders, vocoders and few-step students (:numref:`sec_diffusion-few-step`), and why the limits below are worth knowing." Then the four limits. Also qualify "No likelihood term penalizes a missing mode" against the diffusion chapter's answer ("a likelihood under which a dropped mode costs on every one of its points"), which is a statement about the objective and not a guarantee about samples.

**ADV-19** (minor, thought). Anchor: "the guarantee against mode-dropping minima holds only for a fixed one-to-one pairing of real and generated samples", under "Remaining Limits".
Correct and consistent with the summary of `sec_gan_relativistic` ("pairs redrawn at every step average back to the all-pairs loss"). But the reader has just trained two models with the pairing loss and redrawn pairs (`sec_dcgan`, `sec_gan_conditional`), and should be told plainly that those runs did not have the guarantee. One clause: "which none of our image experiments used".

**ADV-20** (minor, language). Pattern: abstract nouns doing the work of a claim in the second half, 4 instances: "The argument predicts where an adversarial term should survive", "We follow that prediction into three kinds of systems", "Methods respond in three ways", "the third separates the adversarial objective from the game that usually optimizes it". Each announces instead of saying. The first half has none of this. Rewrite as statements of content ("A critic survives where the output is ambiguous given the input and the model must answer in one pass: in one-step students, in decoders, in vocoders.").

## Exercises

Eight exercises, and collectively they are better than the second half of the text: 1 (the factor two of the posterior sampler, and a deterministic predictor with the data's distribution), 2 (mean against median, with a number to compute; I checked that the median offset for $r = \tfrac34$ is 0.98), 4 (phase, including the boundary discontinuity that motivates what a critic must see) and 5 (frozen features and why they can lower FID without improving samples) are excellent. I checked the algebra of 3b (the $\tanh$ posterior mean), 6b (the gradient is half the likelihood gradient) and 8a (minimizer proportional to $q_{\textrm{ref}}^{1-1/\beta} p^{1/\beta}$; the rounds contract only for $\beta > \tfrac12$): all correct.

- Exercise 3b should move into the text (structural finding 1); 3a can stay.
- Exercise 8 is a research-level study of one 2025 paper and ends by asking the reader to guess what in the authors' setting differs. Move to further reading or cut; it is the place where the section's proportion problem is most visible.
- Exercise 7b introduces IMLE, which appears in :numref:`fig_gan_exits` and nowhere in the text. Fine as an exercise once the figure is fixed.
- Missing: nothing on autoencoders. One exercise on a toy "texture" (for instance a decoder that must output a random-phase sinusoid patch from a code that fixes only frequency, trained with pixel loss, with a loss on Fourier magnitudes as the "perceptual" loss, and with a critic) would do for the tokenizer what exercise 4 does for the vocoder, and would make ADV-15 concrete.

## For the chapter review

- **Order and role.** Right place and the right closing question. The section depends on `sec_basic_gan` (optimal critic, non-saturating loss, `make_mlp`) and `sec_gan_convergence` (saved losses) only, so its first half could be read straight after the first section; it needs no appendix. It is the hinge to chapter 17, which opens by citing it ("The previous chapter ended with a fact about squared error").
- **Forward references.** `chap_diffusion` exists and the diffusion chapter says the same things this section says (the four limits, the conditional mean, the critic in one-step students, the patch critic in autoencoders). The references are one-directional: add `sec_diffusion-denoising`, `sec_diffusion-few-step` and `sec_diffusion-latent` here. The one inconsistency between the two chapters is the role of the perceptual loss (ADV-13).
- **Overlap.** With `further-reading.md` (tokenizers, DDO, vocoders: three paragraphs there cover the same systems with numbers), with the end of `sec_gan_convergence` (the table of games against :numref:`fig_gan_exits`), and with `conditional.md` (pix2pix). Give the systems to further reading and keep the argument here.
- **The ending of the chapter** should be a verdict, not a list (ADV-18). As written, a reader finishes chapter 16 knowing four things wrong with GANs and not the one thing right with them that the next chapter spends a section trying to recover.
- **Suspected pattern for the chapter and part:** sections rebuilt in 2026 end with a tour of 2024 to 2026 papers (here two of them from 2026, one a preprint). These will date faster than anything else in the book. A rule for the part: a system is named in a teaching section only if the section derives or runs the idea it stands for; otherwise it goes to further reading.
