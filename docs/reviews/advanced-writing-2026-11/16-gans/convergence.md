## Verdict

The first third of this section meets the bar. The Dirac-GAN is set up in ten lines, the orthogonality argument gives the circle without solving anything, Pythagoras gives the outward spiral, and one changed matrix entry gives the damped spring. Everything in it recomputes. After that the section loses its line. "The Penalized Value Near Equilibrium" changes the question (from "does training converge" to "what does the penalized game measure"), raises the level sharply (an operator inverse, a dual Sobolev norm, a Fourier argument), and its conclusion, that the penalty keeps a signal on separated supports, is stated more strongly in the body than the section's own exercise 2(c) allows. The closing experiment then does not show the section's subject: the unpenalized GAN on 25 Gaussians does not fail to converge (its samples are as sharp as R3GAN's in both stored runs), the arms confound pairing with the penalty, and the one difference claimed needs "about 150 runs" that the reader does not have. The most important fix is the experiment and the claims around it: a reader should finish with the Dirac-GAN picture confirmed on something larger than two parameters, and at present the section proves convergence on the toy and shows mode coverage on the grid. The second fix is to state the general local-convergence theorem with its hypotheses and its scope; today it is one unqualified clause.

## What works

- **"Circles in Continuous Time".** "Both components are the same positive scalar $\ell'(-\psi\theta)$ times the vector $(\psi, -\theta)$, which is orthogonal to the position" is the whole proof, and it holds for every loss at once. Giving the conservation law before the Jacobian is the right order: purely imaginary eigenvalues alone decide nothing (see CONV-5), and the section does not lean on them.
- **"Outward Spirals in Discrete Time".** One line of Pythagoras, "each step adds $h^2\|\mathbf{u}\|^2$ to the squared distance", and "a smaller step only slows the outward spiral". Protect both sentences.
- **The damped spring.** Three regimes, critical damping at $\gamma = 1$ for the logistic loss, the slow eigenvalue $-\ell'(0)^2/\gamma$ when overdamped. This tells the reader that more penalty is not better, which pays off in the experiment.
- **The remark that the common factor is the saturating weight**, "it sets how fast the parameters turn and not where they go". This connects the dynamics to :numref:`sec_basic_gan` in one sentence.
- **$R_1 + R_2 = \gamma\,\mathbb{E}_m\|\nabla_x D\|^2$**, and the two-row table of one-centered against zero-centered penalties. Both are small and exact.
- **Implementation detail that matters**: "each score depends only on its own row, so one backward pass gives every sample's input gradient (batch normalization, which mixes rows, would break this)".
- **"$\gamma$ is a squared length, set by the scale of the data"**, with exercise 4(a). This is the one thing a practitioner needs to know about $\gamma$.
- **Exercises 2, 3, 5, 8 and 9** are research-grade and correct. Exercise 9 ($r^2 \approx 8/t$, cycle of radius $\sqrt{2h}$) reproduces to three digits; exercise 5's alternating iteration stays in a band (0.134 to 0.141 from $(0.1, 0.1)$ at $h = 0.2$ over $10^5$ steps) as its part (a) predicts.
- The phase-portrait figure is clear. "after 300 steps their distance from the equilibrium has doubled" is right (ratio 2.03).

## Structural findings

1. **Three sections in one file.** (i) Dynamics: Dirac-GAN, penalties, phase portraits, one-centered, one or two (about 1,500 words). (ii) What the penalized value measures (about 700 words plus a cell). (iii) R3GAN in code, the 25-Gaussians experiment, and a chapter-wide table (about 1,500 words). Part (i) answers the question in the title. Part (ii) answers a different question at a higher level. Part (iii) saves library code for the next two sections. If the section is to be split, the seam is before "The Penalized Value Near Equilibrium": part (i) plus the code and the experiment make "Gradient Penalties and Convergence"; part (ii) with "The Games of This Chapter" becomes a short optional section "What the Penalized Game Measures", or part (ii) moves to the appendix next to :numref:`sec_mdl-ot-connection` and the main text keeps its result and the Gaussian-shift example. I recommend the second: by the author's rule, a derivation of this weight that the rest of the chapter uses only through "cutoff length $2\sqrt\gamma$" belongs in the appendix or in an exercise, with the result stated here.
2. **The section should cite the appendix and currently does not.** `chapter_mdl-dynamics/mdl-odes-solvers.md` has what this section assumes: "Stability from Eigenvalues" with the six phase portraits including the center (:numref:`sec_mdl-linear-odes-stability`), "Linearization at Fixed Points" (:numref:`subsec_mdl-ode-linearization`), whose worked example is the damped pendulum with characteristic polynomial $\lambda^2 + c\lambda + 1$, the same polynomial as :eqref:`eq_gan_dirac_pen` with $\ell'(0) = 1$, and whose last paragraph says "When an eigenvalue lies on the imaginary axis, the linearization decides nothing"; and the stability region of forward Euler (:numref:`sec_mdl-stiffness-implicit`, :numref:`sec_mdl-gd-as-solver`), which is why the simultaneous step spirals out on the imaginary axis while the RK4 "flow" panel closes. The main text must stay readable alone, so the fix is one restated clause per fact plus the pointer (CONV-5), not a dependency.
3. **The general theorem is absent.** The section proves local convergence for the two-parameter game and then speaks as if it held for networks ("The penalties make the equilibrium locally attracting"). See CONV-7.
4. **The body overstates what the penalty does on disjoint supports**, and an exercise corrects it. See CONV-10.
5. **The experiment tests coverage, not convergence**, and cannot attribute its result. See CONV-14.
6. **A paper's ablation carries the main-text argument for "two penalties".** The StackedMNIST table is quoted, then invoked three more times. See CONV-9.
7. **Two endings.** After the experiment come "The Games of This Chapter" (a recap table for four sections, "Four sections have now derived"), "Reading the Table" (which restates results), a handoff sentence, and the Summary. See CONV-16.
8. **What a reader will look for and not find.** (a) The words "bilinear game": linearized, the Dirac-GAN is $\min_\theta \max_\psi \theta\psi$, the standard example of the min-max literature, and a reader who meets extragradient or optimistic methods elsewhere will not connect them. One clause. (b) What alternating updates do, in the main text, since every training loop in the chapter alternates (CONV-4). (c) Why R3GAN sets $\beta_1 = 0$; the answer is exercise 8 and deserves a sentence. (d) What the penalty costs (a second backward pass, roughly doubling the critic step); the next section says so, and lazy regularization is in the further-reading file.

A good opening: it restates the unsettled losses of the garment run and names the gap between value and dynamics. **CONV-2** (minor, correctness). "The Game on Two Points" and "Circles in Continuous Time". Anchors: "Any differentiable loss with $\ell'(0) > 0$ works." and "Every trajectory is a circle around the solution, for every loss in this family."
The conservation law holds for any differentiable $\ell$, but a trajectory is a full circle only if $\ell'(-\psi\theta)$ stays positive along it; where $\ell'$ has a zero the trajectory is an arc that stops there. "The same positive scalar" assumes $\ell' > 0$ everywhere. Fix: "Any differentiable increasing loss works" (true of every loss in :numref:`sec_gan_objectives` except those with a flat part, which deserve the clause "a hinge stops where it is flat").

**CONV-3** (minor, thought). "Circles in Continuous Time". Anchor: "so no reweighting of this kind turns the circle into a spiral toward the solution."
The next paragraph but one says that the non-saturating generator, which is a reweighting, "gains a slow inward drift". "Of this kind" has to carry the difference. Fix: "so no factor common to both players' updates turns the circle into a spiral. A factor on one player alone can: see the non-saturating generator below."

**CONV-4** (moderate, explanation). "Outward Spirals in Discrete Time". Anchor: "Updating the two players in turn instead of simultaneously does not restore convergence either"
This is all the main text says about alternating updates, and `train_grid`, `train_gan` and `train_r3gan` all alternate. "Every step from any other point moves away from the solution" is therefore a statement about a method the book never runs. What alternating steps do is different and worth one sentence: at the equilibrium the update matrix has determinant one and eigenvalues on the unit circle for $h\,\ell'(0) < 2$, so the iterates neither approach nor leave; numerically they stay in a narrow band (from $(1, 1)$ at $h = 0.2$ the distance stays between 1.34 and 1.42 for 300 steps, against a doubling for simultaneous steps). Fix: state this (it is Mescheder et al.'s lemma on alternating gradient descent, cited from memory) and add the alternating trajectory to the middle panel of the phase portrait. That gives the cell something the derivation above it did not, which also answers the "cells that only confirm" rule. Name the linearized game once: "the bilinear game $\min_\theta\max_\psi \theta\psi$".

**CONV-5** (moderate, explanation and correctness). "Zero-Centered Penalties", first sentence, and "Circles in Continuous Time". Anchors: "The equilibrium attracts the iterates only if the eigenvalues of the linearized dynamics have negative real parts." and "the equilibrium is a center, not an attractor."
(a) "Only if" is false as written. Negative real parts are sufficient and give a linear rate; they are not necessary. The section's own exercise 9 is the counterexample: the non-saturating flow has the same imaginary eigenvalues and converges, with $r^2 \approx 8/t$. (b) A reader from Basics has not seen linearization at a fixed point, "center", or "attractor"; the terms arrive undefined and uncited. Fix: "Near a fixed point the dynamics follow the Jacobian $J$: if every eigenvalue of $J$ has negative real part, trajectories approach at a linear rate; if they lie on the imaginary axis, the linear part only rotates and the outcome is decided by higher-order terms (:numref:`subsec_mdl-ode-linearization`). Here the conservation law has already decided it: closed orbits." Point to the damped pendulum there when introducing :eqref:`eq_gan_dirac_pen`; "As for a damped spring" then has a referent the reader can look up. Say in one clause why the "flow" panel closes although it also takes discrete steps (RK4's stability region covers part of the imaginary axis, Euler's does not).

**CONV-6** (minor, explanation). "Zero-Centered Penalties". Anchor: "and let the critic maximize $V_\ell(D) - R_1 - R_2$ while the generator's objective stays unchanged"
The citation `Roth.Lucchi.Nowozin.ea.2017,Mescheder.Geiger.Nowozin.2018` attaches both papers to this form. Unverified from memory, believed: Roth et al. derive a penalty weighted by $(1 - \sigma(D))^2$ and $\sigma(D)^2$ from adding noise to both distributions; the unweighted $R_1$ and $R_2$ and their names are Mescheder et al.'s simplification. One clause would keep the attribution exact. The same applies under "One Penalty or Two" to "showed that penalizing $\mathbb{E}_p\big[\|\nabla_x D\|^2\big]$ changes the game, to leading order, as if $p$ were convolved with Gaussian noise": the derivation runs from noise to penalty, for the weighted penalty, on both distributions.

**CONV-7** (major, correctness: missing hypotheses and scope). "Damping the Dirac-GAN", "One Penalty or Two", "The R3GAN Recipe". Anchors: "small enough gradient steps converge to it at a linear rate", "the local convergence result of :citet:`Mescheder.Geiger.Nowozin.2018` needs only one", "The penalties make the equilibrium locally attracting".
What is established where: for the Dirac-GAN the continuous-time claim is proved in the text (eigenvalues of $J_\gamma$), the discrete-time claim is exercise 1 (threshold $h < \gamma/\ell'(0)^2$ when underdamped; I confirm it: at $\gamma = 0.3$, $h = 1.1$ converges and $h = 1.3$ settles on a cycle of radius about 0.5). For networks nothing is proved and the theorem is only named. Its statement, from memory and with high confidence in the substance: for the classical GAN objective with $R_1$ or with $R_2$, simultaneous and alternating gradient descent with small enough step sizes converge locally, at a linear rate, to a manifold of equilibria, provided (i) the generator can represent the data and the critic is zero in a neighbourhood of the data support at the equilibrium (realizability); (ii) $\ell'(0) \neq 0$ and $\ell''(0) < 0$, which excludes the linear Wasserstein score; (iii) the critic class can detect every first-order departure of the generator from the equilibrium manifold; (iv) the equilibria form smooth manifolds. It is a local statement and says nothing about reaching the neighbourhood. Two consequences the section should carry: the theorem covers the classical loss with one penalty, not the pairing loss with two, for which Huang et al. give their own local result (their abstract claims it; I have not checked the proof); and "locally attracting" for a network is conditional on realizability, which no finite run satisfies exactly. Fix: a short stated theorem or a labelled paragraph, "**What is known beyond two parameters.**", with the hypotheses in words, the words "we do not prove this", and the scope. Then "The penalties make the equilibrium locally attracting" becomes "make the equilibrium locally attracting under these conditions".

**CONV-8** (minor, correctness). "One-Centered versus Zero-Centered". Anchor: "A penalty that prefers unit slope keeps pushing a generator that already matches the data"

**CONV-10** (major, correctness and thought). "One Penalty or Two", "A Low-Pass Filter", "The Games of This Chapter". Anchors: "Smoothed, two distributions with disjoint supports overlap, and the divergence responds to the distance between them.", "Below the cutoff the generator therefore keeps a signal when nearby supports move apart.", "smooth, near equilibrium", "The constraint decides whether a gradient survives on disjoint supports".
The body says, four times, that the penalty restores a gradient on separated supports. Exercise 2(c) asks the reader to prove the opposite for a free critic: with two point masses and any differentiable $D$ penalized by $\gamma\int m\|\nabla_x D\|^2$, the best-response value is $2\sup_t \ell(t)$ for every $\theta \neq 0$, because $m$ vanishes between the points and the critic may jump there at no cost. So the penalty alone gives no signal across a gap; the signal comes from the critic's restricted class (a linear critic in exercise 2(a), a smooth network in practice) together with the penalty. The local analysis hides this because it assumes $m > 0$ wherever $p - q$ is nonzero. The table entry "smooth, near equilibrium" in a column headed "on disjoint supports" is self-contradictory: near equilibrium the supports are not disjoint. For the experiment's data (width 0.05, spacing 2) the supports are disjoint for practical purposes. Fix: move the content of exercise 2(c) into the body as the stated limit: "The filter acts where $m$ has mass. Across a gap in $m$ the penalty costs nothing, and a critic free to jump there saturates as before. What keeps a gradient across the gap is that a network with a small input gradient at the samples is also smooth between them, an assumption about the critic, not a consequence of the penalty." Change the table entry to "smooth where $p$ and $q$ overlap; across gaps only through the critic's smoothness". Soften the Roth sentence to what the convolution picture gives.

**CONV-11** (moderate, explanation: level and order). "The Local Value". Anchor: "**Proposition.** *Let $\mathcal{L}_m D = -\nabla \cdot (m \nabla D)$. Near equilibrium, the penalized critic problem has the value*"
The mathematics is right (I rederived the expansion, the supremum $\tfrac{\ell'(0)^2}{4}\langle p - q, \mathcal{A}^{-1}(p - q)\rangle$, both limits and the Gaussian-shift solution). The problems are of level and of form. (a) Nothing earlier in the book prepares an operator $\mathcal{L}_m$, its inverse, integration by parts in function space, or a dual Sobolev norm; the proof says "$\mathcal{A}$ is symmetric and positive" and "integrating by parts" without boundary conditions (exercise 6(a) adds "assuming that boundary terms vanish"). (b) "Near equilibrium" inside a proposition is not a hypothesis. The statement is an exact identity for the quadratic functional on its left; the approximation was made in the paragraph before. (c) The abstraction comes before the example, and the example is good. (d) No source is given; if the computation is the book's own, say so, and if not, cite it. Fix: lead with the Gaussian shift, done by hand with a linear critic (three lines: both quadratic terms are $c^2$ times a constant, maximize a parabola in the slope $c$, get $\mu^2/(4 + 16\gamma)$ and the slope shrunk by $1 + 4\gamma$). State the general formula as a result "for the quadratic approximation", move the proof to exercise 6 or to the appendix, and keep the low-pass weight. The reader needs the sentence "the penalty shrinks the critic's slope by $1 + 4\gamma$ and the value with it", and can get it without $\dot H^{-1}$.

**CONV-12** (moderate, correctness: appendix pointer). "A Low-Pass Filter". Anchor: "which equals $W_2(p, q)$ to leading order as $q \to p$ by the Benamou--Brenier form of optimal transport (:numref:`sec_mdl-ot-connection`)"
The target subsection states the Benamou-Brenier theorem (for paths from noise to data, in the flow-matching setting) and defines $W_2$. It does not contain the step used here: freezing the density at $m$, so that the least kinetic energy that moves $p - q$ is $\int m\|\nabla\phi\|^2$ with $-\nabla\cdot(m\nabla\phi) = p - q$, which is the squared $\dot H^{-1}(m)$ norm. Nothing in the appendix mentions a Sobolev norm. The claim is true (a standard linearization) and is cited, not shown. Fix: add the one-line argument just given, mark it "we do not prove this", and cite a source for it next to the appendix pointer; or drop $W_2$ from the general statement and keep it for the Gaussian shift, where the text already verifies it ("since the Wasserstein-2 distance of a shift is $|\mu|$").

**CONV-13** (minor, pedagogy). "A Gaussian Shift", cell `convergence-a-gaussian-shift`. Anchor: "The local formula matches the exact value to within one percent at every $\gamma$."
True (stored: 9.950e-03 against 1.000e-02 at $\gamma = 0$, down to 2.438e-04 against 2.439e-04 at $\gamma = 10$), and "26 times too large" at $\gamma = 0.01$ is right. The check can fail, since it compares the quadratic approximation with the full problem, but at $\mu = 0.2$ it cannot fail by much, so it mostly confirms. It would teach more with the shift varied: I get the local formula 12% high at $\mu = 1$, $\gamma = 0$ (0.223 exact against 0.25), 4% high at $\mu = 1$, $\gamma = 1$, and more than twice too large at $\mu = 3$, $\gamma = 0$ (1.05 against 2.25, the exact value approaching its ceiling $2\log 2$). **CONV-14** (major, pedagogy: the experiment). "Mode Coverage on 25 Gaussians". Anchors: "The R3GAN loss at $\gamma = 1$ fits the grid.", "Over about 150 runs it covered all 25 modes in only about two runs out of five", "With a ten times weaker signal about where the modes sit, the $\gamma = 10$ generator should resolve the grid less sharply within the same budget."

(a) The experiment does not show the section's subject. The unpenalized GAN does not circle or diverge here: its on-mode fraction and distance equal those of R3GAN at $\gamma = 1$ in both tabs. What differs is how evenly it covers the modes, which the chapter attributes to pairing, not to the penalty.
(b) The arms cannot attribute the difference. GAN without a penalty against RpGAN with both penalties changes two things. The missing arms are GAN with $R_1 + R_2$ and RpGAN without a penalty. `dcgan.md` runs the controlled version; this section should too, since the 2-D runs are cheap.
(c) The headline comparison rests on "sixteen runs" and "about 150 runs" that no cell performs. Ranges are allowed by the author's rule, but here the reader's single run is the only evidence on the page and it is a coin flip ("about two runs out of five"). (d) The prediction for $\gamma = 10$ applies the Fourier argument outside both of its hypotheses: $m$ is not "roughly constant" (it is 25 spikes of width 0.05 with nothing between them) and the run does not start near equilibrium. Exercise 4(b) asks "Does the damping predict how sharply each mode is\n       resolved?" (the quote wraps a line; grep "Does the damping predict how sharply each mode is"), which suggests the authors know it does not: by the same formula $\gamma = 1$ already damps the mode width by a factor of about 60,000 and yet fits. Smaller points: "fits the grid" is generous for a distance of 0.11 to 0.15 against "about $0.06$ for a perfect fit" and an on-mode fraction of 0.8 to 0.9 against 0.989. The rendered scatter shows why: in every panel samples lie on lines between neighbouring centers, the mark of a continuous generator stretched over disconnected modes. A reader sees a lattice and the prose says nothing about it; one sentence would turn a puzzle into a lesson. Finally, a convergence section should show a trace: logging the critic loss or the on-mode fraction every few hundred steps for the four arms would show whether the unpenalized runs oscillate, which is the Dirac-GAN's prediction.
