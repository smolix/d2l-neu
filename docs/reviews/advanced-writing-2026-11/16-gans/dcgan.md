# Adversarial Image Generation

Source: `chapter_generative-adversarial-networks/dcgan.md` (lines 1 to 1129, up to the slides marker). About 4,200 words outside code, plus about 1,350 words of exercises, and 30 code cells per tab. Stored outputs read: `outputs/pytorch/.../dcgan.json` and `outputs/jax/.../dcgan.json`; the training traces (both tabs), the three-arm sample grid, the DCGAN samples and both architecture figures were rendered and inspected.

## Verdict

The section is honest about what is old and what is new, which answers the first question a reviewer would ask of a DCGAN section: the 2015 network is presented under the heading "The 2015 Recipe", the second network follows a 2024 paper, and the comparison between them is a designed experiment with one ingredient changed per arm. The metrics subsection is the best part: FID and KID are introduced as two closed forms the chapter already owns, the scores are renamed FD and $\mathrm{MMD}^2$ because the feature network is home-made, and a real-against-real floor gives the numbers a meaning. Two things keep the section from the bar. First, it does not read its own scores candidly. In both stored tabs the 2015 network, trained in "a minute or two", scores better than both arms of the modern recipe, trained for most of half an hour, on FD and by a factor of 1.7 to 5 on $\mathrm{MMD}^2$; the prose says it "lands close to them". The experiment can establish one thing, that the unnormalized backbone collapses without the penalties and trains with them, and the text builds more on it: a causal account of the collapse that uses the wrong weight for the arm it explains, and an attribution of DCGAN's success to "architecture" that no arm isolates. Second, the section is long and a third of its code and several of its paragraphs serve the next section. The most important fix is to state plainly what this experiment resolves and what it cannot, and to cut the explanations that the evidence does not carry.

## What works

- **The framing of the two problems** in the introduction: "a grid of samples reveals a collapse, but two good generators look alike to the eye." This motivates the metric before any formula.
- **The transposed convolution by stamping.** "the input $(1, 2)$ and a kernel of four ones with stride 2 give the stamps $(1, 1, 1, 1)$ and $(2, 2, 2, 2)$, two positions apart, so the output is $(1, 1, 3, 3, 2, 2)$". A small concrete case before the size formula; it checks.
- **Reasons attached to design choices**: tanh "matches its range to images scaled to $[-1, 1]$"; leaky ReLU "so that no unit stops passing gradient to the generator"; bilinear resampling against "the uneven overlap of transposed-convolution stamps"; no batch normalization because it "would make each critic score depend on the whole minibatch and couple the per-sample input gradients that the penalties measure"; the fan-in variance with the leaky-ReLU gain when nothing else rescales the activations.
- **The EMA sentence**: "The iterates of the game rotate around the equilibrium as they contract ... so their average sits closer to it than they do." It uses the previous section to explain a trick that most texts give as folklore.
- **The three-arm design**: "The first two arms isolate the penalty and the last two isolate pairing." This is the controlled comparison that `convergence.md` lacks.
- **"FID and KID from Two Closed Forms".** The one-dimensional case with numbers ($0.25 + 0.01 = 0.26$), the commuting case $\sum_i(\sqrt{a_i} - \sqrt{b_i})^2$, the remark that FID "sees only the first two moments" and is biased at finite $n$, and the unbiased estimate that "can be slightly negative" (stored: $-0.012$ and $-0.010$).
- **The floor and the renaming**: "Because the feature network is our own, we call the scores FD and $\mathrm{MMD}^2$ rather than FID and KID; they use the same formulas but cannot be compared with published values." This is the candour the voice guide asks for.
- **The argument for $32 \times 32$ features** ("A feature network can judge only images that resemble its training data") and the out-of-range column with its explanation (45% of real pixel values equal $+1$).
- **Exercises 2, 4, 5, 8 and 10** are excellent; see below.
- Both generated figures are clear and carry parameter counts.

## Structural findings

1. **Is the recipe current?** Yes, and it is dated correctly. DCGAN is labelled 2015 and its devices are given reasons. The second network follows R3GAN (2024), which is the right modern baseline for a book published in 2026 or 2027, and the chapter introduction already says that diffusion and flow models have replaced stand-alone GANs for most image synthesis. Two reservations. The network actually built is neither DCGAN nor R3GAN: it is "a reduced version of R3GAN's design without its residual blocks, grouped convolutions, and inverted bottlenecks", with an input stage of the book's own (DCG-6). And the section never says where a reader would use this in 2026; one sentence pointing to :numref:`sec_gan_beyond` (adversarial terms in distillation and tokenizers) would place it.
2. **Can sample quality be judged at this scale?** Only coarsely, and the text should say so. At 12,000 steps on $64 \times 64$ sprites every non-collapsed generator draws soft coloured blobs with outlines; none draws a recognizable sprite. The eye separates collapse from non-collapse and nothing else. The scores do the same: 500 samples, 128 features from a CIFAR-10 classifier with 78% accuracy, a floor of about 1.3 that is a quarter to a third of every good score, and a stated dependence on the feature network's seed ("less than a factor of 1.5") that is larger than the gap between the two penalized arms (4.2 against 5.7, and 4.3 against 5.1). See DCG-10 and DCG-11.
3. **Is quality measured or only looked at?** Measured, and with more care than most texts: two scores, a floor, a renaming, and exercises on bias, moments, and precision and recall. What is missing is the reading: the one ranking the scores give that the grids do not is DCGAN first, and the prose passes over it (DCG-10).
4. **The section is three topics**: the 2015 network (about 900 words), the normalization-free backbone and the three arms (about 1,700), and the metrics (about 1,100), with "Limited Data" (about 350) appended. It is the longest of the chapter with code. A split is awkward because the scores need the trained generators. I recommend cutting instead: "Limited Data" to one paragraph or to the further-reading file (DCG-12), the collapse explanation to what was observed (DCG-9), the conditional plumbing out of this section's code and prose (DCG-13), and the JAX determinism cell to a comment. If a split is wanted, the seam is before "Measuring Sample Quality", with the four generators saved to disk at the end of the first notebook.
5. **Three explanations rest on no arm of the experiment**: why DCGAN trains (DCG-4), why the unpenalized arm collapses (DCG-9), and that critics memorize on limited data (DCG-12).
6. **Compute.** "Twenty epochs take a minute or two on one GPU" and "under half an hour on one GPU", plus 15 epochs of CIFAR-10 for the feature network. This is the heaviest notebook of the chapter and is not feasible on a laptop CPU. Say so at the start, with the total, and say which part a CPU reader can run (the scoring functions on saved features).
7. **What a reader will look for and not find.** (a) Latent interpolation, the standard demonstration that a generator has learned a continuous map and not a lookup table; one cell with `tile_images`. (b) Any statement of how many samples published FID uses (50,000), without which "their values depend ... on $n$" has no scale. (c) One clause in the main text that current papers report the Fréchet distance in DINOv2 features beside Inception FID; the further-reading file has it.

## Passage-level findings

**DCG-1** (minor, language: plumbing first). Before the imports, JAX tab. Anchor: "# Deterministic XLA kernels. On an accelerator XLA otherwise picks each"
The first thing a JAX reader meets is a five-line comment on kernel autotuning and `XLA_FLAGS`. The guide puts framework plumbing in comments, but not ahead of the section. Reduce to one line ("deterministic GPU kernels, so that the three arms below start from identical arithmetic") or move the flag into the build environment. Related, three comments address the authors, not the reader: "import warnings  # not in the d2l library preamble", "from PIL import Image  # neither is in the d2l library preamble", "import copy  # the d2l library preamble does not import it".

**DCG-2** (minor, language: terminology). "The 2015 Recipe". Anchor: "and its discriminator shrinks one with strided convolutions"
:numref:`sec_basic_gan` announces "We call it the *critic*". This subsection says "discriminator" for the DCGAN network (text, `D_block` docstring, figure) and "critic" for the same network two paragraphs later ("the critic's loss is far below $\log 2$"), and the saved class of the modern network is `Discriminator`. Either say once "DCGAN's discriminator, our critic" or use one word.

**DCG-3** (minor, correctness; confident). "Training with the Classic Loss". Anchor: "We use the DCGAN settings: minibatches of 256 images with the incomplete last one dropped"
Radford et al. trained with minibatches of 128. The learning rate, $\beta_1 = 0.5$, the leaky slope 0.2 and the $\mathcal{N}(0, 0.02^2)$ weights are theirs; the $\mathcal{N}(1, 0.02^2)$ batch-normalization scales come from the reference implementations, not the paper (unverified, believed). Fix: "the DCGAN settings, with a larger minibatch". The network is also narrower than the paper's (512 channels at $4 \times 4$ against 1,024), which the figure caption could note.

**DCG-4** (moderate, thought: unsupported attribution). "Training with the Classic Loss" and Summary. Anchors: "The architecture, not a change of loss, keeps this game trainable." and "DCGAN trained the classic loss to diverse sprites through its architecture: transposed convolutions, batch normalization, and Adam with $\beta_1 = 0.5$."
The DCGAN run and the unpenalized backbone run differ in batch normalization, initialization scale ($0.02$ against fan-in), tanh, $\beta_1$ (0.5 against 0), batch size (256 against 64), parameter count (6.3 million against 4.4 million) and resampling. No arm isolates any of them, and the Summary lists an optimizer setting under "architecture". The text also never says what batch normalization does for the game beyond "keeps the scale of the activations in range". Exercise 7 tests initialization only. Fix: either add the one arm that would carry the claim (the backbone with batch normalization in the generator and the classic loss, no penalty; or DCGAN with its normalization layers removed), or write what is known: "This network trains with the classic loss and the next one does not. They differ in normalization, initialization and optimizer settings; DCGAN's authors and later practice credit batch normalization most, and Exercise 7 tests the initialization."

**DCG-5** (minor, correctness: unverified paper claims). "Six Principles". Anchors: "(a) a convergent objective, in its experiments the pairing loss with both zero-centered penalties", "relied on about ten such devices; rebuilt from these principles without them, a network of about its size scored slightly better on photographs of faces"
From memory, the six items are those of the R3GAN paper's stripped baseline, and at that step of its roadmap the loss is still the classic one with $R_1$; the pairing loss with both penalties enters at the next step. "In its experiments" may therefore misplace (a). The claim about StyleGAN2 matches my recollection of the roadmap table (a small improvement in FID on FFHQ at $256 \times 256$) but I cannot confirm the numbers. This paragraph retells a paper; it is short and it motivates the next subsection, so it can stay, with (a) checked. The heading "Six Principles" promises more than a list from one paper; "What R3GAN Removed" would be accurate.

**DCG-6** (moderate, explanation). "The Backbone". Anchors: "We build a small network that follows principles (b) to (e) at sprite scale", "The generator starts from a learned $4 \times 4 \times 128$ constant, which holds what all samples share."
(a) The network also uses the constant input of principle (f), so "(b) to (e)" is not what is built. (b) The input stage, a learned constant concatenated with a linear projection of $z$, appears to be the book's own design; it is not StyleGAN's (constant input, $z$ through modulation) and I do not believe it is R3GAN's. Say so. (c) The stated reason does not hold up: `z_proj` is a linear layer with a bias, so its output already contains a learned constant $4 \times 4 \times 100$ map, and the convolution that follows has a bias too. The extra 128 constant channels add capacity to "what all samples share" but no new kind of term. Either give the real reason (it was found to help, with the evidence), or drop the constant and simplify the class and the figure.

**DCG-7** (moderate, explanation: two normalizations of one loss). "Training with the Classic Loss" and "Three Arms". Anchors: "The printed losses are per image; at the equilibrium both would equal $\log 2 \approx 0.69$." and "the classic and pairing losses equal $2 \log 2 \approx 1.39$ and $\log 2 \approx 0.69$"
The classic critic loss is printed twice in this section under two normalizations: `d2l.train_gan` reports the halved sum (equilibrium $\log 2$; stored 0.24 and 0.11), and `d2l.gan_loss_D` the sum of two means (equilibrium $2\log 2$; stored 0.66 and 0.58 for the unpenalized arm). A reader who compares DCGAN's 0.24 with the collapsed arm's 0.66 concludes that the collapsed critic is the weaker one, and a reader who remembers "0.69" from two pages earlier sees 0.66 and reads equilibrium. Fix at the source: one normalization for the chapter (see CONV-15 in the review of `convergence.md`). Until then, print the equilibrium value beside each loss.

**DCG-8** (minor, explanation). "Three Arms". Anchors: "Because $\gamma$ is a squared length on the input scale (:numref:`sec_gan_convergence`), a value tuned on one dataset does not transfer to another", "The penalty adds about a tenth to a fifth to the loss."
(a) The previous section ended with "$\gamma = 10$ blurs the grid"; here $\gamma = 10$ is the working value and the reader is told only that values do not transfer. One clause with the reason for the size would help: an image has 12,288 coordinates, so distances between images are roughly a hundred times those on the grid. (b) "About a tenth to a fifth" is ambiguous between a fraction of the loss (stored: 0.133 of 1.06 and 0.085 of 0.50, so 13% and 17%) and an absolute amount (0.085 is below a tenth). Write "between an eighth and a sixth of the loss".

**DCG-9** (major, thought: explanation not carried by the evidence). "Samples of the Three Arms". Anchors: "so the non-saturating generator, which weights a sample by $1 - w_{\mathrm{Rp}}$, gives every sample the same full push", ":numref:`sec_gan_convergence` showed that gradient training circles the equilibrium unless something damps the critic near the data."
Three problems. (a) The collapsed arm trains the classic loss. Its non-saturating generator weights a sample by $\sigma(-D(x')) = 1 - w_{\mathrm{sat}}(x')$, not by $1 - w_{\mathrm{Rp}}(x')$; the rank weight is the weight of the pairing generator (:numref:`subsec_gan_rp_rank_weight`). For the classic arm the rank weight is a diagnostic, and the paragraph treats it as the mechanism. (b) "Gives every sample the same full push" is what :numref:`sec_gan_relativistic` presents as the purpose of the non-saturating form ("weight $\to 1$: the update is largest"). Here the same property is offered as the cause of collapse, with no step from "uniform weight" to "one grey texture". (c) The link to the previous section is loose: the Dirac-GAN shows rotation, and nothing here shows that this run rotated before it collapsed. The traces support three observations: the unpenalized critic is about a hundred times steeper on real images, its rank weight is mostly near zero with spikes, and its generator collapses. They do not order these in time or establish cause. Fix: report the observations, say "we do not know which comes first; Exercise 7 logs the steepness and the diversity together to find out", and remove the mechanism. If the authors want a mechanism in the text, the experiment that would support it is the one in exercise 7(a), run and plotted in the section.
A related sentence should go: "The collapse is a training failure, not a minimum of the loss" is the "not X; it is Y" pattern and is true of every collapse.

**DCG-10** (major, thought: the scores are not read candidly). "Scoring the Runs". Anchors: "DCGAN lands close to them: the 2015 architecture keeps trainable the same classic loss that collapses on the unnormalized backbone.", "Which of the two scores lower changes from run to run, so this budget does not separate them."
Stored (cell `dcgan-scoring-the-two-runs`), PyTorch: floor 1.26 / $-0.012$; DCGAN 3.54 / 0.181; NS 89.14 / 9.475; NS + $R_1$ + $R_2$ 5.69 / 0.877; RpGAN + $R_1$ + $R_2$ 4.22 / 0.454. JAX: floor 1.28 / $-0.010$; DCGAN 4.17 / 0.290; NS 72.81 / 7.465; 5.05 / 0.655; 4.31 / 0.485.
(a) In both tabs DCGAN has the lowest FD and by far the lowest $\mathrm{MMD}^2$ (2.5 to 5 times below the penalized arms in PyTorch, 1.7 to 2.3 times in JAX). It was trained on about the same number of images (20 epochs of 36,538 against 12,000 steps of 64) in a tenth of the time, and it is scored from its raw weights while the others get an EMA. "Lands close to them" understates the only ranking the scores provide beyond collapse. A reader will see the table and conclude either that the modern recipe is worse or that the authors did not look. The candid reading is available and is more interesting: "At this scale the 2015 network is at least as good and ten times cheaper. What the penalized loss buys is not a better score on sprites; it is that a network with none of DCGAN's devices trains at all, which is what lets the same loss scale to the architectures of :numref:`subsec_gan_r3gan_principles`. That claim is the R3GAN paper's and our notebook cannot test it." If the authors believe the gap is within noise, the noise has to be on the page (c).
(b) In both tabs RpGAN scores below NS + $R_1$ + $R_2$ on both scores, and the prose says the order "changes from run to run". The author's rule allows ranges from repeated runs, but here the claim contradicts what every reader of either tab sees, so it needs its evidence: the spread over seeds as a range ("over five seeds the two arms' FD ranged from ... to ..."), or a second seed run in the notebook.
(c) The instrument's resolution should be stated once, in numbers the section already has: the floor is 1.3, so bias is a quarter to a third of each good score; the feature network's seed moves FD by "less than a factor of 1.5"; the arms differ by a factor of 1.2 to 1.35. Then "does not separate them" follows from the table and not from unseen runs. A cheap improvement: score against all 4,059 held-out sprites and 4,000 samples instead of 500. The bias falls about eightfold and the cost is a few seconds of feature extraction.
(d) "The floor behaves as described above" and "three to five times the floor" are right (3.3 to 4.5). "Dozens of times higher" is right (57 to 70). "Between about one in eight and one in six" is right (0.133 to 0.161).

**DCG-11** (moderate, pedagogy: scale). "Samples of the Three Arms". Anchor: "The two penalized arms produce diverse, creature-shaped sprites on white backgrounds, with softer outlines than real sprites, and no difference between the two is visible."
Accurate. What is not said is how far these samples are from sprites: in the rendered grid no sample is a recognizable creature, and the DCGAN samples earlier ("creature-like shapes ... with plausible palettes and blotchy textures") are of the same kind. A textbook experiment does not have to produce good images, but it should tell the reader what budget would. One sentence would do: how many steps or images the R3GAN paper trains for at $64 \times 64$ against the 0.77 million images seen here (I do not have the figure; it is of the order of tens of millions). Without it "the same quality on sprites at this budget" invites the reading that this is what the method produces.

**DCG-12** (moderate, thought: focus). "Limited Data". Anchors: "Instead of estimating a density ratio, such a critic can start to recognize individual training images.", "Our loop flips only the real images, so the generator is trained toward the flipped distribution"
Neither subsection shows its phenomenon. The printed gaps are null results for the penalized arms (stored $+0.03$, $+0.02$ and $+0.05$, $+0.03$; "within a few hundredths of zero") and uninterpretable for the unpenalized one, as the text says. So "On limited data the critic memorizes first" (Summary) is a statement about another paper's experiments. "Augmenting Both Inputs" describes two papers' method and then notes that the section's own loop does the thing it warns against. Options: (a) demonstrate it, by training the RpGAN arm on 2,000 sprites, where the gap should open within the same budget, and print the gap and FD beside the full-data run; or (b) reduce both subsections to one paragraph placed where `sample_real` is introduced ("we flip real images only; this teaches the generator the flipped distribution, which is harmless for sprites if ..., and Exercise 10 works out when it is not") and move the rest to the further-reading file, keeping exercises 3, 9 and 10. I recommend (b) for length. Small point: "Forty thousand sprites is a small dataset" while the critic trains on 36,538.

**DCG-13** (moderate, pedagogy: code written for the next section). "The Backbone", "Three Arms". Anchors: "Both classes are saved: :numref:`sec_gan_conditional` reuses them at $32 \times 32$, which their `resolution` argument allows, and feeds the class label through the generator's `extra` input", "Given labels, it conditions each generated batch on the labels of its real batch, which :numref:`sec_gan_conditional` uses."
`train_r3gan` has fourteen parameters, of which `labels`, `eval_images`, `eval_labels` and the `y` plumbing are unused here; `Generator` carries `extra_ch` and `extra`; `sample_real` and `pair_stats` return or accept labels. Four sentences in this section explain arguments the reader cannot use yet. The reader of this section pays in attention for the convenience of the next. Fix: keep the saved signatures if the library needs them, but say it once ("the optional label arguments are for :numref:`sec_gan_conditional`; ignore them here") and delete the other three mentions.

**DCG-14** (minor, language). "Training Traces". Anchor: "The losses do not say how the critic separates the two sets, but three logged statistics do."
The paragraph that follows is clear and the reading of the three panels matches both rendered tabs (penalized steepness near 0.01, unpenalized between 0.1 and about 5; accuracy above 0.9 with dips; rank weight 0.25 to 0.4 against mostly below 0.2). One observation is missed and is the most instructive in the figure: with the penalty the ranking accuracy stays near 0.95 while the rank weight rises to about a third. The critic still orders nearly every pair, but by a small margin (score differences below one). That is what "limits how steep the critic is" means in the generator's terms, and it would replace the vaguer "the weight stays graded".

**DCG-15** (minor, correctness: one implicit comparison). "Scoring the Runs". Anchor: "gens = {'DCGAN': net_G, **{name: ema_G for name, (ema_G, _) in runs.items()}}" (cell `dcgan-scoring-the-runs-1`, once per tab)
The DCGAN generator is scored from its last iterate in evaluation mode and the three arms from their EMA. The prose does not say so. Given that DCGAN scores best without the average, a clause is needed; it strengthens DCG-10(a).

**DCG-16** (moderate, language: second ending). End of "Augmenting Both Inputs". Anchor: "We can now build an image generator from either decade, change its loss one ingredient at a time, and score the result against a floor whose meaning we know."
This is the "We can now ..." recap the author has asked to remove, directly above the Summary, which says the same. Delete the sentence. Keep the next two, which are a good handoff: "The generator still answers no request: it returns a random sprite, never the one we ask for."

**DCG-17** (minor, correctness). Summary. Anchor: "On limited data the critic memorizes first, and augmenting both of its inputs counters this."
See DCG-12: nothing in the section shows this. If "Limited Data" is cut down, the Summary sentence goes with it. The Summary is otherwise accurate but at seven sentences it lists the section; after the cuts it can state the three results: the penalty decides whether the plain network trains, the pairing makes no measurable difference here, and scores mean something only against a floor.

## For the chapter review

- The section is the chapter's only image experiment before `conditional.md` and the only place quality is scored. It assumes `d2l.train_gan` and the weights from `gan.md`, the Wasserstein-2 and MMD closed forms from `objectives.md`, the rank weight from `relativistic.md`, and the losses, penalties and step functions saved in `convergence.md`. It uses nothing from the appendix. It sets up `Generator`, `Discriminator`, `train_r3gan`, `fid_score`, `kid_score` and the feature network for `conditional.md`.
- Its three-arm design is the model for what `convergence.md`'s grid experiment should be.
- Chapter patterns seen here as well: a "We can now ..." recap before the Summary (also at the end of `relativistic.md`); code and prose generalized for a later section; two normalizations of the classic loss; the R3GAN paper's results used as the frame for experiments that are too small to test them.
