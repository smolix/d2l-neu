# Likelihood, Weighting and the Design Space

Source: `chapter_diffusion-models/design-space.md` (read to the `<!-- slides -->` marker). About 3,900 words of prose, 1,650 words of exercises, 13 code cells in the PyTorch tab. Stored outputs checked in `outputs/pytorch/` and `outputs/jax/`; `fig_diffusion-weightings` and the noise-predictor sample grid were rendered and looked at.

## Verdict

The mathematics of this section is exact and unusually well chosen: the bound's weight comes out as $\tfrac12(e^{\lambda_{k-1} - \lambda_k} - 1)$, a form I have not seen stated this cleanly elsewhere; every entry of the weighting table and every EDM coefficient checks; and "Noise Levels Are Scales" is a passage at the level of the model texts. But the section is three topics under one title (the likelihood bound, the weighting view, the design space), and the reader who asked the opening question, "which of the choices made along the way ... matter?", does not get an answer. The mixture experiment trains one model with one weighting and so cannot say whether the weighting matters; the Fashion-MNIST comparison changes target, weighting, path and level distribution together, on one seed at a third of the usual budget, and the two frameworks' stores disagree on the same row by more than several of the gaps the prose ranks. The single most important fix: make the experiments answer the section's question, by training the bound-weighted model next to the simple-loss model on the mixture, and by turning the four-recipe table into comparisons that change one thing at a time. A close second is to end with a recommendation: what a reader should choose for a new model, and why the book chose the velocity on the linear path.

## What works

- **The opening paragraph.** "For four sections we have trained networks by regression onto a per-example target and accepted the result because it worked". This is the voice the guide asks for: an author admitting what has been taken on trust and asking the density model's first question. Keep it word for word.
- **The weight as a log-SNR step.** "The bound charges every step by how much it lowers the log-SNR." The identity $\beta_k / (2(1 - \beta_k)\sigma_{k-1}^2) = \tfrac12(e^{\lambda_{k-1} - \lambda_k} - 1)$ is derived in two lines and turns the DDPM weights from a formula into a statement. I verified it against the printed table (at $k = 2$: $\tfrac12(e^{0.780} - 1) = 0.5906$, as stored).
- **The worked weighting.** The noise loss on the linear path, carried from $t/(1 - t) = e^{-\lambda/2}$ to $w = \tfrac14\cosh^{-2}(\lambda/4)$, "a bump centered at $\lambda = 0$, where signal and noise are equally strong". One case in full before the table; I rederived it and all six closed forms in the table, and they are right.
- **"A weighting in disguise."** "Since in practice nobody divides by $p(\lambda)$, a time distribution such as the logit-normal ... is a weighting in disguise." A sentence that makes a reader stop treating the time distribution and the loss weight as separate knobs.
- **Noise levels are scales.** A question ("What does a noise level remove from an image?"), a twelve-line cell that computes the answer on the reader's own data, a reading of the output (0.3 hides from 9 cycles, 1 from 4), and two consequences: coarse-to-fine sampling, and why a schedule must shift with resolution, with the $2 \times 2$ averaging argument in two sentences. This is the best passage in my two sections. It should stay intact through any restructuring.
- **Paired likelihood differences.** `bound` evaluates both predictors on shared points, levels and noise, and the prose says why. The network's excess over the exact field is resolved to a thousandth of a nat with a standard error printed. This is how such a comparison should be done.
- **The diagnosis of the noise predictor.** $\alpha_1 \approx 0.007$ "magnifies the noise predictor's error about 150-fold". A concrete number that explains a visible failure (the blotchy rows), and the sample grid shows exactly what the prose says.

## Structural findings

1. **Three topics.** "The Likelihood Bound" (about 1,500 words and five cells) answers "is this maximum likelihood?". "Every Objective Is a Weighting" (about 700 words, no cell) answers "how do the losses differ?". "The Design Space" (about 1,400 words and six cells) is EDM's preconditioning, the frequency view, the comparison and a paragraph on parameterization. The title lists them with a comma and an "and". At 3,900 words the section is inside the length ceiling, so I do not recommend a split on length. If the comparison is repaired as proposed below it will grow, and then the seam is before `## The Design Space`: the first two parts form "Likelihood and Weighting", the third "The Design Space" with EDM, scales and the comparison.
2. **The section's question is not answered.** See DSP-6 and DSP-12. A reader finishes knowing that all objectives are one integral, and not knowing which weighting, target or schedule to use, nor how much the choice is worth.
3. **Proportions.** The likelihood computation takes two functions of about 30 lines each per framework (`pf_nll`, `bound`) plus the surrounding prose, more than a quarter of the section, to establish a number that the Summary reports in half a sentence. The weighting view, which is the idea a practitioner will use, has no cell at all, and the weightings figure is pre-generated. The effort is spent where the payoff is smallest.
4. **Reliance on the appendix.** The ELBO is introduced as "the evidence lower bound (ELBO) of :numref:`sec_mdl-latent-em-elbo`". No earlier main-text section of the book derives it (I searched the GAN chapter and the three preceding sections of this one). So the main text cannot be read here without the appendix, against the author's rule. The fix is one line of Jensen's inequality (DSP-1). The Gaussian KL formula is taken from the GAN chapter by equation label twice and never restated; it is small and should be restated. The other appendix references are used correctly and all resolve to passages that hold the cited content (`sec_mdl-continuous-normalizing-flows` for the instantaneous change of variables, `subsec_mdl-paired-comparisons`, `sec_mdl-bayes-importance`, `eq_mdl-ode-hutchinson` in `mdl-neural-odes.md`).
5. **"Parameterization at Finite Capacity" is a stub.** Three sentences, one of them a paper's finding. It either becomes the reason for EDM's preconditioning (it is the same question: what should the network output at each level?) or goes to further reading. See DSP-14.

## Passage-level findings

**DSP-1** (moderate, explanation). "The Chain as a Latent-Variable Model". Quote: "but any distribution over the latents gives a lower bound on it, the evidence lower bound (ELBO) of". The bound that the whole first third rests on is cited, not shown, and the display that follows states it with the gap given in a separate sentence. A reader from the Basics has not met an ELBO. Fix: add the line $\log p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf{x}_0) = \log \mathbb{E}_{q}\big[p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf{x}_{0:T}) / q(\mathbf{x}_{1:T} \mid \mathbf{x}_0)\big] \ge \mathbb{E}_q \log[\cdots]$ with "by Jensen's inequality", then cite the appendix for the general theory.

**DSP-2** (moderate, explanation). "The Chain as a Latent-Variable Model". Quote: "The second line reverses each forward step by Bayes' rule with $\mathbf{x}_0$ held fixed". The three-line aligned display does the entire DDPM derivation, and the explanation comes after it in one paragraph that covers the Markov property, Bayes' rule, the telescoping product and a cancellation. The mathematics is right. The pace is that of a paper's appendix: a first-time reader has to match four clauses to three lines. Fix: say before the display why we rewrite ("The forward step $q(\mathbf{x}_k \mid \mathbf{x}_{k-1})$ runs the wrong way to be compared with the learned reverse step; Bayes' rule with $\mathbf{x}_0$ fixed turns it around"), and put the telescoping identity on its own line.

**DSP-3** (moderate, pedagogy). "The Chain as a Latent-Variable Model". Quote: "The smallest case shows the three kinds of terms. With one coordinate, $T = 2$ and". The example computes $L_2 = x_0^2/8 + 0.019$ (correct; I recomputed it) and then describes $L_1$ and $L_0$ in words. It stops at the point where it would start to teach: what the denoising term looks like when written out, and how large it is against the prior term. Exercise 8c asks the reader to finish it. Fix: either finish it in the text for a point-mass or Gaussian data distribution (two more lines), or shrink it to the one sentence about the prior term and say that the exercise completes it.

**DSP-4** (moderate, correctness). "From the Bound to Weighted Regression". Quotes: "Both are largest at the first steps, where the log-SNR falls by $\log(k/(k-1))$ per step" and "where the log-SNR falls by $\log 2$ from the first step to the second whatever $T$ is". The stored output of the cell just above contradicts the first: the half log-SNR step is 0.390 at $k = 2$ and 0.078 at $k = 10$, so the steps are 0.78 and 0.157, against $\log 2 = 0.69$ and $\log(10/9) = 0.105$. The formula assumes constant $\beta$; on this path $\int_0^t \beta = 0.1t + 9.95t^2$, and the quadratic term is already as large as the linear one at $k = 10$. For the second: the first-to-second drop is 0.78 at $T = 1000$ and 1.10 at $T = 100$ (I computed both); it tends to $\log 2$ from above as $T$ grows. I am sure of these numbers. Fix: "where the log-SNR falls by at least $\log(k/(k-1))$ per step" and "falls by more than $\log 2$ from the first step to the second for every $T$". The argument that follows (the chain's bound is loose at the data end) is unaffected and is in fact strengthened.

**DSP-5** (minor, explanation). "A Number for the Mixture". Quote: "`pf_nll` integrates the point and the divergence by fourth-order Runge--Kutta on 100 steps that crowd toward the data;". A 60-word sentence that describes five implementation choices. Fourth-order Runge-Kutta has not appeared in the main text (the previous section taught Euler and Heun); cite :numref:`sec_mdl-euler-runge-kutta` or use `heun_sample`'s method with more steps. Split the sentence: integrator and grid; where the velocity comes from; how the divergence is computed and what it costs in $d$ dimensions (the cost is the point exercise 5 builds on, and it is buried here in a subordinate clause).

**DSP-6** (major, thought). "A Number for the Mixture" and Summary. Quotes: "so on the mixture regression onto the noise is maximum likelihood to within about a hundredth of a nat." and "Diffusion training is maximum likelihood up to a weighting." The experiment shows that one network, trained with the simple loss on a two-dimensional mixture, has a density within 0.003 nats (flow) or 0.011 nats (bound) of the truth. It does not show that the simple loss "is" maximum likelihood, and it cannot say whether weighting matters: with a network this large on a problem this small, every weighting with full support has the same minimizer, the exact noise prediction, and any of them would land within a hundredth of a nat. The section poses "Is 'predict the noise' maximum likelihood in disguise" and then reads an experiment that cannot distinguish yes from no as a yes. The mathematics two subsections later gives the true answer (it is the bound's integrand with a non-constant, non-monotone weight, so it is not a likelihood bound at all), and that answer is in tension with the sentence quoted. Fix: train a second `TimeMLP` on the bound's weighting ($\lambda$ uniform, which `bound` already knows how to draw) and report the paired difference of the two models under both likelihood measures, at the full capacity and at a small one (say 32 hidden units), where the weightings should separate. Exercise 3b already describes most of this; it belongs in the text because it is the section's question. Then write the conclusion the data support, for example: "With capacity to spare the weighting does not matter: both models are within a hundredth of a nat of the truth. With a small network it does, by ..."

**DSP-7** (minor, language). "The Weighting Figure" (table), "The Log-SNR Form" and exercise 4. Quote: "| noise | cosine, $t$ uniform | $\cosh^{-1}(\lambda/2)$ |". $\cosh^{-1}$ is the standard notation for the inverse hyperbolic cosine. Here it means $1/\cosh$. The same hazard, less acute, applies to $\cosh^{-2}(\lambda/4)$. Write $\operatorname{sech}(\lambda/2)$ and $\operatorname{sech}^2(\lambda/4)$, or $1/\cosh$, in the table, the prose and the exercise (5 occurrences).

**DSP-8** (moderate, correctness). "The Weighting Figure". Quote: "By this criterion $\mathbf{v}$-prediction and flow matching are likelihood training of slightly noisy data". "Slightly" is wrong for this weighting, as far as I can derive. A monotone $w$ is a mixture of step functions with mixing density $-w'(\mu)$ over the noise level $\mu$ of the augmented data. For $w = e^{-\lambda/2}$ that density is $\tfrac12 e^{-\mu/2}$, which grows without bound toward low log-SNR: almost all of the mixture's mass sits on heavily noised data, and the unnoised data get the weight $e^{-\lambda_{\max}/2}$, which is tiny. So flow matching is the bound for data augmented with noise of a random level that is mostly large. I am confident of the calculation (about 85% that I have the theorem's normalization right; Kingma and Gao's statement is "the ELBO with data augmentation by Gaussian noise", without "slightly"). Fix: drop "slightly", or say "likelihood training of data noised to a random level, with heavy noise favoured". The corrected statement is more interesting: it says why these losses give good samples and poor likelihoods.

**DSP-9** (minor, correctness). "The Time Distribution as Importance Sampling". Quote: "where the bound's integrand is close to zero, and :citet:`Nichol.Dhariwal.2021` reweighted them for this reason". Unverified from the paper, but as I recall it, Nichol and Dhariwal's two remedies have different motives: the cosine schedule answers the wasted high-noise steps of the linear schedule, and the importance sampling of $k$ (which exercise 2c describes correctly) answers the gradient noise of the bound. The sentence merges them. Fix: "Nichol and Dhariwal changed the schedule for this reason, and sampled the steps of the bound by importance (exercise ...)".

**DSP-10** (moderate, explanation). "EDM's Table". Quote: "so it should see inputs and produce outputs of unit variance at every level. For data of standard deviation". The code then uses `sigma_data=0.5` on Fashion-MNIST in $[-1, 1]$ without checking it. From the commonly quoted statistics of the dataset (mean 0.286 and standard deviation 0.353 on $[0, 1]$; unverified here, the data are not on this machine) the pixels have standard deviation about 0.71 and mean about $-0.43$ after rescaling, so the root-mean-square pixel value is about 0.83, and the "unit variance" input is off by a factor of about 2 to 3 in variance at low noise. EDM's 0.5 is a value for the natural-image datasets of the paper. The derivation also assumes zero mean, which the text says only in exercise 10. Fix: print `X.std()` and `X.mean()` in the cell, set `sigma_data` from it or say that 0.5 is the paper's value and how far off it is here, and state the zero-mean assumption with the formula. Also say in one clause that EDM passes $\tfrac14 \ln \bar\sigma$ to the network where `EDM` passes the path's $t$; a reader comparing with the paper will look for the fourth coefficient.

**DSP-11** (minor, correctness). "Noise Levels Are Scales". Quote: "this kept Stable Diffusion from generating very bright or very dark images". The sentence before it is about "The VP path", this book's schedule, which ends at $\lambda \approx -10$. Stable Diffusion used a different schedule whose end is much less noisy (from memory of Lin et al., a terminal signal coefficient near 0.07, so $\lambda \approx -5$; unverified). At $\lambda = -10$ the leak would be about a tenth as large in amplitude. As written the reader infers that the book's own schedule has the defect at the reported strength. Fix: "Stable Diffusion's schedule ended at a still higher log-SNR, about $-5$, and at high resolution ... this kept it from ...".

**DSP-12** (major, thought). "A Recipe Ablation". Quotes: "We compare four recipes on Fashion-MNIST with everything else fixed" and "The recipes separate clearly. The velocity on the linear path scores best with every sampler, with EDM next". Four objections, in order of weight.

(a) It is not an ablation. The four recipes differ from one another in target, weighting, path and (for EDM) the distribution of training levels and the network's input and output scaling, two to four factors at a time. "Everything else fixed" is true only of the network and the budget. The text concedes the consequence itself: $\mathbf{v}$ on VP "trails the velocity model, a gap that :exref:`ex_design-space-implied-weighting` probes". An experiment whose main unexplained gap is handed to an exercise has not done its job.

(b) The noise between runs is as large as several of the gaps. The same recipe and sampler in the two stores: noise with $\eta = 0$, 14.9 and 21.4; velocity with $\eta = 1$, 3.9 and 6.8; velocity with $\eta = 0$, 2.95 and 4.02. The gap between "best" and "next" that the prose ranks: velocity against EDM with $\eta = 0$, 2.95 against 4.43 in PyTorch and 4.02 against 4.51 in JAX. These are different frameworks, not seeds, but they are the only evidence of run-to-run spread the reader has, and by it "The recipes separate clearly" holds for noise against the rest and for $\mathbf{v}$ on VP against velocity and EDM, and not for velocity against EDM.

(c) The budget. "5,000 updates leave all four models far from the data" means the table ranks how fast each recipe trains in the first third of the usual budget, not how good it is. For a noise predictor on a path with $\alpha_1 = 0.007$ this is the whole story, as the text explains well; for the others it is a confound. (The phrase also clashes with the previous section, where two to three times the floor is the reference quality of the thousand-step chain, and here "about three times that" is "far from the data".)

(d) "EDM, vp" is not the EDM of the table above it: it is sampled by DDIM on the VP grid and by Heun on the VP grid, not on the $\rho = 7$ grid. Given the previous section's finding that the $\rho = 7$ grid is worse on this model the choice is defensible, but it should be said.

Fix: replace the $4 \times 3$ table by comparisons that change one thing. (1) Target at fixed path and sampler: noise, $\mathbf{v}$ and (preconditioned) clean image on VP. This is three of the present rows and supports the present, good, explanation of the noise predictor's failure. (2) Weighting at fixed target: $\mathbf{v}$ on the cosine path against velocity on the linear path, which the table two subsections earlier says are the same objective. This is exercise 4d; it is the direct test of the section's central claim that only the weighting matters, and it belongs in the text. If the two match, the claim is confirmed on images; if not, the reader learns that the equivalence holds for the loss and not for a finite network and a finite budget. (3) Level distribution at fixed everything else: velocity with uniform against logit-normal $t$. State each conclusion only where the gap exceeds the spread between the two stores, and train at the budget of :numref:`sec_diffusion-images` if the compute allows.

**DSP-13** (minor, thought). "A Recipe Ablation". Quote: "and the noise loss gives these levels too little weight for a short run to make that error small". In terms of the noise error, the simple loss gives every $t$ the same weight; what the high-noise levels lack is not weight but precision: the target there is nearly the input, the loss is already tiny, and an error of one part in 150 is invisible to it. "Too little weight" is right only relative to the $\mathbf{v}$ or velocity weightings. Fix: "the noise loss is nearly satisfied there by copying the input, and has no reason to push the remaining error below the one part in 150 that the division needs".

**DSP-14** (moderate, correctness). "Parameterization at Finite Capacity". Quote: "while the noise fills every dimension, so the clean image is the simpler target at every level". "At every level" contradicts the EDM subsection 200 lines earlier, whose whole point is that the best target depends on the level: at low noise $F$ should predict (a multiple of) the noise, because predicting the clean image there means copying the input and any error is amplified when it is converted to a noise estimate, and only at high noise should it predict the clean image. The manifold argument in this paragraph is about what a network can represent; the EDM argument is about error amplification; the paragraph gives one and forgets the other. The subsection then rests on one sentence about one paper. I believe the description of Li and He's finding is accurate (pixel-space transformers on large patches work when the network predicts the clean image and fail with noise or velocity outputs; unverified in detail). Fix: merge the paragraph into "EDM's Table" as a second consideration next to variance: "EDM chooses the output so that its target has unit variance; a second consideration is what the network can represent ..." and drop "at every level". Or move it to further reading.

**DSP-15** (major, pedagogy). Summary. Quote: "The parameterization decides what a finite network must compute, and the schedule matters through its endpoints and the resolution." The section ends without a recommendation. A reader about to train a model wants to know: which target, which weighting or time distribution, which schedule end, and what to change with resolution. The material for an answer is all in the section. Draft:

> What should we choose? Predict a quantity that stays well scaled at both ends of the path: the velocity on the linear path, $\mathbf{v}$ on a variance-preserving one, or EDM's preconditioned output; a bare noise predictor needs a path that stops short of pure noise and a stochastic sampler to hide its error there. Let the path reach pure noise. Put the training levels where the image's structure is decided, around $\lambda = 0$ at this resolution, and shift them down by the logarithm of the pixel ratio as the resolution grows. After that the weighting is a matter of a few tenths of FD at our scale, and of likelihood against sample quality at any scale: monotone weightings are bounds, the peaked ones are not.

Whether the last sentence stands depends on the repaired experiments. The book's own choice (velocity, linear path, uniform $t$) should be named as such and defended here, since this is the section that claims to compare the choices.

**DSP-16** (minor, language). Opening, second paragraph. Quote: "The section ends with the design choices of :citet:`Karras.Aittala.Aila.ea.2022`, noise levels as image scales, and four recipes on Fashion-MNIST." The paragraph is a roadmap told through three papers, and its last sentence is a list. The first paragraph has already set the two questions; the second can be two sentences that say what will be shown (the bound is a weighted sum of the regressions we have been running; all published objectives are that sum with other weights; then we measure).

**DSP-17** (minor, explanation). "The Log-SNR Form". Quote: "Every target is affine in the noise prediction at fixed $\mathbf{x}_t$, so each squared error is". True, and the step from "affine" to "a scalar multiple of the noise error" needs that the linear part is a scalar, which holds for every target of the dictionary but is not said. One clause: "with a scalar slope that depends only on $t$".
