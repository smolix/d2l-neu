# Discrete Diffusion

Source: `chapter_diffusion-models/discrete-diffusion.md` (to the slides marker). About 3,950 words of prose, 2,300 words of exercises (10 problems), 38 code cells (PyTorch and JAX counted separately; several are shared). Stored outputs read for both frameworks; the masking figure and the stored sample grids rendered and inspected; the binary posterior example, the matrix product, both propositions, the linear-schedule weight, the continuous-time limit, the floor $H + I/T$ and Exercises 6b and 8a, 8b recomputed by hand.

## Verdict

The strongest of the three closing sections, and close to the bar. It teaches one result (the variational bound of an erasing chain is a weighted masked-token cross-entropy) with two short, complete proofs, and one phenomenon (tokens revealed together are drawn independently) on a two-token example where every quantity is a table that can be computed exactly: the error is $\mathrm{TV}(p^1 \otimes p^2, p)/S$, the bound of a perfect network is $H + I/T$, and confidence-ordered unmasking shrinks the smallest mode from 0.2 to 0.07 without any training at all. That is the method of MacKay: a case small enough to enumerate, chosen so that the general lesson is visible in it. I found no error in the mathematics. What holds it back is the frame around the mathematics. The opening motivates the section by a limitation of autoregressive models ("never revise a token once it is written") that the method taught shares, as the section itself says 500 lines later. The comparison with autoregressive language models, which is the question a reader of 2026 brings, is spread over three places and rests on one citation used for half of what its paper found. And the last body paragraph runs from BERT through order-agnostic models into the closing of the whole chapter without a break. The single most important fix: write the comparison with autoregressive models once, as its own short subsection, using the two-token table the section already has (the fixed order attains the entropy; $T$ parallel steps pay $I/T$), and separate the chapter's closing from it.

## What works

- "Adding a small real number to the index of the word "cat" does not produce a slightly noisy cat." The problem in one sentence, and the best opening image in the chapter. Protect it.
- "The chain never confuses one token with another; it only erases." (Singled out in October; still here, still right.)
- The binary flip example under "Transition Matrices": two steps, flip probability 0.1, posterior $(1/2, 1/2)$, "the flip is equally likely to have happened at the first step or at the second". It makes the posterior formula concrete with numbers a reader can do in the margin, before the formula is used.
- The collapse of the matrix product through $\mathbf{P}^2 = \mathbf{P}$, and the remark that $\bar\alpha_t$ "has a direct meaning, the probability that a token survives $t$ steps without being masked". The Gaussian chain's symbol is reused and given a plainer reading than it had.
- Both propositions are stated with their hypotheses and proved in under fifteen lines each; the $t = 1$ term is handled explicitly instead of waved through.
- The reading of the weight: "every step carries the same expected weighted count of masked positions, $L/T$ for $L$ tokens; the steps differ only in how hard their masked tokens are to predict." This is the sentence that makes $1/t$ understandable.
- The independence error on two tokens, and above all the sentence "The floor is not just looseness of the bound: the thirty-two-step sampler of an exact network models $(1 - 1/32)\,p + (1/32)\,p^1 \otimes p^2$ rather than $p$." The bound and the sampler are shown to agree about what is lost. Few treatments say this.
- The confidence-remasking cell: an exact computation, no training, a result the reader does not expect (one token per step, yet worse than two random jumps), and an honest sentence after it: "But the bound does not describe this sampler and cannot detect its bias."
- The speckle count is introduced with its limits: "The count is not a measure of sample quality; it measures the independence error".
- Exercises 7 and 8 (the floor for a general schedule; the order-agnostic bound through a Beta integral) are among the best in the part: each completes a claim of the text with a derivation of the right size.

## Structural findings

1. **The comparison with autoregressive models has no home.** It is the question the opening raises and it is answered in fragments: one clause under "Diffusion Language Models in Practice" ("In all of these samplers a revealed token is never revised"), one paragraph at the end of that subsection ("At equal compute on fresh data, autoregressive models reach a lower loss"), and the first half of "Masked Language Models as One Step" (BERT, order-agnostic models, left to right as a fixed order). Collect these into one subsection, placed after the experiments, with this content in this order: (a) one token per step in a fixed order is the language model of :numref:`sec_gpt`, and on the pair it attains the entropy (the "fixed order" row, TV 0.000); (b) a random order is an order-agnostic model and the continuous-time bound is its loss (now Exercise 8; state the result in the text); (c) $S$ parallel steps pay the independence error, $I/S$ on the pair; (d) what is gained (many tokens per evaluation, infilling) and what is not (likelihood at equal compute, revision); (e) BERT as one masking level. The material is all present. Only the order and the heading are missing.
2. **The motivation and the method disagree about revision.** See DISC-1. This needs a decision, not a patch: either the opening stops presenting "never revise" as the thing to fix, or the section says early that masked diffusion does not fix it and names what does (remasking samplers and uniform noise, both already in `further-reading.md`).
3. **The continuous-time Markov chain view is absent from the section and that is defensible, but the reader needs the bridge.** The section reaches continuous time as a Riemann sum of the discrete bound, which is the shortest route to the result and all that masked diffusion needs. Rate matrices, ratio ("concrete") scores and discrete flow matching live in four lines of `further-reading.md`. A reader who opens any 2024 to 2026 paper on the subject will meet those words first. Add two sentences after the continuous-time limit: that the same limit is a continuous-time Markov chain with a rate of masking $-\bar\alpha'/\bar\alpha$, that the literature often parameterizes the reverse chain by ratios of probabilities between sequences differing in one token, and that for the absorbing kernel such a ratio is a known time factor times the clean conditional the network here learns, with the pointer to further reading. The same applies to continuous diffusion on token embeddings, which the opening's "cat" sentence implicitly rules out and which further reading says is "returning": one clause.
4. **Uniform corruption is an exercise only.** Acceptable as a scope decision, with one condition: the text's reason for choosing the absorbing kernel ("it gave the best likelihoods on text in the comparisons of Austin et al., and its posterior is the simplest") should add what is given up, which is revision, and that is the link to finding 2.
5. **Two experiments, of unequal value.** The quantized mixture is the section's instrument and every number from it is used. The binary Fashion-MNIST experiment adds the code-length reading (0.17 against 0.70 bits per pixel) and the four-step failure picture, which is worth having. It does not add a comparison that would let the reader judge 0.17 (see DISC-13). At 3,950 words the section is the longest of the three; if length must come down, the image experiment's intermediate-states grid (cells `-5`) is the part that teaches least.
6. **Exercise volume.** 2,300 words of exercises against 3,950 of body (58%), ten multi-part problems, several of which are small research projects. The quality is high, but two results that the body's argument depends on are only in exercises: the order-agnostic equivalence (8) and the general floor (7). State both in the text and let the exercises derive them.

## Passage-level findings

**DISC-1** (moderate, thought). Under the opening. Anchor: "token at a time from left to right, and never revise a token once it is" The opening lists three properties of the language models of :numref:`sec_gpt` (tokens, left to right, no revision) and asks whether text can be generated "the way this chapter generates images". A reader takes all three as limitations to be lifted. Under "Diffusion Language Models in Practice" the section then says "In all of these samplers a revealed token is never revised, so an early mistake stays", and the figure caption says "a revealed token is never changed". Masked diffusion lifts the order, not the commitment. Fix in the opening: drop "and never revise a token once it is written", or keep it and add at the place where the absorbing kernel is chosen: "Like a left-to-right model, the erasing chain commits to each token once; chains that resample tokens, and samplers that mask them again, do not (further reading)."

**DISC-12** (moderate, correctness). Same subsection. Anchor: "At equal compute on fresh data, autoregressive models reach a lower loss" The citation is Prabhudesai et al., whose title and main finding are the opposite regime: diffusion wins when data are limited and reused, which this section's own Exercise 10 reports ("make better use of repeated data than autoregressive models"). ## Code and experiments

- `discrete-diffusion-parallel-reveals-and-the-independence-error-2`: exact table, entropy, mutual information, predicted error. No training, nothing that can drift; the right kind of cell.
- `discrete-diffusion-diffusion-language-models-in-practice` (four orders): the best cell in the three sections. The "fixed order" row (TV 0.000) is a check that cannot fail, but it is there as the reference row of a table, which is fine.
- `discrete-diffusion-the-quantized-mixture-3` (exact bound against floor): stored 4.856 against 4.855 in both frameworks; the prose reads it at the right precision ("of the order of a thousandth of a nat").
- `discrete-diffusion-the-quantized-mixture-4` (sampled TV): at 32 and 8 steps the measured distances (0.050, 0.067) sit close to the sampling floor of 20,000 draws over 1,024 cells (0.037), so the cell cannot show the $1/S$ law there; the prose concedes this ("the sampling floor and the model error add a few hundredths") and then says "The distance tracks", which holds only for $S = 1, 2$. With two tokens the $S$-step sampler's output table can be computed exactly from the network, as `exact_bound` already does for the loss: $q_S = (1 - 1/S)\,q_{\text{seq}} + (1/S)\,q^1 \otimes q^2$ with the network's own tables, summed over the jumps in which each token is revealed since the network is given the time. That removes the sampling floor and would show the law at every $S$. Recommended.
- `discrete-diffusion-images-as-binary-tokens-3`: 0.170 and 0.702 bits per pixel in both frameworks; "about 135 bits" and "about 550" are right (133 and 550).
- `discrete-diffusion-images-as-binary-tokens-7`: 0.0037, 0.0067/0.0076, 0.0356/0.0334; "about twice" and "about nine times" hold in both.
- Numbers no cell prints: none in the body. Exercise 10c quotes "about 2.3 nats per byte after 1,000 updates, where `GPT` reaches about 1.3" from "a scoping run on the WikiText-103 bytes", a different dataset from the one the exercise uses and a run that is not in the book. This is an author's note (guide 4.5). Replace by a qualitative expectation or remove.
- `from scipy.special import ndtr, xlogy` sits in its own cell after the imports cell; fold it into the imports cell.
- Tabs: several cells are shared between frameworks (`%%tab pytorch, jax`), which keeps the prose unfragmented. No TensorFlow or MXNet material.
- Scale: appropriate. The pair model trains in seconds; the image model is 6,000 updates of the chapter's U-Net.

## For the chapter review

- The section closes the chapter and the Advanced part and currently does so by accident (DISC-16). The chapter review should decide whether the chapter has a closing at all, and where: a short final paragraph here, or nothing beyond the introduction's promise and the further-reading file.
- It assumes the variational bound from `design-space.md` (restated, not rederived, which is right), the mixture from `denoising.md`, `train_regression` and the U-Net. Appendix references (`sec_mdl-bayes-mcmc`, `sec_mdl-latent-em-elbo`, `sec_mdl-coding-compression`, `subsec_mdl-perplexity`, `sec_mdl-tv-pinsker`, `sec_mdl-information_theory`, `sec_mdl-mutual-information`) all resolve, and none is needed to follow the argument: entropy, mutual information and total variation are each used in a way the text defines on the spot.
- Seven appendix references in one section is the chapter's habit in concentrated form; here they are harmless because each is a parenthesis.
- Relation to the Language Models part: nothing here depends on the skeleton chapters. That part's plan depends on this section for the masked-diffusion bound, so the proposition's label (`subsec_diffusion-masked-ce`, `eq_diffusion-masked-loss`) should be treated as stable.
- Relation to chapter 11: the masked-token objective is taught in `encoders-decoders.md`; this section should cite it instead of the Attic's BERT section (DISC-17), and the two should agree on the 15% rate and the 80/10/10 rule.
- Across the three closing sections: `latent-diffusion.md` and `few-step.md` end with survey paragraphs and lean on single-seed FD differences; this section does neither, and its two-token device is the model the other two could follow (an exact small case instead of a noisy larger one).
