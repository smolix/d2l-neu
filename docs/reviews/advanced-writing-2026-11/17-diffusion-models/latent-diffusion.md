# Latent Diffusion and Transformers

Source: `chapter_diffusion-models/latent-diffusion.md` (to the slides marker). About 3,250 words of prose, 1,250 words of exercises (12 problems), 38 code cells (PyTorch and JAX counted separately). Stored outputs read for both frameworks; the two figures and the stored sample grids rendered and inspected; the compression arithmetic, the KL term, the log-SNR shift and the exercise formulas recomputed by hand.

## Verdict

A clear, honest and technically sound section that is not yet a memorable one. I found no wrong equation, the autoencoder and the DiT are described accurately and compactly enough to implement, and the prose reads its outputs at the right precision in both frameworks. Against the bar it has a structural problem and an evidential one. Structurally it answers three questions (why a latent, why a transformer, how a caption steers) and gives the third two paragraphs of citations with no equation, figure or experiment, although the opening promises it on equal terms. Evidentially, the two experiments are read for conclusions they cannot carry at this scale: the latent experiment shows the latent model losing to the pixel model (said honestly), and the scaling experiment shows only that a larger DiT is better than a smaller one, which would be true of any network. The claim that matters, that the transformer keeps improving where the U-Net does not, is never tested because the U-Net is run at one size, and "DiT-L beats it" rests on 1.41 against 1.50 in PyTorch, inside the ranges the section itself quotes. The single most important fix: make the scaling experiment a comparison (scale the U-Net too, or state plainly that the experiment reproduces the monotone trend and that the case for the transformer is the paper's, not ours), and either teach text conditioning with a small experiment or cut it to one sentence and a pointer.

## What works

- The cost argument is concrete and arithmetic from the first paragraph: "$512 \cdot 512 \cdot 3 = 786{,}432$ numbers", "48 times fewer numbers", "With one token per pixel the image would have 16 times as many tokens, and attention would cost 256 times as much." A reader can redo every number.
- The three requirements on a latent (known scale, no outliers, nearby latents decode alike) are stated before the VAE is introduced, so the KL term arrives as a remedy for a named problem. The cell that prints the latent standard deviation, the encoder's standard deviation and the excess kurtosis then checks each requirement with one number. Keep this pairing.
- "This bounds every latent model trained with this autoencoder: perfect latents would decode to reconstructions and score the same." The reconstruction FD as a floor is the idea a reader keeps, and it is set up early and used twice.
- The adaLN-Zero passage is the best in the section: one displayed block equation, one sentence on what the six vectors are, and the consequence of the zero initialization stated as a testable fact, which the cell then tests ("output at initialization all zero: True"). This is a check that could fail and teaches the mechanism.
- Candour: "At $28 \times 28$ the trade is not attractive: the pixel model is cheap anyway, and the autoencoder needs a training run of its own." The section does not pretend its small experiment shows the benefit.
- The exercises on the scale factor (1) and on patch 16 against the autoencoder (2c) ask the questions a sharp reader would ask of the main text.

## Structural findings

1. **Three questions, two answered.** The opening asks "Why do current systems diffuse something other than pixels, why is their network a transformer, and how does a caption steer it?" The first two get about 1,400 words and an experiment each. The third gets the subsection "Cross-Attention and Joint Attention", one paragraph that names CLIP, T5, cross-attention, MMDiT, caption dropout and the logit-normal time distribution, and "Video", one paragraph of three citations. This is the survey ending that guide section 12.2 rules out, and the reader cannot build anything from it. Two acceptable outcomes: (a) teach it, with the label of Fashion-MNIST as a one-token "caption" entering the DiT through a cross-attention sublayer, compared against the adaLN label path the section already has, which would also give the adaLN-versus-cross-attention comparison of Peebles and Xie at our scale; or (b) drop the third question from the opening, keep two sentences on how a caption enters, and move the model names and the video paragraph to `further-reading.md`, which already has "Latent Spaces and Backbones" and "Video" entries. I recommend (b) unless the Image Models part is not going to teach text conditioning; the opening says "Systems at full scale are the subject of a later part of this book", with no label, and nothing in the current tree stands behind it.
2. **The section is two sections that share one experiment.** "Why a Latent Space" plus "Diffusion in the Latent Space" is one complete argument (cost, autoencoder, same regression, floor). "The Transformer Backbone" is another (tokens, conditioning, scaling). They meet only in the last cell (latent DiT-M). At 3,250 words a split is not required, but the title's "and" is accurate: the reviser should treat them as two arguments with one closing paragraph that joins them, not as one.
3. **The explanation of the latent result is deferred to an exercise.** The section diagnoses the gap between latent samples and reconstructions as "the decoder's response to latents that no training image produced", which is the third requirement of "The Autoencoder" (nearby latents decode alike) failing. With $\beta = 10^{-4}$ and an encoder standard deviation of 0.02 to 0.03 against a latent scale of 1, the KL term does nothing to smooth the decoder, and the text says as much ("the KL term barely blurs each code"). The reader is not told that these two facts are connected, and the experiment that would show it (the $\beta$ sweep) is Exercise 7. One sentence closing the loop is needed where the result is read; better, run two values of $\beta$ in the text.
4. **Missing for a 2026 reader, in one clause each:** that pixel-space transformers have returned (the section's own Exercise 5 and `further-reading.md` know this, the body says only "most large text-to-image systems since 2022 diffuse latents"); and why our reconstructions are sharp without the patch critic and perceptual loss that the text says large autoencoders need (our code keeps a quarter of the numbers of a simple binary-like image, Stable Diffusion's keeps a forty-eighth of a photograph).

**LAT-3** (minor, correctness). Under "The Cost of Pixels". Anchor: "Rate–distortion theory makes this precise: for each level of distortion," Rate-distortion theory with a squared-error distortion does not make "a viewer rarely notices a wrong texture" precise; it is silent about perception, which is exactly why the perceptual loss exists. The sentence is also the only use of the appendix reference here. Fix: "Rate-distortion theory gives the vocabulary: for each level of distortion the rate-distortion function is the fewest bits a code needs (ref). Rombach et al. observed that ...". Sure of this.

**LAT-4** (minor, explanation). Under "The Cost of Pixels". Anchor: "calls this a trade between rate, distortion and" The three-way trade is named and attributed, then dropped. It is the organizing idea of the whole first half (rate = 196 numbers, distortion = reconstruction FD, modelability = decoded FD minus reconstruction FD). Use the three words when the three numbers are measured, or do not introduce them.

**LAT-5** (moderate, explanation). Under "The Autoencoder". Anchor: "reconstruction stays in charge, and the KL term only stops the encoder from" The paragraph before lists three properties the VAE "supplies"; this sentence says that at our weight it supplies only the first two (scale, no blow-up). The reader is left unsure whether the smoothness property holds. Say it: "At this weight the KL term fixes the scale and keeps the encoder's noise from collapsing; it is far too weak to make neighbouring latents decode alike, and we shall see the cost of that below." Then cash it in at LAT-9.

**LAT-6** (minor, explanation). Under "The Autoencoder". Anchor: "The autoencoders of large systems add a patch critic and a perceptual loss," Agrees with `adversarial-losses.md` ("add a critic that scores image patches, because the code does not fix the exact texture"), and the citation of :numref:`sec_gan_beyond` is correct. Two gaps: the reader will wonder why ours needs neither (see structural finding 4), and "others quantize the latent into discrete tokens" gives VQ regularization half a sentence with no word on why one would (a bounded code with no scale to calibrate, and the input that :numref:`sec_diffusion-discrete` needs). Add one clause each. Note also that the GAN chapter reports ViTok-v2 replacing the adversarial term by a fixed perceptual loss; "add a patch critic" should read "have usually added".

**LAT-7** (minor, correctness). Under "The Autoencoder". Anchor: "1,000 the floor is about six times higher." No cell here prints the floor at 1,000 images. The next section prints it: 0.79 (PyTorch) and 0.86 (JAX) against 0.17 here, a factor of 4.6 to 5. Write "about five times higher" and cite the cell in :numref:`sec_diffusion-few-step`, or drop the number.

**LAT-8** (minor, correctness). Under "The Autoencoder" and "Reading the Result". Anchors: "This bounds every latent model" and "It cannot fall below the reconstruction FD of 0.6 to 0.8, and it sits". A Fréchet distance between fitted Gaussians is not monotone under "is a worse generator", so "cannot" is too strong: a sampler could in principle score below the reconstructions. What is true is that samples decoded from the true latent distribution are reconstructions, so a perfect latent model scores the reconstruction FD. Fix: "A perfect latent model would score this FD; in practice none goes below it."

**LAT-9** (moderate, thought). Under "Reading the Result". Anchor: "decoder's response to latents that no training image produced." This is the section's explanation of its main negative result and it is asserted, not shown. The two candidate causes (latent model error, decoder off-distribution) could be separated cheaply: decode training latents perturbed by Gaussian noise of the size of the latent model's error and score them. Without that, write "we cannot separate the two here" and point to Exercises 6 and 7, and connect to LAT-5.

**LAT-10** (moderate, correctness). Under "Reading the Result". Anchor: "times cheaper. This is why most large text-to-image systems since 2022" The factor 4,096 compares latents with $2 \times 2$ patches against pixels with $2 \times 2$ patches, which nobody would run. The honest comparison is the one Exercise 2c makes: patch 16 on pixels gives the same 1,024 tokens at almost the same cost, so for a transformer the autoencoder does not save tokens; it replaces a linear patch embedding by a learned nonlinear one. As written, the main text gives a reason for latents that its own exercise refutes. Fix: keep the factor of 48 for convolutions, and for the transformer say that a patch must then hold $16 \times 16 \times 3 = 768$ raw pixel values, which a linear embedding of a patch that size handles badly unless the network predicts the clean image (the point of Exercise 5 and of JiT in further reading). Sure of the arithmetic; the judgment about practice is the further-reading file's own.

**LAT-11** (minor, language). Under "Reading the Result". Anchor: "diffuse latents, and why their autoencoder limits their quality, a limit we" The sentence ends the subsection on a forward promise ("a limit we measure once we have a second network to try") that reads as a cliffhanger. End on the result.

**LAT-12** (minor, explanation). Under "Adaptive Layer Norm". Anchor: "one linear function of $\mathbf{c}$, shared by all tokens of an image. One" The code applies a SiLU before the linear map (`self.ada = nn.Sequential(nn.SiLU(), nn.Linear(...))`), so the six vectors are not a linear function of $\mathbf{c}$. Say "one linear layer applied to $\mathbf{c}$ after a nonlinearity", or drop the SiLU claim from the prose by defining $\mathbf{c}$ after it.

**LAT-13** (minor, explanation). Under "Adaptive Layer Norm". Anchor: "appending the condition as extra tokens, and found that the cheapest worked" The comparison is retold, not shown; and the reader has just met, in :numref:`sec_diffusion-images`, a U-Net that adds the time embedding inside each block. **LAT-14** (major, correctness). Under "Scaling with Compute". Anchor: "GFLOP, matches or slightly beats DiT-M, which spends the same arithmetic with six times the" and the sentence it ends, "and DiT-L beats it." Stored outputs: PyTorch DiT-L 1.41, U-Net 1.50; JAX DiT-L 1.19, U-Net 1.62. The section's own ranges are "1.2 to 1.5" for DiT-L and "1.5 to 1.6" for the U-Net, which touch. **LAT-15** (major, pedagogy). Under "Scaling with Compute". Anchor: "much on a small budget, and the transformer overtakes the U-Net only by" The paragraph concludes "What makes it the backbone at scale is that it keeps improving as it grows". The experiment grew only the DiT. A U-Net at `ch=96` and `ch=128` would very likely also improve, so the plot (three DiT points and one U-Net diamond) shows no difference between the backbones in how they scale, which is the claim. Either add two U-Net sizes to the plot, which turns it into the comparison the prose makes, or reword: "Our three points reproduce the trend at small scale. The house rule is one imports cell; either add it to the preface imports or say in prose why it is local. The cell IDs `-5` and `-4` are out of order in the file (harmless).
- Stored numbers against prose: all quoted figures match both frameworks (2.40M, 0.17, 0.65/0.79, 0.94/0.95, 1.50/1.62, 2.03/2.37, 15/11 and 203/192 GFLOP, 7.39M, the four-row table, 1.90/2.26). - Numbers no cell prints: the floor at 1,000 images (LAT-7).
- Cost. The section trains seven networks for 20,000 updates each. By the printed GFLOP counts that is roughly 23,000,000 GFLOP for the six diffusion runs, of which DiT-L is half. No runtime is printed or stated. On a single consumer GPU this is tens of minutes; on a laptop without one it is many hours (my estimate, not measured). Say what the reader should expect, and consider 10,000 updates for DiT-L or dropping it to an exercise if LAT-15 is resolved by adding U-Net sizes instead.
- Tabs: the two tabs do not fragment the prose; there are no tab-specific paragraphs. No TensorFlow or MXNet material.
