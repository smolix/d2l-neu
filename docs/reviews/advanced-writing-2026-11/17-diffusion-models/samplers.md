# Samplers, Inversion and Editing

Source: `chapter_diffusion-models/samplers.md` (read to the `<!-- slides -->` marker). About 3,600 words of prose, 1,700 words of exercises, 17 code cells in the PyTorch tab. Stored outputs checked in `outputs/pytorch/` and `outputs/jax/`; the FD plot, the reconstruction grid, the editing grid and `fig_diffusion-two-samplers` were rendered and looked at.

## Verdict

This is one of the better sections I have read in the Advanced part, and its first half is close to the bar. The DDIM family is derived in half a page from one observation (training fixes the marginals and nothing else), the equivalence with Euler's method falls out of a change of coordinates, and quality is measured, with a real-against-real floor and a count of network evaluations, not looked at. What the section does not do is finish the argument it starts. It measures seven sampler and grid combinations and then stops without telling the reader how to choose a sampler and a step count for a model they have not seen; the claim that fresh noise corrects errors is never put to a test the stochastic sampler could win; and several sentences read differences of a tenth or two of FD inside what the section's own numbers show is a plateau. The single most important fix: end "Fewer Steps" with a decision procedure (what to try first, how to know when to stop adding steps, when a second-order method and when noise pay), supported by one added row (deterministic and stochastic DDIM at 1,000 steps) and by saying plainly that Heun's curve is flat from about 20 evaluations because the remaining error is the model's.

## What works

- **The opening.** "Which of the thousand steps do we need, and how few can we take?" followed by the second question about running the field backwards. Two questions, both answered by the section, both felt by a reader who has just waited for a thousand-step chain. Keep it as it is.
- **The organizing idea is stated once and carries everything.** "The regression ... sees single noisy points $\mathbf{x}_t$ of training examples, never a chain of them, so it determines the distribution of $\mathbf{x}_t$ at each time and nothing more." The family :eqref:`eq_diffusion-ddim-family` then follows in four lines, with the variance bookkeeping visible ("The two noise variances add to $\sigma_s^2$"). This is the best short derivation of DDIM I know of in a textbook. Preserve it.
- **The collapse example.** Standard Gaussian data on the VP path, one stride from $t = 1$ to $t = 0$ returns $\alpha_1 \mathbf{x}_1$ and "the whole cloud collapses onto its mean". A sharp, MacKay-style case: the exact answer is "move nothing", and the sampler's error is visible without any computation.
- **The worked Heun step.** From $\bar\sigma = 1$ to $0.5$: exact factor $0.791$, Euler $0.75$, Heun $0.80$. I recomputed all three ($0.7906$, $0.75$, $0.80$; errors $0.041$ and $0.009$). A reader can redo it by hand, and it makes "a fifth of the error for twice the cost" concrete.
- **Measurement.** FD against a floor from two disjoint real sets, the same 1,000 noise images for every sampler, evaluations counted instead of steps (including the $2n - 1$ and $2n - 2$ accounting for Heun). The printed grids of $\bar\sigma$ for the three schedules are an excellent small cell: three rows of numbers that explain more than a figure would.
- **Honest negative result.** The crowded EDM grid loses on this model and the section says so instead of repeating the paper's recommendation. That is the candid voice the guide asks for.
- **The two stochastic samplers kept apart.** The paragraph beginning "Two stochastic samplers must be kept apart" (forward-step variance against posterior variance, factor $\sigma_s^2/\sigma_t^2$) clears up a confusion that most presentations leave in place.
- **Editing by class means.** Using the ten class-mean images as the "rough input" is a cheap, clever stand-in for a sketch, and it lets fidelity and realism both be printed as numbers.

## Structural findings

1. **The section has two halves of unequal quality, but should not be split.** "One Field, Two Samplers" and "Fewer Steps" (about 2,300 words) are a complete argument. "Inversion and Editing" (about 1,100 words) is four short applications, each with one cell and a paragraph. At 3,600 words there is no case for a split by length. The second half would improve by losing its weakest item (see SMP-17) and by tightening the inversion explanation, not by growing.
2. **The sampler study has no conclusion a reader can act on.** The brief for this part asks whether the reader ends able to choose a sampler and a step count for a new model and say why. They do not. The study ends with "On this model the even grid of the linear path serves both methods best", a statement about one model, and the Summary repeats numbers. Missing is the general procedure that the experiment itself demonstrates: fix the noise, double the evaluations until the score stops moving, and read the plateau as model error. See SMP-9 for a draft.
3. **Two things a 2026 reader will look for and not find.** (a) Multistep solvers. The solvers that practitioners use at 10 to 25 evaluations (DPM-Solver++ and its relatives) reuse the previous step's network output to get second order at one evaluation per step. The section's only second-order method costs two. One paragraph, or one row in the experiment, would close the gap; at present DPM-Solver gets a single sentence that is also imprecise (SMP-8). (b) Guidance. Every measurement is on an unconditional model. The previous section taught classifier-free guidance, which doubles the evaluations per step and changes the field being integrated. A reader choosing a sampler for a guided model gets no evidence here; exercise 11 uses `d2l.cfg` but only for editing.
4. **Dependence on the appendix is mostly healthy, with one exception.** The DDIM-is-Euler result is re-derived in three lines and the appendix is cited for the proof and the two corollaries: correct use under the author's rule. All appendix and cross-section labels resolve, and the cited passages hold what is claimed (`sec_mdl-ddim-guidance` has the proposition and both corollaries; `sec_mdl-sampling-learned-dynamics` has the Euler-against-Heun experiment; `sec_mdl-euler-runge-kutta` has Heun). The exception is the paragraph that retells the appendix's one-dimensional experiment (SMP-6): it cannot be followed without the appendix, and the image experiment then overturns half of it.
5. **Order.** Sound. One local reordering: the sentence "Since the spread rewards contraction, and a stochastic sample has no thousand-step counterpart, we compare $\eta = 0$ with $\eta = 1$ on images instead" defers the comparison the previous subsection promised ("We expect the deterministic sampler to win at small budgets") by 250 lines. Say at the promise where it will be kept.

## Passage-level findings

**SMP-1** (minor, language). Opening, second paragraph. Quote: "separating two choices that earlier samplers had made together: the order of". The paragraph introduces the section through three papers, one sentence each. The first sentence earns its place because it states the idea. The Karras and Meng sentences are a roadmap by citation. Fix: keep the Song sentence, fold the other two attributions into the places where the work is done (both are cited again there), and let the third paragraph, which already is the roadmap, stand.

**SMP-2** (minor, pedagogy). "The DDIM Family and Euler's Method". Quote: "The two samplers agree to the rounding error of 32-bit arithmetic". Cell `samplers-the-ddim-family-and-euler-s-method-1` checks an identity that the text has just derived by substituting one equation into another. It cannot fail unless the library is wrong, and it teaches nothing the algebra did not. Under the author's decision on confirming cells, this is the one to drop in this section. The definitions it carries (`mix`, `vp`, `lin`, `pred_exact`, `z`) move to the next cell. If it stays, say what it is: a test of the two library functions.

**SMP-3** (moderate, correctness). "Stochastic and Deterministic Steps" and again in "Quality against Evaluations on Images". Quotes: "nearly agree; on fifty steps the ancestral sampler adds more noise than the" and "ten, because it adds more noise than the posterior allows; its images are". "More than the posterior allows" treats the posterior variance as the correct one. It is the correct variance only when the data are a single point. The section's own exercise 8 (`ex_samplers-optimal-variance`) has the reader show that the best Gaussian variance is $\tilde\sigma^2 + k^2 \bar\sigma_t^2 \bar e_t$, which lies between the posterior variance and the forward-step variance and equals the forward-step variance for standard Gaussian data. So the forward-step variance is an upper extreme, not a violation. Fix: "the ancestral sampler adds the largest variance any data could call for (exercise ...); for images, whose clean point is nearly determined at low noise, the right variance is close to the posterior's, and the excess shows as speckle." I am sure of the mathematics; the reading of the speckle is the section's and is plausible.

**SMP-4** (major, correctness). "Stochastic and Deterministic Steps" and the reading of the image experiment. Quotes: "catch up as the steps shorten." and "gap narrows to about a fifth at a hundred, as the trade-off above predicts." The subsection makes two claims: fresh noise corrects errors, and long stochastic steps add error. The experiment tests only the second. There is no budget at which a stochastic sampler beats a deterministic one at equal cost: $\eta = 0$ wins at 10, 20, 50 and 100 steps, and the deterministic curves stop at 100 while the ancestral chain goes to 1,000. The one place where the two meet is the plateau, where the frameworks disagree about the order (PyTorch: chain 2.21, Heun at 48 evaluations 1.96; JAX: chain 1.92, Heun 2.09). "As the trade-off predicts" therefore claims more than was shown: a narrowing gap is predicted equally by "noise only hurts, and hurts less on short steps". The Summary then states "Fresh noise corrects errors" as a result. Fix, in order of preference: (a) add `ddim_sample` at 1,000 steps with $\eta = 0$ and $\eta = 1$ to `families` (the cost equals the ancestral row that is already there), and report whether the stochastic sampler overtakes; (b) point forward to the one case in the chapter where noise visibly helps, the noise predictor of :numref:`sec_diffusion-design-space`, whose stochastic DDIM scores about 9 to 11 against 15 to 21 deterministic; (c) at the least, say that this experiment shows the cost of noise and not its benefit. Whatever the outcome of (a), it is a better lesson than the present sentence.

**SMP-5** (minor, explanation). "Skipping Steps on the Running Example". Quote: "Since the spread rewards contraction, and a stochastic". The sentence gives two reasons in fourteen words for not doing something, and a first-time reader will not decode "the spread rewards contraction" (a sampler that contracts too much scores a small spread, so the statistic cannot be used as "smaller is better"). Also, the reference value 0.474 is the thousand-step sampler's, while the data's own spread ("just under 0.5", :numref:`sec_diffusion-denoising`) is not printed here. Fix: print the data's spread next to the table, and replace the sentence by: "The spread cannot rank samplers, since contracting too much lowers it. For the stochastic samplers we therefore turn to images and a score that penalizes error in either direction."

**SMP-6** (moderate, thought). "Second Order and the Step Grid". Quote: "wins at every budget from ten evaluations when the steps are crowded toward". This is the sentence that was corrected recently. I checked it against `chapter_mdl-dynamics/mdl-flow-matching-transport.md` ("Solver Order at Equal Cost" and that section's Summary): the two now agree (Heun loses at 10 and 20 evaluations on equal steps and leads from 40; on the squared grid it wins at every budget; Euler's signed errors cancel to about an eighth on equal steps). It is also consistent with `mdl-odes-solvers.md`, which gives Heun as second order at two evaluations. So the statement is right. The problem is its role. The paragraph asks the reader to absorb an appendix experiment in one 60-word sentence, including a subtle mechanism ("Euler's step errors of opposite sign largely cancel while Heun's do not, an accident that crowding the steps removes"), only for the image experiment to contradict its practical conclusion two pages later. The main text cannot be followed here without the appendix. Fix: reduce to "Whether the second evaluation pays depends on where the steps are (:numref:`sec_mdl-sampling-learned-dynamics` works a one-dimensional case in which it does only on a grid crowded toward the data). Karras et al. crowd the steps by ..." and let the image experiment speak.

**SMP-7** (moderate, correctness). "Quality against Evaluations on Images". Quotes: "The one-dimensional result does not transfer, and the printed grids suggest" and "evaluations on larger images." Three problems. (a) Half of the one-dimensional result does transfer: in the appendix "the squared grid more than doubles [Euler's] error", and here crowding hurts Euler at every budget. What fails to transfer is Heun's gain. Say that. (b) The explanation (four steps wasted above $\bar\sigma = 4$, three below $0.12$ "where a $28 \times 28$ garment barely changes") is offered with "suggest" but never tested, and there are at least two rival explanations the section does not mention: the feature network behind the FD was trained to classify garments and may not see what the low-noise steps change, and the model was trained with uniform $t$ on the linear path, which puts about 2% of its draws below $\bar\sigma = 0.02$, whereas EDM trains its network where its sampler steps. One extra row would discriminate: the same grid with $\rho = 3$, or with $\bar\sigma_{\min}$ raised to $0.05$. (c) "On larger images": as far as I recall, the 35-evaluation figure in Karras et al. is for CIFAR-10 at $32 \times 32$ and the 79 for $64 \times 64$ (unverified from the paper, but I am fairly confident). $32 \times 32$ is not meaningfully larger than $28 \times 28$, so size cannot be the explanation; the step count (18 or more Heun steps against 5 and 10 here) and the training distribution are the likelier differences. Fix: state the finding, give the step-count and training-distribution reasons as the candidates, and drop "larger images" unless checked.

**SMP-8** (moderate, correctness). "Second Order and the Step Grid". Quote: "such as DPM-Solver :cite:`Lu.Zhou.Bao.ea.2022` take steps in $\log". An exponential integrator is not defined by where it puts its steps. It solves the linear part of the ODE exactly and approximates only the network term, expanding it in the half log-SNR. The step grid (usually even in $\log \bar\sigma$) is a separate choice, which is the very distinction this subsection has just credited to Karras et al. The sentence also misses the fact that would tie it to the section: the first-order member of that family is DDIM, so the change of coordinates the reader has just seen is the exponential-integrator idea, and the higher-order members differ in how they extrapolate $\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}$. Exercise 6 states DPM-Solver-2 correctly (I checked the update against the paper's form in these coordinates). Fix: "In these coordinates the linear part of the ODE has already been removed, which is what an exponential integrator does; DDIM is the first-order member of that family. DPM-Solver :cite:`Lu.Zhou.Bao.ea.2022` raises the order by evaluating $\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}$ at a midpoint in $\log\bar\sigma$ (exercise ...), and its multistep successors reuse the previous step's evaluation, reaching second order at one evaluation per step; these are the usual choice for noise-prediction models at 10 to 25 evaluations."

**SMP-9** (major, thought). End of "Quality against Evaluations on Images" and Summary. Quotes: "do the two grids tie. On this model the even grid of the linear path serves" and "chain at about fifty evaluations, with the grid mattering as much as the". The stored numbers show a plateau that the prose does not name. Heun on the linear grid: 7.1, 2.1, 2.0 (PyTorch) and 7.6, 2.4, 2.1 (JAX) at 8, 18 and 48 evaluations. The chain at 1,000: 2.2 and 1.9. Two real sets against each other: 0.8 to 0.9, itself moving by 0.05 to 0.08 between the two sections' draws. So from about 20 evaluations Heun's method has delivered what this model can give, and what remains is the model's error and the estimator's. Three sentences read differences inside this plateau as findings: crowding "hurts ... Heun's at eight and at forty-eight evaluations" (1.96 against 2.14, and 2.09 against 2.28), "only near twenty do the two grids tie", and the Summary's "matched the chain at about fifty evaluations" (in the PyTorch store Heun is below the chain already at 18). The section has the right experiment and stops before the lesson. Draft for the closing paragraph of the subsection:

> How should we choose for a new model? Fix a set of noise images and a score, start with Euler's method on the grid the model was trained on, and double the number of evaluations until the score stops moving; what remains is the model's error, and no sampler removes it. Here Euler is still improving at fifty evaluations, while Heun's method is flat from about twenty, at the level of the thousand-step chain: for this model about twenty evaluations of a second-order method are enough, and more buy nothing. Below ten evaluations no method on any grid comes close, which is the subject of :numref:`sec_diffusion-few-step`. Noise is worth its cost only when the budget is large or when the model's errors at high noise dominate (the noise predictor of :numref:`sec_diffusion-design-space` is such a case).

**SMP-10** (minor, language). "Second Order and the Step Grid" and "Quality against Evaluations on Images". Quotes: "= 6$, where all noisy points look alike, and reaches the data from $0.34$." and "4$, where all noisy images look alike, and three below $0.12$, where a $28". The same phrase with two thresholds (6 and 4) and no statement of what sets the threshold. One clause fixes it: noise dominates once $\bar\sigma$ is several times the data's standard deviation (about 0.5 to 0.7 per pixel here, and the mode separation of 5 on the mixture).

**SMP-11** (minor, correctness). "Quality against Evaluations on Images". Quote: "posterior allows; its images are". No cell shows the 50-step ancestral images; the four displayed rows are the 1,000-step chain, DDIM at 10 and 50, and Heun. "Speckled" is an observation the reader cannot check without doing exercise 7c. Fix: swap the first displayed row (the 1,000-step chain, whose only role is "does not match") for the 50-step ancestral images, or add them as a fifth row.

**SMP-12** (moderate, explanation). "The Latent Code". Quotes: "at the current point but tells it the noise level of the step's upper end" and "upper point, the encoder at the lower one, the only point it has; the two". The reader gets two descriptions of where the noise estimate is evaluated, and they have to be combined to understand the code: the encoder evaluates the network at the lower point but labels it with the upper level (`pred(x, a[i + 1], s[i + 1])`), on every step and not only on the first, where $\sigma = 0$ forces it. The displayed formula $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t'} = \bar{\mathbf{x}}_t + (\bar\sigma_{t'} - \bar\sigma_t)\, \hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}$ does not show the arguments of $\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}$ at all. This is not the usual DDIM inversion, which evaluates at $(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$ on every step after the first, and the choice may matter for the results (SMP-13). Fix: write the arguments, $\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_t, \bar\sigma_{t'})$; say in one sentence why the level is shifted on all steps (simplicity, or accuracy if that was measured); or shift only the first step.

**SMP-13** (moderate, correctness). "The Latent Code". Quotes: "the step size, since the learned field adds errors of its own: at fifty" and "standard deviation lies below one, approaching one only as the steps get". Two points. (a) The explanation for the slow decay (squared error 0.061, 0.039, 0.017, 0.010 at 10, 20, 50, 100 steps, so the root-mean-square error falls by a factor of 2.5 for ten times the steps) is asserted. The learned field is one candidate; the mislabelled level of SMP-12 and the stiffness of the field near the data are others. Exercise 5 has the reader run exactly the experiment that would decide (the exact predictor on the mixture). Move one line of it into the text, or replace "since" by "we suspect". (b) A code whose standard deviation is 0.55 to 0.80 instead of 1 is a poor inversion, and the text reports it in a neutral voice. An error of 0.010 per pixel at 100 steps on a $[-1, 1]$ scale is a root-mean-square error of 0.1, a twentieth of the range. The reader should be told that this is the weak point of DDIM inversion in practice and the reason later work added correction steps, not left to infer that the method works.

**SMP-14** (moderate, thought). "The Latent Code". Quote: "smaller by about $\sqrt{2}$, which noise images of this dimension almost". The argument for spherical interpolation is that a decoder must be fed points of typical norm. But the cell that precedes it has just printed that the 50-step codes have standard deviation about 0.72, that is, a norm 28% below typical, and they decode well (they are the third row of the reconstruction grid). So the stated reason does not apply to these codes: they decode because the decoder inverts the encoder, not because they look like noise. Spherical interpolation is still the right choice (it keeps the norm at that of the endpoints, whatever it is), but the justification should be that one. Fix: "A straight line shortens the code: halfway between two nearly orthogonal codes the norm is smaller by about $\sqrt 2$, and the decoder has never been started from such a point. Spherical interpolation keeps the norm of the endpoints."

**SMP-15** (minor, correctness). "Editing by Partial Noising". Quote: "keeps the silhouette of its mean image and gains the seams, prints and edges". The printed FD at $t_0 = 0.3$ is about 30 to 33, ten times that of any sampler in the section and a third of the unedited inputs' 89 to 93. In the rendered grid the $t_0 = 0.3$ row shows sharpened silhouettes with blotches, not real garments. The prose reads the images more kindly than its own number. Fix: "At $t_0 = 0.3$ the outputs are sharper than the mean images but still far from real garments (FD about 30); only at $0.7$ does the FD approach a sampler's, and by then class and pose are all that survive of the input." That is also the more useful lesson about the trade-off.

**SMP-16** (minor, explanation). "Stochastic and Deterministic Steps". Quote: "(:numref:`sec_mdl-time-reversal`). A member that adds noise of variance $h$". The decomposition of a stochastic step into a Langevin step plus an ODE step was derived in the main text of :numref:`sec_diffusion-gaussian-paths` (the display that splits the ancestral step "to first order into two"). Cite that, which the reader has, and keep the appendix as the second reference.

**SMP-17** (moderate, pedagogy). "Inpainting and Posterior Sampling". Quote: "measurement's log-likelihood to the score, as guidance did for a class label". Two things. The heading promises posterior sampling, and the subsection never says that replacement inpainting is not a sampler of the posterior given the known pixels (each step draws the unknown pixels as if the known ones were unconstrained, then overwrites; the only coupling is through the network's next look at the noisy copy). The seam is a symptom of that, and the text explains the seam without naming the cause. Second, the closing paragraph is a one-sentence pointer to a paper's method that the section does not do. Fix: name the approximation in one sentence; retitle the subsection "Inpainting"; move the Chung et al. sentence to further reading or to exercise 9, where RePaint already is.

**SMP-18** (minor, pedagogy). Figure `fig_diffusion-two-samplers`, panel (b). Six jagged 200-step paths overlap into one tangle in the middle of the panel; no single path can be followed from its circle to its cross. The caption's point ("may end in a different mode") is asserted by the caption, not shown by the picture. Three paths, or the same six with thinner lines and the first half of each path faded, would let the eye follow one.

**SMP-19** (minor, language). Pattern: "The cell" plus verb as the introduction of a cell, 4 instances ("The cell runs DDIM on the variance-preserving grid", "The cell saves `edm_grid` and `heun_sample`", "The cell encodes and decodes 100 test images", "The cell prints the squared distance per pixel"). Not frequent enough to be a tic, and the other eleven cells are introduced by the argument. Vary two of them when the passages are touched. No "we can now" recap before the Summary; one ending, as decided.

It is too coarse for the claims about Heun at 48 against 49 evaluations and about matching the chain: 1,000 samples, one training run, a floor of 0.8 to 0.9 and a spread of 0.3 between frameworks on the same row.

- `samplers-editing-by-partial-noising`: matches the store; over-read at $t_0 = 0.3$ (SMP-15).
- Inpainting and interpolation are judged by eye only. That is acceptable for illustrations, and exercise 9 measures the seam.
- Two tabs: the prose never refers to framework-specific behaviour and reads the same under both. No TensorFlow or MXNet material.

## For the chapter review

- Fits its slot: it answers the question the previous section's sampler left open and hands "why this loss" to the next. Its closing paragraph is a clean handoff.
- Classifier-free guidance (formula, effect on the distribution, scale and cost) lives entirely in `image-diffusion.md`. Whoever reviews `image-diffusion.md` should check the formula; from a skim it is the standard $\mathbf{u}_\varnothing + \gamma(\mathbf{u}_c - \mathbf{u}_\varnothing)$.
