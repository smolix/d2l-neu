# Modules and Model Construction

Source: `chapter_builders-guide/model-construction.md`. The PyTorch reader sees about 2,650 words of body prose (1,800 shared, 800 inside 18 PyTorch tab blocks) and about 560 words of exercises. The source holds about 5,000 words of body prose across the four tabs.

## Verdict

Good textbook prose with one structural problem. The section has a real idea (a model is a tree; the framework finds parameters by walking registered children; a child that is not registered is skipped without an error) and teaches it the right way: state it, build the mechanism by hand, break it, repair it. The plain-list failure is the best passage in the first half of the chapter. Against the bar, the section falls short in the second half, where four loosely related topics (residual connections, constants, eager and compiled execution, lazy initialization) are strung together under "the forward method is ordinary Python", and where the PyTorch reader is promised an example that only the TensorFlow and JAX tabs deliver. The most important fix: decide what the PyTorch reader's version of "Forward Computation" and "Lazy Initialization" teaches, and cut what is there only because another framework needs it.

## What works

- **The opening** ("stores its parameters in 291 arrays ... An array that is left out raises no error: it is simply never trained, or never saved."). A number, a consequence, and a threat, in six sentences. The figure 291 is correct for Llama 3 8B (32 blocks of 9 arrays, plus the embedding, the final norm and the output layer).
- **"Two details make this work."** (under "Subclasses of the Module Class"). The explanation that `self.hidden = ...` "is not an ordinary attribute assignment" is the moment the section stops being API description: it tells the reader what the framework does behind a line they have typed twenty times.
- **"Layers in a Plain List".** A question ("Does the framework still find the layers inside it?"), an experiment whose output is `(torch.Size([2, 10]), 0)`, and the sentence "The model computes, yet it owns zero parameters." Then the repair with the count 7,946 worked out as 20 · 256 + 256 and 256 · 10 + 10. This is the MacKay pattern (show the method failing, name the cause, repair it) and should be the model for the rest of the chapter.
- **Counting parameters from the config.** The hand count 200,960 + 4 · 131,584 + 2,570 = 729,866 is checked by the cell (stored output: 729,866 and 4,609,546), and "more than six times" matches (6.3).
- **Figure `fig_blocks`** carries weight: the highlighted path from the root to `1.body.0.weight` makes "the name of a parameter is its path" visible. Keep it.
- **Exercise 1** (predict what `apply`, `eval` and `load_state_dict` do to the unregistered list, then explain "all three from the same missing registration") is the best exercise in the first half.

## Structural findings

- **Four frameworks fragment this section more than any other in the first half.** There are 18 places where the prose splits into tabs, and the frameworks do not share the lesson: PyTorch and Gluon lose a plain list silently, NNX rejects it at construction, Keras tracks it. The shared prose asks one question and the four tabs give three different answers, so the PyTorch tab paragraph after the plain-list cell has to carry the whole lesson in 14 sentences. The section reads best when treated as the PyTorch section it is at heart.
- **"Forward Computation" has no single idea.** Its opening promises three uses of Python freedom. The first (a residual connection) introduces what the text itself calls "The most important example in this book". The second (a constant) exists to set up buffers in the next section. The third (a data-dependent loop) is shown only to TensorFlow and JAX readers. A reader finishes the subsection unable to say what it was about.
- **"Lazy Initialization" interrupts the arc and argues against itself.** It sits between forward computation and configs, explains a mechanism the PyTorch reader has used since chapter 2, and is followed at once by a config section that says "Every layer receives both of its widths, so the model needs no dry run before its parameters can be counted." The section never says which to prefer. It should, in one sentence, and the mechanism can shrink to half.
- **"Models from a Config" is the right ending** and is undersold. The claim that a checkpoint cannot tell ReLU from tanh ("nothing in them reveals a choice without parameters, such as the activation function") is the strongest argument in the section and arrives in the middle of a long paragraph.

## Passage-level findings

- **MC-1** (moderate, explanation). Opening, figure caption. Quote: "Gluon folds the ReLU into the first dense layer, so its second dense layer is child 1 of the body; Keras names the children after their classes." The caption of the section's key figure spends three of its five sentences on framework differences the reader has not met. Fix: caption only what the picture shows (edges are names, the path is the parameter's name); move the per-framework remarks into the tabs under "Counting" where the names are printed.
- **MC-2** (moderate, language). "Subclasses of the Module Class". Quote: "In PyTorch the forward method is `forward`." A one-sentence tab, mirrored by three others, to say that a method is called what the code two lines below shows. Six of the 18 tab blocks are of this kind. Fix: fold into the shared sentence ("a forward method, `forward` in PyTorch and Gluon, `call` in Keras, `__call__` in NNX").
- **MC-3** (major, pedagogy). "Layers in a Plain List", PyTorch tab. Quote: "A model that is only partly registered is worse." The case the text itself ranks as the worst (outer layers registered, blocks in a list, "the only sign is a model that learns less than it should") is described with "Suppose" and never run, while the less dangerous case (zero parameters, which the optimizer rejects) gets the cell. The paragraph that carries it is 14 sentences and covers five consequences, two devices and the repair. Fix: split the paragraph in three. Show the partly registered model: take one optimizer step and print which weights changed. That is five lines of code and turns the section's central warning from an assertion into an observation.
- **MC-4** (minor, thought). Same paragraph. Quote: "GPU the failure is at least loud: `Trainer(num_gpus=1)` moves only the". "At least" appears twice in the paragraph ("the optimizer at least complains") and the loud/silent distinction, which is the point, is buried under it. Fix: organize the paragraph by that distinction: what fails loudly (empty optimizer, device mismatch) and what fails silently (partial registration on one device, an empty `state_dict`).
- **MC-5** (major, pedagogy). "Forward Computation". Quote: "freedom three times: for a residual connection, for a constant, and for a". The PyTorch reader gets the first two. Under "Eager and Compiled Execution" the PyTorch tab has two sentences and no code; the data-dependent loop is shown and broken only in the TensorFlow and JAX tabs. Fix: either give PyTorch the loop (it runs, which is the point: eager execution allows it) and state the restriction as a pointer to the performance chapter, or drop "three times" and the subsection for PyTorch and Gluon and keep it as a tab-only remark where the training step is compiled.
- **MC-6** (moderate, thought). "Residual Connections". Quote: "The most important example in this book is the *residual connection*," The superlative is unearned here: the reader has not seen a network that needs one, and the paragraph has to assert in two sentences what the ResNet section argues over pages. The Jacobian remark that follows ("The Jacobian of the block is") has no consequence in this section. Fix: introduce the block for what it is here, the simplest module whose forward method is not a chain, and say in one sentence that later chapters build on it. Drop the Jacobian sentence or move it to the initialization section, which is where the scale of $\mathbf{x} + f(\mathbf{x})$ is analyzed.
- **MC-7** (moderate, thought). "Constants in the Forward Method". Quote: "self.alpha = torch.tensor(alpha)  # Fixed by design, never trained". The example motivates buffers with a scalar hyperparameter wrapped in a tensor. A reader will ask why `self.alpha = alpha` (a Python float, which needs no saving or moving and is what the JAX tab does) is not the answer, and the config subsection later in this same section says such choices belong in a config. The honest example of state that must travel with the model is an array computed from data, which the next section supplies. Fix: either cut this subsection and let the next section open the topic, or make the constant something a float cannot be (a per-feature scale vector).
- **MC-8** (minor, explanation). "Shapes from Data", PyTorch tab. Quote: "`Trainer` initializes the weights afterwards by calling the model's method" and, two paragraphs later, "The `Trainer` calls". The text says the `Trainer` of earlier chapters already calls `apply_init`, and then defines `apply_init` with `#@save` in the next cell. A front-to-back reader cannot tell whether this method is new. Fix: say plainly "this is the method the `Trainer` has been calling since :numref:`sec_linear_concise`; here is its definition".
- **MC-9** (moderate, pedagogy). "Initialization after a Dry Run". Quote: "Lazy layers spare us the arithmetic of input widths: in a deep model the". The paragraph weighs the benefit and the cost of lazy layers and stops without a verdict, while every model built afterwards in the chapter passes both widths. Fix: end with the rule the chapter follows: lazy layers for quick experiments on new data, explicit widths for anything that is counted, initialized by a rule, or built from a config.
- **MC-10** (minor, explanation). "Configs and Builders". Quote: "vary from one experiment to the next therefore belong in a small". The paragraph is nine sentences and makes three points (arrays do not rebuild a model; shapes reveal widths but not activations; numbers go in the config and topology stays in code). The second is the memorable one. Fix: open the subsection with it as a question: "Could you rebuild a model from its saved weights alone?"
- **MC-11** (minor, language). Summary. Quote: "A module owns parameters, child modules, and a forward computation. Layers," The summary is a faithful list of six facts. It would serve better as the three that matter: tree and names, registration and its silent failure, config plus code.

## Exercises

Six for the PyTorch reader, well graded. Exercise 1 is excellent. Exercise 4 (a module system from scratch in NumPy) is the right capstone and should stay. Exercise 5 ("Depth family") asks what a zero-block model can represent and for a training sweep; it tests the content of the MLP chapter, not of this section, and is the one to cut if the count must fall. Exercise 6 for JAX ("Shared children") is a different and harder exercise than exercise 6 in the other three tabs; that is acceptable but should be marked.
