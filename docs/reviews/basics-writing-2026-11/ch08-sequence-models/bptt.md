# Backpropagation Through Time

Source: `chapter_recurrent-neural-networks/bptt.md`. About 2,300 words of prose (all shared), 770 words of exercises, two schematic figures, one code cell.

## Verdict

The best section of the chapter and the closest to the bar. It opens with a recurrence a reader can compute in their head, gets $0.9^{40} \approx 0.015$ and $1.1^{40} \approx 45$ out of it before any derivative is written, and then shows that the general case keeps the same form. The paragraph on why clipping cannot help a vanishing gradient is the clearest statement of that point I have read. Two things hold it back. The only experiment confirms an identity that is true by construction, while the measurements that would connect the theory to the model the reader has just trained are left to starred exercises. And the derivation changes viewpoint between its scalar and vector halves without saying so.

## What works

- **The opening three paragraphs.** A question ("Where do such gradients come from?"), the mechanism in a sentence ("a gradient that travels backward through the sequence is multiplied by a similar factor at each step"), an image ("the way interest compounds a deposit"), the smallest case with numbers, and why both outcomes matter. "as forty years at 10% interest multiply a deposit 45-fold" is Strang's compound interest put to work. This is the model opening for the book.
- **Why vanishing is the worse failure.** "The model receives almost no signal about how a token forty steps back should have changed its prediction now, because that signal is buried under the gradients of nearby tokens". "Buried under" is exactly right: the gradient is a sum, and the reader will need that picture two pages later.
- **Shared weights by analogy.** "the gradient of a parameter that is used in many places is the sum of its gradients at each place, just as the gradient of a convolution kernel sums over the positions where the kernel is applied." It ties the new rule to chapter 6.
- **The abstraction $a_t = b_t + c_t a_{t-1}$**, followed by "Each $b_i$ enters once, multiplied by all the $c_j$ that come after it." The reader sees the structure before the partial derivatives clutter it.
- **Clipping and vanishing.** "rescaling multiplies every term by the same factor, so the balance between near and distant lags is unchanged. For a vanishing gradient that balance is the whole problem." This is the insight of the section and it follows from the sum the reader has just derived.
- **Truncation, quantified.** "never more than 31 steps, and about 16 on average. This is a hard limit."
- **The closing paragraph** says what the reader can now do and what remains, in four sentences.

## Structural findings

- **The experiment is a tautology** (BPTT-3) and the informative ones are exercises 5 and 6.
- **The link to the trained model is thin.** The model of the previous section has a $\tanh$, random nonsymmetric weights, and 32-step windows. The section analyses a linear recurrence with a symmetric matrix and says of the rest "The exercises explore what this changes" (BPTT-4). A reader wants to know whether their model's gradients vanish, and after how many steps.
- **Repetition with `rnn-implementation.md`.** The clipping formula appears a second time and the truncate, carry, detach paragraph a second time (IMP-11). If the two sections were swapped (see the chapter file) both would be said once, here.
- One numpy cell brings four framework import cells and a tab selector with it.

## Passage-level findings

- **BPTT-1** (minor, thought). Opening. Quote: "The RNN language model of :numref:`sec_rnn-scratch` trained only because we". "Trained only because we clipped" is stronger than anything the previous section showed (IMP-7). Until that section shows an unclipped run or a gradient trace, write "was trained with clipped gradients, because an occasional enormous gradient can undo many updates".
- **BPTT-2** (minor, language). "The Unrolled Computational Graph". Quote: "This recursion has the form $a_t = b_t + c_t a_{t-1}$ with $a_0 = 0$, since". The chapter took care in `rnn.md` to reserve $b$ for the minibatch size ("We write $b$ for the minibatch size"), and the next subsection here uses it that way ("a minibatch of $b$ examples") thirty lines after $b_t$ was a coefficient. The section also uses $g$ for the state update and $\mathbf{g}$ for the gradient, and $h$ for the scalar state and the number of hidden units. Fix: write the recursion as $a_t = u_t + c_t a_{t-1}$, and call the update $\phi$ or `step`.
- **BPTT-3** (moderate, pedagogy). "Vanishing and Exploding Gradients". Quote: "operator norm of its $k^\textrm{th}$ power is exactly $\rho^k$. We draw a". The text explains that for a symmetric matrix the norm of the $k$-th power is exactly $\rho^k$, then computes it and finds $0.9^{40}$ and $1.1^{40}$, "whatever the random matrix". The cell confirms an identity; the plot is three straight lines the reader could have drawn. Nothing is learned that the scalar example did not teach. Fix: use the cell for something the theory does not hand over for free. Best: take the model trained in the previous section and plot the norm of the gradient of the last-step loss with respect to the state $k$ steps earlier (this is exercise 6); the reader then sees their own model's memory horizon next to its 32-step window. Second best: a nonsymmetric matrix and a $\tanh$ recurrence at the same three radii (exercise 5), which shows transient growth at $\rho < 1$ and saturation taming $\rho > 1$.
- **BPTT-4** (moderate, explanation). "Vanishing and Exploding Gradients", end. Quote: "derivative, a number between 0 and 1 that depends on the state. The". The sentence continues "exercises explore what this changes." But the reader's model has a $\tanh$, and the natural question (does the linear analysis still apply to it?) deserves an answer in the text. Fix, three sentences: "Each step's Jacobian is then $\mathbf{W}_\textrm{hh}$ with each unit's entries scaled by $1 - h^2$, a factor at most 1. The nonlinearity can therefore only shrink the product: it makes vanishing more likely, most of all when units saturate, and it does not rule out explosion when $\mathbf{W}_\textrm{hh}$ is large and the units are near zero. A nonsymmetric matrix adds a transient: the product can grow for a while even when $\rho < 1$."
- **BPTT-5** (moderate, explanation). "Vanishing and Exploding Gradients". Quote: "it has the form of :eqref:`eq_bptt_at` with matrices. Unrolled, it reads". The scalar derivation tracked $\partial h_t / \partial w_\textrm{h}$ forward in time (how an early use of the weight reaches a later state). The vector derivation tracks $\mathbf{G}_t = \partial L / \partial \mathbf{H}_t$ backward (how a later loss reaches an earlier state). Both are sums of products of Jacobians, and the text says the second "has the form" of the first, but the index runs the other way ($j = t, \ldots, T$ against $i = 1, \ldots, t-1$) and a reader trying to match the two will be puzzled. Fix: one bridging sentence. "The scalar derivation followed the weight's influence forward from each step. Backpropagation computes the same sum from the other end: it carries the gradient of the loss backward, one state at a time."
- **BPTT-6** (minor, thought). "Vanishing and Exploding Gradients". Quote: "matrix. A gradient that has been shrunk to 1.5% or amplified 45-fold carries". "Carries little reliable information" is true of the shrunk gradient (it is swamped by nearer terms) and not obviously true of the amplified one, which dominates the sum. The two failures differ, as the opening said. Fix: "A contribution shrunk to 1.5% is lost among the nearer ones; one amplified 45-fold swamps them."
- **BPTT-7** (minor, explanation). "Truncated Backpropagation Through Time". Quote: "longer than a segment, however good its memory path is. The windows of 32". "Memory path" has not been defined (the nearest antecedent is "a path along which the state is copied" in the paragraph on gated cells). Fix: "however well its recurrence preserves the state".
- **BPTT-8** (minor, pedagogy). "Truncated Backpropagation Through Time". Quote: "Truncating at random lengths, and reweighting the surviving terms, gives an". A method the section does not use, cannot explain in the space, and does not return to. Cut the two sentences, keep "Fixed-length truncation remains the common choice" attached to the previous paragraph, and move the citation to Further Reading.
- **BPTT-9** (minor, pedagogy). "Remedies for Exploding and Vanishing Gradients". Quote: "nonlinearity from the state update and parameterize the state matrix so that". The paragraph on gated cells and linear recurrences is the chapter's main forward lean. It is done properly: each design is described by what it changes in the product of Jacobians, in two sentences, and nothing here depends on it. Keep it. One small fix: "inside the unit circle by construction" will mean little to a reader who has only seen real symmetric matrices in this section; "eigenvalues of magnitude at most 1" is enough.

## Exercises

Demanding and good. Exercise 1 (one step of the backward pass by hand, with the $\tanh$) and exercise 4 (implement it and compare with automatic differentiation) are a classic pair, credited to CMU 11-785. Exercise 2 (power iteration: the gradient aligns with the leading eigenvector) adds a direction to the section's statement about norms. Exercises 5 and 6 are the experiments the section should run itself (BPTT-3); if one is promoted, the other still stands. Exercise 3 ("Propose two methods ... One method should act on the parameters and the other on the optimizer step") is over-constrained in its wording and under-specified in its answer; either name the intended methods as hints or drop it.
