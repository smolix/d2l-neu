5. The $\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X}$ paragraph (SYN-2).
   - Before: "On the diagonal each product is either the square of the
     constant $1$ or a squared feature $z^2$, with mean $1$ and variance
     $\mathbb{E}[z^4] - 1 = 3 - 1 = 2$ for a standard normal $z$. These sums
     are therefore close to $n$, within about $\sqrt{2n}$. [...] For
     $n = 1{,}000$ the diagonal entries are $1{,}000$ for the column of ones
     and about $1{,}000 \pm 45$ for the features, and the entries off the
     diagonal are about $0 \pm 32$."
   - After: "In general the inverse of $\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X}$ is awkward
     to reason about. For our data, luckily, the matrix is simple. [...] On
     the diagonal we average the square of the constant $1$ or of a standard
     normal feature, and either has mean $1$. Off the diagonal we average the
     product of a feature with the constant or with the other, independent
     feature, which has mean $0$. An average over $n = 1{,}000$ examples lies
     close to its mean, here to within a few percent."

6. The threshold (SYN-3).
   - Before: "For least squares, an error above $10^{-3}$, more than three

   - After: "An error above $10^{-3}$, more than three standard errors, would
