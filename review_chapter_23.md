# Review of Chapter 23: Recommender Systems

## Scope

Reviewed all tracked Markdown sources in `chapter_recommender-systems/`: `index.md`, `recsys-intro.md`, `movielens.md`, `mf.md`, `autorec.md`, `ranking.md`, `neumf.md`, `seqrec.md`, `ctr.md`, `fm.md`, and `deepfm.md`. The audit covers narrative prose, section order, equations and notation, figure captions, experiments, summaries, exercises, and every slide block.

## Executive assessment

This is the least stylistically mature chapter in the reviewed appendix range. Its conceptual progression—data, explicit-feedback models, implicit ranking, sequence models, and feature-rich recommenders—is sensible, and several implementation comments have recently become precise. The surrounding prose, however, still has the generated-text artifacts targeted by the style guide: promotional openings, generic claims of “power” and “expressiveness,” malformed long sentences, mechanical transitions, vague references, and summaries that claim more than the experiment establishes. Several explanations also hide the decisive assumption: unobserved interactions are not observed negatives. Technical cleanup is needed around AutoRec masking, the BPR prior, evaluation splits, and Caser notation.

Scores (0–10): **writing quality 3.5**, **explanation/pedagogy 4.5**, **technical/logical quality 5.0**.

## Architecture and logical order

The broad order works, but the chapter lacks a single problem-driven spine. It should establish the observation model and evaluation protocol before presenting algorithms: explicit ratings versus implicit events; missing-not-at-random exposure; warm-start versus cold-start; rating prediction versus ranking; random versus chronological splits; and offline metrics. Only then should it move from matrix factorization to nonlinear, pairwise, sequential, and feature-rich models. At present, methodological qualifications appear late or not at all, so each model looks like an isolated recipe.

The chapter also devotes separate sections to older named architectures without helping the reader compare their inductive biases. A compact comparison table after the overview should state inputs, objective, negative-sampling assumption, prediction task, and evaluation protocol for MF, AutoRec, NeuMF, Caser, FM, and DeepFM.

## Math and notation

Highest-priority fixes are the AutoRec mask (`C23-08`), BPR prior/precision (`C23-11`), NeuMF recurrence (`C23-13`), and Caser filter shapes (`C23-15`). Across the chapter, choose one convention for user/item counts, embeddings, scores, observed sets, and masks. Define `I_u^+`, sampled-unobserved sets, and exposure separately. Place shapes immediately after the first equation and verify the prose against the implementation.

## Figures, captions, and slides

The diagrams are useful architectural anchors, but most captions are labels rather than explanations. The slides should not include import/setup material and should not inherit notebook headings verbatim. A stronger deck would use claim titles such as “Mask the reconstruction loss, not the parameters,” “BPR learns from sampled order constraints,” and “Chronological holdout changes the question being measured.”

## Code and experiment pedagogy

Implementation parity comments in `mf.md`, `neumf.md`, and `seqrec.md` are valuable and should be preserved. Add a short “what to observe” sentence before every result and an interpretation after it. Every comparison needs the split, candidate set, negative-sampling scheme, metric definition, seed policy, and tuning protocol. Avoid treating a single test set as validation. Report runtime shortcuts as limitations, not invisible implementation details.

## Recurring artifacts

- Marketing adjectives and unearned superlatives.
- “In this section, we will introduce …” and other itinerary prose.
- “Obviously,” “clearly,” “worth noting,” “on the one hand,” and “on the other hand.”
- Long paragraphs that combine motivation, definition, result, and qualification.
- Vague claims that nonlinearity “enriches expressiveness” without naming a representable interaction.
- Generic summaries and exercises that ask only to vary hyperparameters.
- Captions and slide titles that name a topic instead of communicating a claim.

## Strengths to preserve

- The progression from explicit feedback to ranking and sequence-aware models.
- Framework-parity notes that explain otherwise surprising numerical differences.
- The use of one compact dataset to make implementations reproducible.
- The complement-based negative-pool code and explicit exclusion of held-out positives.
- Architectural diagrams as recurring visual maps, once their captions are repaired.

## Prioritized revision plan

1. Correct the technical defects in AutoRec masking, BPR regularization, split methodology, NeuMF notation, and Caser operations.
2. Rewrite the overview around observation/exposure, task, split, and metric; add the cross-model comparison table.
3. Rewrite every section opening and summary in problem–mechanism–evidence order, removing promotional and itinerary prose.
4. Make experiments methodologically explicit and qualify all empirical comparisons.
5. Rewrite captions and slide titles as self-contained explanations and single claims.
6. Replace generic tuning exercises with derivations, counterexamples, protocol design, and ablations.
